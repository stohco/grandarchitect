You cannot guarantee literal perfection across an infinite procedural universe, but you can guarantee something nearly as valuable:

Every visible, physical, temporal, and behavioral result is traceable to explicit specifications, measured in consistent units, validated against references, and rejected when the engine cannot prove that it satisfies the intended design.

The bible cannot remain ordinary prose. It must become a multiverse ground-truth system that simultaneously serves as:

a creative design bible;
a visual reference bible;
a physical-scale specification;
a simulation contract;
a machine-readable schema;
an asset-production standard;
a procedural-generation grammar;
an engine-capability specification;
a validation and acceptance-test catalog.

The engine, asset pipeline, procedural generators, editor, and Grand Architect should all consume the same ground truth.

1. Establish a hierarchy of truth

Every claim in the bible must be classified. Otherwise the AI will mix firm requirements, aesthetic ideas, approximations, and generated details as though they were equally authoritative.

Use five truth levels:

Level	Meaning
Canonical invariant	Cannot be changed without formally revising the universe
Derived requirement	Logically follows from one or more canonical invariants
Art-direction decision	Deliberate visual or experiential interpretation
Procedural possibility	Valid range from which generated instances may vary
Unresolved question	Not yet established and must not be silently invented

For example:

Canonical invariant:
The capital city is constructed for beings averaging 2.4 meters tall.

Derived requirement:
Doors, stairs, furniture, ceiling heights, weapons, and camera framing
must reflect that body scale.

Art-direction decision:
The architecture should feel monumental but still inhabited.

Procedural possibility:
Ordinary door heights may vary from 3.0 to 3.8 meters depending on region.

Unresolved:
Whether ceremonial buildings use larger symbolic proportions.

The Grand Architect should never silently convert an unresolved question into a permanent fact.

Every unresolved issue needs one of these responses:

Ask the user.
Preserve it as a variable.
Create several labeled alternatives.
Use a temporary proxy marked as noncanonical.
Block final production until resolved.
2. Create one authoritative measurement system

Everything must use a consistent world metric standard.

Do not let one document describe a mountain as “enormous,” another asset use arbitrary Blender units, and a generator choose a different scale.

Use meters, seconds, kilograms, radians, Kelvin, and explicit game-time units internally.

Every physical entry should define:

interface PhysicalSpecification {
  dimensions: {
    widthMeters?: Range;
    heightMeters?: Range;
    depthMeters?: Range;
    diameterMeters?: Range;
    lengthMeters?: Range;
  };

  massKilograms?: Range;
  densityKgPerCubicMeter?: Range;

  speedMetersPerSecond?: Range;
  accelerationMetersPerSecondSquared?: Range;
  angularSpeedRadiansPerSecond?: Range;

  durationSeconds?: Range;
  reactionTimeSeconds?: Range;

  temperatureKelvin?: Range;
  forceNewtons?: Range;
  energyJoules?: Range;

  measurementConfidence: "exact" | "derived" | "art-directed" | "estimated";
  rationale: string;
}

The player does not need to see SI units in the UI. The engine needs them to preserve consistency.

Scale anchors

Define familiar anchors so the AI and user can judge dimensions visually:

Every editor viewport should offer optional scale overlays:

This will prevent visually impressive assets from quietly being five or fifty times the intended size.

3. Separate objective scale from perceived scale

Correct measurements alone do not guarantee that something feels correctly scaled.

A kilometer-wide fortress can look like a toy if it has:

oversized windows;
repetitive textures;
insufficient surface detail;
a wide field of view;
overly fast camera motion;
no atmospheric perspective;
tiny shadow distances;
no people or objects nearby for comparison.

The bible therefore needs two scale specifications:

Physical scale:
The measurable dimensions of the object.

Perceptual scale:
The visual cues required for the player to correctly perceive those dimensions.

For every massive object, document:

near, medium, and far detail frequencies;
recognizable human-scale features;
atmospheric effects;
texture density;
structural subdivisions;
camera constraints;
movement parallax;
sound delay or reverberation;
shadow behavior;
LOD transition requirements;
comparison anchors.

Example:

OBJECT: Celestial Mountain Gate
Physical height: 620 meters

Perceptual requirements:
- Human-height doors remain visible at the base.
- Main pillars include structural segments every 15–25 meters.
- Far surfaces use large material variation rather than tiled microtexture.
- Clouds may intersect the upper third.
- Footstep-scale props exist at ground level.
- Camera fly speed is reduced while entering the gate corridor.
- Audio uses long reverberation and delayed distant impacts.

5. Quantify speed and motion

Speed is frequently misrepresented because developers specify only a maximum value.

Every moving entity or effect needs a motion profile:

interface MotionProfile {
  idleBehavior: MotionDescription;
  startDelaySeconds: Range;
  accelerationCurve: CurveId;
  maximumSpeedMetersPerSecond: Range;
  decelerationCurve: CurveId;
  turnRateRadiansPerSecond: Range;
  minimumTurnRadiusMeters?: Range;
  anticipationSeconds?: Range;
  recoverySeconds?: Range;
  motionStyleTags: string[];
  cameraPresentation?: CameraMotionProfile;
}

A sword technique, for example, might require:

Anticipation: 0.18 seconds
Initial weapon acceleration: 90 m/s²
Peak blade speed: 38 m/s
Strike duration: 0.22 seconds
Impact pause: 0.06 seconds
Recovery: 0.35 seconds
Projectile speed: 120 m/s
Visible trail persistence: 0.3 seconds
Terrain effect delay: less than 1 simulation tick

The visual effect, hit detection, animation, sound, and terrain response must agree on the same temporal structure.

Different time layers

Document time at several levels:

Simulation time
Authoritative event timing

Animation time
Character pose and movement

VFX time
Particles, trails, impacts and aftermath

Perceived time
Camera shake, hit stop, slow motion and sound timing

Strategic time
Travel, cultivation, economies and history

A technique can be physically fast but visually readable through anticipation and lingering aftermath.

11. Validate visual correctness at several distances

Every visible thing needs distance bands.

Inspection distance
Player can examine fine details.

Interaction distance
Object must communicate function and material.

Gameplay distance
Silhouette and action readability dominate.

Regional distance
Mass, grouping and landmark identity dominate.

Celestial distance
Only macro-shape, atmosphere and light relationships remain.

A model can be accurate up close and completely unreadable at the normal camera distance.

The bible should state what information must survive each distance.

13. Accuracy for procedural generation

A procedural generator should never generate directly from vague tags.

Use this pipeline:

World truth
→ culture grammar
→ environmental constraints
→ historical state
→ functional requirement
→ structural blueprint
→ candidate generation
→ constraint solving
→ visual preview
→ validation
→ accepted runtime instance

Every generated object must retain provenance:

Which definitions influenced it?
Which style grammar shaped it?
Which generator produced it?
Which constraints were active?
Which seed stream was used?
Which compromises were made?
Which validation tests passed?

The Grand Architect should be able to click anything and explain why it looks the way it does.

14. Build contradiction detection into the bible

A giant bible will inevitably contradict itself unless continuously validated.

The system should detect:

inconsistent scale;
impossible travel times;
incompatible species proportions;
buildings too small for their users;
weapons larger than animation reach allows;
creatures whose wings cannot clear nearby geometry;
planets whose gravity conflicts with movement;
techniques whose stated speed disagrees with animation;
rooms inaccessible through their doors;
settlements without sufficient water or food;
VFX brighter than established art direction permits;
material combinations impossible under the world’s rules;
asset styles appearing outside valid regions or eras.

Every bible change should run validation.

15. Distinguish physical truth from supernatural truth

Xianxia does not need to obey ordinary physics everywhere. It does need to obey its own rules.

For each supernatural exception, define:

What ordinary rule is being overridden?
What power enables the exception?
What limits apply?
What visible cues communicate the exception?
What happens when the power is removed?
How does the exception interact with other systems?

Example:

Floating mountain

Ordinary expectation:
Mountain mass would fall under local gravity.

Override:
Ancient spatial formation distributes effective weight into a pocket domain.

Visible cues:
Slow debris orbit
Localized distortion below the mountain
Formation nodes embedded along the lower surface

Failure:
If three major nodes are destroyed, the mountain gradually loses altitude.

Gameplay:
The mountain remains physical; only its gravitational support is altered.

This creates believable supernatural phenomena rather than arbitrary spectacle.

18. Prevent the agent from taking shortcuts

The agent must classify every output:

Status	Meaning
Specification only	Fully described but not implemented
Proxy	Temporary placeholder
Prototype	Demonstrates the concept
Approximation	Deliberately compromises requirements
Production candidate	Believed complete; awaiting validation
Validated production	All required tests and approvals passed
Rejected	Failed requirements
Blocked	Current tools cannot produce it correctly

The agent must never call a proxy “finished.”

When capability is missing, it must say:

The requested result requires capability X.
The current engine provides only Y.
Using Y would compromise A, B, and C.
I recommend implementing capability X as a reusable plugin.

Then it can build the missing capability through the sandbox process.

23. Mark validated production
Exact instruction for the agent

Give it this as a controlled amendment to the architecture baseline:

Create a Multiverse Ground Truth, Visual Fidelity, and Validation Architecture. The purpose is to ensure that all spatial, visual, physical, temporal, behavioral, architectural, environmental, biological, supernatural, animation, VFX, audio, and gameplay representations are explicitly specified, machine-readable, measurable, traceable, and testable.

Do not interpret “verbose” as decorative prose. Every subject must be exhaustively specified through dimensions, units, ranges, proportions, construction, materials, silhouette, movement, timing, environmental interaction, presentation, procedural variation, forbidden interpretations, engine dependencies, unresolved questions, provenance, and acceptance tests.

Establish five truth levels:

canonical invariant;
derived requirement;
art-direction decision;
procedural possibility;
unresolved question.

Never silently convert an unresolved question into a permanent decision. Preserve uncertainty, ask the user, create labeled alternatives, or use an explicitly marked proxy.

Establish one authoritative metric and temporal system for the entire engine. Every physical or temporal quantity must use explicit units and define whether it is exact, derived, art-directed, estimated, or procedurally ranged.

Define both physical scale and perceptual scale. Large objects must include visual, acoustic, atmospheric, camera, texture, structural-detail, LOD, motion-parallax, and comparison-anchor requirements that make their true dimensions perceptible.

Create a reusable Visual Truth Packet schema for characters, species, creatures, environments, biomes, architecture, settlements, equipment, vehicles, celestial bodies, techniques, animations, VFX, audio and UI. Each packet must include all applicable physical, visual, temporal, behavioral, technical and validation requirements.

Establish motion profiles for every moving entity and effect. Specify anticipation, acceleration, velocity, turning, trajectory, duration, recovery, impact timing, animation timing, VFX timing, audio timing and camera presentation. Ensure authoritative gameplay timing and visual presentation remain synchronized.

Define visual and structural design grammars for every culture, faction, species, world, region, era and craft tradition. Include preferred proportions, shapes, structures, materials, color relationships, decoration density, motifs, motion language, VFX language, audio language, permitted variation and forbidden generic interpretations.

Preserve editable semantic blueprints for assets whenever possible. Final meshes must not be the only source of truth. Buildings, characters, creatures, vegetation, weapons, terrain features, animations and VFX should retain editable component structures, parameters, dependencies, generation provenance, collision settings and LOD settings.

Implement a Visual Accuracy Oracle that compares actual runtime output against the approved ground truth. It must inspect world measurements, proportions, silhouettes, materials, animation timing, motion paths, collision, lighting, LOD, camera presentation, environmental context, gameplay readability, performance and prohibited representations.

Generate standardized review captures for important assets and systems, including orthographic views, three-quarter views, gameplay-distance views, scale references, collision overlays, skeleton overlays, material channels, animation trajectories, terrain diagnostic layers and target-environment previews.

Establish golden validation scenes covering neutral lighting, multiple outdoor conditions, interiors, fog, night, combat, crowds, terrain, flight, underwater, large-scale environments and celestial views. Run visual regression and performance validation across applicable scenes.

Define inspection, interaction, gameplay, regional and celestial distance bands. Every asset must specify which identifying information, function, material cues, silhouette and effects must remain legible in each applicable band.

Procedural generation must operate through authoritative definitions, style grammars, environmental constraints, history, functional requirements, structural blueprints, candidate generation, validation and provenance. Never generate final production content directly from vague descriptive tags.

Add contradiction detection across the bible and engine. Detect incompatible scales, timings, travel distances, architecture, anatomy, animation, physics, collision, environmental needs, style rules, procedural constraints and gameplay requirements.

Supernatural exceptions must identify the ordinary rule being overridden, the power responsible, limits, visual cues, failure behavior and interactions with other systems. Do not use supernatural framing to excuse unspecified behavior.

Every bible subject must have a human-readable specification, machine-readable schema, reference package, acceptance-test package, implementation dependencies and unresolved-question record.

Classify every implementation and asset as specification-only, proxy, prototype, approximation, production candidate, validated production, rejected or blocked. Never present a proxy, prototype or approximation as final.

When the current engine lacks a capability required to produce an accurate result, do not take shortcuts. Explain the missing capability and the compromises of available alternatives. Then propose, research, implement, test and register a reusable plugin, pipeline or Architect tool capable of producing the intended result.

Use separate reviewer roles for lore, consistency, scale, proportion, technical art, animation, physics, gameplay readability, performance, procedural generation and adversarial visual analysis. The creator may not be the sole certifier.

An entry is complete only when the engine can trace:

Source truth → specification → blueprint → implementation → generated instance → rendered presentation → validation evidence

The objective is not merely a large bible. The objective is a compiled, queryable and testable multiverse specification from which the engine, procedural generators, asset pipelines, editor and Grand Architect all derive the same reality.

The key is that the bible becomes a compiler input for reality, not a collection of evocative descriptions. Every appearance, dimension, proportion, speed, movement, material, atmosphere, sound, and supernatural exception should have enough explicit structure that the Grand Architect can create it, measure it, explain it, test it, reject inaccuracies, and continue building new tools rather than quietly approximating your intended universe.
