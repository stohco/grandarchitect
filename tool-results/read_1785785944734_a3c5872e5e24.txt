    44→### 1.3 The thirty disciples
    45→
    46→The sect has thirty disciples on the rolls at the protagonist's arrival. Twelve are inner disciples (Qi Condensation, mid-to-peak); eighteen are outer (Qi Induction to early Qi Condensation). The eight specified below are the ones whose presence the simulation must track from session ~120 onward, because each will, at some point in Acts 2 or 3, do something the protagonist cannot ignore.
    47→
    48→**Wang Meili (王美麗)** — the protagonist's cousin (father's brother's daughter), twenty-two at the protagonist's arrival, early Qi Condensation, healer-track. She entered the sect at fourteen against her father's wishes (her father, the protagonist's uncle Wang Shouye, distrusts Old Chen and by extension all retired cultivators; he sent Meili to the Cangwu Sect because it was a sect, which is to say an institution, which he understands, rather than a hermit, which he does not). She is small, watchful, precise, with calloused hands from the herb garden and a quiet steadiness the sect's other outer disciples find either reassuring or unnerving depending on their own temperaments. She has, by the time the protagonist arrives, been the sect's effective healer for two years — the previous healer, an old inner disciple named Lin Shu, died of a stroke the previous winter and Wu Changqing has not replaced her. Meili's practice is wood-phase (good for healing, good for growth) with a water-phase secondary. She is the protagonist's first ally in the sect and, eventually (doc 26 §9.1), the lineage bridge.
    49→
    50→**Yan Boyan (嚴伯言)** — twenty-eight, late Qi Condensation, the protagonist's first rival in the sect. Yan is the son of the headman of Cangwu Sect's largest tenant village, sent to the sect at eight on a sect-recruitment tour. He is broad-shouldered, handsome in a hard-jawed way, meticulous about his appearance, and ambitious in a way that has curdled over twenty years of being the most talented disciple in a sect too small to reward him. He attempted Foundation Establishment at twenty-five and failed — not as catastrophically as Wu Changqing (his meridians are intact) but badly enough that he has not tried again. He believes (without proof) that Wu Changqing has held back a key technique from him. He believes (with some proof) that the Azure Sword Sect would treat him better. He is the leader of what the inner disciples quietly call the **Northern Faction** — those who favor accepting Azure Sword Sect patronage. When the protagonist arrives — a Foundation Establishment cultivator who will, by sect custom, be ranked above Yan despite having done nothing for the sect — Yan's resentment finds a new object. The rivalry is professional at first (spirit-stone allocations, mission assignments, scripture-hall access), then personal, then (by Act 3, depending on player choices) factional.
    51→
    52→**Zhao Wujiu (趙五九)** — twenty-four, mid Qi Condensation, the protagonist's first friend in the sect. "Wujiu" is a childhood name (Fifth-Nine, from his birth order in his father's lineage and his mother's) that he never replaced with a courtesy name because, he says, he likes being numbered. He is tall, soft-voiced, ugly in a kind way (a long nose, a crooked smile), and the son of a herdboy from a tenant village. He entered the sect at twelve (a late recruit — the sect found him when he was helping drive cattle past the Stone Gate and a senior disciple noticed he could perceive the creek's qi without instruction). His practice is earth-phase, slow and steady, and his cultivation has been the slowest in his cohort — he is twenty-four and only mid Qi Condensation, where Yan Boyan is twenty-eight and late Qi Condensation. Zhao Wujiu does not mind. He likes the sect. He likes the routine. He likes Wu Changqing. He will become the protagonist's first friend in the sect because he is the only inner disciple who, on the protagonist's first day, talks to the protagonist without calculating.
    53→
    54→**Su Wenli (蘇文理)** — thirty-one, Qi Condensation peak, the betrayer. Su is the son of a county-school teacher from Cangli County Seat who paid the sect a silver fee to take his son as an outer disciple at fifteen (a practice the sect accepts when the treasury is low, which is most years). Su is intelligent, well-read, articulate, and possessed of a self-regard that he has learned to disguise as humility. He is the sect's effective Scripture Hall steward (the actual hall head, an elderly inner disciple named Old Liu, is in early-stage cognitive decline and lets Su run the hall). Su is also, as the protagonist will discover over Act 2, in quiet correspondence with Elder Rong Zhaoyuan of the Azure Sword Sect (the Merit Hall head and leader of the Azure Sword expansionists, §2.5). The correspondence began four years ago, after the Azure Sword Sect's last tribute-collection visit, when Rong Zhaoyuan complimented Su on a memo he had written about the Cangwu Sect's vein output. Su does not think of himself as a traitor. He thinks of himself as a realist. The Cangwu Sect is dying; the Azure Sword Sect is the future; the protagonist's arrival is a complication in an arrangement he has spent four years building. He will betray the sect in Act 2 by leaking the protagonist's Foundation Establishment status and the vein's deep-tap location to Rong Zhaoyuan — information that accelerates the Azure Sword Sect's formal claim at session ~300.
    55→
    56→**Old Liu (劉師兄, Liu Shixiong)** — forty-seven, mid Qi Condensation, the sect's Scripture Hall head in name and the outer disciples' de facto father in practice. Old Liu entered the sect at seven (the youngest recruit in sect history) and has not left the valley in thirty-eight years. He is short, round, perpetually amused, and unable to read anything more complex than the sect's basic manuals — which is why he let Su Wenli take over the Scripture Hall's administrative work. He teaches the outer disciples' morning scripture class himself (from memory; he has the foundational manual memorized in its entirety, with all Wu Changqing's annotations) and he is the one who, when an outer disciple is crying in the dormitory at two in the morning, gets up and makes them tea. He is, in his way, the sect's actual heart. He will not betray the sect, will not rise in it, and will die (in Act 3, of a stroke, at sixty-one, in the Scripture Hall with a cup of tea in his hand) having never perceived his own anchor.
    57→
    58→**Ma Yuening (馬月寧)** — nineteen, early Qi Condensation (broke through three months before the protagonist's arrival; the breakthrough was the sect's social event of the year), the youngest inner disciple. She is from a fishing family on the Eastern Sea coast — recruited on the same tour that produced Yan Boyan — and she is, in temperament, the opposite of Yan: open, excitable, generous with her opinions, prone to crushes (on Yan at sixteen, on Su at eighteen, on Zhao Wujiu at nineteen — the last of which Zhao has not noticed). Her practice is water-phase (matching the vein), and she is the most talented pure cultivator the sect has produced in two generations. Wu Changqing, privately, believes she might be the one — the disciple who reaches Foundation Establishment legitimately. He has not told her this; he is afraid of putting the same pressure on her that broke him.
    59→
    60→**The Cao Twins — Cao Shan (曹山) and Cao Hai (曹海)** — both thirty, late Qi Condensation, sons of a deceased inner disciple (Cao Ping, who died in a beast hunt twelve years ago) and raised in the sect. They are not identical — Cao Shan is the older by half an hour, taller, slower, kinder; Cao Hai is shorter, quicker, sharper — but they have spent thirty years finishing each other's sentences and the sect has stopped trying to tell them apart except by height. They are Yan Boyan's wingmen in the Northern Faction, more out of lifelong habit than conviction. They will, if the protagonist befriends Yan, follow Yan; if the protagonist befriends them, they will (reluctantly, then with relief) follow the protagonist. They are the sect's swing vote in any inner-disciple dispute.
    61→
    62→**Peng Li (彭力)** — thirty-five, mid Qi Condensation, the sect's woodworker and the dormitory's eldest occupant. He is the only disciple who entered the sect as an adult (he was a carpenter in Qinghe Market Town; he perceived qi at twenty-eight after a long illness and walked to the Stone Gate on foot; Wu Changqing admitted him on the spot). He is quiet, slow-speaking, large-handed, and the only disciple in the sect who can repair a warded door or rebuild a qi-channel in the spirit-vein tap. He is indispensable and knows it. He is also, like Zhao Wujiu, fundamentally content — he wants nothing from the sect except to keep working wood and practicing in the spring's half-circle. He is the protagonist's unlikely ally in Act 2: the practical man who, when the politics gets thick, asks the question that deflates it ("Will it still need a door?").
    63→
    64→**Tong Sijing (童思敬)** — fourteen, early Qi Induction, the sect's youngest disciple and, by Wu Changqing's quiet assessment, its most unusual. Tong Sijing was found by the last recruitment tour at age eleven in a charcoal-burner's hut in the southern foothills, the daughter of a charcoal-burner and a deaf-mute mother who had walked out of the mountains years before and would not say from where. She did not speak Cangli dialect; she spoke a mountain dialect no one in the sect recognized, and she has, in three years, learned Cangli dialect with an accent the other outer disciples tease her about (gently; she is small and watchful and they are mostly kind). She has, however, an inexplicable talent for the founder's water-phase primary route — she picked it up in two weeks where most outer disciples take three months — and a perception of qi-residue that Wu Changqing has, in private, called "Core Formation-clear at Qi Induction," a thing he has never seen in fifty years of cultivation and does not know how to explain.
    65→
    66→Tong Sijing does not know how to explain it either. She says, when asked, that the qi "has always been there, the same way the creek has always been there," and that she does not understand why this is remarkable. Wu Changqing has not told anyone else about her residue-perception; he is afraid of what it might mean and afraid of what the Azure Sword Sect would do if they knew. He has written, in his notebook, in the small careful hand he uses for the entries that frighten him: "If the vein has produced a perception-talent, the vein is deeper than we know." Tong Sijing, for her part, is too young to have an arc; she has, instead, a trajectory — a line the simulation must track from her arrival to her late teens, when her talent will either flower into something the sect cannot hide or be quietly suppressed by a sect master who knows what the Azure Sword Sect does to perception-talents it cannot recruit. The protagonist, on arrival, notices her in the first week (she is the only disciple younger than the protagonist, and the only one who watches the protagonist practice without calculation). The protagonist's relationship with Tong Sijing, across Act 2, is the relationship of a senior cultivator who sees a junior talent and must decide what to do about it — teach her, hide her, or, hardest, let her find her own way.

    68→### 1.4 The spirit vein: the Green Mirror Vein (青鏡脈)
    69→
    70→The sect's spirit vein is the **Green Mirror Vein** (青鏡脈), a yin-dominant, water-phase-primary vein with a wood-phase secondary, running northeast-southwest through the granite of the central Cangwu range. Its surface trace is the Green Mirror Spring (§1.1); its main current lies roughly 240 meters below the valley floor, flowing slowly northeast at an estimated rate of (the sect's last measurement, fifteen years ago) enough to sustain thirty-five to forty Qi Condensation cultivators at full practice. The vein's history: it was discovered 240 years ago by a wandering Qi Condensation cultivator named Daoist Cangwu (the founder's courtesy name; his personal name is not recorded), who settled the valley, built the Founder's Hall, and slowly attracted the first disciples. The sect was formally constituted 180 years ago (doc 24 §4.4), at which point the vein's output was nearly double its current level. The decline is real (Wu Changqing's notebook records it; the Azure Sword Sect's tribute-collectors know it); the cause is contested. Wu Changqing believes the Azure Sword Sect's deep-tap upstream — at the Jade Edge Grotto (§2.3) — is drawing more than the tribute records state. The Azure Sword Sect maintains the decline is geological, the vein aging naturally. The vein's phase-affinity shapes the sect's entire practice tradition: the founder's manual is a water-phase primary circulation with wood-phase secondary integration, and every disciple the sect has ever produced has practiced it. This is why the sect's cultivators tend toward healer and diviner paths and away from combat — water and wood are gentle phases. It is also why the Azure Sword Sect (metal-phase) considers the Cangwu Sect's tradition soft and the Cangwu Sect's territory, by extension, an underdeveloped resource.
    71→
    72→### 1.5 The relationship to the Azure Sword Sect: vassalage
    73→
    74→The Cangwu Sect is, in everything but name, a vassal of the Azure Sword Sect. The arrangement is not formalized in a treaty (the Azure Sword Sect does not treaty with Qi Condensation sects); it is a generational fact sustained by three things. First, **tribute**: the Cangwu Sect sends, every autumn, a chest of six low-grade spirit stones and four bundles of wood-phase spirit herbs (the sect's only export-grade crop) to the Azure Sword Sect, delivered by outer disciple escort to the foothills and there handed over to an Azure Sword Sect outer disciple who comes down to meet them. The tribute is roughly a quarter of the sect's annual surplus; Wu Changqing pays it because not paying it would invite a visit. Second, **protection**: in theory, the Azure Sword Sect defends the Cangwu Sect from external threats. In practice, the last Azure Sword Sect intervention on the Cangwu Sect's behalf was twelve years ago, when a Foundation Establishment beast (a jade-scaled python from the southern foothills) threatened the tenant villages and Wu Changqing, whose own combat ability is limited, requested help. The Azure Sword Sect sent a single Foundation Establishment elder, who killed the python in an afternoon and stayed the night in the Founder's Hall and complimented the tea. Third, **non-competition**: the Cangwu Sect does not recruit in villages the Azure Sword Sect claims; the Azure Sword Sect does not, in principle, recruit in the Cangwu Sect's territory. In practice, the Azure Sword Sect's definition of "its territory" extends to the entire central Cangwu range, and the Cangwu Sect's effective recruitment zone is six villages within thirty li of the Stone Gate.
    75→
    76→The relationship is resented. Every disciple in the sect knows the tribute is excessive; every inner disciple knows the protection is theoretical; Yan Boyan and Su Wenli know, in addition, that the arrangement is structurally unchangeable from below. Wu Changqing's resentment is the most private and the most pointed: his teacher (the previous sect master) was friends, in his youth, with the Azure Sword Sect's previous sect master (the Zhou Wugui-killer, doc 24 §4.4) — they had hunted together once, in the foothills, as young cultivators. That friendship was the reason the tribute, in the previous sect master's time, was three stones and two bundles. After the previous Azure Sword sect master died and Leng Wushuang succeeded him, the tribute was "reassessed" upward. Wu Changqing has never forgiven this. He has also never mentioned it to anyone except Old Liu, who is the only one who remembers the old amount.

    78→### 1.6 Internal politics: three factions and the succession question
    79→
    80→The sect's internal politics are organized, at the protagonist's arrival, into three informal factions. None is named; all are real.
    81→
    82→The **Old Mountain faction** is led by Old Liu (in his amiable, distracted way) and includes Peng Li, Ma Yuening, and most of the outer disciples by default. Its position: the sect's tradition is correct, the vassalage is regrettable but endurable, and the path forward is patient cultivation and the hope of a Foundation Establishment successor. The Old Mountain faction is the largest and the quietest; it does not meet as a faction, it does not strategize, it is simply the disposition of the sect's contented majority.
    83→
    84→The **Northern faction** (Yan Boyan, Su Wenli, the Cao twins, and two other inner disciples) favors accepting formal Azure Sword Sect patronage. The argument: the Cangwu Sect cannot survive another generation at its current trajectory; the Azure Sword Sect offers resources, recruitment access, and a path for ambitious disciples (Yan) into the larger sect; the sect's independence is, in any case, a fiction. The Northern faction is the most politically active; it meets, in effect, whenever Yan and Su walk together in the valley, which is most evenings. The Northern faction's internal tension: Su Wenli is in correspondence with the Azure Sword Sect (§1.3); Yan Boyan does not know this. Yan thinks the Northern faction's strategy is to negotiate from a position of internal consensus. Su knows the negotiation is, in fact, already happening, and the question is only what terms the Cangwu Sect will accept.
    85→
    86→The **Independence faction** is Wu Changqing alone, with the protagonist (on arrival) as its only potential ally. Its position: the vassalage must be broken, the vein must be defended, the Azure Sword Sect is a predator, and the path forward is to find allies outside the central Cangwu range — the Jade Void Holy Land (distant, but historically interested in the Cangli Riverlands), the rogue cultivator network (Old Chen's connections, which the protagonist inherits), or the Wang lineage itself (which is, after the protagonist's Foundation Establishment, technically a cultivation-lineage-in-waiting). The Independence faction is the smallest and the most desperate; Wu Changqing knows he cannot win a confrontation with the Azure Sword Sect and is betting everything on the protagonist.
    87→
    88→The succession question: Wu Changqing has no children and no designated heir. The elder council, in the sect's history, has convened twice to discuss succession — once when Wu Changqing was forty (he fought off a quiet movement to designate Yan Boyan as heir-apparent) and once when he was forty-eight (after a chest infection that winter that frightened everyone, including him). The council is, in practice, the inner disciples plus Old Liu, and the question is who will be sect master when Wu Changqing ages out or dies. The candidates, in descending order of likelihood at the protagonist's arrival: Yan Boyan (the senior inner disciple, ambitious, the Northern faction's choice); the protagonist (Foundation Establishment, the only cultivator in the sect qualified by realm, but a newcomer with no faction); Su Wenli (Qi Condensation peak, intelligent, but scarier to the Old Mountain faction than Yan); Ma Yuening (talented, young, but three years a Qi Condensation cultivator and not yet ready). Wu Changqing's private preference, which he has not stated and will not state until forced: the protagonist, with Ma Yuening as a future successor after Foundation Establishment. This preference is the engine of the sect's Act 2 internal tension.

    90→### 1.7 The protagonist's daily life as an outer disciple
    91→
    92→The protagonist, despite being Foundation Establishment, enters the sect as an outer disciple — a deliberate choice by Wu Changqing, endorsed by Old Chen's letter, on the grounds that the protagonist has no sect training and must learn the sect's tradition from the bottom. The arrangement is unusual and the sect knows it; the outer disciples, on the protagonist's first week, watch the protagonist carry water and weed the herb garden with a mixture of awe and unease. The protagonist's daily schedule, from session ~120 onward:
    93→
    94→**Dawn (寅時, roughly 4-6 a.m.):** silent practice at the Green Mirror Spring's half-circle. The protagonist has a reserved seat (Wu Changqing's order; Yan Boyan, who had the seat, was moved one stone to the left). Practice runs 90 minutes. The protagonist practices the sect's water-phase primary route (different from Old Chen's wood-phase route; the protagonist is learning it for the first time) alongside the other dawn-rotation cultivators.
    95→
    96→**Early morning (卯時, 6-8 a.m.):** communal breakfast in the Pill Hall — millet porridge with pickled vegetables, with a small bowl of spirit-herb broth twice a week. After breakfast, the protagonist's outer-disciple duties: three mornings a week, herb-garden weeding with Wang Meili (this is how the protagonist and Meili rebuild their cousin-friendship); two mornings, scripture-hall maintenance with Old Liu (sweeping, sanding the warded door, copying damaged manuals); one morning, spirit-vein tap inspection with Peng Li (the protagonist's Foundation Establishment perception is useful here — Peng Li asks, in the protagonist's second week, whether the vein "feels right" at depth, a question Peng Li has wanted to ask a Foundation Establishment cultivator for years).
    97→
    98→**Mid-morning to midday (辰時-午時, 8 a.m.-12 p.m.):** sect missions or free practice. Missions are posted on the Merit Hall board; the protagonist, as Foundation Establishment, is offered higher-tier missions (beast suppression in the foothills, escort to Qinghe Market Town, investigation of qi-anomalies in the tenant villages) that pay more spirit stones than the outer-disciple standard. The protagonist may take these or decline; declining is allowed but noted by Su Wenli, who tracks the Merit Hall's books.
    99→
   100→**Midday meal:** rice with whatever the kitchen has, eaten in the Pill Hall or, on warm days, on the steps of the Founder's Hall.
   101→
   102→**Afternoon (未時-申時, 1-5 p.m.):** free practice, private study, or — by Wu Changqing's invitation, twice a week — private instruction in the sect's higher techniques. These sessions are the protagonist's actual sect education. Wu Changqing teaches the water-phase integration theory (the theory he could not himself complete) and watches the protagonist practice it with the focused, painful hope of a man watching his own unfinished work being finished by another hand.
   103→
   104→**Evening (酉時, 5-7 p.m.):** evening meal, then the outer-disciple scripture lecture in the scripture hall. Old Liu teaches the foundational manual; the protagonist attends (Wu Changqing's order, again) and sits at the back. The protagonist already knows the material at a level Old Liu cannot teach, but Wu Changqing's instruction is that the protagonist must be seen to learn the sect's tradition in front of the sect's younger disciples. This is political theater; it works. By the protagonist's third month, the outer disciples who were uneasy about a Foundation Establishment cultivator in their dormitory have decided the protagonist is, in some undefinable way, one of them.
   105→
   106→**Night (戌時-亥時, 7-11 p.m.):** private practice in the Long Dormitory (the protagonist has a corner cot, separated from the next by a hanging cloth) or, by the second month, in a small practice room Wu Changqing has had cleared in the Founder's Hall. Sleep by 11.
   107→
   108→**Social dynamics:** the protagonist, as Foundation Establishment, is socially anomalous — too powerful to be a true outer disciple, too junior (in sect standing) to be an inner disciple. Yan Boyan treats the protagonist with cold courtesy. Zhao Wujiu treats the protagonist as a friend, which the protagonist, after the long solitude of Old Chen's hermitage, finds almost unbearably welcome. Wang Meili treats the protagonist as a cousin, which means she is both warmer and ruder than anyone else. Su Wenli treats the protagonist as a curiosity, asks careful questions, and writes the answers in a small notebook the protagonist does not see. Ma Yuening develops, in the protagonist's first month, a hero-worship that the protagonist must (gently, carefully) deflect. The Cao twins watch the protagonist for two months before deciding, on the basis of an afternoon the protagonist spent helping them repair a warded door, that the protagonist is acceptable. Peng Li offers the protagonist a piece of carved wood — a small horse, badly done — as a house-warming gift the first week, and never mentions it again. Old Liu, on the protagonist's second night, sits on the cot next to the protagonist's and says, "Welcome home, little cultivator. The tea is in the brown pot. Don't drink the green one, it's Old Chen's leftovers from his visit three years ago and it's gone off." The protagonist, who has not slept in a room with nine other people since childhood, sleeps deeply for the first time in years.

   110→### 1.8 The founder's manual and the practice tradition
   111→
   112→The Cangwu Sect's founder (Daoist Cangwu, §1.4) left a single manual — the **Green Mirror Water-Wood Scripture** (青鏡水木經) — stored in the Scripture Pavilion's Foundation-locked inner chamber. The manual is short: 4,200 characters across three scrolls, hand-copied onto paper now yellowed and brittle, the founder's own brushwork (a small, precise, slightly cramped hand that Wu Changqing has spent thirty years studying and still finds, in places, opaque). The first scroll describes the water-phase primary circulation route (the route the outer disciples practice at the spring's half-circle at dawn). The second scroll describes the wood-phase secondary integration (the route the inner disciples add at mid Qi Condensation). The third scroll describes the Foundation Establishment integration theory — the route Wu Changqing attempted at twenty-one and could not complete.
   113→
   114→The third scroll is the sect's central tension. The first two scrolls are sufficient for Qi Condensation; any disciple with patience can practice them safely. The third scroll is the route to Foundation Establishment, and it is the route that broke Wu Changqing. The route's difficulty is not in its qi-circulation (the route is, by all accounts, elegant and well-designed) but in its psychospiritual demand: the water-wood integration requires the cultivator to hold a grief-stable heart-mind at the moment of integration, because water-phase (associated with grief in the five-phase emotion correspondence) and wood-phase (associated with anger) must be balanced against each other under pressure. A cultivator with unprocessed grief cannot balance them. Wu Changqing had unprocessed grief (his brother's death) and the integration collapsed. Old Chen, who studied the same manual at the Cangwu Sect sixty years ago (Old Chen was, briefly, a Cangwu Sect disciple before he left in his dispute with the previous-previous sect master), also failed — for the same reason, in his case the grief of a different kind.
   115→
   116→The protagonist arrives at the Cangwu Sect with two advantages Wu Changqing and Old Chen lacked. First, the protagonist has already achieved Foundation Establishment (via Old Chen's teaching, which was not the Cangwu manual but a wood-phase primary route Old Chen developed independently). The protagonist's Foundation Establishment is therefore not at risk; the third scroll is, for the protagonist, a text to study rather than a route to attempt. Second, the protagonist has (per Scene 4 and Scene 5) already processed the grief of Wang Xiaodi — the protagonist's own water-phase heart-mind is stable. This means the protagonist can, in principle, teach the third scroll's route to a Cangwu Sect disciple who has not yet attempted Foundation Establishment. Ma Yuening is the obvious candidate. Wu Changqing's private hope (§1.6) is, in concrete terms, the hope that the protagonist will teach Ma Yuening the third scroll's route and that Ma Yuening will succeed where Wu Changqing failed.

   118→### 1.9 The seasonal rhythm and the ritual year
   119→
   120→The Cangwu Sect's year is organized around the twenty-four solar terms (二十四節氣, doc 00 §4), with four days of particular sect-weight. **The Spring Founding Day** (立春後第三日, the third day after the Beginning of Spring) commemorates the founder's arrival in the valley; the sect observes it with a recitation of the manual's first scroll in the Founder's Hall and a small feast (rice with pickled fish, a luxury the sect cannot really afford). **The Summer Tribute Day** (夏至前五日, five days before the Summer Solstice) is when the autumn tribute is prepared: the spirit stones are counted out, the herb bundles are tied, and the outer-disciple escort is chosen (the protagonist, by their second year, is the obvious choice; the Azure Sword Sect's receiving disciple is, by then, used to seeing them). **The Autumn Recruitment Tour** (秋分前後, around the Autumn Equinox) sends Wu Changqing and one inner disciple (Yan Boyan, by tradition, though Wu Changqing would prefer Zhao Wujiu) through the six tenant villages and three further-flung villages to test children for spiritual roots. The tour is the sect's lifeblood; a year with no recruits is a year the sect's future dims. **The Winter Solstice Vigil** (冬至守夜) is the sect's only night-cultivation: the entire sect sits in the spring's half-circle from sundown to sunrise, the one night the outer disciples are excused from their dormitory curfew. The vigil is cold, long, and quietly beloved. Old Liu makes tea every hour.
   121→
   122→The protagonist's first year in the sect is structured by these four days. The first Recruitment Tour the protagonist attends (autumn of the protagonist's first year, age 23 in mortal time but the sect does not track mortal ages) is the tour where the protagonist, accompanying Wu Changqing, returns to Wang Family Bend for the first time since Foundation Establishment — the first return (doc 26 §5.3, §8, the viral moment). The protagonist, walking into the village with Wu Changqing and Yan Boyan behind them, perceives the qi of the place and weeps. Wu Changqing, perceiving the protagonist's state with Qi Condensation perception (he cannot perceive the grief's content, only its disturbance), says nothing. Yan Boyan, perceiving nothing he can name, says nothing either. The tour, that year, finds two children with spiritual roots in Wang Family Bend's cousin-branch. One of them, a girl of seven named Wang Yanrong (王燕容, distant cousin), will, in Act 3, become the protagonist's second disciple in the "found a lineage" ending. This is the structural reason the Recruitment Tour scene is load-bearing: it is where the lineage's future is seeded, even as the protagonist is grieving the lineage's past.
   123→
   124→### 1.10 The Wang lineage as latent cultivation lineage
   125→
   126→The protagonist's arrival at the Cangwu Sect as a Foundation Establishment cultivator is, for the Wang lineage (the mortal lineage of Wang Family Bend, doc 04 §2.1), a structural transformation the lineage itself does not yet understand. Per doc 12 §2.1, a mortal lineage becomes a cultivation lineage (修真宗族) when it has produced at least one Nascent Soul cultivator whose anchor persists in bardo. The Wang lineage has not done this. But it has, now, produced a Foundation Establishment cultivator — the protagonist — and a Foundation Establishment cultivator is, per doc 12 §2.5, a candidate for lineage office and a potential future lineage head. The Wang lineage is, in the cultivation world's classification, a **latent cultivation lineage** — a mortal lineage that has produced its first cultivator of significant realm and is, thereby, on the path (across generations, if the cultivator's descendants also cultivate) to cultivation-lineage status.
   127→
   128→This status is invisible to the Wang lineage itself. The lineage head, Wang Shouzheng, knows the protagonist has "become a cultivator" in the way villagers know such things — a mixture of pride, fear, and the conviction that the protagonist has left the lineage's world for one the lineage cannot follow. He does not know that the protagonist's Foundation Establishment has, in the cultivation world's ledgers, made the Wang lineage a thing of mild interest to mid-level sect intelligence halls (the kind of interest that produces a single line in a single report, filed and forgotten). He does not know that the protagonist's eventual death (if the protagonist reaches Nascent Soul and persists their anchor) will, nine generations from now, make the Wang lineage's hall a qi-anchor that Core Formation descendants can commune with. He does not know that the protagonist's joining the Cangwu Sect — a Wang-lineage cultivator in a Cangwu-Sect robe — is, in the cultivation world's quiet accounting, the first formal link between the Wang lineage and a sect, and that this link will, across the protagonist's long life, become the spine of the Wang lineage's cultivation-future.
   129→
   130→Wu Changqing understands all of this. It is, in part, why he admitted the protagonist as an outer disciple despite the protagonist's Foundation Establishment realm — the protagonist, in the sect's robes and the sect's dormitory, is the protagonist's lineage's first emissary to the cultivation world, and Wu Changqing wants the protagonist to learn the sect's tradition from the bottom not as humiliation but as inheritance. The protagonist, when they eventually leave the Cangwu Sect (in Act 2 or Act 3, depending on player choices), will carry the Cangwu Sect's water-phase manual with them — the founder's manual, copied by the protagonist's own hand, the third scroll now comprehensible to a Foundation Establishment cultivator who has already crossed the route it describes. This copy is, in the cultivation world's accounting, the Wang lineage's first cultivation manual. It will be stored, eventually, in the Wang lineage hall (or in Old Chen's hermitage, which the protagonist inherits at session ~450, doc 26 §12.4). It will be the seed of the Wang lineage's cultivation school (doc 26 §7.1, ending 1). Wu Changqing, giving the protagonist access to the manual in the protagonist's first month, knows all of this and says nothing. He writes, in his notebook, in the careful hand: "The Wang lineage will outlast us. This is well."

   134→## Part 2 — The Azure Sword Sect (碧劍宗)
   135→
   136→### 2.1 The compound and the grotto-heaven's anchor
   137→
   138→The Azure Sword Sect sits on **Biluo Peak** (碧螺峰, the Green-Spiral Peak), the highest of the Northern Cangwu range, five hundred li north of Wang Family Bend. The peak is a single granite massif rising 2,800 meters above the surrounding highland, with a flat upper third that the sect has terraced into seven cultivated ledges. The compound walls — twenty meters of dark stone, qi-reinforced, with three gates (the South Gate for disciples, the East Gate for supply caravans, the North Gate for the sect master and honored guests) — enclose the upper third entirely. Inside the walls: the **Sword-Ancestor Hall** (劍祖堂, the founder's hall, where the founder's sword is enshrined rather than a jade bead — the Azure Sword Sect's token is a sword, a single-edge straight sword of unknown metal, three feet long, that the founder carried down from the peak's summit the day she broke through to Core Formation); the **Three Sword Peaks** (三劍峰, three sub-peaks within the wall, each held by a peak master); the **Eight Halls** (merit, scripture, pill, punishment, sword-craft, formation, intelligence, and guest — the Azure Sword Sect's bureaucratic tier is more developed than the Cangwu Sect's, befitting a sect of four hundred); the **inner disciples' courtyard city** (a dense grid of two-story buildings housing three hundred inner disciples); the **outer disciples' camp** (a less dense grid on the lowest terrace, housing one hundred outer disciples); and the **Founder's Garden** (a quiet courtyard with a single wutong tree, the sect's emblem, where the founder is said to have sat the night before her Core Formation breakthrough).
   139→
   140→Behind the compound, beneath the North Gate, the grotto-heaven's anchor: the **Sword-Washing Waterfall** (洗劍瀑), a sixty-meter fall from a spring on the peak's north face into a deep, cold pool. Behind the waterfall, in the cliff, a jade gate — a single slab of green-veined white jade, two meters tall, one meter wide, carved with the founder's sword-seal. The gate is the grotto-heaven's anchor (doc 19 §2.3). A cultivator with the sect's sword qi-signature may pass through the jade as if through water; a cultivator without it cannot. The gate is the only entrance. Destroying it (an act that would require a Spirit Severance cultivator sustained over hours, or a Mahayana cultivator in moments) would seal the grotto-heaven; the Azure Sword Sect has, in its 180-year history, never had to defend it.
   141→
   142→### 2.2 The sect master: Leng Wushuang (冷無霜)
   143→
   144→Leng Wushuang is three hundred and forty years old and looks thirty-five. She is tall, narrow-shouldered, with a face that the sect's outer disciples privately call "the cold face" — high cheekbones, a long straight nose, dark eyes that observe without appearing to, and hair (still black, no gray) she wears in a single braid down her back, fastened with a metal hairpin that is also a sword. She wears white. She has worn white every day for seventy years, since her husband Mu Tianqing (穆天青, Foundation Establishment, a sword cultivator of her own sect) died in a tribulation gone wrong; the white is mourning that has long since become identity. Her Core Formation qi, perceived by another Core Formation cultivator, is dense, cold, sharp — metal-phase primary, water-phase secondary, perfectly disciplined. Her dantian, perceived at Foundation Establishment resolution, looks like a small, bright, cold star.
   145→
   146→She was not born into the Azure Sword Sect. She was a rogue Core Formation cultivator from the eastern provinces who arrived at Biluo Peak at age ninety-two, seeking the founder's manual on sword-cultivation (the Azure Sword Sect's library, even then, was the best in the central Cangwu range). The previous sect master (the Zhou Wugui-killer) admitted her as a senior guest, then as an elder, then (after thirty years of demonstrated loyalty) as his designated successor. She became sect master at age two hundred and sixty, eighty years ago, when the previous master died of old age. She has held the seat since.
   147→
   148→Her history, in three acts. **The first act (ages 90-220):** the rogue cultivator who became an insider. She earned the sect's trust by fighting, by teaching, and by refusing to take a peak master's seat until the council offered it twice. She married Mu Tianqing at age one hundred and fifty (he was her junior by sixty years; the match scandalized the conservative elders and delighted the rest); they were together for one hundred and twenty years.
   149→
   150→**The second act (ages 220-270):** the sect master's first decade. She inherited a sect at the height of its regional power and presided over its consolidation — the three-vein system (the sect's three spirit veins, all metal-phase primary, were tapped in her first decade), the grotto-heaven's first full ecological survey, the establishment of the intelligence hall (her innovation; the sect had no intelligence capability before her).
   151→
   152→**The third act (ages 270-present):** the long, slow turn toward Nascent Soul. She attempted Nascent Soul at age three hundred and ten (failed; the integration collapsed at the spirit-severance phase; her Core Formation was destabilized for a decade and she did not attempt again for thirty years). She attempted again at age three hundred and thirty (failed; this time the tribulation was harder than her Core Formation tribulation and she barely survived). She is now, at three hundred and forty, preparing a third attempt. The third attempt requires resources — spirit stones, grotto-heaven time, concentrated qi — beyond what the sect's three current veins produce. The Cangli Riverlands' latent vein (the deep extension of the Green Mirror Vein, §2.4) is, for her, the resource that funds the third attempt. This is the personal stake beneath the political: a woman who has failed twice and will not survive a third failure, gambling on one more attempt.
   153→
   154→Her personality is disciplined, principled by discipline rather than by conviction, and cold in a way that is not unkindness but efficiency. She does not waste words. She does not waste qi. She does not waste affection. She loved her husband; she has not, since his death, allowed herself the kind of attachment that could distract her from the third attempt. She loves her daughter (Leng Qingxue, born when Leng Wushuang was two hundred and twenty) in the same disciplined way — carefully, with attention, but without the softness that she associates (correctly, she would say) with her own failed breakthroughs. She is aware that her daughter opposes her. She has not, in twelve years, mentioned this to her daughter directly. She has mentioned it, twice, to her own journal, which is kept in the Sword-Ancestor Hall behind the founder's sword and which no one else has read.
   155→
   156→Her relationship to the Cangli Riverlands is structural. The Azure Sword Sect's territory, properly understood, is the Northern Cangwu range; the Cangli Riverlands are below the range, on the plain. But the Cangli Riverlands' vein system (the Green Mirror Vein and its deep extension) is geologically continuous with the Northern Cangwu veins — the same granitic arc, the same fault system. Leng Wushuang believes (correctly, per the vein map her intelligence hall has produced) that the Green Mirror Vein's main current, deep below the Cangwu Sect's shallow tap, runs northeast into the Azure Sword Sect's territory at depth. She considers this vein, at depth, legitimately contestable. She considers the Cangwu Sect's shallow tap a historical accident. She has not said this to Wu Changqing. She has said it, in formal terms, to the Azure Sword Sect's elder council, who have agreed.

   158→### 2.3 The grotto-heaven: the Jade Edge Grotto (碧玉緣洞天)
   159→
   160→The Jade Edge Grotto is a major grotto-heaven (doc 19 §2.1) of approximately eighty square kilometers — a mountain valley inside the pocket, four kilometers long and two wide, with a central lake (the **Sword-Mirror Lake**, 劍鏡湖, eight kilometers in circumference, cold, clear, metal-phase-saturated) fed by three streams descending from interior peaks (the grotto has its own sky, a pale blue-white false sun that does not move; the light is always mid-morning). The grotto's qi-climate is metal-phase dominant (cool, sharp, faintly astringent — a cultivator breathing it feels their meridians tighten slightly, which is the metal-phase effect on the lung and large-intestine channels) with water-phase secondary (the lake, the streams, the perpetual mist). Time rate is 1:1 with the Mortal Stratum.
   161→
   162→The grotto's law context differs from the Mortal Stratum in one respect: the metal-phase generation cycle is intensified, meaning metal-phase cultivation is approximately 30% faster inside than outside, and wood-phase cultivation (metal conquers wood in the five-phase cycle) is approximately 20% slower. This is why the Azure Sword Sect is a metal-phase sword sect — the grotto made it so. The founder was metal-phase already; the grotto's qi-climate, over six generations of cultivators, has shaped the sect's entire practice tradition.
   163→
   164→The grotto's ecology (per doc 19 §3) is isolation-evolved. The herb population includes **sword-grass** (劍草, a sedge with blade-shaped leaves that cut unprotected skin; the basis of the sect's metal-phase healing poultices), **blue-flame lotus** (青焰蓮, a lotus that grows in the Sword-Mirror Lake's shallows and whose petals burn with a cold blue qi-flame when bruised; the sect's most valuable pill ingredient), and **iron-root pine** (鐵根松, a pine whose roots are dense enough to be forged like low-grade iron; the sect's sword-craft hall uses iron-root pine wood for practice swords). The beast population includes **silver-furred foxes** (銀狐, mid-Qi Condensation equivalent, the grotto's dominant predator, intelligent, curious, and dangerous in packs), **metal-phase hawks** (鐵羽鷹, late Qi Condensation, territorial, nesting on the interior peaks), and a single **ancient beast**: a jade-scaled serpent (玉鱗蛇, Foundation Establishment equivalent, six meters long, that lives in the lake's deep and is, by sect tradition, left alone — it was here when the founder arrived, and the founder's manual records her decision to coexist with it). The serpent is the grotto's tacit guardian; no Azure Sword Sect cultivator has ever harmed it, and the sect's intelligence hall has, in the last thirty years, observed Foundation-foreign cultivators attempting to enter the grotto (rogue cultivators, mostly, seeking pills or herbs) and being driven off by the serpent before the sect's patrol could respond.
   165→
   166→The grotto is the sect's primary cultivation accelerator. Three hundred and seventy of the sect's four hundred disciples cycle through it on six-month rotations; the remaining thirty (the sect master, the three peak masters, the eight hall heads, and the inner disciples currently on sect duty) practice in the compound's main cultivation hall, which is fed by a direct tap from one of the three surface veins. The grotto's rotations are managed by the merit hall; access is the sect master's prerogative and the most contested resource in the sect's internal politics.

   168→### 2.4 The expansionist interest: the Cangli spirit vein dispute
   169→
   170→The Azure Sword Sect's expansionist interest, formalized at session ~300 (doc 26 §4.2), is in the deep extension of the Green Mirror Vein. The intelligence hall's vein map (produced over thirty years by Foundation Establishment elders using the deep-perception method described in doc 14 §1.3) shows the vein's main current running northeast at depth, passing beneath the Cangwu Sect's shallow tap and continuing under the central Cangwu range's granitic spine to a point roughly sixty li southwest of Biluo Peak. At that point, the vein's current is dense enough to support a second grotto-heaven — if an anchor can be established and a grotto-heaven opened (an act requiring, per doc 19, a Nascent Soul cultivator's sustained attention over months).
   171→
   172→The Azure Sword Sect's formal claim, when it is filed with the Immortal Alliance (doc 12 §6) at session ~300, asserts that the vein's deep extension is part of the Azure Sword Sect's geological territory (the granitic spine is continuous with Biluo Peak's massif) and that the Cangwu Sect's shallow tap is an overdraw that threatens the vein's long-term health. The Cangwu Sect's counter-claim asserts that the vein's surface trace has been the Cangwu Sect's for 180 years, that the deep extension is part of the same vein, and that the Azure Sword Sect's intelligence hall's vein map is, in fact, intelligence reconnaissance for an act of aggression. The Immortal Alliance's adjudication (which will, by Act 3, have not concluded — the Alliance is slow) is the formal political backdrop. The informal political backdrop is the Azure Sword Sect's clear willingness to act on its claim regardless of adjudication: the sect has, by session ~300, sent two "geological survey" teams into the central Cangwu range, both of which the Cangwu Sect's outer disciples have observed and reported. Wu Changqing's notebook, by this point, contains a third column: dates, observed survey-team sizes, and the protagonist's name written in the margin next to a question mark.
   173→
   174→The dispute is the political tension of Act 2. The protagonist is caught between Wu Changqing (who wants the protagonist to defend the sect), Leng Qingxue (who wants the protagonist to mediate), and the protagonist's own interest (which is, primarily, in Pei Liang, who is the actual threat, not the Azure Sword Sect). The protagonist's choices in the dispute (which side to support, which intelligence to share, which duels to fight in the Exchange of Cuts with Leng Qingxue) shape the Act 2 political landscape and determine whether the Cangwu Sect survives the dispute intact.

   176→### 2.5 Internal factions: the expansionists and the isolationists
   177→
   178→The Azure Sword Sect's four hundred disciples and thirty-eight elders are organized, at the protagonist's arrival, into two broad factions, with the sect master holding the balance.
   179→
   180→The **Expansionist faction** is led by **Elder Rong Zhaoyuan (容昭遠)**, Foundation Establishment, age one hundred and eighty, head of the Merit Hall, a tall, soft-spoken man with a long beard and a smile that does not reach his eyes. The Expansionists' position: the Azure Sword Sect is the regional hegemon; the Cangli Riverlands' vein system is, geologically, the sect's; the Cangwu Sect is a dying sect whose resources are wasted on it; the formal claim should be filed, the dispute won, and the vein tapped. The Expansionists control the Merit Hall (which manages sect missions and grotto-heaven rotations — i.e., the sect's patronage system) and the Intelligence Hall (which produces the vein maps). Rong Zhaoyuan is Su Wenli's correspondent (§1.3); the correspondence is the Expansionists' back channel into the Cangwu Sect's internal politics.
   181→
   182→The **Isolationist faction** is led by **Elder Jing You (靜幽)**, Foundation Establishment, age two hundred and ten, head of the Scripture Hall, a small, thin, elderly woman who looks one hundred and fifty and is, by reputation, the sect's best teacher since the founder. The Isolationists' position: the expansion risks war with the Jade Void Holy Land (which has historic interest in the Cangli Riverlands — the holy land's founder, two thousand years ago, was born in the Cangli region, and the holy land considers the region a spiritual ancestor-territory); the expansion risks the protagonist (already a Foundation Establishment cultivator with Old Chen's connections and, through Old Chen, the Jade Void's goodwill); the sect's resources are better spent on Leng Wushuang's third Nascent Soul attempt than on territorial acquisition. The Isolationists control the Scripture Hall (which controls the sect's practice tradition and inner-disciple training) and have the quiet sympathy of the Sword-Craft Hall and the Formation Hall (whose heads consider the expansion a distraction from craft).
   183→
   184→Leng Wushuang holds the balance. Her personal position is expansionist — the vein is the resource for her third attempt — but she is wary of the Jade Void (correctly; the Jade Void's intelligence network is real and the holy land's interest in the Cangli region is documented) and she is wary of her own daughter's dissent. She has not committed. The commitment, when it comes (session ~300, with the formal claim), is the political turn that begins the Act 2 endgame.

   186→### 2.6 Leng Qingxue (冷清雪): position, dissent, arc
   187→
   188→Leng Qingxue is one hundred and twenty years old, looks thirty, and is the youngest Core Formation elder in the Azure Sword Sect's history. She is the head of the Punishment Hall (刑堂堂主) — a factional balancing appointment by Leng Wushuang, who gave her daughter real authority (the Punishment Hall enforces the sect's rules and adjudicates internal disputes) specifically to keep the Expansionist-controlled Merit and Intelligence Halls from controlling enforcement. Leng Qingxue is, in this sense, her mother's creature — placed where she is to serve her mother's balance strategy. She is also, in another sense, her mother's opposite.
   189→
   190→Her reason for dissenting is older than her position. At age ninety (before her Core Formation breakthrough, when she was a Foundation Establishment inner disciple on grotto-heaven rotation), she was sent by the previous Punishment Hall head to investigate a complaint from a Cangwu Sect tenant village about an Azure Sword Sect outer disciple who had beaten a farmer over a grain dispute. Leng Qingxue investigated, found the complaint valid, and punished the outer disciple (a demerit and a fine). She also, in the course of the investigation, spent three weeks at the Cangwu Sect as Wu Changqing's guest (Wu Changqing was forty-two at the time, three years into his sect master-ship). The three weeks shaped her. She found the Cangwu Sect's water-phase tradition elegant (she had been trained in metal-phase; water-phase was, to her, a revelation of softness). She found Wu Changqing's grief and discipline moving. She found the vassalage arrangement, which she had not previously understood in concrete terms, morally indefensible. She returned to Biluo Peak and argued, in the elder council, against the tribute's current rate. The argument was overruled (the council, then as now, was Expansionist-led); Leng Qingxue accepted the overruling but did not change her view. Her Core Formation breakthrough, three years later, was achieved with a quiet conviction that the sect she was rising to serve was, in one respect, wrong.
   191→
   192→Her arc (per doc 26 §9.3): the principled dissident within the sect (Act 2), the protagonist's political opposite and romance (Act 2-3), the exile who co-founds the Wang lineage school (Act 3, ending 1). Her arc is the arc of principle: she must choose, in Act 3, between her sect (her mother, her lineage, her duty, her Core Formation seat) and her principles (the Cangli Riverlands, the Cangwu Sect's legitimate claim, the protagonist, the law). She chooses principle. The choice costs her the sect. She does not, in the end, regret it — but the cost is real, and the simulation tracks it.

   194→### 2.7 The three spirit veins
   195→
   196→The Azure Sword Sect's three spirit veins are all metal-phase primary (a geological consequence of the Northern Cangwu range's granitic substrate, which is metal-phase-bearing; doc 14 §1.1) with varying secondary phases. They are, in descending order of output:
   197→
   198→The **Biluo Vein** (碧螺脈) is the sect's primary vein, breaking the surface inside the compound walls beneath the Sword-Ancestor Hall. Its current runs deep (approximately four hundred meters below the peak's summit) and its output is sufficient to sustain two hundred cultivators at full Qi Condensation practice, or thirty at Foundation Establishment. The vein's surface trace is the **Founder's Spring** (祖師泉), a small spring in the Founder's Garden whose water is faintly metallic to the taste and which the sect's inner disciples drink from each morning as a minor cultivation aid. The Biluo Vein has been tapped for 180 years and shows no sign of thinning; its flow is stable, its channel wide. This is the vein that feeds the compound's main cultivation hall and the grotto-heaven's anchor (the grotto-heaven encloses the vein's main current; the surface trace is the anchor — per doc 24 §2.7).
   199→
   200→The **North Cloud Vein** (北雲脈) is the sect's secondary vein, breaking the surface two li north of the compound, on the far side of Biluo Peak. Its current is shallower (one hundred and fifty meters) and its output is roughly half the Biluo Vein's. The vein's surface trace is a cold, mineral-heavy spring in a small hanging valley that the sect has developed into the **North Cloud Garden** (北雲苑), a terraced herb garden growing metal-phase and water-phase spirit herbs for the Pill Hall. The garden is managed by a Foundation Establishment elder (currently Elder Qi Shoujing, an Isolationist-leaning quiet ally of Jing You) and staffed by six inner disciples on rotating assignment. The North Cloud Vein's output is not directly tapped for cultivation; it feeds the herb garden, which feeds the Pill Hall, which produces the sect's pills. The chain is the sect's pharmaceutical backbone.
   201→
   202→The **Iron Root Vein** (鐵根脈) is the sect's tertiary vein, breaking the surface four li southwest of the compound, in a wild region of the peak's lower slopes. Its current is the shallowest of the three (sixty meters) and its output is the smallest (sufficient for thirty Qi Condensation cultivators). The vein's surface trace is a stand of iron-root pine (the same species that grows in the grotto-heaven, doc 2.3 — the surface trace and the grotto-heaven population share a common ancestor population isolated when the grotto-heaven's boundary formed). The Iron Root Vein is the sect's sword-craft resource: the iron-root pine's wood, saturated by the vein's metal-phase qi, is dense enough to be forged into practice swords and, with sufficient treatment, into low-grade artifact-grade weapons. The Sword-Craft Hall manages the stand and the forge.
   203→
   204→The three veins together produce enough qi to sustain the sect's four hundred disciples at Qi Condensation practice and approximately forty at Foundation Establishment practice — but not enough to fund a Nascent Soul breakthrough. Leng Wushuang's two failed attempts depleted the sect's spirit-stone reserves (the sect held, before the first attempt, a reserve of three thousand stones; the reserve is now four hundred). The third attempt requires, by Leng Wushuang's estimate, two thousand stones of concentrated qi and six months of uninterrupted grotto-heaven access. The Cangli Riverlands' deep vein extension (§2.4) is, for Leng Wushuang, the resource that closes this gap — either by direct tapping (if the formal claim succeeds) or by trade (if the Jade Void Holy Land can be induced to supply stones in exchange for territorial concessions).
   205→
   206→### 2.8 The grotto-heaven rotation system
   207→
   208→The Jade Edge Grotto's six-month rotations are the Azure Sword Sect's most contested internal resource. The system: every inner disciple is eligible for a six-month rotation once per three-year cycle; the rotation places them inside the grotto-heaven for intensive cultivation, with access to the grotto's metal-phase-accelerated qi, the herb population, and (under supervision) the beast population for combat practice. Three hundred and seventy of the four hundred disciples cycle through; the remaining thirty (the sect master, the three peak masters, the eight hall heads, and senior inner disciples on sect duty) practice in the compound's main cultivation hall, which is fed by a direct tap from the Biluo Vein.
   209→
   210→Rotation slots are allocated by the Merit Hall (Expansionist-controlled, §2.5) on the basis of merit-points accumulated through sect missions, grotto-heaven herb-gathering, and (in practice) factional alignment. A disciple in good standing with Elder Rong Zhaoyuan's faction can expect a slot every three years; a disciple out of favor may wait five. The protagonist, on first contact with the Azure Sword Sect (session ~200, doc 26 §4.2), is offered a courtesy slot by Leng Wushuang — a political gesture (the protagonist is not a sect member and would not normally be offered grotto-heaven access) that the Expansionists receive as provocation and the Isolationists receive as a sign that Leng Wushuang is, despite her expansionist position, willing to extend the sect's courtesies to a powerful outsider. The protagonist's acceptance or refusal of the slot is the first Azure Sword Sect political choice and shapes the factional response to the protagonist for the rest of Act 2.
   211→
   212→The grotto-heaven's rotation system has, over the sect's 180-year history, produced one structural problem: the grotto's ecology is not infinite. The sword-grass stands, the blue-flame lotus beds, and the iron-root pine groves are renewable but only at a rate set by the grotto's qi-climate; over-harvesting depletes them. The herb population has thinned measurably in the last sixty years (Jing You's Scripture Hall has documented this; Rong Zhaoyuan's Merit Hall has not acknowledged it). The silver-furred fox population is also down (the Isolationists attribute this to over-hunting by rotation disciples; the Expansionists attribute it to a natural cycle). The jade-scaled serpent, by all observations, is unaffected — but no one in the sect is willing to test whether that would change. The grotto's slow ecological decline is, for the Isolationists, an argument against expansion (the sect cannot manage what it has); for the Expansionists, an argument for it (the sect needs a second grotto-heaven to relieve the first).
   213→
   214→### 2.9 The regional position: the Jade Void, the Alliance, and the courts
   215→
   216→The Azure Sword Sect is the regional hegemon of the central and northern Cangwu range, but it is not the regional hegemon of the broader Cangli watershed. That position belongs, in cultivation-world accounting, to the **Jade Void Holy Land** (玉虛聖地, doc 24 §4.3) — a Nascent Soul+ holy land on another continent, with three thousand disciples and a peak grotto-heaven, whose founder (Daoist Jade Void, two thousand one hundred years old) was, per the holy land's own records, born in the Cangli Riverlands. The Jade Void's interest in the Cangli region is, formally, ancestral veneration — the holy land maintains a small shrine (staffed by two Foundation Establishment elders on rotating assignment) at the site of the founder's birth village, which is, by the holy land's archives' best estimate, somewhere in the central Cangwu foothills within thirty li of Wang Family Bend. The shrine's exact location has been lost (the founder's birth village was abandoned in a flood fourteen hundred years ago and the site has shifted); the Jade Void's rotating elders search for it, quietly, on their assignments, and have not found it. The Azure Sword Sect knows of this search and tolerates it, because the Jade Void's interest is, on balance, a strategic asset — a holy land's claim to the region deters other holy lands from contesting it.
   217→
   218→The Azure Sword Sect's relationship to the Jade Void is therefore careful. The sect pays nominal homage to the holy land (a small tribute of one mid-grade spirit stone per decade, delivered by a Foundation Establishment elder to the Jade Void's rotating shrine staff); the Jade Void, in return, extends the sect its implicit protection against other holy lands. The arrangement is unwritten and is, in the cultivation world's accounting, fragile. If the Azure Sword Sect's expansion into the Cangli Riverlands is perceived by the Jade Void as a violation of the region's ancestral-veneration status, the holy land's protection could withdraw — and a holy land's withdrawn protection is, in the cultivation world's brutal accounting, an invitation to other powers to contest the now-unprotected region. This is the Isolationist faction's strongest argument (§2.5): the expansion risks the Jade Void's goodwill, and the Jade Void's goodwill is the sect's long-term strategic anchor.
   219→
   220→The Azure Sword Sect's relationship to the **Immortal Alliance** (仙盟, doc 12 §6, doc 20 §1.3) is formal. The sect holds a mid-tier alliance seat (one of thirty in the eastern region), pays annual alliance dues (twelve mid-grade spirit stones), and is entitled to alliance adjudication of disputes with other member sects. The Cangli spirit vein dispute (§2.4), when it is filed at session ~300, will be filed with the alliance. The alliance's adjudication is slow (the average dispute resolution takes eight years) and the alliance's enforcement is weak (the alliance has no standing army; it relies on collective action by member sects). The Azure Sword Sect knows this; the Cangwu Sect knows this; both know that the formal adjudication is, in practice, a stalling tactic while the two sects maneuver on the ground. The protagonist's intervention (mediation, alliance lobbying, or — if the player chooses — bringing in the Jade Void as a third party) is the Act 2 political verb.
   221→
   222→The Azure Sword Sect's relationship to the **Courts of Heaven** (天庭, doc 15, doc 20 §1.3) is distant. The courts have no direct jurisdiction over Acquired-stratum sect disputes; the courts' concern is records of the dead and heaven-sealed oaths. The Azure Sword Sect, however, has one courts-related obligation: the sect's Foundation Establishment+ cultivators, when they die, are subject to the courts' soul-rites (per doc 25 §5). The sect maintains a small temple (the **Sword-Ancestor Side-Shrine**, a single-room building near the Founder's Garden) staffed by a Qi Condensation lay cultivator who performs the basic soul-rites for the sect's dead. The shrine is not a temple in the institutional sense (doc 12 §4); it is a side-function the sect performs for its own. The courts know of the shrine and tolerate it; the courts do not, in practice, intervene in the sect's funerary practice as long as the basic rites are performed. This distant relationship is the sect's only direct contact with the Precelestial, and it is, for the sect's cultivators, an abstract concern — the courts are a thing one deals with at death, not in life. The protagonist, at Foundation Establishment, will deal with the courts directly only if and when a sect member's anchor requires formal processing (the death of Old Chen, doc 26 §12, is the first such event the protagonist navigates).

   228→### 3.1 What the hexagrams are and how Wang Lun casts them
   229→
   230→Wang Lun (王倫, 41, the lineage school teacher, doc 06 Scene 1) casts the hexagram by the yarrow-stalk method — the slow, careful procedure (fifty stalks, divided and counted in eighteen passes, producing six lines from bottom to top, each line solid or broken, each line possibly changing) that yields one of the sixty-four hexagrams of the Zhou Book of Changes (周易). The cast is deterministic from `seed + playerState` (doc 06 Scene 1, Procedural implications): the same seed and the same player state produce the same hexagram. The player state, at Scene 1 (autumn of the player's sixteenth year), is determined by the player's choices over the first five sessions — primarily the player's responses to the first two qi perceptions, the player's dialogue with Wang Lun during lessons, and the player's behavior in the lineage hall (whether they have hidden the perception, spoken of it, grieved in it, etc.). The state is one of eight profiles. Each profile produces one hexagram. The hexagram, in Wang Lun's reading, names what the player is becoming; the player's journal records what they heard.
   231→
   232→The eight specified below are the eight most likely. (The other fifty-six hexagrams are reachable in principle but require player states that the first five sessions make unlikely — states of profound depression, ecstatic mania, or already-formed cultivation intent that the sixteen-year-old mortal protagonist is not yet capable of.) Each entry gives: the hexagram's name (Hanzi), the judgment text (the cryptic line Wang Lun reads aloud from the Book of Changes, in the traditional form), how the hexagram points toward Old Chen (the direction Wang Lun gives), and the journal entry the protagonist writes that night.
