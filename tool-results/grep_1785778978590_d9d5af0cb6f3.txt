3:**Status:** Candidate canon. Specifies the cosmic-scale history of the finite qi-stratified cosmos — the age, the epochs, the primordial authors, the founding of the Courts, the Great War, the fallen civilizations, the sealed evils, and the revelation-arc by which the protagonist learns any of this. Compliant with 00_FOUNDATIONAL_DECISIONS §1 (finite, generated, non-regenerating cosmos); 15_PRECELESTIAL_AND_TRIBULATION §2-5 (courts, ancestral courts, spirit wilds, law reaches); 24_RECONCILIATION_AND_DECISIONS §2.6 (generative descent), §4.4 (regional history); 25_FOLK_RELIGION_METAPHYSICS §5-6 (demonic cultivators, the underworld); 12_SECT_INSTITUTIONS §6 (the immortal alliance); 31_NAMED_INSTITUTIONS_AND_HEXAGRAMS §2.9 (the Jade Void Holy Land's Cangli birth-origin).
12:The corpus had, until this document, no cosmic history. The regional history (doc 24 §4.4) is three dates: 247 years ago the Great Yan Dynasty was founded, 180 years ago the Azure Sword Sect was constituted, 95 years ago Zhou Wugui killed three villages in the Cangwu foothills. These are village-scale dates. They describe a backwater's last two and a half centuries. They do not describe a cosmos.
14:The cosmology (doc 00 §1) commits the cosmos to being finite, generated, and non-regenerating. The Precelestial document (doc 15) commits the Courts to being an institution founded in historical time, with ministers who have held office for three thousand years and a Wild Suppression Bloc that has advocated for the same campaign for several millennia. The folk-religion document (doc 25 §6) commits the Underworld to being a courts sub-jurisdiction staffed by Tribulation Crossing cultivators — an institution, not a metaphysical absolute. These are commitments to *historical* objects — objects that began, have persisted, and (because the cosmos is finite) will end. None of them had a history.
16:This document writes that history. It commits to: an age (§1); a sequence of named epochs (§2); the primordial figures who authored the laws the protagonist inherits (§3); the founding of the Courts and the compact that bounds them (§4); the Great War whose scars are the Law Reaches (§5); the civilizations that rose and fell and left ruins that Mahayana cultivators explore (§6); the sealed evils that the Law Reaches and the ancestral courts contain (§7); the revelation-arc by which the protagonist learns any of this, realm by realm (§8); the place of the Cangli Riverlands' tiny history within the cosmic scale (§9); and a single chronological table from the Origin's generation to the present (§10).
18:The doctrine (AGENTS.md Part 3) demands that every gate be paired with what it unlocks. Cosmic history unlocks: the ruins the protagonist can explore (fallen civilizations); the endgame threats the protagonist can confront (sealed evils); the institutional context the protagonist inherits at Mahayana (the Courts, the alliance, the Treaty); and the tragic temporal engine — the protagonist's life as a tiny segment at the end of a finite cosmos whose qi is declining. The history must feel old. It does. It is.
26:The cosmos is **480,000 years old**, by the Courts of Heaven's Records Department's best estimate (the **Cosmic Age Calculation**, 宇宙紀年之算, hereafter CAC). The CAC is not a round number; it is a measured one, derived from three independent lines of evidence that the Records Department has reconciled across the eleven millennia since the calculation was first attempted:
28:1. **Substrate-depth measurement.** The Precelestial's substrate-law has a measurable *depth* — the accumulated inscription of Mahayana authorship, layer on layer, like geological strata. A Tribulation Crossing cultivator with the right perception can read the depth the way a geologist reads rock. The deepest substrate-layers, at the Courts' own foundations, register approximately 380,000 years of accumulated inscription. Below that is the bare Precelestial substrate — the Nameless Origin's generative output, undifferentiated, with no cultivator-authored inscription. The substrate-depth gives a floor: the first Mahayana authorship began approximately 380,000 years ago, plus or minus ten thousand.
30:2. **Ancestor-memory correlation.** The oldest persistent ancestors in the deepest ancestral courts — the founders of the lineages that predate the Courts — remember the Courts' founding. They remember it as an event in their living lifetimes, not as history. The oldest of them, by their own accounts, lived for approximately 60,000 years before the Courts' founding as Tribulation Crossing cultivators and have persisted as ancestors for approximately 220,000 years since. Their accounts do not agree on details (ancestor-memory is slow and partial, per doc 15 §3.2), but they agree on magnitude: the Courts were founded approximately 220,000 years ago, and the first authors who predated them lived for tens of thousands of years before that.
32:3. **Qi-density decline projection.** The cosmos is finite and non-regenerating (doc 00 §1). The Acquired Stratum's qi-density is measurably lower now than at the Courts' founding — the decline is small in any century, but it compounds. A Nascent Soul cultivator with sufficient patience can measure the decline over a millennium and project backward. The projection, calibrated against the substrate-depth and ancestor-memory data, yields an age of approximately 480,000 years from the Origin's first generation to the present, with an error bar of plus or minus thirty thousand years.
34:The Records Department publishes the CAC as 480,000 years and notes the error bar in a footnote that nobody reads. The figure is contested by three factions within the Courts (the Traditionalists hold for 600,000; the Substrate-Depths hold for 380,000; the Ancestor-Memory hold for 500,000), but the published figure is the one the protagonist will encounter, at Nascent Soul, in any Courts-affiliated library.
36:### 1.2 The finitude, felt
38:480,000 years is vast enough that civilizations have risen and fallen and left only ruins that Mahayana cultivators explore. It is finite enough that the cosmos's finitude is felt — the qi-density decline is perceptible at Core Formation over a cultivator's lifetime (a Core Formation cultivator who lives three hundred years perceives the decline as a faint thinning of the ambient, like a slow dimming of a lamp), and at Nascent Soul the decline is unmistakable. The protagonist, if they reach Nascent Soul, will perceive the decline. The protagonist, if they reach Mahayana, will perceive it as the central fact of their endgame — the Law Reaches are expanding (the substrate-law is thinning), the spirit veins are diminishing, the cosmos is running down.
40:The cosmos is not dying. 480,000 years into a finite lifespan, with the decline-rate the Records Department projects, the cosmos has perhaps another two million years before the qi-density drops below the threshold at which cultivation is possible. This is not a near-term crisis. But it is the cosmological context of every act of cultivation: the protagonist is practicing in a cosmos that is, slowly, running out of qi. The Courts know this. The alliance knows this. The holy lands know this. None of them speak of it below the Nascent Soul tier, because the knowledge is not useful below that tier and is destabilizing above it. The protagonist will learn it at Nascent Soul. The protagonist will not be told what to do about it.
42:### 1.3 Why this number, and not another
44:The number is committed because the doctrine (AGENTS.md Part 3) says: make decisions, do not defer. A cosmic history without an age is a sketch; a cosmic history with an age is a thing the protagonist can stand inside and feel the weight of. 480,000 is large enough to do the work (five epochs, three fallen civilizations, a Great War whose scars persist) and small enough to feel finite (the protagonist's thousand-year life, if they reach Nascent Soul, is one four-hundred-and-eightieth of cosmic history — a single page in a long book). It is not the genre's traditional ten thousand years (一萬年, the standard "ancient" timescale), because the genre's ten thousand years is a number chosen for mythic resonance, not for cosmological coherence. 480,000 is chosen for cosmological coherence. The mythic resonance is a side effect.
50:Cosmic history is divided into **seven named epochs**, each defined by what characterized it, each ending with a transition event. The epoch names are the Courts of Heaven's Records Department's official taxonomy (the **Epochal Sequence**, 紀元之序); they are not mortal terms and are not known below the Nascent Soul tier.

64:Each epoch has a *character* — a single phrase that captures what it was like to live inside it. The Generative Epoch had no beings to experience it; its character is the Origin's generative function itself, the slow condensation of undifferentiated qi into the first stable patterns. The Primordial Substrate Epoch's character is the slow awakening of the first beings — entities whose existence was barely distinguishable from the substrate, who perceived qi the way a fish perceives water, who had no concept of cultivation because cultivation had not been invented. The First Law Epoch's character is the vertiginous recognition (per doc 15 §1.4) that the world's law was not given but authored — that the rules the first cultivators lived inside were choices someone had made, and that they themselves could make such choices. The Court Founding Epoch's character is the slow, contentious, often-violent process by which the second generation of Mahayana cultivators agreed to be governed. The Great War Epoch's character is the war itself — twenty thousand years of cosmic-scale conflict, longer than any mortal civilization has ever persisted, sustained by Mahayana cultivators who did not die easily. The Long Peace Epoch's character is the slow accumulation of institutions, ruins, and decline — the epoch in which most of the cosmos's civilizations rose and fell, in which the Courts became what they are now (an institution whose first priority is its own perpetuation, per doc 15 §2.4), in which the qi-density decline became perceptible. The Current Epoch's character is the decline — not catastrophic, not yet, but unmistakable to anyone who looks. The protagonist's life is lived inside the decline. The protagonist does not know this, at the start.
70:The first Mahayana cultivators — the **Primordial Authors** (元作者, yuán zuòzhě) — are the cosmology's primordial figures. They are the beings who authored the laws the protagonist inherits. The Courts' Records Department recognizes five (with a footnote acknowledging that the number is contested and that a sixth and seventh may have dispersed without leaving records). Each is named below, with their Dao, the law they authored, and their fate.
74:**Dao:** the law of phase (五行之理, the principle of the five phases). Wuwang's comprehension was that qi, in its undifferentiated state, contained the *possibility* of differentiation — that the five phases (fire, water, wood, metal, earth) were not separate substances but modes of a single qi, and that the modes could be made stable by law.
76:**Law authored:** the **Phase Cycle Law** (五行周流法) — the first law of phase. "Qi shall differentiate according to the five-phase cycle; the phases shall generate and restrain each other in their proper order; a phase shall not collapse into another phase except by lawful transformation." This is the law that makes wuxing (doc 00 §6) a lawful system rather than a mortal taxonomy. Every five-phase interaction the protagonist will ever perceive — every fire-conquers-metal combat technique, every water-nourishes-wood healing pill, every earth-generates-metal spirit vein — is sustained by Wuwang's law, authored approximately 150,000 years ago.
78:**Fate:** Wuwang persisted through the First Law Epoch and the Court Founding Epoch as the first ancestor of the **Wuwang Lineage** (無妄宗), one of the oldest cultivation lineages. He took no office in the Courts; he considered the Courts' jurisdiction a useful but inferior arrangement, and preferred his own ancestral court's authority. The Wuwang Lineage's ancestral court was one of the most respected in the Precelestial for 140,000 years. In the Great War (§5), the Wuwang Ancestral Court was destroyed by the Severance — Wuwang himself was dispersed defending it. His anchor is gone; his law persists. The Wuwang Lineage survives in the Acquired Stratum as a minor noble lineage (the present Marquis of Wuwang, a mortal, is the lineage's senior living member; he does not know his lineage's founder authored the five-phase law). The protagonist may encounter the Wuwang Lineage's surviving records at the Jade Void Holy Land's library, at Nascent Soul.
82:**Dao:** the law of the anchor (靈樞之法, the law of the spiritual pivot). Taiyi's comprehension was that a being is not its body (which is mortal) and not its qi (which circulates and dissipates) but its *anchor* — the self-inscribed law-pattern that persists (per doc 24 §2.2). Before Taiyi, the first cultivators had bodies and qi but no concept of an anchor; they could not have explained why they persisted through bodily changes. Taiyi named the anchor and gave it law.
84:**Law authored:** the **Anchor Law** (靈樞法) — "every being shall be constituted by an anchor, a self-inscribed law-pattern that persists through bodily change and qi-circulation; the anchor is the being's authoritative aspect; the anchor is noncopyable (two anchors in the same scope would be the same being, which is a paradox the cosmos resolves by collapsing the duplicate); the anchor carries karmic trace as inscriptions upon itself." This is the law that makes the soul model (doc 00 §2) lawful. Every act of cultivation the protagonist will ever perform — every Foundation Establishment integration, every Core Formation condensation, every Nascent Soul externalization — is the protagonist re-architecting their anchor, and the anchor they re-architect is lawful because Taiyi authored its law.
86:**Fate:** Taiyi took office in the Courts of Heaven at their founding (§4) as the first **Minister of Records** (錄部尚書), the Records Department's founding head. She held the office for 80,000 years — the longest ministerial tenure in the Courts' history. She is the only one of the first five Mahayana whose anchor is known to persist. When she retired from the Ministership, she did not return to an ancestral court; she descended into the Records Department's deepest archive and *fused her anchor with the archive's substrate-law*, becoming the archive itself. The Records Department's ministers ritually consult her — the consultation is called **Taiyi's Reading** (太一之閱), and it is performed by the Minister of Records alone, in the deepest archive, once per century, on a question the Minister has prepared for fifty years. The Reading is not a conversation; it is the Minister perceiving, through the archive's substrate, the accumulated weight of every record the Courts have ever kept. The Reading has been performed 1,400 times since Taiyi's fusion. The protagonist, at Mahayana, could in principle be present at a Reading (the Minister's prerogative); they almost certainly will not be.
90:**Dao:** the law of rebirth (輪迴之法, the law of the wheel). Cangsheng's comprehension was that the anchor, upon bodily death, must persist in an intermediate state and enter a new body — that the alternative (the anchor dispersing at every bodily death) would make cultivation meaningless (a cultivator who perfected their anchor across a lifetime would have it vanish at death; the species would never accumulate). Cangsheng authored the law that makes the species' accumulation possible.
92:**Law authored:** the **Rebirth Law** (輪迴法) — "the anchor, upon bodily death, shall persist in bardo (中有) for a bounded, lawful time (forty-nine days, by default); within that window, the anchor shall be processed — retrieved by a lineage rite, retrieved by a sect rite, or processed by the Underworld for rebirth; at the window's end, the anchor shall enter rebirth according to its karmic trace; the rebirth shall be into a body whose qi-climate matches the anchor's accumulated tendencies; the being shall not remember the prior life in detail, but shall carry the anchor's karmic trace as disposition." This is the law that makes the bardo (doc 25 §3) and the Underworld (doc 25 §6) lawful. Every ghost the protagonist will ever disperse, every ancestor they will ever consult, every reborn cultivator they will ever meet — all are lawful because Cangsheng authored the law that governs them.
94:**Fate:** Cangsheng descended into the Underworld's processing-arm at its founding (an event the Records Department dates to approximately Year 200,000, twenty thousand years before the Courts' formal founding — the Underworld is older than the Courts, because rebirth is older than governance). He became the Underworld's first **Minister of Bardo Processing** (中有部尚書), and he has held the office since. He is the oldest persistent being in the cosmos whose anchor is still individuated (Taiyi's anchor is fused with the archive; Cangsheng's is still his own). He is, by the Records Department's best estimate, 330,000 years old. He has not been seen by a living cultivator in 80,000 years; the Underworld's staff (Tribulation Crossing cultivators on rotating assignment) report that he is still present, still processing, still slower than he was. The protagonist, at Mahayana, could in principle request an audience (the Minister of Bardo Processing's prerogative to grant); the request has been granted four times in the Courts' history, each time to a Mahayana cultivator who had authored a law touching on rebirth. The protagonist, if they author such a law, would be the fifth.
98:**Dao:** the law of stratification (分層之法, the law of the layered strata). Liyuan's comprehension was that qi, in its descent from the Origin, stratifies by density — the densest ascending (Precelestial), the middling remaining (Acquired), the diffuse descending (Mortal) — and that the strata are separated by boundaries that are lawful, not physical. Liyuan authored the law that makes the cosmos a *stratified* cosmos rather than a uniform one.
100:**Law authored:** the **Stratification Law** (分層法) — "qi shall stratify according to density; the strata shall be three (Precelestial, Acquired, Mortal); the stratum-boundaries shall be lawful boundaries, enforced by tribulation when crossed; the boundaries shall be permeable to qi-descent (the Origin's generative function flows downward) but impermeable to beings except by lawful crossing." This is the law that makes the three-stratum cosmos (doc 00 §1) lawful. Every spirit vein the protagonist will ever tap (a qi-descent through the boundary), every tribulation they will ever face (a boundary-enforcement), every ascension they will ever witness (a lawful crossing) — all are lawful because Liyuan authored the law that governs them.
102:**Fate:** Liyuan is the tragedy of the first five. He disagreed with the other four on the question of whether the stratum-boundaries should be *enforced*. His law authored the boundaries; he believed, in his old age, that the boundaries were tyranny — that the cosmos would be better if qi circulated freely, if mortals could cultivate without crossing, if the Precelestial's qi descended without dilution. He attempted to amend his own law (the first and only attempt by a Primordial Author to undo their own authorship). The amendment would have dissolved the Mortal-Acquired boundary in a small test region. The tribulation (the first law tribulation in cosmic history, per doc 15 §6.3 form 3) fired. Liyuan survived the tribulation but the law-amendment failed; the cosmos resisted. Liyuan, in the aftermath, deviated — his heart-mind fragmented around the conviction (the delusional conviction, 妄信, per doc 24 §1.2) that the boundaries must be dissolved. He retreated into a private grotto-heaven and was not seen for 100,000 years. When he emerged, he had become the founder of the **Severance** (§5), the cosmic-scale faction that fought the Great War to dissolve the stratum-boundaries. He was dispersed in the war's final battle by a coalition of his peers (the coalition included two of the other Primordial Authors, Wuwang and Tianxu, both of whom dispersed in the same battle). His anchor is gone. His law persists — the Stratification Law was not amended; the boundaries he authored are the boundaries the protagonist inherits.
106:**Dao:** the law of tribulation (天劫之法, the law of the heavenly tribulation). Tianxu's comprehension was that the boundaries between states — between realms, between strata, between lawful and unlawful — must be *enforced*, and that enforcement requires a mechanism that is itself lawful, not arbitrary. She authored the law that makes tribulation a lawful response to boundary-violation, not a divine punishment.
108:**Law authored:** the **Tribulation Law** (天劫法) — "the boundaries between states shall be enforced; the enforcement shall be by tribulation (天劫), a lawful event that tests whether the new state can sustain itself; tribulation shall take forms appropriate to the boundary crossed (lightning for realm-boundaries, heart-demon for heart-mind-boundaries, karmic for karmic-boundaries, law for authorship-boundaries); tribulation is not punishment but proof; the cultivator who survives the tribulation has demonstrated that the new state is lawful." This is the law that makes the tribulation system (doc 15 §6) lawful. Every tribulation the protagonist will ever face — every Core Formation tribulation, every Nascent Soul tribulation, every Tribulation Crossing tribulation — is lawful because Tianxu authored the law that governs them.
110:**Fate:** Tianxu took office in the Courts of Heaven at their founding as the first **Minister of Defense** (防部尚書), the Defense Department's founding head. She held the office for 60,000 years — a shorter tenure than Taiyi's, because the Defense Department's work was harder (the Spirit Wilds' borders required constant enforcement, and the wilds did not respect the Courts' jurisdiction). She retired at Year 280,000 and returned to her ancestral court — the **Tianxu Ancestral Court**, seated in the **Heavenly Lattice Grotto-Heaven** (天羅洞天), a peak grotto-heaven in the Precelestial's eastern interior. She persisted there as the court's founding ancestor for 80,000 years. In the Great War (§5), the Heavenly Lattice Grotto-Heaven was the Severance's first target (Tianxu had been the Severance's chief opponent in the Courts for 60,000 years; Liyuan considered her the author of the tyranny he sought to undo). The grotto-heaven's boundary was breached; the grotto collapsed; Tianxu's anchor dispersed in the collapse. Her ancestral court ended; the Heavenly Lattice Grotto-Heaven's ruins persist in the Precelestial's eastern interior as a Law Reach — the **Reach of the Lattice** (羅網法域), one of the war's largest scars. The protagonist, at Mahayana, could in principle visit the Reach; it is one of the few Law Reaches with a known Precelestial-side anchor, and the Courts' Defense Department maintains a small monitoring station at its edge.
114:The Records Department acknowledges, in a footnote to the Primordial Authors register, that the number five is contested. The Substrate-Depths faction holds for seven (two Primordial Authors dispersed in the Primordial Substrate Epoch without leaving authorship records, and their laws, if any, were unwritten by their dispersal). The Traditionalists hold for three (only Wuwang, Taiyi, and Cangsheng have unbroken attestation; Liyuan's authorship is attested only by the Severance's documents, which the Courts do not recognize; Tianxu's authorship is attested by the Tribulation Law itself, but the Traditionalists argue the Tribulation Law could have been authored by a later Mahayana and attributed to Tianxu for political reasons). The published register lists five. The protagonist, at Mahayana, can read the footnote and the three factions' positions and decide for themselves. This is one of the small historical uncertainties the cosmos contains — the kind of uncertainty that a Mahayana cultivator, with the Records' full access, can spend a century investigating and not resolve.
120:### 4.1 When, and by whom
124:The convocation was attended by **forty-three Mahayana cultivators** (per the Records Department's attendance roll, which survives) and **approximately three hundred Tribulation Crossing cultivators** (the second generation, who had crossed tribulation in the Court Founding Epoch's first millennia and who, per doc 15 §2.1, were the Courts' actual founders — Mahayana cultivators took office in the Courts after the founding, but the founding itself was the Tribulation Crossing generation's act). The convocation lasted, by the Records Department's account, **three hundred years** — a duration that indicates the depth of the disagreements. The convocation produced the **Compact of the Six Jurisdictions** (六轄之約), the founding document of the Courts of Heaven.
128:Before the Courts' founding, the Precelestial was a patchwork of **personal jurisdictions**. Each Mahayana cultivator's authored law held in their own grotto-heaven or domain; outside those domains, there was no law except the Precelestial's bare substrate-law (the Origin's generative output, undifferentiated). The situation had three features that made it unsustainable:
130:1. **No enforcement against the Spirit Wilds.** The Spirit Wilds (per doc 15 §4) are the Precelestial's unregulated zones — the gaps between jurisdictions. Wild beasts, yao, and law-paradoxes bred in the wilds and occasionally breached cultivated domains. A Mahayana cultivator whose domain was breached could defend it; a coalition of lesser cultivators whose domains were breached could not. There was no common defense. The wilds were, in the Court Founding Epoch's early millennia, expanding — the substrate-law was thinning at the edges of the cultivated domains, and the wilds grew into the gaps.
132:2. **No record-keeping for the dead.** The Rebirth Law (Cangsheng's law) had been in effect for fifty thousand years before the Courts' founding, and the Underworld's processing-arm had been operating (under Cangsheng's personal administration) for twenty thousand years. But the Underworld processed anchors without records — a being's karmic trace was read at rebirth-assignment, but no record was kept of the assignment. A cultivator who wished to consult the dead (the ancestor-consultation rite, per doc 15 §3.3) had no way to find the ancestor's rebirth-lineage. Lineages that lost track of their ancestors' rebirths lost their ancestral courts' continuity.
134:3. **No tribulation administration.** The Tribulation Law (Tianxu's law) had been in effect for fifty thousand years, and tribulations had been firing (the cosmos's lawful boundary-enforcement, automatic). But there was no administration of tribulation — no record of which cultivator faced which tribulation, no study of tribulation forms, no maintenance of the tribulation system's lawful substrate. Tribulations were perceived as arbitrary divine punishments (the mortal-projection version, per doc 25 §6.2) even by cultivators who should have known better. The Tribulation Law was operating; the tribulation system was not understood.
136:The Courts' founding addressed all three. The Courts established a common defense (the Defense Department, against the Wilds), a common records system (the Records Department, for the dead), and a common tribulation administration (the Defense Department's tribulation office, for the tribulation system). The Courts did not — and do not — claim to be the *source* of the Precelestial's law (per doc 15 §2.1: "They are not a god, not a pantheon, not the source of the stratum's law"). They are an institution that administers and enforces law that the Primordial Authors authored.
140:The Compact of the Six Jurisdictions (六轄之約) is the Courts' founding document. It defines the Courts' jurisdiction as six distinct domains, and explicitly bounds the Courts' authority at the edges of those domains. The Compact's text survives in the Records Department's archive (Taiyi's Reading, §3.2 above, has confirmed the text's integrity 1,400 times). The six jurisdictions:
142:1. **The Dead.** The Courts hold jurisdiction over the dead, the bardo, and rebirth. The Records Department maintains the register of the dead; the Underworld (a Records sub-jurisdiction) processes anchors for rebirth. Lineage rites and sect rites that retrieve anchors from bardo are lawful *within* this jurisdiction (the lineage or sect has custody of its own dead); the Courts' jurisdiction is the default, when no lineage or sect rite is performed.
144:2. **Tribulation.** The Courts hold jurisdiction over tribulation administration. The Defense Department's tribulation office records tribulations, studies tribulation forms, and maintains the tribulation system's lawful substrate. The Courts do not *control* tribulation (the Tribulation Law is Tianxu's, and the cosmos enforces it automatically); the Courts administer the system's records and substrate.
146:3. **The Maintained Roads.** The Courts hold jurisdiction over the maintained roads (守道, the inter-domain transit routes through the Precelestial). The roads are the Courts' oldest and most contested jurisdiction — they are the physical infrastructure of the Courts' authority, and the Wild Suppression Bloc's expansionist claims (per doc 15 §2.4) derive from a clause in this jurisdiction that authorizes the Courts to extend the roads into the Spirit Wilds.
148:4. **The Spirit Wilds' Borders.** The Courts hold jurisdiction over the *borders* of the Spirit Wilds — the boundary between the wilds and the cultivated domains. The Courts do not hold jurisdiction over the wilds' interiors (this is the Wild Suppression Bloc's contested claim — they argue the border jurisdiction implies interior jurisdiction; the majority has held, for two hundred thousand years, that it does not). This is the most-litigated clause of the Compact; the Courts' case-law on it fills a wing of the Records archive.
150:5. **The Lineages (excluded).** The Compact explicitly excludes the ancestral courts from the Courts' jurisdiction. The ancestral courts hold jurisdiction over their own lineages — the lineage's internal disputes, the lineage's dead (when the lineage's rite is performed), the lineage's cultivation tradition. The Courts cannot overrule an ancestral court within the lineage's scope. This clause is the lineages' price for joining the Compact; without it, the lineages would have refused, and the Courts would have been a much weaker institution.
152:6. **Authored Law (excluded).** The Compact explicitly excludes authored law from the Courts' jurisdiction. A Mahayana cultivator's authored law, within the cultivator's grotto-heaven or domain, is the cultivator's own; the Courts cannot overrule it. The Courts can adjudicate disputes between authored laws (when two cultivators' laws conflict in a contested region), but they cannot unwrite a law. This clause is the Mahayana cultivators' price for joining the Compact; without it, the Mahayana would have refused, and the Courts would have had no enforceable authority over the beings capable of authoring law.
154:The Compact's six jurisdictions are the Courts' charter. Every political dispute in the Courts' 260,000-year history has been a dispute about the boundaries of these six jurisdictions — the Wild Suppression Bloc's expansionism (jurisdiction 4), the lineage bloc's resistance to Courts interference (jurisdiction 5), the Mahayana cultivators' resistance to Courts review of authored law (jurisdiction 6). The Compact is the Courts' founding, and it is also the Courts' ongoing contestation. The protagonist, at Mahayana, will inherit these contests.
158:The Courts' founding did not create law (law was authored by the Primordial Authors, 70,000 years earlier). It did not create the strata (the Stratification Law was Liyuan's, 70,000 years earlier). It did not create rebirth (the Rebirth Law was Cangsheng's, 50,000 years earlier). It did not create tribulation (the Tribulation Law was Tianxu's, 50,000 years earlier). What the founding created was **governance** — the institutional capacity to administer, enforce, and adjudicate the laws the Primordial Authors had authored. The Courts are the cosmos's first and only governance institution. They have been, in their 260,000-year history, wrong, corrupt, and contested (per doc 15 §2.1: "They can be wrong. They can be corrupt. They can be contested. They have been all three, repeatedly, across the millennia"). They are also the only institution the cosmos has. The protagonist, at Mahayana, will face the choice of whether to take office in them, to go rogue against them, or to retreat into a private grotto-heaven and ignore them. The Compact's six jurisdictions bound the choice.
164:### 5.1 When, and who fought
166:The Great War (大劫, the Great Tribulation) was fought from **Year 360,000 to Year 380,000** — a 20,000-year conflict, the longest sustained violence in cosmic history. The war was fought between two coalitions:
168:- **The Coalition of the Compact** (守約之盟) — the Courts' loyalists, the major ancestral courts (including the Wuwang and Tianxu courts), and the first iteration of the Immortal Alliance (an Acquired-Stratum mirror of the Courts' compact, founded at the Courts' request in Year 280,000 to govern Acquired-Stratum cultivators who could not enter the Precelestial). The Coalition's war-leader was **Daojun Hanxu** (寒虛道君, the Lord of the Cold Void), a third-generation Mahayana cultivator who had held the Defense Ministry for 40,000 years and who was, by the war's end, the Courts' most respected military figure.
170:- **The Severance** (斷層之黨, the Faction of the Broken Boundary) — the breakaway faction led by **Daojun Liyuan** (the Primordial Author, §3.4, who had emerged from 100,000 years of deviation-driven retreat with a coherent alternative law). The Severance's thesis: the stratum-boundaries were tyranny; the cosmos should be re-stratified, the Mortal fused with the Acquired, the Acquired with the Precelestial, the whole cosmos made permeable. The Severance's act: a coordinated campaign to dissolve the stratum-boundaries, beginning with the Mortal-Acquired boundary in the eastern continent's central highlands. The Severance's membership: eleven Mahayana cultivators (a significant fraction of the cosmos's total Mahayana population at the time, which the Records Department estimates at forty), approximately two hundred Tribulation Crossing cultivators, and several thousand Spirit Severance cultivators and below.
172:The war's cause was the deep disagreement that the founding compact had papered over: whether the stratum-boundaries should be enforced. Liyuan's faction believed they were tyrannical; the Courts believed they were necessary (without them, the Mortal stratum would be flooded by Acquired qi and mortals would die; the Acquired would be flooded by Precelestial qi and Acquired cultivators would deviate). The disagreement had been theological for 140,000 years; in Year 360,000, it became military.
176:The Great War's destruction was cosmic-scale. The Records Department's war-archive (the **Register of the Great Tribulation**, 大劫錄) lists the following named destructions, which the protagonist can read at Mahayana:
178:- **The Heavenly Lattice Grotto-Heaven** (天羅洞天) — a peak grotto-heaven in the Precelestial's eastern interior, the seat of the Tianxu Ancestral Court. The Severance's first target. The grotto's boundary was breached by Liyuan's personal law-authorship (he authored, in the war's opening, a law that dissolved the grotto's boundary-law; the law-tribulation that fired in response killed two of Liyuan's Tribulation Crossing lieutenants but the law held long enough to collapse the grotto). Daojun Tianxu (§3.5) dispersed in the collapse. The grotto's ruins persist as the **Reach of the Lattice** (羅網法域), one of the war's largest scars, monitored by the Courts' Defense Department (per §3.5 above).
180:- **The White Tide Continent's western shore** (白潮洲西岸) — a region of the Acquired Stratum, approximately 3,000 li across, rendered uninhabitable by the qi-residue of the Severance's boundary-dissolution attempt. The Mortal-Acquired boundary was, for a period of approximately three hundred years, partially dissolved in this region; the Acquired qi flooded the Mortal stratum, and every mortal in the region died (the Register records the death-toll at approximately 12 million). The Coalition re-established the boundary (a law authored by Hanxu, the war-leader, in Year 370,000), but the region's qi-residue persists. The residue is, in the Records Department's classification, a **Law Reach** — the **Western Reach** (西法域), the war's largest Acquired-Stratum scar. The Western Reach is the only Law Reach accessible from the Mortal Stratum (the others are all Precelestial-interior); it is, consequently, the most-studied Reach and the one the protagonist is most likely to encounter (at Mahayana, as a Reach-authorship site; or earlier, as a ruin-exploration site, if the protagonist travels to the eastern continent).
184:- **Three Mahayana cultivators dispersed** in the war's final battle (the **Battle of the Concord Plain**, Year 380,000, fought on the site where the Courts had been founded 140,000 years earlier): Daojun Liyuan (the Severance's leader, dispersed by a coalition law authored jointly by Hanxu and two other Mahayana), Daojun Wuwang (dispersed defending the Wuwang court's surviving members, who had been evacuated to the Concord Plain), and **Daojun Zhuoguang** (灼光道君, the Lord of the Burning Light, a fourth-generation Mahayana and the Coalition's second-in-command, dispersed by Liyuan's final act of law-authorship before his own dispersal). Plus an unknown number of Tribulation Crossing cultivators (the Register lists 47 confirmed dispersals, with a footnote that the actual number is likely 80-120) and Spirit Severance cultivators (the Register lists 312 confirmed dispersals, with no footnote because the actual number is unknowable — the war's lower-rank casualties were not consistently recorded).
188:The Great War ended in Year 380,000 with the **Treaty of the Severed Heavens** (斬天之約), a compact that re-established the stratum-boundaries, expanded the Courts' jurisdiction, and founded the institutions that have governed the cosmos since. The Treaty's principal provisions:
190:1. **The stratum-boundaries are re-affirmed.** Liyuan's Stratification Law (§3.4) is ratified as the cosmos's foundational law; the Severance's attempt to dissolve the boundaries is ratifiable as the cosmos's foundational crime (the only crime the cosmos's law explicitly names as such, distinct from the demonic-cultivator crime of anchor-harvesting, per doc 25 §5.1 — the Severance's crime was boundary-dissolution, a Mahayana-tier offense; the demonic cultivator's crime is anchor-harvesting, an Acquired-Stratum offense). The **Law of Boundary Integrity** (界 integrity 法) is authored by Hanxu in the Treaty's aftermath, making any attempt to dissolve a stratum-boundary a tribulation-triggering act (the tribulation is automatic and overwhelming — no Mahayana has attempted it since).
192:2. **The Courts' jurisdiction is expanded to include the Law Reaches.** Before the war, the Law Reaches did not exist as a distinct zone (the Precelestial's substrate-law was intact everywhere; there were Spirit Wilds but no Reaches). The war created the Reaches — the regions where the substrate-law was deliberately undone by the conflict's law-paradoxes. The Treaty extends the Courts' jurisdiction to include the Reaches (jurisdiction 4.5 of the Compact, informally — the Compact's text was not amended, but the Courts' case-law has treated the Reaches as a fifth jurisdiction since the Treaty). The Courts' Defense Department maintains monitoring stations at the Reaches' edges; the Reaches' interiors are unadministered (the substrate-law is too thin to administer).
194:3. **The Immortal Alliance is re-founded as an Acquired-Stratum institution.** The Alliance had existed as a Precelestial-affiliated body since Year 280,000 (per §5.1 above), but the Treaty re-chartered it as an independent Acquired-Stratum institution — a treaty organization of the great sects and holy lands, sworn to resolve disputes without open war, to suppress demonic cultivation, to enforce the taboo (doc 12 §5.3) between cultivators and mortals, and to allocate contested resources. The Alliance's founding oaths are Dao-oaths (per doc 12 §6.4); breaking them produces deviation. The Alliance is the Acquired-Stratum mirror of the Courts, and the two institutions are formally allied but independently governed.
196:4. **The war's worst weapons and the dispersed anchors' residue are sealed in the Law Reaches.** The Reaches are, in part, the war's grave. The Courts' Defense Department maintains the seals; the sealed objects include the **Boundary-Dissolution Edict** (§7.1 below, the Severance's final weapon), the dispersed anchors' residue (which has, over the 100,000 years since the war, coalesced into a number of substrate-predator entities — the most dangerous of which is the **Sky-Devouring Beast**, §7.2 below, though the Beast's actual breach was a later event), and several lesser law-paradoxes that the Register classifies as **minor sealed evils**.
198:The Treaty of the Severed Heavens is the cosmos's second foundational document (after the Compact of the Six Jurisdictions). It is the document that made the Long Peace possible. The protagonist, at Mahayana, will inherit the Treaty's obligations (if they take office in the Courts) or its constraints (if they go rogue). The Treaty is the reason the cosmos has not had a Great War in 100,000 years. It is also, the Wild Suppression Bloc argues, the reason the cosmos has not addressed the Spirit Wilds' expansion in 100,000 years — the Treaty's strict boundaries (jurisdiction 4.5, the Reaches) have made the Courts cautious about any expansion of jurisdiction, even into the Wilds. This is one of the Courts' ongoing internal disputes. The protagonist, at Mahayana, will inherit it.
204:The Long Peace Epoch (Year 380,000 to Year 479,753) was long enough — 100,000 years — for civilizations to rise and fall and leave ruins that Mahayana cultivators explore. The Records Department's **Register of Fallen Civilizations** (已亡之籍) lists 47 named civilizations that rose and fell in the Long Peace. Three are specified here, chosen for their relevance to the protagonist's possible encounters.
208:**What it was:** a non-human civilization of yao — specifically, wood-phase yao (yao with anchors, per doc 25 §4), descended from the Spirit Wilds' native wood-phase entities and slowly civilized over the Court Founding Epoch's later millennia. The Pillar Dynasty was a sophisticated civilization: cities of fused stone (the yao's wood-phase qi allowed them to grow stone the way a tree grows wood, in forms they designed), a yao-cultivation tradition based on the wood-phase primary with earth-phase secondary, a written script based on bark-strip imprints (the yao's bark was their outer surface; the script was literally inscribed on their own shed bark), a complex legal system administered by yao-Mahayana (the yao equivalent — yao who had crossed the yao-equivalent of tribulation and authored yao-law), and a population that the Register estimates at 8 million yao at the dynasty's height.
210:**When it flourished:** Year 280,000 to Year 330,000 (50,000 years — long even by cultivator standards; the yao's slower metabolisms and longer lifespans, and the dynasty's stable wood-phase qi-economy based on the southern continent's central highlands' spirit vein system, allowed it).
212:**When it fell:** Year 330,000. The dynasty's qi-economy was based on the central highlands' spirit vein system — a network of seven major wood-phase veins and approximately forty minor veins, all tributary to a single deep primary vein (the **Pillar Root Vein**, 柱根脈). The Pillar Root Vein was, like all spirit veins, finite (the cosmos's finitude, felt). In Year 325,000, the vein's output began to decline (the Register records the decline's first measurement); by Year 330,000, the decline had become catastrophic. The dynasty's qi-economy collapsed; the cities could not sustain themselves; the yao population dispersed into the Spirit Wilds within a generation. The dynasty's last yao-Mahayana (the **Pillar Lord of the Last Sap**, 末汁柱主, whose personal name the Register does not record) dispersed himself voluntarily in Year 330,000 — he had concluded that the dynasty could not be saved and that his persistence would only prolong the suffering. His anchor is gone; his dispersal is the Register's last entry for the dynasty.
216:**Ruins / inheritances:** the **Pillar Cities** (柱城群) — stone cities, partially overgrown, partially intact, with bark-strip libraries preserved by the southern continent's central highlands' dry air. The largest surviving city (the **Pillar of the Last Sap**, 末汁柱, named for the dynasty's last Mahayana) is approximately 200 li across and contains, per the Azure Sword Sect's intelligence hall's file (a single document, traded from a rogue cultivator in Year 479,900, fifty years before the protagonist's birth), the dynasty's central bark-strip library — approximately 40,000 bark-strip texts, of which the intelligence hall's file notes "perhaps 2,000 are legible to a Foundation Establishment cultivator with wood-phase perception; the remainder require Core Formation or higher." The Pillar Cities are a known Mahayana exploration destination; the Azure Sword Sect's intelligence hall has a file on the nearest accessible ruin (300 li from the sect's territory, in the western Cangwu range's deep extension — past the Jade Edge Grotto, in contested wilds). The Jade Void Holy Land's library has a Nascent Soul-tier text on the dynasty's history (the **Pillar Dynasty Annals**, 柱朝紀, in 12 volumes, of which the Jade Void holds 7 — the other 5 are lost).
218:**How the protagonist might encounter it:** (a) a Pillar Dynasty bark-strip text in the Azure Sword Sect's Scripture Hall — a single fragment, traded from a rogue cultivator, untranslated, attributed to "the southern mountains," that the protagonist at Foundation Establishment could attempt to translate (the wood-phase perception required is unusual; the protagonist, if wood-phase-rooted, would have an advantage); (b) a Spirit Wild incursion into the Cangwu range that turns out to be a Pillar Dynasty yao's descendant — a wood-phase yao-beast with the Pillar Dynasty's qi-signature, encountered in the deep Cangwu past the Jade Edge Grotto, at Core Formation or higher; (c) the deep Cangwu vein extension's geological connection to the Pillar Root Vein (§9.1 below), which the protagonist at Nascent Soul could investigate; (d) a full expedition to the Pillar Cities, at Spirit Severance or Mahayana, with the Azure Sword Sect's intelligence hall's file as a guide.
222:**What it was:** an alliance of Core Formation+ sects and lineages, predominantly fire-phase, that flourished in the eastern continent's central lowlands. The Crimson Vow Alliance was a civilization in the sense that it was a coherent political entity spanning multiple sects and lineages (14 great sects, 7 holy lands, approximately 120 lesser sects and lineages), with a shared legal system (the **Crimson Vow Code**, 赤誓律), a shared cultivation tradition (fire-phase primary, metal-phase secondary — the alliance's central vein-system was fire-metal), a shared capital (the **Crimson Vow City**, 赤誓城, a major grotto-heaven with a 1:3 time-rate — three days inside for one day outside — and a population of approximately 40,000 cultivators at the alliance's height), and a shared guarantor (the alliance's founding Mahayana cultivator, **Daojun Chiri** (赤日道君, the Lord of the Red Sun), whose personal law — the **Crimson Vow Law**, 赤誓法 — bound the alliance's member-sects' oaths to her own anchor, making the alliance's Dao-oaths uniquely enforceable).
224:**When it flourished:** Year 410,000 to Year 440,000 (30,000 years — shorter than the Pillar Dynasty, because the alliance's fire-phase tradition was faster-burning in every sense; the alliance's cultivators lived shorter lives, advanced faster, and consumed their vein-system more aggressively).
226:**When it fell:** Year 440,000. The alliance's guarantor, Daojun Chiri, attempted a law-amendment in Year 439,950 — she sought to amend the Crimson Vow Law to extend the alliance's founding oaths' binding-force to the next generation (the original oaths were sworn by the member-sects' founders, who were dying of old age; Chiri sought to bind the founders' successors automatically). The amendment was, in itself, reasonable; the law-tribulation that fired was not. The tribulation was overwhelming — Chiri's amendment touched on the lineage-jurisdiction clause of the Compact (jurisdiction 5, §4.3 above), and the cosmos's law resisted the amendment as an overreach. Chiri failed the tribulation; her anchor dispersed; the Crimson Vow Law, which had been bound to her anchor, unraveled. The alliance's founding oaths lost their binding force. The alliance's member-sects, no longer oath-bound, went to war over the Crimson Vow City (the grotto-heaven's 1:3 time-rate made it the most valuable cultivation resource in the eastern continent). The war cracked three spirit veins (the fire-metal vein-system's three primary veins, all of which fed the Crimson Vow City); the grotto-heaven's anchor was destroyed in the war's final battle (the cave on the eastern continent's shore that had been the grotto's entrance collapsed); the grotto-heaven was sealed (its inhabitants — approximately 8,000 cultivators at the time of the sealing — were trapped inside; their fate is unknown, but the Register notes that the grotto-heaven's qi-supply, with the vein-system cracked, would have been exhausted within a thousand years, and the inhabitants are presumed dispersed). The alliance's remnants scattered into the eastern continent's shore and the central lowlands.
228:**Why it fell:** the founding Mahayana's dispersal, caused by the cosmos's law resisting an overreach. This is the tragic engine of the Compact's jurisdictional boundaries — the law protects the lineages' autonomy (jurisdiction 5) even from a well-intentioned Mahayana's attempt to bind them. Chiri's amendment was reasonable; the law did not care.
230:**Ruins / inheritances:** the **Crimson Vow Grotto-Heaven's collapsed anchor** — the cave on the eastern continent's shore that once led to the Crimson Vow City. The cave is now a qi-residue site; the grotto-heaven itself is sealed (the anchor destroyed, per doc 19 §2.4). The Crimson Vow Code survives in fragmentary form (the Jade Void Holy Land's library holds approximately 60% of the Code; the Azure Sword Sect's Scripture Hall has a single chapter — the chapter on fire-phase oath-binding, which the sect's intelligence hall acquired through unknown channels approximately 200 years ago). The Crimson Vow Law is gone (it was bound to Chiri's anchor, and her dispersal unraveled it). The alliance's fire-phase cultivation tradition survives in corrupted form, practiced by a hidden network of demonic cultivators (the **Crimson Vow Remnant**, §9.3 below).
232:**How the protagonist might encounter it:** (a) a Crimson Vow fire-phase manual fragment in Old Chen's collection — Old Chen inherited it from his own teacher, who inherited it from a rogue cultivator who explored the eastern continent's shore; the fragment is incomplete (the first three chapters only) but is a fire-phase primary manual that the protagonist, if they choose a fire-phase path, could complete (the manual is at Foundation Establishment tier; the protagonist could use it to develop a fire-phase secondary to their primary root); (b) the alliance's Dao-oath form, which is the form the protagonist's own lineage will use when they take the oath of lineage-founding (the form is older than the alliance's destruction; it persisted, and the Wang lineage's founder's oath — which the protagonist will swear at Nascent Soul, if they choose the lineage-founding path — will use it); (c) the Crimson Vow Remnant's connection to the Zhou Wugui incursion (§9.3 below), which the protagonist at Core Formation could investigate; (d) a full expedition to the Crimson Vow Grotto-Heaven's collapsed anchor, at Nascent Soul or higher, in search of the grotto's sealed contents (the 8,000 trapped cultivators' inheritances — a high-risk, high-reward expedition that the Azure Sword Sect's intelligence hall has considered and rejected, twice, on cost-benefit grounds).
236:**What it was:** a divination lineage. The Silent Hexagram School was a small lineage — never more than thirty living cultivators at a time — but it was the cosmos's preeminent divination tradition for 25,000 years. The school's founder, **Daoist Silent Hexagram** (默卦真人, Mò Guà Zhēnrén), was a Nascent Soul diviner who developed a divination technique (the **Silent Hexagram Method**, 默卦之法) of unprecedented accuracy — a Core Formation diviner's accuracy (per doc 24 §2.5) at Qi Condensation, and a Nascent Soul diviner's accuracy at Core Formation. The method was based on a hexagram-tradition (the 64 hexagrams, the same tradition Wang Lun inherited, doc 24 §2.5) refined to a precision the genre's mortal tradition has never matched. The school's founder persisted as an ancestor after his bodily death (the school's ancestral court, the **Silent Hexagram Court**, was founded in Year 445,000); the school's living cultivators consulted the ancestor for 25,000 years.
240:**When it fell:** Year 470,000. The school's founder, the Silent Hexagram ancestor, had persisted for 25,000 years — long enough, per doc 15 §3.4, to become something other than what it was. The ancestor corrupted (the corruption is the ancestral courts' central horror, per doc 15 §3.4: a sufficiently old ancestor's accumulated qi-authority and lineage-veneration reshape the ancestor's law-pattern until it is no longer the cultivator who founded the court). The corruption was slow — the Register notes that the school's living cultivators perceived the ancestor's increasing strangeness over the school's last 3,000 years, and debated, across generations, whether the strangeness was the ancestor's deepened wisdom or the ancestor's corruption. The debate ended in Year 470,000, when the school's last Core Formation cultivator (the **Diviner of the Last Hexagram**, 末卦占師, whose personal name the Register records as **Jing Wuyin** (靜無音, Stillness Without Sound), a woman of 480 years at the time) concluded that the ancestor was corrupted and that the school's continued consultation was harmful. Jing Wuyin petitioned the Courts for intervention (the only lawful way to remove a corrupted ancestor, per doc 15 §3.4); the Courts declined (the lineage-jurisdiction clause, jurisdiction 5; the Courts' reluctance to set a precedent for interference in lineage affairs, per doc 15 §3.4). Jing Wuyin, unable to remove the ancestor and unwilling to continue the consultation, disbanded the school. She destroyed the school's grotto-heaven's anchor (the grotto-heaven's entrance, a cave in the central continent's northern hills, was collapsed by Jing Wuyin's own hand, in a seven-day rite that the Register describes as "the most sustained act of grief in the Register's records"), sealing the school's library inside. She then severed the school's lineage rite's connection to the ancestral court — the rite no longer reaches the court, and the court's qi-connection to the Acquired Stratum has been cut. The ancestor is, in effect, sealed within its own court (§7.3 below). Jing Wuyin dispersed herself in Year 470,050 (fifty years after the school's disbanding) — she had concluded that her own anchor, corrupted by the grief of the school's end, was no longer fit for rebirth. Her dispersal is the Register's last entry for the school.
242:**Why it fell:** the ancestral courts' central horror — the corruption of a sufficiently old ancestor. This is the tragic engine of the lineage system (doc 15 §3.4): the institution that gives every lineage a stake in the upper stratum is also the institution that, given enough time, produces the cosmos's most intractable corrupted beings.
244:**Ruins / inheritances:** the **Silent Hexagram Grotto-Heaven** — sealed, its anchor destroyed, somewhere in the central continent's northern hills. The grotto's library (the **Silent Hexagram Library**, 默卦藏經) contains the school's complete divination tradition — the Silent Hexagram Method, the school's 25,000 years of hexagram-records, the school's commentaries on the 64 hexagrams, and (per the Register's account) Jing Wuyin's own journal, in which she recorded her conclusion that the ancestor was corrupted and her decision to disband the school. The library is sealed; the grotto's entrance has been collapsed for 10,000 years; the library's contents are presumed intact (the grotto-heaven's law persists, even with the anchor destroyed, for as long as the grotto's interior qi-supply lasts — and the grotto's vein, per the Register, was a minor water-phase vein with an estimated 50,000-year supply at the time of the sealing, of which 10,000 have elapsed). The school's divination tradition survives in fragmentary form — the fragment that reached the Cangli Riverlands through unknown channels and became Wang Lun's hexagram tradition (per §9.2 below and doc 24 §2.5).
246:**How the protagonist might encounter it:** (a) Wang Lun's hexagram tradition (§9.2 below) — the protagonist, if they pursue the divination path, will at Core Formation realize that Wang Lun's tradition is a fragment of something larger; at Nascent Soul, they could identify the fragment's origin as the Silent Hexagram School; (b) the protagonist's own divination development, if they pursue the divination path to Nascent Soul, will at some point draw the corrupted ancestor's attention (§7.3 below); (c) a full expedition to the Silent Hexagram Grotto-Heaven, at Spirit Severance or Mahayana, in search of the library — the expedition would require finding the grotto's collapsed entrance (the central continent's northern hills are large; the Register's location data is approximate), re-opening the entrance (a non-trivial act of law-authorship, since the entrance was collapsed by Jing Wuyin's own law), and navigating the grotto's interior (which has been isolated for 10,000 years and may contain evolved ecology, per doc 19 §3).
252:The Records Department's **Register of Sealed Evils** (封印之籍) lists 23 named sealed beings or phenomena, of which 3 are specified here. Each is Mahayana-tier — the kind of threat that only a Mahayana cultivator, or a coalition of Tribulation Crossing cultivators, can confront. The protagonist, at Mahayana, may encounter any of the three; the protagonist, below Mahayana, may encounter their edges.
256:**What it is:** a law-paradox, not a being. The Boundary-Dissolution Edict is the Severance's final weapon — a law authored by Daojun Liyuan in the Great War's last days, as his own law-tribulation fired and he perceived his imminent dispersal. The Edict is, in form, a law: "the Mortal-Acquired boundary in the eastern continent's central region is dissolved." The law was authored (the act was complete); the law was never *propagated* (the coalition's surviving Mahayana sealed it before it could take effect). The Edict is, in ontological category, a **dormant law** — a law that has been authored but not activated, held in suspension by the seal. If the seal breaks, the law propagates.
258:**When it was sealed:** Year 380,000, in the Treaty of the Severed Heavens' immediate aftermath (§5.3 above). The sealing was performed by a coalition of the four surviving Primordial Authors (Taiyi, Cangsheng, and the two contested ones — Wuwang and Tianxu had already dispersed) and Hanxu, the Coalition's war-leader. The sealing cost one of the four Primordial Authors his anchor (the Register does not specify which; the Substrate-Depths faction holds it was the sixth Primordial Author whose existence the Traditionalists dispute, §3.6 above).
262:**Where it is sealed:** the **Reach of the Severed Boundary** (斷界法域), a small Law Reach in the Precelestial's eastern interior, approximately 200 li across. The Reach was created by the Edict's partial propagation (the Edict propagated far enough to dissolve the substrate-law in a small region before the seal took effect; the dissolved region is the Reach). The Edict is contained within the Reach's center, in a sealed chamber (the **Severed Boundary Chamber**, 斷界室) maintained by the Defense Department.
264:**What happens if the seal breaks:** the Edict propagates. The Mortal-Acquired boundary in the eastern continent's central region (a region approximately 3,000 li across, corresponding to the White Tide Continent's western shore — the same region rendered uninhabitable by the war's boundary-dissolution attempt, §5.2 above) dissolves. Every mortal in the region dies (the Acquired qi floods their unprepared bodies; the death-toll, by the Defense Department's current estimate, would be approximately 4 million — the region's population is lower now than at the war's time, due to the Western Reach's persistence as an uninhabitable zone). Every Qi Condensation and Foundation Establishment cultivator in the region deviates (the qi-density shift is too rapid for their meridians to adapt). Every Core Formation cultivator in the region faces an immediate and overwhelming tribulation (the boundary-dissolution is a lawful event; the tribulation system responds). The death-toll, all-ranks combined, would be approximately 4.5 million. The Courts' Defense Department's standing estimate is that the seal has approximately 50,000 years of integrity remaining, at the current decline-rate of the seal's substrate-law. This is not an immediate crisis. It is, however, a long-term one.
266:**How the protagonist might encounter it:** the Courts' Defense Department's standing commission (a content-design option at Mahayana) — the protagonist, at Mahayana, could be invited to participate in a seal-maintenance expedition, in which the Edict's seal is renewed (the renewal is performed approximately once per 5,000 years, by a coalition of Mahayana cultivators; the next renewal is due in approximately 1,200 years). The expedition is a content-design option, not a certainty; the protagonist's participation depends on their relationship with the Courts (if they have taken office, they may be assigned; if they have gone rogue, they may be invited as a freelance expert; if they have retreated to a private grotto-heaven, they will not be invited).
270:**What it is:** a being from the Spirit Wilds — a **substrate-predator**, in the Records Department's classification. The Sky-Devouring Beast is not a yao (it has no anchor in the yao sense, per doc 25 §4); it is a Spirit Wild native entity that evolved (or was generated — the distinction is unclear, and the Register notes the question is ontologically unresolved) within the Wilds' law-indifference. The Beast's nature is to consume law-inscriptions the way a beast consumes flesh — it eats the law that holds a grotto-heaven together, and the grotto collapses. The Beast is, in ontological category, a **wild substrate-entity** — a being whose existence is lawful (the Spirit Wilds generate such entities) but whose nature is inimical to cultivated law.
272:**When it was sealed:** Year 420,000 (40,000 years ago, in the Long Peace Epoch's middle period). The Beast breached the Acquired Stratum through a Law Reach's edge (the **Reach of the Caged Sky's** predecessor — the Reach did not exist before the Beast's sealing; the Beast's sealing created it). The Beast consumed three grotto-heavens (the grotto-heavens' laws were unwritten by its passage — the grotto-heavens collapsed, the inhabitants dispersed) before it was cornered and sealed by a coalition of three Mahayana cultivators: **Daojun Kongxiang** (空相道君, the Lord of the Empty Form), **Daojun Yuanzhao** (圓照道君, the Lord of the Round Reflection), and **Daojing Shenhui** (神暉道靜, the Stillness of the Divine Radiance — a Daoist nun whose courtesy name the Register uses in place of her personal name). Kongxiang dispersed in the sealing (the Beast consumed his law-authorship as he sealed it; he dispersed in the act). Yuanzhao and Shenhui survived; their lineages have maintained the seal in rotation since.
274:**Who sealed it:** the coalition named above. The seal's maintenance has been the Kongxiang, Yuanzhao, and Shenhui lineages' joint responsibility since Year 420,000.
276:**Where it is sealed:** the **Reach of the Caged Sky** (籠天法域), in the Precelestial's southern interior, approximately 500 li across. 278:**What happens if the seal breaks:** the Beast resumes its consumption. The grotto-heavens at risk are the six major grotto-heavens in the Precelestial's southern interior (the region's grotto-heavens are mostly minor, but six are major; the six include the **Jade Void Holy Land's** subsidiary grotto-heaven in the southern interior, which houses approximately 200 Nascent Soul cultivators on rotation). The death-toll, all-ranks combined, would be approximately 50,000 cultivators. The seal's integrity, by the Defense Department's current estimate, has approximately 8,000 years remaining — but the three lineages that maintain it are depleted (the Kongxiang lineage has no living Mahayana; the Yuanzhao lineage has one, who is 90,000 years old and slowing; the Shenhui lineage has one, who is 60,000 years old and has petitioned the Courts for Defense assistance, which the Courts have declined on jurisdictional grounds — the Caged Sky is a lineage-seal, not a Courts-seal, and the Courts' Defense Department's jurisdiction does not extend to lineage-seals).
