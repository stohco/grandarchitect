Every qi flow is a current with sources, sinks, and seasonal modulation — not a static field. 20-### 1.1 What they are
21-
22:A spirit vein is a subterranean river of dense, differentiated qi — a flowing current of Acquired-stratum qi that has intruded into the Mortal stratum along geological fissures. It is not a deposit; it is a *current*. It moves, slowly, at the pace of magma or groundwater. Its phase-affinity is set by the rock it flows through: a vein running through granite acquires metal-phase signatures; through limestone, earth-phase; through coal measures, fire-phase; through old river gravel, water-phase; through living root-bound soil, wood-phase. Yin-yang polarity is set by depth: deep veins are yin-dominant (cold, still, condensing); shallow veins are yang-dominant (warm, moving, dispersing). Most veins carry a mixed phase signature with one dominant phase and one or two secondary phases, the way a real river carries a dominant current with eddies of crossflow.

24-### 1.2 How they form
--
36-Detection requires **Foundation Establishment (筑基)** at minimum. A Foundation Establishment cultivator, sensing qi in the deep mode their integrated body permits, can perceive the vertical plume of phase-aligned qi rising through the ground. The plume is faint at the surface, denser below; following the density gradient downward through the rock requires sustained perception and is exhausting. Core Formation (金丹) cultivators can map a vein's full course in days; Nascent Soul (元嬰) cultivators in hours.
37-
38:There are also **indicator ecologies** that any cultivator can read. A spirit vein's surface trace produces characteristic vegetation: spirit herbs of the vein's dominant phase grow in dense stands above it; mortal plants grow larger and greener; certain animals aggregate. A wood-phase vein under a forest produces a grove where the canopy is denser, the trees are older, and the undergrowth hums with herbivorous qi-beasts. A water-phase vein under a valley produces a spring that never fails and never freezes. A metal-phase vein under a hill produces a particular reddish grass that mortals call "blood-grass" and that herbalists know as a lead-indicator for metal-affinity ore. A fire-phase vein produces warm ground, snow-free in winter, and a particular moss that smokes faintly in the rain. An earth-phase vein produces a heavy clay and a particular worm that glows faintly in the dark.

40-### 1.4 How they are tapped
--
50-A vein's death is not quiet. When a vein fails — through over-tapping, through enemy action, through geological accident — the surface ecology that depended on it collapses in a predictable sequence:
51-
52:1. **The indicator herbs wither first**, within a season. The dense stands of phase-aligned spirit herbs thin and die; their roots can no longer find the qi they fed on.
53-2. **The herbivorous qi-beasts migrate or starve**, within a year. Beasts that fed on the herbs must move or die. Their migration brings them into neighboring territories, where they displace or are killed by local fauna.
54-3. **The predatory qi-beasts follow**, within two to three years. Predators that fed on the herbivores find their prey gone; they too migrate or starve, often arriving in mortal-settled areas where they become a sudden, dangerous problem.
55:4. **Mortal crops fail**, within three to five years. The mortal agriculture that benefited from the vein's diffused qi — without ever knowing it benefited — loses the subsidy. Yields drop. A village that fed itself for nine generations cannot feed itself. Children stunt. The lineage blames the earth god, the weather, the ancestors. They are wrong.
56-5. **Sect war**, within ten years if there is a neighboring sect with a viable vein. The dying sect, knowing what is happening, will attack the neighbor. The neighbor, knowing what the attack means, will fight to the death. Vein wars are the most total wars in the cultivation world; there is no negotiated peace, because the loser dies.

73:## 2. Spirit beasts (妖獸 / 靈獸)
74-
75-### 2.1 What they are, and what they are not
76-
77:A spirit beast is an animal that has absorbed ambient qi over its lifetime and developed a crude internal circulation of its own. It is not a monster. It is not a demon. It is not a spawn. It is an animal — born an animal, living an animal's life, eating what its species eats, mating as its species mates, dying as its species dies — that has, through long exposure to dense ambient qi (typically by living over a spirit vein, or in a sacred place, or in a long-lived forest), begun to cultivate without intending to.
78-
79:The distinction between 妖獸 (the "wayward beast" — the term used when the beast is wild, dangerous, or unaffiliated) and 靈獸 (the "spirit beast" — the term used when the beast is tamed, allied, or affiliated with a sect) is a *human* distinction about the same creature. A tiger that has reached Foundation Establishment-equivalent cultivation in the wild is a 妖獸 to the villagers it menaces; the same tiger, oath-bound to a sect, is a 靈獸. The beast does not change; the human relationship to it does.
80-
81-### 2.2 The four tiers
--
83-Spirit beasts parallel the human cultivation ladder at four correspondences, with the caveat that the correspondence is *structural*, not identical — a beast's cultivation is slower, less flexible, and more strongly phase-locked than a human's.
84-
85:**Low-tier (Qi Induction equivalent).** The beast has begun to absorb ambient qi. Its senses sharpen; its body grows stronger than its species' norm; its lifespan extends modestly (a low-tier rat might live five years instead of two; a low-tier deer might live twenty instead of ten). It cannot use qi techniques. It cannot perceive qi as a separate modality; it merely benefits from it. A low-tier beast is, to a mortal, simply an unusually large, unusually healthy, unusually wary specimen of its species. Most spirit beasts in the world are low-tier; most animals living over a spirit vein eventually become low-tier if they live long enough. **Mortals hunt them as they hunt any animal**; the hunter simply notes that this particular boar was harder to kill.
86-
87-**Mid-tier (Qi Condensation equivalent).** The beast has begun to circulate qi internally. It can use simple qi techniques: a mid-tier tiger can harden its pelt against arrows; a mid-tier hawk can route qi to its eyes for far-sight; a mid-tier snake can vent a breath of phase-aligned qi (fire-breath for a fire-phase snake, poison-chill for a water-phase snake). Its lifespan extends further (a mid-tier beast lives two to four times its species' norm). It is dangerous to mortals — a mid-tier boar will kill a hunting party of villagers; a mid-tier wolf will prey on livestock and occasionally on children. **A Qi Condensation cultivator is a mid-tier beast's peer**, competitor, and occasional predator.
88-
89:**High-tier (Foundation Establishment equivalent).** The beast's body, qi, and spirit integrate. It can speak — haltingly at first, in the human tongue of the nearest settled region, learned by listening over decades. It can form simple oaths and keep them. It can use complex qi techniques and often develops a signature technique unique to itself. Its lifespan extends to ~200 years. It is a presence in its territory — not merely an animal but a power. A high-tier beast typically claims a territory and enforces it against other beasts and against humans. **A Foundation Establishment cultivator treats a high-tier beast as a peer**, often negotiating territory boundaries rather than fighting; sect cultivators sometimes befriend, oath-bind, or recruit high-tier beasts as sect guardians. The beast that eats a high-tier beast's chosen prey has signed its death.
90-
91-**Peak-tier (Core Formation equivalent).** The beast forms an internal core (內丹, "inner elixir") analogous to a human's golden core. It can shapeshift — taking human form, imperfectly at first (a tail that won't vanish, eyes that stay beast-shaped, a scent that betrays it), more fluently with practice. It can found a lineage — its descendants inherit a stronger starting qi-state, and over generations a peak-tier beast can produce a true breeding population of mid-tier or higher beasts. It can live ~500 years. A peak-tier beast is a regional power. It negotiates with sects as a sovereign, not as a beast. **A Core Formation cultivator treats a peak-tier beast as a sovereign peer**; a Nascent Soul cultivator may treat one as a rival or an ally.
92-
93:There is no Nascent Soul-equivalent beast tier in the Mortal stratum. Beasts that would advance past peak-tier must cross into the Acquired stratum, where they become something else entirely — a spirit (妖仙, "wayward immortal"), no longer an animal, no longer fully of this world. Such beings are rare, are mostly legend, and are out of scope for the Mortal stratum ecology.
94-
95-### 2.3 Their ecology
96-
97:Spirit beasts eat what their base species eats. A spirit tiger is a carnivore that eats deer (mortal and spirit), wild pigs (mortal and spirit), and the occasional human. A spirit deer is a herbivore that eats grass, leaves, and (if it can find them) spirit herbs. A spirit fish eats smaller fish and aquatic plants. The qi absorption does not replace food; it supplements it. A spirit beast that cannot eat starves, the same as any animal; it merely starves more slowly and can survive on less.
98-
99:Where they live is set by their base species' habitat and by qi density. A mid-tier beast requires denser ambient qi than a low-tier beast, both to sustain its cultivation and to advance. Beasts therefore aggregate where qi is densest — over spirit veins, in sacred places, in old-growth forests where the qi has accumulated for centuries. A mid-tier beast driven out of dense-qi territory into thin-qi territory will, over months or years, lose its mid-tier cultivation and revert toward low-tier — a slow, painful decline that the beast will avoid at all costs. This is why mid-tier beasts are territorial: their territory *is* their cultivation.
100-
101:How they reproduce is the base species' biology, modified by qi. Spirit beasts breed with mortal specimens of their species; the offspring are mortal with a slight qi affinity, and may develop low-tier cultivation if they live in dense qi. Two spirit beasts breeding produce offspring with stronger starting qi-state, sometimes skipping the mortal phase entirely. A peak-tier beast's lineage, over generations, can produce a breeding population of mid-tier beasts — this is how a true spirit-beast territory is established. The **lineage inheritance** is the spirit-beast analogue of a sect's founding: a peak-tier beast that has held a territory for two hundred years has produced children, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren who fill the territory's mid-tier niches.

103-### 2.4 How they interact with humans
--
105-The human-beast relationship is set by relative tier. A mortal is prey to a mid-tier carnivore and a competitor to a mid-tier herbivore (both want the deer, both want the herbs). A Qi Condensation cultivator is a mid-tier beast's peer — sometimes ally, sometimes rival, sometimes predator, sometimes prey. A Foundation Establishment cultivator is a high-tier beast's negotiating partner or its enemy; the calculus depends on territory and on whether their phase-affinities are compatible. A Core Formation cultivator is a peak-tier beast's sovereign counterpart; they may treaty, trade, or war.
106-
107:Beast parts are economically important. A mid-tier beast's pelt, bones, and core-region organs are alchemical materials; a high-tier beast's parts are precious; a peak-tier beast's inner core (內丹) is a treasure that sects will kill for. This economic pressure means that humans hunt spirit beasts at every tier, and that spirit beasts, in turn, kill humans who enter their territories. The hunting is not random; it is a managed predation, with rules. A sect that over-hunts a region's mid-tier beasts will, within years, find its high-tier beasts starving, its peak-tier beast preparing for war, and its own Foundation Establishment cultivators sent to die in the resulting conflict.

117-1. **Spirit veins** are the source. Dense, phase-aligned qi flows underground through the vein network. The vein's qi is the densest qi in the Mortal stratum; it is the reason the Mortal stratum can support a spirit-beast ecology at all.
118-2. **Ambient qi** is the diffusion. Vein qi diffuses upward through rock and soil, mixing with the qi descending from sun, moon, and weather, and the qi radiating from living things. This mixed, diluted qi is what most beings perceive as "the qi of a place." Its density is set by the nearest vein's strength and depth.
119:3. **Spirit herbs** are the primary producers. A spirit herb's roots draw phase-aligned qi from the diffused vein-plume; its leaves draw phase-aligned qi from sun and air. The herb metabolizes the qi into medicinal or alchemical compounds — the same way a green plant metabolizes sunlight into sugar. The herb *is* a qi-organism; its medicinal properties are the qi it has accumulated and transformed.
120:4. **Herbivorous spirit beasts** are the primary consumers. A low-tier spirit deer that grazes on spirit herbs accumulates their qi; over years, the deer becomes itself a low-tier spirit beast. A mid-tier herbivore may seek out specific herbs for specific qi effects — a mid-tier goat will travel miles to graze a particular wood-phase herb during a particular solar term, because its body craves that qi at that season.
121:5. **Predatory spirit beasts** are the secondary consumers. A mid-tier tiger that eats a low-tier spirit deer absorbs the deer's accumulated qi, much of it transformed and concentrated. Predators advance faster than herbivores at the same ambient qi density, because their food is qi-richer.
122-6. **Peak-tier beasts** are the apex. A peak-tier beast may prey on mid-tier predators, on high-tier herbivores, or on the rare human cultivator it can overpower. Its inner core is the densest, most-transformed qi-package in the local food web — which is why humans hunt it.

126-Humans tap the food web at every level:
127-
128:- **Gathering herbs.** A mortal herbalist, a Qi Induction cultivator, or a sect's outer disciples harvest spirit herbs for medicine, alchemy, or sale. Each harvest removes a node from the producer layer.
129:- **Hunting beasts.** A mortal hunting party, a Qi Condensation cultivator, or a sect's hunting team kills spirit beasts for meat, parts, or core-region organs. Each kill removes a node from the consumer layer.
130-- **Mining veins.** A sect with Foundation Establishment+ cultivators taps a vein directly, drawing qi that would otherwise have fed the surface ecology. This is the largest human draw on the system.
131-- **Settling dense-qi land.** A sect that builds its compound on a dense-qi site displaces the beasts and herbs that lived there, redirecting the qi-flow into its cultivators' practice.

135-Every tap has cascading effects, because the food web is a web, not a pipeline. Three illustrative chains:
136-
137:**The herb cascade.** A sect's alchemists over-harvest a 100-year wood-phase herb (靈草, see §4) from a particular grove. Within a year, the herbivorous spirit beasts that grazed that herb lose their preferred food source. The mid-tier goats migrate to a neighboring grove; the mid-tier deer switch to a less-nutritious herb and begin to decline in condition. Within three years, the predatory beasts that fed on those herbivores find their prey scattered or weakened; a mid-tier tiger begins ranging into mortal-settled land in search of livestock. Within five years, a villager is dead, the yamen is investigating, a Daoist has been summoned to perform an exorcism (the village misreads the tiger as a demon), and the sect's alchemists — who caused the cascade — are nowhere in the village's account of the catastrophe.
138-
139:**The beast cascade.** A sect's hunting teams over-hunt a region's mid-tier spirit wolves, for their pelts and their phase-aligned bones. Within two years, the wolf's prey — a particular low-tier spirit deer — has overpopulated. The deer overgraze the spirit herbs of their groves. The groves' herb-diversity collapses; the rarest herbs, grazed before they can seed, vanish within five years. The sect's alchemists, who depended on those herbs, are now impoverished. The sect elders, investigating, find that their own hunting teams caused the loss. This is the most common form of self-inflicted ecological wound in the cultivation world, and the lesson is learned only at cost.
140-
141:**The vein cascade.** A sect increases its draw on its primary vein to support a push for a Core Formation breakthrough. Within a decade, the vein's pressure drops; the surface ecology thins; the indicator herbs wither; the spirit beasts begin to migrate. Within two decades, the sect's outer disciples cannot find herbs within a hundred li of the sect; the sect's hunting teams must travel further for less; the sect's income drops. Within three decades, the sect is at war with its neighbor over the neighbor's vein. The Core Formation elder whose breakthrough caused the draw increase is, by then, either a Core Formation patriarch (in which case he defends his decision) or dead of deviation (in which case the sect blames him posthumously).

151-### 4.1 What they are
152-
153:A spirit herb is a plant that has absorbed ambient qi over its lifetime and developed medicinal or alchemical properties beyond its species' mortal norm. The transformation is the same as a spirit beast's: long exposure to ambient qi, gradual integration, expression in the plant's chemistry. A mortal ginseng is a root that mildly tonifies. A hundred-year spirit ginseng that grew over a wood-phase vein is a medicine that can save a Qi Condensation cultivator from deviation. The difference is the qi the root has accumulated.
154-
155-### 4.2 Classification by phase-affinity
--
157-Spirit herbs classify primarily by the phase of the qi they metabolize, which is set by the environment they grew in:
158-
159:- **Wood-phase herbs (木性靈草)** grow in forests, in old-growth groves, in the canopy layer of dense woodland. They are the most common spirit herbs, because wood-phase qi is the most broadly distributed (forests are large; wood-phase veins are common). Wood-phase herbs affect growth, healing, and the liver-gallbladder axis in the body's qi-correspondence.
160-- **Fire-phase herbs (火性靈草)** grow near volcanoes, hot springs, charcoal-burns, and over fire-phase veins. They are rare in the Mortal stratum; most fire-phase herbs grow in the Acquired stratum or in grotto-heavens with volcanic activity. Fire-phase herbs affect heat, agitation, the heart-small intestine axis, and the firing processes of internal alchemy.
161-- **Earth-phase herbs (土性靈草)** grow in heavy clay soils, in deep loess, on stable grasslands, and over earth-phase veins. They are slow-growing, dense-rooted, and long-lived. Earth-phase herbs affect stability, integration, the spleen-stomach axis, and the body's ability to absorb other qi.
--
167-### 4.3 Growth cycles
168-
169:Spirit herbs grow slowly because qi-metabolism is slow. A mortal ginseng reaches harvestable size in three to five years. A spirit ginseng takes thirty years to reach a potency a mortal herbalist would notice, a hundred years to reach a potency an alchemist would value, and three hundred years to reach a potency a Foundation Establishment cultivator would seek. The growth cycle is set by the herb's species, the ambient qi density, the dominant phase, and the herb's age.
170-
171:Some herbs are **single-harvest** — the entire plant, root and all, is taken at maturity, and the plant dies. Most medicinally valuable spirit herbs are single-harvest; their potency is in the root, and the root cannot be taken without killing the plant. Other herbs are **repeat-harvest** — leaves, bark, fruit, or flowers are taken, and the plant survives to regrow them. Repeat-harvest herbs are less potent per harvest but sustainable; single-harvest herbs are potent but finite.
172-
173-Some herbs have **seasonal windows**. A particular wood-phase herb flowers only at Clear and Bright (清明); the flowers must be gathered within three days or they lose their qi. A particular water-phase herb seeds only at White Dew (白露); the seeds are the medicinal part. A particular fire-phase herb must be harvested at noon on the summer solstice, when yang-qi peaks, or its potency collapses. These windows are the herb-gatherer's calendar, and missing a window means waiting a year.
--
175-### 4.4 Harvesting consequences
176-
177:Harvesting a single-harvest spirit herb kills the plant. This is not a small thing. A hundred-year spirit herb has, over its century, established a root system that draws qi from a wide column of soil, and the qi-diffusion pattern around the plant has stabilized around that root system. When the root is taken, the diffusion pattern collapses. The qi that was being drawn into the plant now pools, disperses, or seeks another sink. The neighboring plants — mortal and spirit — find their own qi-patterns disturbed. The herbivorous beasts that grazed the herb's leaves or depended on its presence lose a food source. The microbial community in the soil, which had adapted to the root's qi-exudates, shifts.
178-
179:Over a single harvest, this is a small disturbance. Over many harvests, in a grove rich with hundred-year herbs, it is the herb cascade of §3.3: the grove's qi-economy reorganizes around the absence of the harvested plants; the grove's diversity drops; the rarer herbs, deprived of the community that sustained them, fail to seed and vanish. A grove that has been strip-harvested by a careless sect is, within a generation, no longer a grove of spirit herbs. It is a forest of mortal trees with a faint memory of qi.
180-
181-The sustainable practice — known to competent herbalists and sect alchemists, ignored by desperate ones — is **selective harvest with replanting and fallow cycles.** A herb is taken; a seed from the same plant is replanted in the same soil; the surrounding community is left intact; the grove is not harvested again for a full growth cycle (which may be decades). This is the practice the protagonist, if they study herbalism, will learn. It is also the practice that distinguishes a sect with a future from a sect without one.

197-Ambient qi is not uniform. It varies by location, by time of day, by season, by weather, and by event. This variation is the qi climate — the generator's primary input for the qi topology.
198-
199:**By location.** Ambient qi is densest over spirit veins, dense in sacred places and old-growth forests, moderate in settled mortal lands with a vein subsidy, thin in barren lands (deserts, high mountains, deep oceans, and any land whose vein system has died). A cultivator traveling from the Cangli Riverlands to a barren northern steppe would perceive the qi thinning over days of travel, like the air thinning at altitude. The phase-affinity of ambient qi varies with the local ecology and geology: a forest's ambient qi is wood-dominant; a desert's is fire-and-earth mixed; a river valley's is water-dominant; a mountain's is metal-and-earth.
200-
201-**By time of day.** Ambient qi is thicker at night (yin-phase qi rises with the moon and the cooling earth) and thinner at noon (yang-phase qi peaks but disperses with the sun's heat). A cultivator practicing a yin-phase technique does so at midnight; a cultivator practicing a yang-phase technique does so at dawn. The hour matters. A herb harvested at noon has a different qi-signature than the same herb harvested at midnight, and an alchemist who knows this can choose the harvest hour for the desired effect.

231-**Disease.** A disease is, in the qi model, a parasitic qi-organism — a coherent pattern of corrupted qi that reproduces by infecting a host and using the host's qi to propagate. Schistosomiasis (血吸蟲病), endemic in the Cangli lowlands, is a water-phase parasitic qi that colonizes the host's liver-gallbladder axis, draining the host's wood-phase qi and producing the chronic fatigue, abdominal swelling, and stunted growth the village observes as "water disease." Malaria ("the shakes") is a fire-water mixed parasitic qi that cycles with the host's yin-yang rhythm, producing the alternating fever-and-chill. The snails in the paddies are the *reservoir host* — they carry the parasitic qi in a dormant form that activates when a human wades in the water. The disease is not just a biological infection; it is a *qi-pattern* that the snail, the water, and the human all participate in. This is why a Qi Condensation cultivator can treat it (by disrupting the parasitic qi-pattern in the host's body) but a mortal herbalist, working only with mortal chemistry, can only palliate.
232-
233:**Cultivation gone wrong.** A deviation (走火入魔, per document 00 §8) leaves residue. A cultivator who routes qi incorrectly, who pushes a practice past the breaking point, or who breaks a heart-mind (心魔) and does not recover — that cultivator's body and practice-site accumulate the residue of the deviation. A false-circuit (假周天) leaves a metal-phase corruption in the meridian where the loop ran. A cross-current (逆流) leaves a chaotic mixed-phase residue. A heart-mind break leaves a yin-shadow that can persist for years. A deviation site — a cave where a Qi Condensation cultivator died of deviation, a meditation platform where a Foundation Establishment cultivator went mad — accumulates the residue, and the next cultivator to practice there inherits a predisposition toward the same deviation. This is why sects mark deviation sites as forbidden and why a wise cultivator, choosing a practice-site, asks first what happened there.

235-### 6.3 The contaminated ecology
--
237-Turbid qi does not stay in the cultivator's body or the murder-site's soil. It diffuses into the ambient qi, and from there into the food web.
238-
239:- **Contaminated herbs.** A spirit herb growing in turbid qi absorbs the corruption. Its medicinal properties are distorted — a healing herb may become a poison, a tonifying herb may become a disturbing herb, a clarifying herb may become a confusing herb. The herb's outward appearance is usually normal; only a Qi Induction cultivator perceiving its qi, or an alchemist testing its tincture, can detect the contamination. A mortal herbalist who gathers and prescribes a contaminated herb poisons their patient unknowingly.
240:- **Mutated beasts.** A spirit beast that absorbs turbid qi over years develops mutations — extra limbs, distorted coloration, behavioral abnormalities, unstable qi-signatures. These beasts are the source of most "monster" tales in the mortal world. A mid-tier beast that absorbs murder-residue may become aggressive beyond its species' norm; a mid-tier beast that absorbs deviation-residue may develop a 扭曲 (twisted) qi-field that drives it mad. The mutated beast is not a separate species; it is a corrupted specimen of its species, and the corruption is treatable if the source turbid qi is cleansed.
241:- **Sick humans.** A mortal who lives in turbid qi — over a contaminated vein, near an execution ground, in a house where a deviation occurred — develops chronic illness that no mortal medicine can treat. The illness manifests as a persistent low-grade malaise, disturbed sleep, frequent nightmares, and a slow decline in vitality. Mortal doctors call it "bad placement" (the village's term for ill-omened siting) or "ghost-sickness"; a cultivator recognizes it as qi-contamination and can treat it by cleansing the location or by removing the sufferer from the source.
242-
243-### 6.4 Cleansing
--
245-Cleansing turbid qi is a real practice with specific techniques, environments, and limits. It is not a "purify" button.
246-
247:- **By ritual.** The lineage hall's grief-rites, the earth-god shrine's bi-monthly incense, the Clear and Bright (清明) tomb-sweeping — these are *controlled* contamination-cleansing rituals. They process the residue of human grief and death in a lawful way, converting raw grief-residue into the dense, old, "human" qi the lineage hall is known for. A Daoist priest's exorcism (驅邪) is the same practice applied to a contaminated house or site — a structured rite that processes the residue and returns the location to coherence. These rituals work for low-grade, accumulated contamination. They do not work for severe contamination (a massacre site, a deep deviation site).
248-- **By specific cultivation.** A Qi Condensation cultivator with a water-phase dominant root can practice a cleansing circulation (a specific routing that draws ambient turbid qi into the reservoir, transforms it through the water-phase, and vents it as clarified qi). This is slow, exhausting, and limited in throughput; it cannot cleanse a region, only a small site over many sessions. A Foundation Establishment cultivator with metal-phase root can do the same more efficiently, cutting the corruption from the qi-field rather than transforming it. Cleansing-cultivation is a recognized practice-track and a sect service; many sects have disciples whose primary duty is cleansing the sect's practice-sites and the surrounding mortal lands.
249-- **By specific environment.** Some environments cleanse qi by their nature. A fast-moving river carries turbid qi away (water-phase dispersion). A mountain peak exposed to strong wind disperses it (metal-phase cutting). A sacred place (see §7) is by definition pure qi, and a contaminated object or being brought into a sacred place is gradually cleansed by the ambient pure qi. Cleansing by environment is slow but reliable, and is the preferred method for severe contamination: a deviation-corrupted artifact is left in a sacred place for a generation; a haunted house is abandoned for a century and the wind does the work.

289-### 8.2 Per-tier
290-
291:**At Mortal scale.** A mortal is prey. A mortal hunting party can kill a low-tier beast; a mortal village can drive off a low-tier predator. But a mid-tier beast — a mid-tier tiger, a mid-tier wolf, a mid-tier boar — is beyond a mortal village's capacity. A mid-tier beast that ranges into mortal-settled land kills livestock, kills children, and is killed only by a Qi Condensation cultivator's intervention or by a lucky massed-village effort that costs lives. The mortal world's fear of "demons" and "monsters" is, in part, the mortal world's accurate assessment of its own ecological vulnerability.

293-**At Qi Condensation.** A Qi Condensation cultivator is a mid-tier beast's peer. They can hunt mid-tier beasts; mid-tier beasts can hunt them. They are competitors for the same dense-qi territories and the same spirit-herb resources. A Qi Condensation cultivator who enters a mid-tier beast's territory uninvited is, from the beast's perspective, an invasive competitor; the beast will challenge, drive off, or kill them. This is why Qi Condensation cultivators travel in groups and why a lone Qi Condensation cultivator in the wild is a cautious creature.

295-**At Foundation Establishment.** A Foundation Establishment cultivator is a predator of mid-tier beasts and a negotiating peer of high-tier beasts. They can hunt mid-tier beasts reliably; they can defend a mortal village from a mid-tier incursion; they can claim a territory and hold it against high-tier challenge. They are the human tier at which the ecological calculus flips: below Foundation Establishment, humans are at the mercy of the beast ecology; at Foundation Establishment, humans become a shaping force within it. A Foundation Establishment cultivator who settles in a region changes the region's ecology within a decade — the mid-tier beasts they hunt decline, the herbs they protect flourish, the mortal villages they defend expand.

297:**At Core Formation+.** A Core Formation cultivator is an ecological force. They can create or destroy spirit veins (a Core Formation cultivator with the right technique can open a new vein by controlled geological stress, or close one by sealing the channel). They can cleanse or contaminate a region's qi by sustained practice. They can war with peak-tier beasts and win. They can hold a sacred place. A Core Formation cultivator is not a node in the food web; they are a *modifier* of the food web. This is why Core Formation+ cultivators are treated as sovereigns: their decisions reshape the ecology for hundreds of li around them, and the mortals, beasts, and lesser cultivators who live in that radius have no say in the reshaping.
