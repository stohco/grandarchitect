   121→The 15 named households are not a list; they are a network. The Wang Senior Household (Household 1) sits at the center, but the network does not radiate from it — it loops through it. The Wang Tenant Household (2) shares a pig with the Senior Household and a landlord with no one. The Salt Merchant Household (3) is bound to the Senior Household by blood (Lady Wang is Shouzheng's sister, exiled from his threshold for 20 years) and to the village by salt. The Lin Household (4) is bound to the Senior Household by Shouzheng's niece (Lady Wang the carpenter's wife). The Widow's Household (5) is bound to the Senior Household by Shouzheng's quiet charity and to the Tenant Household by her late husband (a Wang cousin). The Zhou Household (6) is bound to every household by birth. The He Household (7) is bound to the Salt Merchant by the dock. The Wu Household (8) is bound to the Lin Household by marriage (Lady Lin is Lin Aqiao's sister). The Zhao Household (9) is bound to the Senior Household by Shouzheng's niece (Lady Wang the soldier's wife — a different Lady Wang, a different niece). The Li Household (10) is bound to no Wang by blood but to the Salt Merchant by trade (Li Asheng sells rice to Hu for the river trade). The Wang Second Tenant Household (11) is bound to the Tenant Household by shared tenancy and shared river-god piety. The Wang Bare-Stick Household (12) is bound to the Senior Household by the half-brother wound. The Wang School Household (13) is bound to the Senior Household by cousinship and by the school obligation. The Wang Second Branch Household (14) is bound to the Senior Household by brotherhood and by the disowned daughter (Wang Meili). The Pan Tofu Household (15) is bound to the Senior Household by Pan Siniang's late husband (Shouzheng's cousin) and to the widows (Xu, Zhou) by the practice of mourning-with-the-widow.
   122→
   123→This is the society the player enters. Every household has at least one tie to another named household, and most have three or four. The three tensions of doc 28 do not run through the Senior Household alone; they run through the network. The salt license tension touches Hu, He, Li, Zhao, Pan, Yang, and Wang Lun. The missing-son tension touches Widow Xu, Zhou, He, Wu, Shoucheng, Zongbao, and Pan. The betrothal tension touches Shouzheng, Sanniang, Wang Lun, Wang Shouli, Zhao, Simei (Shoucheng's daughter), and Li Asheng. No household is a spectator.

   129→### 2.1 Wang Meili (王美麗) — the lineage bridge
   130→
   131→- **Age at introduction (session ~50, Act 1):** 22. **Age at endgame:** ~120, appearing ~30. **Realm at introduction:** Qi Induction. **Realm at endgame:** Foundation Establishment (if she survives).
   132→- **Household:** the Wang Second Branch Household (Household 14), headed by her father Wang Shouli (王守禮, 56), who formally disowned her when she went to the Cangwu Sect. Her mother Lady Qin (秦氏, 53) dies of old age at ~78 (session ~100, the autumn of the first return). Her older brother Wang Zongyao inherits the household; he is mortal and dies at ~62 (session ~300). She has one younger brother, Wang Zongping (19), unmarried.
   133→- **Personality:** gentle, observant, stubborn in private. She has her father's distrust of Old Chen (which she never quite shook) and her mother's quiet competence. She speaks softly. She remembers everything. She does not lie.
   134→- **Secret:** she perceived qi before the protagonist did — at 14, a year before the protagonist's first onset. She told no one because her father distrusted cultivation and would have beaten her for "demonic influence." She has been quietly practicing on her own, with stolen manuals from the Cangwu Sect's outer library, since 15. When the protagonist's perception became known (Scene 1), she felt a complicated mix of relief (not alone) and resentment (the protagonist got the teacher she could not). She did not speak of this for years. When the protagonist left for Old Chen, she went to her father and demanded to be sent to the Cangwu Sect. Her father refused. She went anyway, walking 100 li to the sect gate at 19. The sect took her as a low-ranking healer-track disciple.
   135→- **Grief:** her mother, Lady Qin, died in the autumn of the protagonist's first year at Old Chen's hermitage. Meili was at the Cangwu Sect. She did not return in time. The death was a fever, like Wang Xiaodi's, three days from onset to end. Meili carries the same guilt the protagonist carries — she was not there. She has not said this to the protagonist. The protagonist does not know. This shared unspoken grief is the silent chord between them.
   136→- **Ambition:** she wants to be the Wang lineage's first cultivator-healer — a woman who can be at the bedside of the dying and ease their passing with qi, who can prevent the fevers that took her mother and Wang Xiaodi. She does not want power; she wants to be present. This is the inverse of the protagonist's ambition (the protagonist wants to undo the past; Meili wants to be present for the future).
   137→- **Why she travels with the protagonist:** she joins the protagonist's circle in Act 2 (session ~400) when her Cangwu Sect duties bring her to the Cangli Riverlands to gather herbs. She has heard of the protagonist's Foundation Establishment (the Wang lineage's pride) and seeks them out at Li Family Creek. She travels with the protagonist because (a) she has, by now, perceived her own qi clearly enough to want a teacher who is not bound by sect politics; (b) she wants to see the village again, which she has not visited in 8 years; (c) she carries her mother's unburned tablet, which she wants to install in the Wang lineage hall (the lineage council refused because her mother was a Second Branch wife of lower standing; Meili wants the protagonist, as the Senior Household's cultivator, to intercede). The third reason is the load-bearing one.
   138→- **Arc across the three acts:**
   139→  - **Act 1 (sessions 1-100):** offstage. The protagonist hears of her only as "your cousin who ran away to the sect." Her father has disowned her. The protagonist does not know her secret grief or her secret qi perception.
   140→  - **Act 2 (sessions 100-500):** becomes a companion at session ~400. By then a Qi Condensation healer-track cultivator, ~38 (appearing ~25). She rejoins the protagonist's circle, travels through the Cangli spirit vein dispute and the first meetings with Leng Qingxue and Liang Zhu. She is the one who insists on returning to the village for the autumn ancestor rite. She is the one who stays at the hermitage when Old Chen is dying (the protagonist arrives too late; Meili is there, having walked from the Cangwu Sect on a rumor that Old Chen was failing). She tells the protagonist, gently, that Old Chen's last circulation route was complete.
   141→  - **Act 3 (sessions 500-1500+):** by now Foundation Establishment (having broken through shortly after the protagonist, around session 200, with Old Chen's quiet guidance via letter). She is the protagonist's first disciple in the "found a lineage" ending. She is the one who greets the protagonist on every return to the village. She is the one who, in the Confront path, helps the protagonist bury Pei Liang's harvested anchors. She is the one who, in the Accommodate path, is harvested by Pei Liang (her anchor taken as leverage; her body found at the edge of the Cangwu foothills, the qi drained).
   142→- **Relationship to the other companions:**
   143→  - **To Liang Zhu:** she finds him irreverent and kind; they bond over a shared fondness for woodwinds (she plays the qin, he plays the xiao, and they have a few evenings of duet at the hermitage before his death). She grieves him quietly, in the way of healers — by tending the people he left behind.
   144→  - **To Leng Qingxue:** initially intimidated (Leng Qingxue is Core Formation, the daughter of a great-sect master, a sword cultivator; Meili is a Qi Condensation healer from a village). She grows into a quiet, mutual respect. They do not become friends — they are too different — but they become allies. Meili is the one who tells Leng Qingxue, in Act 3, "He speaks of you. He does not say what he feels. You should ask him." This is the push that triggers the seventh bout of the Exchange of Cuts.
   145→- **When and how she leaves the party:** she does not leave in the Confront or Transcend paths. In the Accommodate path, she is killed (Pei Liang harvests her anchor) — this is her "leaving." In the "found a lineage" ending, she stays with the protagonist at the hermitage as co-teacher and dies, eventually, of old age at ~490 (Foundation Establishment lifespan), having outlived her own students. Her tablet is installed in the Wang lineage hall — the first woman's tablet to be installed there, which the protagonist achieves by lineage council vote in her 200th year.

   147→### 2.2 Liang Zhu (梁柱) — the first peer
   148→
   149→- **Background:** born in a small town 300 li north of Qingzhou Prefectural Seat, the son of a failed merchant. He perceived qi at 14 and was admitted to a small sect (the Iron Bamboo Sect) as a low-ranking disciple. Showed promise — broke through to Foundation Establishment at 35. Was on track for Core Formation when, at 50, he discovered that his sect master was harvesting anchors from mortals in the sect's territory to fuel his own Core Formation breakthrough. Liang Zhu reported this to the Azure Sword Sect (the regional great sect). The Azure Sword investigated, found the accusation true, and executed Liang Zhu's sect master. But the investigation also revealed that Liang Zhu had known about the harvesting for 5 years before reporting it (he discovered it at 45, reported at 50). The Azure Sword judged him complicit by silence. They stripped his sect standing and exiled him. He has been a rogue cultivator for 30 years. He has never returned to his hometown. He has not broken through to Core Formation; he believes he does not deserve to.
   150→- **Realm:** Foundation Establishment (peak), ~80 at introduction, appears ~40. Lifespan to ~200, but he has not attempted Core Formation in 30 years.
   151→- **Personality:** irreverent, kind, weary, funny in a mordant way. He drinks too much. He plays the xiao (簫, vertical bamboo flute) beautifully — the only thing he does that he is proud of. He speaks of his past rarely and never with self-pity. He notices when someone is hurting and sits with them without speaking.
   152→- **Secret:** the 5 years of silence. He discovered his sect master's harvesting at 45. He did not report it because he was afraid — of the sect master's power, of exile, of being wrong (the sect master was his teacher). He reported it at 50 only because he walked in on the harvesting of a child. The child's face has not left him. He has never told anyone about the 5 years. The Azure Sword's judgment was correct.
   153→- **Grief:** the child. Her name was A-Qing (阿青), a mortal girl of 6, the daughter of the sect's herb-gatherer. Liang Zhu had taught her to read. He found her in the sect master's private practice chamber, her anchor half-harvested, her body still warm. He carried her to her father. He reported the sect master the next morning. He has played the xiao for her every year on the anniversary of her death, at whatever tavern he happens to be in, for 30 years. He has never named the tune.
   154→- **Ambition:** he wants to die well. Not bravely — well. He wants to die having done one thing right that he can perceive, in the moment of dying, as right. He does not expect to break through to Core Formation. He does not want to. He wants, in his last moment, to be the man he should have been at 45.
   155→- **Why he travels with the protagonist:** he meets the protagonist at Qinghe Market Town at session ~250. The protagonist, ~50 (Foundation Establishment, appears ~30), is investigating Pei Liang's raid on the Cangwu Sect. Liang Zhu has heard of the raid (the rogue cultivator network is efficient). He approaches the protagonist because he heard the description of Pei Liang's qi-signature — the corrupted, borrowed residue — and recognized it. He has perceived that signature before: his sect master's qi, in the months before exposure, was similarly stained. He knows what Pei Liang is. He travels with the protagonist because Pei Liang is the second sect master, and Liang Zhu will not be silent a second time.
   156→- **Arc across the three acts:**
   157→  - **Act 2 (sessions 250-500):** the protagonist's first peer and friend outside the sect and the village. He travels with the protagonist through the Cangli spirit vein dispute, the first meetings with Leng Qingxue, the first encounters with Pei Liang's network. He teaches the rogue cultivator's skills: how to read a sect's politics from its outer hall, how to find safe lodging in a strange town, how to spot a hustler. He plays the xiao at the hermitage on the evenings when the protagonist is weary. He tells the protagonist, drunk, at session ~350, about A-Qing. The protagonist does not know what to say. Liang Zhu does not expect them to.
   158→  - **Act 3 (sessions 500-1500+):** joins the pursuit of Pei Liang. He knows he will not survive it (Foundation Establishment peak against a Nascent Soul antagonist). He joins because he will not be silent a second time. At session ~700 he identifies one of Pei Liang's harvest-stores by its qi-signature — the same stained residue as his sect master's. At session ~750 he tells the protagonist, soberly, that he does not expect to see the end of the pursuit. The protagonist does not accept this. Liang Zhu does not argue.
   159→- **His death:** at session ~800, the protagonist and companions are ambushed by Pei Liang at a cave system in the Cangwu foothills (one of Pei Liang's harvest-stores). Pei Liang, now Nascent Soul (approaching Spirit Severance), outclasses them all. The protagonist is wounded; Leng Qingxue holds the line; Meili drags the protagonist toward the cave mouth. Liang Zhu stays behind to delay Pei Liang. He plays a single note on the xiao — a qi-residue technique he developed, leaving his signature in the qi of the cave. The note is the same tune he has played for A-Qing every year for 30 years. He plays it now for the protagonist — a final teaching, in the form of a sound. Pei Liang strikes him down. His anchor disperses (Pei Liang's harvest-technique, applied at the moment of death, prevents the anchor from entering bardo — the dispersal is immediate and irreversible). The protagonist, retreating, perceives the dispersal. The protagonist screams — the only time in the game they do.
   160→- **What his death means to the protagonist:** the second grief that mirrors the first (Wang Xiaodi's). The protagonist, again, was not enough to save someone they loved. The difference: this time, the protagonist was present. The grief is different — not the guilt of absence but the grief of presence. In the months after, the protagonist perceives Liang Zhu's last note as a residue-trace in the qi of every cave they enter. The trace is faint and fading, but it is there. The protagonist carries it for the rest of the game. This is the Residual Error Sense's deepest use: perceiving the teaching of the dead. The protagonist's Nascent Soul breakthrough (session ~1000-1200) is driven, in part, by the desire to perceive Liang Zhu's last note one more time, at a higher realm, before the trace fades entirely.

   162→### 2.3 Leng Qingxue (冷清雪) — the sect dissident and romance
   163→
   164→- **Position in the sect:** the only daughter of Leng Wushuang (冷無霜, Azure Sword Sect master, Core Formation, 340). Her father (Leng Wushuang's husband) was a Foundation Establishment elder of the sect who died in a tribulation 80 years ago, before Leng Qingxue was conceived (Leng Wushuang, at Core Formation, preserved his seed in a qi-bound vessel and conceived Leng Qingxue 40 years later — a technique the sect considers legitimate but unusual). Leng Qingxue is also the formal student of Elder Han (韓長老, the sect's sword-arts master, Core Formation, 280). She is, by any measure, the sect's heir apparent — except that she has publicly opposed her mother's expansionist policy, which has made her politically untouchable.
   165→- **Realm:** Core Formation (mid-stage), ~120 at introduction, appears ~30. Broke through at 80 — fast for the Azure Sword Sect (average 120). Considered a generational talent. Has not attempted Nascent Soul; does not intend to until the political situation resolves.
   166→- **Reason for dissenting:** the Azure Sword Sect, under Leng Wushuang, is quietly expanding toward the Cangli Riverlands to claim the latent spirit vein beneath the Cangwu Mountains (doc 24 §4.3, doc 26 §2.3). The expansion is lawful (the vein is unclaimed) but ruthless — the sect's outer hall has been buying land, establishing "survey posts" that are in fact military outposts, and pressuring the Cangwu Sect to submit as a vassal. Leng Qingxue's dissent is principled: (a) the Cangwu Sect's claim, while weak, is older and should be adjudicated, not overrun; (b) the expansion will provoke the Jade Void Holy Land (which has its own claim to the broader vein system); (c) the expansion is, at root, her mother's grief — Leng Wushuang's husband died in the Cangwu Mountains 80 years ago on a survey mission for the vein, and Leng Qingxue believes her mother's expansion is an 80-year act of mourning that has hardened into policy. She has said the third point to her mother, once, in private. Her mother did not speak to her for a year. The dissent has been public for 20 years.
   167→- **Personality:** precise, cold in manner (her name means "clear snow"), warm in private, slow to laugh but quick to smile. Sword cultivator — qi is metal-phase dominant, sharp, fast. Speaks in short sentences. Does not waste words. In private, surprisingly funny — a dry wit the protagonist does not expect from a Core Formation great-sect daughter.
   168→- **Secret:** she has been corresponding, for 30 years, with a Core Formation cultivator of the Jade Void Holy Land — Xu Yunfeng (許雲峰, 200), whom she met at a regional conference. The correspondence is political, not romantic. They have been quietly building a coalition to oppose both the Azure Sword expansion and the Jade Void counter-claim, proposing a regional compact that would leave the Cangwu Mountains' spirit vein under the Cangwu Sect's stewardship with Azure Sword and Jade Void as guarantors. The correspondence is treasonous, technically — she is negotiating against her own sect's policy. Her mother does not know. Xu Yunfeng is the only person outside the Cangli Riverlands who knows the full shape of her dissent.
   169→- **Grief:** her father. She never met him (he died 80 years before her conception), but she grew up in his shadow. Her mother never spoke of him except as a survey-mission casualty. Leng Qingxue found his journals in the sect archive at 60 — 200 pages of careful observation of the Cangwu Mountains' qi-topography, written in the year before his death. The journals are the reason she knows the Cangwu Mountains as well as she does. She has read them 40 times. She has never told her mother she found them.
   170→- **Ambition:** she wants the regional compact. She wants the Cangwu Sect to steward the vein. She wants her mother to retire (Leng Wushuang is 340; Core Formation lifespan ~500; she has 160 years left, and Leng Qingxue does not want to spend them in opposition). She wants, personally, to be free of the sect without leaving it — to be a sword cultivator who chooses her own contracts, her own travels, her own (eventually) partner. She does not want to be the sect's heir. She does not want to be her mother's successor.
   171→- **Why she travels with the protagonist:** she is sent by the Azure Sword Sect at session ~200 to investigate Pei Liang's raid on the Cangwu Sect. The sect's official reason: assess the threat to Azure Sword interests in the region. Her private reason: assess the protagonist (the Azure Sword has heard of the Wang lineage's first cultivator; Leng Wushuang wants to know if the protagonist is potential ally, pawn, or obstacle). She travels with the protagonist initially as an observer. She stays because she finds, in the protagonist, the first person in 100 years who does not want anything from her — the protagonist is not intimidated by her sect, not interested in her mother, not trying to court her. The protagonist is, in her assessment, the only honest cultivator she has met since Xu Yunfeng.
   172→- **The courtship verb — the Exchange of Cuts (交劍, jiaojian):** two cultivators spar. The loser owes the winner a truthful answer to one question. The questions cannot be about combat; they must be about the self. Three bouts (three questions, three answers) = courtship acknowledged. Seven bouts = betrothal. Either party may decline a bout; declining three times ends the courtship. The Exchange of Cuts makes courtship a kind of cultivation: you learn the other's qi through their sword, the other's self through their answers. The truth-debt is enforced by the simulation — a lie in an answer registers as a karmic trace (doc 24 §2.1) and is perceivable, at Core Formation, by the wronged party.
   173→  - First bout (session ~300, after the Cangli spirit vein dispute): Leng Qingxue wins. Asks: "Why did you join the Cangwu Sect?" The protagonist must answer truthfully.
   174→  - Second bout (session ~400): the protagonist wins. Asks: "Why do you oppose your mother?" Leng Qingxue answers truthfully — with the third point (her mother's grief).
   175→  - Third bout (session ~500, after Core Formation): they spar to a draw. No questions. They sit in silence. This is the courtship acknowledgment.
   176→  - Subsequent bouts (sessions ~700-1200): developing toward betrothal.
   177→  - Seventh bout (before the final confrontation with Pei Liang): the question, asked by whichever party the player is not, is "Will you come back?" The answer, whatever it is, is the truth.
   178→- **Arc across the three acts:**
   179→  - **Act 2 (sessions 200-500):** introduced as the sect investigator. Initially cold, then cautiously warm, then — by session ~500, after the third bout — openly allied with the protagonist. Provides the Azure Sword Sect's intelligence on Pei Liang. Does not, in Act 2, break with her sect. Her dissent is still private.
   180→  - **Act 3 (sessions 500-1500+):** the break. At session ~700, when Liang Zhu identifies one of Pei Liang's harvest-stores, Leng Qingxue recognizes the harvest-technique's signature — it is the same technique her mother's late husband was investigating when he died 80 years ago. The realization: Pei Liang's technique is descended from the same source as the technique Leng Wushuang's husband died investigating. The Cangwu Mountains have been a harvest-ground for demonic cultivators for centuries. Leng Wushuang's expansion is, in part, an 80-year war against this legacy — but the war has hardened into territorial ambition. Leng Qingxue confronts her mother at session ~900 (a scene at the Azure Sword Sect's main hall). Her mother disowns her. Leng Qingxue leaves the sect. She rejoins the protagonist as a full companion, not an observer.
   181→- **Whether she can die:** yes, in two ways.
   182→  - In the **Confront path**, if the protagonist loses the final confrontation with Pei Liang, Leng Qingxue (present) is killed trying to protect the protagonist's retreat. Her anchor disperses. The protagonist, if they survive, returns to Wang Family Bend and reports her death to her mother — a scene in which Leng Wushuang, for the first time in 80 years, weeps.
   183→  - In the **Accommodate path**, if the protagonist accepts Pei Liang's offer, Leng Qingxue refuses to join. She attempts to leave with Wang Meili. Pei Liang, perceiving her as a threat, kills her. Her anchor is harvested. The protagonist perceives this and does nothing — the moment that defines the Accommodate path's moral cost.
   184→  - In the **Transcend path**, she survives. She is the one who, in the "cross the tribulation" ending, stays in the mortal world and continues the protagonist's work. She is the one who, in the "final death" ending (if the protagonist fails the Tribulation Crossing), reports the protagonist's death to Wang Family Bend. She lives, in the Transcend path, to ~500 (Nascent Soul lifespan), and dies at her mother's grave, having buried her 160 years before.

   186→### 2.4 The three companions together
   187→
   188→The three companions are not a party in the Baldur's Gate sense. They are three witnesses, each with a different tempo of aging, each with a different relationship to the protagonist's grief. Wang Meili ages with the village (slower than mortals, faster than the protagonist) and is the lineage bridge. Liang Zhu ages with the protagonist (both Foundation Establishment) but dies before the end — the first peer lost. Leng Qingxue outlives the protagonist's mortal world and is the political partner. The three together — lineage, peer, romance — are the protagonist's social world. The genre's solitude is preserved because the protagonist is always the point of view and always the one who outlives.

   190→### 2.5 The companions' key scenes
   191→
   192→Each companion has three to four scripted scenes (the "narrative-critical" beats per doc 26 §11.2) that the simulation flags and the player encounters by playing. These are the moments the companion's arc turns.
   193→
   194→**Wang Meili:**
   195→- **Session ~400 — the reunion.** Meili arrives at Li Family Creek as the protagonist is consulting Old Chen. She carries her mother's unburned tablet. The protagonist must choose whether to intercede with the lineage council (the load-bearing choice). The scene establishes her as a companion and her third reason for traveling (§2.1).
   196→- **Session ~445 — the vigil.** Meili, having heard a rumor that Old Chen is failing, walks from the Cangwu Sect to the hermitage. She is there when the protagonist arrives too late (session ~450). She tells the protagonist, gently, that Old Chen's last circulation route was complete. This scene is the protagonist's first encounter with someone who was present when they were not — the inverse of the protagonist's own pattern.
   197→- **Session ~900 — the confrontation with Wang Shouli.** Meili, now Foundation Establishment, returns to Wang Family Bend with the protagonist and confronts her father, who is old and frail. The father weeps. The reconciliation is partial. The scene is the protagonist's first witness to a mortal reconciliation that cultivation made possible (Meili's extended lifespan gave her the time to be ready; her father's mortality gave him the time to soften).
   198→- **Session ~1500 (Accommodate path only) — the death.** Meili is harvested by Pei Liang. Her body is found at the edge of the Cangwu foothills. The protagonist perceives, in the harvest-residue, the texture of her qi — the healer-track signature, the gentle steady warmth she cultivated for 80 years. The protagonist, if they chose the Accommodate path, must perceive this and live with it.
   199→
   200→**Liang Zhu:**
   201→- **Session ~350 — the confession.** Drunk, at a tavern in Qinghe Market Town, Liang Zhu tells the protagonist about A-Qing. He does not cry. The protagonist does not know what to say. The scene is the first time the protagonist hears the shape of complicity-by-silence — the same shape the protagonist's own Accommodate path would take.
   202→- **Session ~700 — the recognition.** Liang Zhu identifies one of Pei Liang's harvest-stores by its qi-signature. The recognition is not intellectual; it is somatic — his hands shake. The protagonist perceives, in Liang Zhu's qi, the resonance of a 30-year grief surfacing. This scene is the moment the pursuit becomes personal for Liang Zhu, not just for the protagonist.
   203→- **Session ~750 — the warning.** Liang Zhu tells the protagonist, soberly, that he does not expect to see the end of the pursuit. The protagonist does not accept this. Liang Zhu does not argue. The scene is the companion's last full conversation with the protagonist; the dialogue system records every word the player chooses, and Liang Zhu's residue-trace (perceivable after his death) carries the echo of those words.
   204→- **Session ~800 — the last note.** Liang Zhu plays the xiao one last time. The note is a qi-residue technique; it is also the tune he has played for A-Qing for 30 years. The protagonist, retreating, perceives the dispersal. The protagonist screams.
   205→
   206→**Leng Qingxue:**
   207→- **Session ~300 — the first bout.** The Exchange of Cuts begins. She wins; she asks "Why did you join the Cangwu Sect?" The protagonist must answer truthfully. The scene establishes the courtship verb as a real mechanic, not a cutscene.
   208→- **Session ~500 — the third bout (the draw).** They spar to a draw. No questions. They sit in silence. This is the courtship acknowledgment — the moment the relationship becomes real, expressed as the absence of a question.
   209→- **Session ~700 — the recognition.** Leng Qingxue recognizes the harvest-technique's signature as the one her father was investigating. The scene is her grief becoming political — the moment her private dissent (her father's journals) and her public dissent (the expansion) merge with the antagonist.
   210→- **Session ~900 — the break.** Leng Qingxue confronts her mother at the Azure Sword Sect's main hall. Her mother disowns her. The scene is the romance's cost made concrete: the protagonist's partner has given up her sect, her lineage, her duty, for principle and for the protagonist. The player perceives, in Leng Qingxue's qi after the break, a new signature — lighter, sharper, the qi of someone who has chosen.
   211→- **Session ~1500 — the seventh bout.** Before the final confrontation. The question, asked by whichever party the player is not: "Will you come back?" The answer, whatever it is, is the truth. This is the last scene before the final confrontation, and its residue-trace carries through the confrontation and into the ending.

   215→## Part 3: The Antagonist — Pei Liang (裴良)
   216→
   217→### 3.1 Background and realm progression
   218→
   219→Pei Liang (裴良) was born 180 years before game start, in a small town 200 li west of the Cangwu Mountains. His father was a country doctor; his mother died in childbirth. He perceived qi at 12 and was admitted to the Iron Bamboo Sect (鐵竹派, a small sect 50 li from his hometown) as a low-ranking disciple. His sect master was Daoist Iron Bamboo (鐵竹道人, Foundation Establishment, deceased at 110 when Pei Liang was 60). The Iron Bamboo Sect had no political ambition and no enemies; it was dissolved 80 years ago after Daoist Iron Bamboo's death, its disciples scattered. Pei Liang was the sect's most talented disciple, but not its heir — he left at 70 to pursue Core Formation as a rogue.
   220→
   221→At 85 (95 years before game start), while traveling home to visit his dying father, Pei Liang encountered the aftermath of Zhou Wugui's massacre (doc 24 §4.4 — Zhou Wugui killed three villages in the Cangwu foothills, harvesting their anchors to fuel a breakthrough attempt; the Azure Sword Sect's previous sect master pursued and killed him).    227→### 3.2 Personality, secret, grief, motivation
   228→
   229→- **Personality:** calm, courteous, soft-spoken. He does not monologue. He does not threaten. He speaks to mortals and cultivators with the same quiet courtesy. He is, in conversation, almost gentle. He is also — and this is what makes him terrifying — completely convinced that what he is doing is right. He is not a sadist; he takes no pleasure in the harvest. He considers it a regrettable necessity. He has wept, in private, after every harvest. He has not stopped.
   230→- **Secret:** he has harvested the anchor of his own son. His son, Pei An (裴安), was a Foundation Establishment cultivator who died in a law tribulation 50 years ago (when Pei Liang was 130). The tribulation tested a minor Acquired-law amendment Pei An was attempting; he failed. Pei An's anchor, by lawful process, should have entered bardo and dispersed. Pei Liang, in the moment of his son's death, harvested the anchor — the first time he harvested an anchor he loved. He has kept it in his harvest-store for 50 years. He speaks to it, in private, every night. He believes his son's anchor is still conscious, still him, still Pei An. He is wrong (an anchor in a harvest-store is not conscious; it is fuel, slowly drained). He has not been able to verify this because the harvest-technique, by design, prevents the cultivator from perceiving the harvested anchor's interiority — to perceive it would be to perceive its suffering, and the technique's continued use requires the cultivator not to perceive. The secret is: he is, at the deepest level, harvesting anchors to keep his son's anchor company. The 200 anchors are, in his grief-addled heart, companions for Pei An.
   231→- **Grief:** Pei An. His only child. His wife (Lady Mu, 木氏) died 80 years ago in a beast attack, before Pei An's tribulation. Pei An was the only person Pei Liang had left. When Pei An died in the tribulation, Pei Liang's grief broke something in him — the part that distinguished between "I will not let my son go" and "I will undo what the law has done." He has not been able to distinguish the two since.
   232→- **Motivation (why he harvests anchors):** Pei Liang wants Spirit Severance because Spirit Severance is the realm of externalized law (化神, doc 03 Station 7). He wants to author a domain where anchor-harvesting is lawful, where his son's anchor can be restored to consciousness, where the 200 anchors in his store can be returned to their bodies. He believes this is possible. It is not — the harvest is irreversible; the drained anchors cannot be reconstituted; Spirit Severance authorship is bounded and cannot undo the harvest. His motivation is not power. It is grief. He wants his son back. The 200 harvested anchors are, in his mind, the cost of a grief-undoing law he believes he can write at Spirit Severance. He is wrong about the law. He is wrong about his son (Pei An's anchor is not conscious). He is right about one thing: the law, as it stands, is cruel. The bardo window is bounded, and the bound is a grief the cosmos does not acknowledge. His dissent from the law is, at root, a dissent from a cosmos that took his son and called it lawful.

   234→### 3.3 The dark mirror
   235→
   236→The protagonist and Pei Liang have both lost someone (Wang Xiaodi / Pei An). Both want them back. Both have, at some level, considered breaking the law to do so (the protagonist's Accommodate path is exactly this — joining Pei Liang). The difference is that the protagonist chose to mourn (Scene 5, the successful Foundation Establishment, requires mourning first); Pei Liang chose not to. The dark mirror: Pei Liang is what the protagonist would have become if they had not mourned. This is the load-bearing thematic parallel. When the protagonist perceives Wang Xiaodi's anchor in Pei Liang's harvest-store (session ~550), they perceive, in the same store, Pei An's anchor — and recognize, in the recognition of Pei An, the recognition of their own grief in Pei Liang's. This is the moment the personal conflict and the antagonist merge (doc 26 §4.3).
