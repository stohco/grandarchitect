   110→**Act 1 ends:** The protagonist is Foundation Establishment. They are a cultivator. They have outlived their first decade of mortal-time. The first return to the village (§5, session 100) is the threshold to Act 2.
   111→
   112→### 4.2 Act 2: Foundation Establishment to Core Formation (ages 23 to ~150, sessions 100-500)
   113→
   114→**Key beats:**
   115→
   116→- **Session ~120 — The Cangwu Sect.** The protagonist joins the Cangwu Sect (the small local sect, 100 li west, doc 24 §4.3) as a junior disciple. Old Chen, who left his own sect decades ago, endorses the joining but warns: "A sect is a tool, not a home." The protagonist learns sect practice, takes on sect responsibilities, and begins to perceive the cultivation world beyond the Cangli Riverlands.
   117→
   118→- **Session ~150 — The first return (the joy of Act 2).** The protagonist returns to Wang Family Bend for the first time since Foundation Establishment. The village is the same; the people are older. **Lady Chen** (陳氏, Old Chen's mortal wife, ~75 at game start, now ~100, frail but alive, sustained by Old Chen's qi-care) greets the protagonist at the hermitage. The protagonist's father **Wang Shouyi** (王守義) has gray hair. The graveyard has new tablets — three, including **Wang Lun** (王倫, who died of pneumonia the previous winter at 47 — the protagonist was at the hermitage, practicing for Foundation Establishment, and did not return in time) and two infants from the cousin-branch. The protagonist perceives the qi of the place: it is denser than they remember, because their perception has sharpened. They perceive, in the lineage hall, the residue of their own childhood — fading. They weep. This is the joy of Act 2: the first return, the recognition that power costs time and the time costs love. (This is also the viral moment, §8.)
   119→
   120→- **Session ~200 — Pei Liang's introduction (§3.3).** The raid on the Cangwu Sect. The first meeting with Leng Qingxue. The protagonist enters the antagonist's orbit.
   121→
   122→- **Session ~250 — Liang Zhu (companion, §9).** The protagonist meets **Liang Zhu** (梁柱, ~80, Foundation Establishment, appears ~40, a rogue cultivator and woodwinds player) at Qinghe Market Town. Liang Zhu chose the rogue path over sect politics. He becomes the protagonist's first peer and friend outside the sect.
   123→
   124→- **Session ~300 — The Cangli spirit vein dispute.** The Azure Sword Sect, under Leng Wushuang, formally claims the Cangwu Mountains' latent spirit vein. The Cangwu Sect objects. The protagonist, caught between, must choose a side — and Leng Qingxue (who opposes her mother) becomes the third companion (§9). The Exchange of Cuts (§10) begins here.
   125→
   126→- **Session ~400 — Wang Meili (companion, §9).** The protagonist's cousin **Wang Meili** (王美麗, ~40, the protagonist's childhood playmate, now a Qi Condensation healer-track cultivator at the Cangwu Sect) joins the protagonist's circle. She is the lineage bridge — the one who will age with the village, slower than mortals but faster than the protagonist.
   127→
   128→- **Session ~450 — Old Chen's death (§12).** Old Chen dies at ~165 (extended by his partial Foundation Establishment integration), appearing ~95. The protagonist, ~130 (Foundation Establishment, appears ~45), arrives at the hermitage to consult him about Pei Liang's traces and finds him gone. Old Chen's anchor enters bardo. The protagonist, at Foundation Establishment, cannot perceive it. The desire to perceive Old Chen's anchor one last time — and Wang Xiaodi's — drives the protagonist toward Core Formation.
   129→
   130→- **Session ~500 — Core Formation.** The protagonist forms the golden core (結丹, jiedan — doc 03 Station 5). Lifespan extends to ~500 years. The protagonist can now perceive spirit anchors. The first anchor they seek is Wang Xiaodi's. They do not find it. Then the antagonist's plan is revealed (§3.4): Wang Xiaodi's anchor is in Pei Liang's harvest-store.
   131→
   132→**Thesis:** Power costs time and the time costs love. Every realm the protagonist gains extends their lifespan and, with it, the number of mortals they will outlive. Old Chen is dead. Lady Chen will die soon. Wang Shouyi will die. The protagonist will attend their funerals, perceive their anchors' fading residue, and continue. This is the genre's central tragic engine (time-debt) at the personal scale.
   133→
   134→**Joy:** The first return (session ~150). The recognition that the village is the same and the player is the one who changed.
   135→
   136→### 4.3 Act 3: Core Formation to endgame (sessions 500 to ~1500+)
   137→
   138→**Key beats:**
   139→
   140→- **Session ~550 — The revelation.** The protagonist, at Core Formation, perceives Wang Xiaodi's anchor in Pei Liang's harvest-store. The Residual Error Sense, extended at Core Formation to perceive others' anchors, confirms the signature: it matches the residue-traces Wang Xiaodi left in the protagonist's own practice decades ago (the protagonist, as a Qi Condensation cultivator, practiced near the infant Wang Xiaodi; the infant's anchor left faint traces in the protagonist's residue, which the protagonist's Residual Error Sense preserved). This is the moment the personal conflict (§2) and the antagonist (§3) merge.
   141→
   142→- **Session ~600-1000 — The pursuit.** The protagonist, with companions (§9), pursues Pei Liang across the Cangli Riverlands and beyond. The pursuit reveals Scribe Yan's corruption (§2.3) and the Azure Sword Sect's political maneuvering (§2.3). The protagonist must navigate all three conflicts. Liang Zhu dies in this arc (§9) — killed in Pei Liang's ambush, his anchor dispersed. This is the second grief that mirrors the first.
   143→
   144→- **Session ~1000-1200 — The Nascent Soul breakthrough.** The protagonist, driven by the pursuit and by Liang Zhu's death, attempts Nascent Soul (元嬰 — doc 03 Station 6). Success: the protagonist's spirit can now act beyond the flesh. The protagonist can survive bodily death if the anchor is preserved. This is the realm required to confront Pei Liang (who is, by now, also pursuing Spirit Severance).
   145→
   146→- **Session ~1200-1500 — The choice.** The protagonist tracks Pei Liang to the Cangwu Mountains' latent spirit vein. The final confrontation (§3.4). The protagonist must choose:
   147→
   148→  - **Confront.** Kill Pei Liang, free the harvested anchors (including Wang Xiaodi's). Wang Xiaodi's anchor, freed, enters bardo — but the bardo window closed decades ago. The anchor disperses naturally, lawfully, into the cosmos's law-substrate. The protagonist perceives the dispersal. This is the teaching's resolution: Wang Xiaodi is gone, finally and lawfully, and the protagonist lets them go. If the protagonist wins the confrontation and survives, they return to Wang Family Bend and found the lineage (§7, ending 1). If the protagonist wins but is mortally wounded, their anchor disperses (§7, ending 3).
   149→
   150→  - **Accommodate.** Accept Pei Liang's offer: share Wang Xiaodi's anchor, become complicit in the harvest, gain Pei Liang's protection and a share of his breakthrough. The protagonist's karmic trace (doc 24 §2.1) becomes heavy. At the next tribulation (karmic tribulation, doc 24 §2.1), the protagonist cannot sustain the ledger. Their anchor disperses. (§7, ending 3 — the corrupted variant.)
   151→
   152→  - **Transcend.** Pursue Mahayana law-authorship. Cross Tribulation (most die — doc 03 Station 9). If the protagonist survives, author a law (doc 03 Station 10) that closes the bardo-harvest loophole: a scoped Acquired-authorship (doc 24 §1.1) that makes anchor-harvesting unlawful in the Cangli Riverlands and, by precedent, in the broader Acquired Stratum. The law, if ratified (the tribulation tests compatibility — doc 24 §1.1), disperses Pei Liang's harvest-store lawfully — including Wang Xiaodi's anchor, which disperses as in the Confront path. The protagonist, having authored the law, becomes a Mahayana law-author. They leave the mortal world for the Precelestial (the law-authorship venue for Reach-authorship, per doc 24 §1.1, is in the Precelestial's Law Reaches). (§7, ending 2.)
   153→
   154→**Thesis:** The world's law is not fixed; but authoring it costs everything. The Confront path costs the protagonist their grief (they let Wang Xiaodi go) but not their life. The Accommodate path costs the protagonist their moral self (and then their life). The Transcend path costs the protagonist their world (they leave the mortal stratum for the Precelestial, and the time-debt becomes absolute — 1 day above = 1 year below, doc 00 §1).
   155→
   156→**Joy:** The law-authorship moment (Transcend path only). The protagonist, at Mahayana, perceives the Acquired Stratum's law-substrate directly. They write the law that closes the bardo-harvest loophole. The law takes effect. The protagonist perceives, across the Cangli Riverlands, the harvest-stores of every demonic cultivator in the region emptying lawfully — the anchors dispersing into the cosmos's law-substrate, going to their lawful next rebirths. Among them, Wang Xiaodi's. The protagonist perceives Wang Xiaodi's anchor, one last time, as it disperses — and recognizes, in its dispersal, the residue of the infant they practiced near decades ago. This is the deepest form of the first awe: not "the world is larger than I knew" but "the world's law is the world's love, and I have written a small part of it."

The first verb (§13.1) is **carry water**: the protagonist takes the shoulder-pole and two buckets to the river, fills them, and carries them up the rutted path to the household cistern. The younger brother Wang Xiaodi is dead (two years); his tablet is also in the lineage hall.

   169→
   170→### 5.2 Session 10: The first awe
   171→
   172→The protagonist has perceived qi twice — briefly, uncertainly (doc 05 §1.2). It holds for thirty seconds. The world gains its second layer (doc 05 §4.2): the river's sound has a voice beneath it; the sun's warmth has a direction and a weight; the protagonist's own hands have an interior. The protagonist perceives that these qualities were always there. The world did not change; the protagonist did.
   173→
   174→The protagonist weeps (doc 05 §1.2). Not from sadness — from recognition. They are smaller in a larger world, and the smallness is relieving. This is the first awe. It is the gate from Mortal to Qi Induction (doc 05 §4.4).

This is the second grief of absence — the protagonist was not there for Wang Lun's death, just as they were not there for Wang Xiaodi's.

They perceive, in the lineage hall, the residue of Wang Lun's last lesson — fading. They perceive, in the graveyard, the residue of Wang Xiaodi's anchor — faint, almost gone. They weep.    190→## 6. The session-level loop
   191→
   192→**One sentence:** Tomorrow you return because the world has moved since yesterday — a solar term has turned, a sect has made a move, a lineage has had a birth or a death, a beast has migrated, a qi-tide has shifted — and your practice has left new residue-traces in the world for you to read with your Residual Error Sense, refining what you attempt tomorrow.
   193→
   194→The loop has two halves: the world's motion (generated per session from world-state, per §11) and the player's motion (the residue-traces left by yesterday's practice, perceivable via Residual Error Sense, doc 00 §5). The world half is the *given* (what the player responds to); the player half is the *made* (what the player has done and can now read). The loop is the rhythm between them.

   198→## 7. The endings
   199→
   200→### 7.1 Ending 1: Found a lineage (mortal-time)
   201→
   202→**Reachable via:** Confront path, if the protagonist wins and survives. Also reachable without confronting Pei Liang, if the protagonist chooses to abandon the pursuit and stay (a valid but harder choice — the grief of Wang Xiaodi's anchor remaining in Pei Liang's store is unresolved).
   203→
   204→**What happens:** The protagonist, at Core Formation or Nascent Soul, returns to Wang Family Bend (or to Old Chen's hermitage at Li Family Creek) and founds the Wang lineage's cultivation school. The first disciple is **Wang Meili** (王美麗, the cousin, §9), who has by now reached Foundation Establishment herself. The protagonist teaches the next generation: the village children who perceive qi, the cousin-branch's grandchildren, Wang Meili's own students. The protagonist watches them grow, age, and (if they do not cultivate) die. The protagonist watches their students' students grow. The protagonist's Residual Error Sense, extended by teaching, perceives the residue of their own teaching in their students' practice — the traces of the protagonist's corrections, fading in the students' qi as they internalize them. This is the deepest form of the first awe: the world's continuation through teaching.
   205→
   206→The protagonist's last act: writing, in the Wang lineage genealogy (族譜, zupu), the character 修 (xiu, "cultivate") next to their own name. The protagonist dies of old age at ~500 (Core Formation lifespan). Their anchor enters bardo and is reborn, lawfully, into the lineage they founded. The cycle continues. The game's final image: the lineage hall, generations later, the protagonist's tablet among the ancestors, a child perceiving qi for the first time.
   207→
   208→### 7.2 Ending 2: Cross the tribulation (Precelestial, time-debt)
   209→
   210→**Reachable via:** Transcend path only. Requires reaching Tribulation Crossing (doc 03 Station 9) and crossing the tribulation (most die). If the protagonist succeeds and authors the law (Mahayana, doc 03 Station 10), they become a law-author in the Precelestial's Law Reaches (doc 24 §1.1).
   211→
   212→**What happens:** The protagonist, having authored the law that closes the bardo-harvest loophole, perceives Wang Xiaodi's anchor disperse lawfully — into the cosmos's law-substrate, going to its next rebirth. The protagonist recognizes, in the dispersal, the residue of the infant they practiced near. They weep — not from grief this time, but from recognition.
   213→
   214→Then the time-debt becomes absolute. The protagonist is in the Precelestial, where 1 day = 1 year below (doc 00 §1). The protagonist returns to the Mortal Stratum only briefly, every century or so, and finds it aged. The Great Yan Dynasty falls. A new dynasty rises. The Cangli Riverlands change. Wang Family Bend becomes a memory, then a name, then nothing. The protagonist, alone in the Precelestial, perceives the qi of a mortal world they no longer belong to. The game's final image: the protagonist, in the Law Reaches, perceiving the law they authored — the bardo-harvest loophole closed, the anchors of the Cangli Riverlands going to their lawful rebirths — and, in the law's texture, the faint residue of Wang Xiaodi's dispersal, permanent now, written into the world's law.
   215→
   216→### 7.3 Ending 3: Final death
   217→
   218→**Reachable via:** Confront path, if the protagonist loses. Accommodate path, via karmic tribulation. Transcend path, if the protagonist fails the Tribulation Crossing or fails the law-authorship (the law is contested and fails, costing the protagonist's anchor — doc 24 §1.1).
   219→
   220→**The specific scene:** The protagonist's anchor is dispersed (寂滅, jimie — doc 00 §2). The scene is the same in all three variants: the protagonist perceives their own anchor beginning to dissolve. The world does not end; the simulation continues. The companion who survives (Wang Meili, if she is present; Leng Qingxue, if she is) returns to Wang Family Bend and reports the death. A tablet is added to the lineage hall. The game's final image: a slow zoom out from the empty practice room — Old Chen's hermitage, or the Wang household, or the Cangwu Sect hall — the village continuing, the river flowing, the qi-residue of the protagonist's practice fading over the following days and weeks, then gone. The world goes on. The protagonist is not in it.
   221→
   222→### 7.4 Which is "true"
   223→
   224→**Ending 1 (found a lineage) is the true ending.**
   225→
   226→The game's thesis (doc 05 §4.3) is that cultivation is becoming less limited. The least limited ending is not the one where the protagonist leaves the world (Ending 2) or the one where the protagonist ends (Ending 3). It is the one where the protagonist becomes part of the world's continuation — perceiving the lineage as a flowing thing (the dead, the living, the yet-to-be-born, connected by the qi-residue of teaching and love), and adding their own perception to it.
   227→
   228→Ending 1 is also the most genre-subversive. The genre says ascension is the goal; this game says no — the goal is to stay, to teach, to be present. This is the doctrine's "confront the central tension directly" (AGENTS.md Part 3): we forbid power acceleration (the protagonist does not ascend), and we give the player something else (the continuation of the lineage through teaching).
   229→
   230→Ending 1 resolves the central conflict (the grief over Wang Xiaodi) by sublimating it: the protagonist cannot retrieve Wang Xiaodi's anchor (that is the antagonist's crime), but they can ensure the next Wang Xiaodi doesn't die of a fever while the cultivator is away. They do this by founding the lineage, training a healer-track cultivator (Wang Meili, and her students), and being present. The protagonist's grief becomes the lineage's first teaching.
   231→
   232→Endings 2 and 3 are valid, playable, and meaningful. They are not true. Ending 2 is the genre-conventional ending (ascension), which this game respects but does not endorse. Ending 3 is the tragic ending, which this game honors but does not celebrate. Ending 1 is the ending the game is built to deliver.

   252→## 9. The companion system
   253→
   254→**In scope. Three companions.**
   255→
   256→The doctrine (AGENTS.md Part 3) says "Design for joy first." Companions are joy: they are the people who witness the protagonist's long life alongside them, who age differently (faster or slower), who grieve with and against the protagonist, who can be lost. The genre's central tragic engine (time-debt) needs witnesses — without them, the protagonist's long life is solitary, and the grief is private. Companions make the grief shared, which makes it bearable, which makes the game playable.
   257→
   258→### 9.1 Wang Meili (王美麗) — the lineage bridge
   259→
   260→**Relation:** The protagonist's cousin (father's brother's daughter).
   261→**Realm at introduction (session ~50):** Qi Induction, ~22 years old.
   262→**Realm at endgame:** Foundation Establishment (if she survives), appearing ~30 at age ~120.
   263→**Arc:** Wang Meili is the protagonist's childhood playmate and the first person in their generation to perceive qi (after the protagonist). She does not study with Old Chen (her father, the protagonist's uncle, distrusts Old Chen); she studies at the Cangwu Sect, on the healer-track. She is the lineage bridge: she ages with the village (slower than mortals, faster than the protagonist), she keeps the protagonist tied to the family, she is the one who greets the protagonist on every return. In Act 2, she becomes a companion. In Act 3, she is the protagonist's first disciple in the "found a lineage" ending (§7.1). Her arc is the arc of the lineage itself: she is the next generation, and her students will be the one after.
   264→
   265→**Her death (if it occurs):** Wang Meili can die in Act 3 if the protagonist chooses the Accommodate path — Pei Liang, learning of the protagonist's attachment, harvests her anchor as leverage. This is the cruelest possible outcome and the strongest deterrent to the Accommodate path.
   266→
   267→### 9.2 Liang Zhu (梁柱) — the first peer
   268→
   269→**Relation:** None (rogue cultivator, met at Qinghe Market Town).
   270→**Realm at introduction (session ~250):** Foundation Establishment, ~80, appears ~40.
   271→**Realm at death (session ~800):** Foundation Establishment (he never breaks through to Core Formation), ~110, appears ~50.
   272→**Arc:** Liang Zhu is the protagonist's first peer and friend outside the sect and the village. He chose the rogue path over sect politics; he plays the *xiao* (簫, vertical bamboo flute) at taverns for spirit stones; he is irreverent, kind, and weary. He joins the protagonist's pursuit of Pei Liang in Act 2. In Act 3, he dies in Pei Liang's ambush — killed protecting the protagonist's retreat. His anchor disperses (final death). This is the second grief that mirrors the first (Wang Xiaodi's): the protagonist, again, was not enough to save someone they loved. The difference: this time, the protagonist was present. The grief is different — not the guilt of absence but the grief of presence. Liang Zhu's death is the engine of the protagonist's Nascent Soul breakthrough (the protagonist, in grief, integrates the loss and crosses the realm).
   273→
   274→**His death scene:** Liang Zhu, mortally wounded, plays one last note on the *xiao*. The note is a qi-residue trace — a technique he developed, leaving his signature in the qi of the place. The protagonist, perceiving it, recognizes it as Liang Zhu's teaching: a final lesson, in the form of a sound. The protagonist carries the residue-trace for the rest of the game. This is the Residual Error Sense's deepest use: perceiving the teaching of the dead.
   275→
   276→### 9.3 Leng Qingxue (冷清雪) — the sect dissident and romance
   277→
   278→**Relation:** Daughter of Leng Wushuang (Azure Sword Sect master). Romance option (§10).
   279→**Realm at introduction (session ~200):** Core Formation, ~120, appears ~30.
   280→**Realm at endgame:** Nascent Soul (if she survives), ~300, appears ~35.
   281→**Arc:** Leng Qingxue is sent by the Azure Sword Sect to investigate Pei Liang's raid on the Cangwu Sect (§3.3). She is the protagonist's first contact with the great-sect world. She is also the protagonist's political opposite: her mother wants the Cangli spirit vein; Leng Qingxue believes the expansion is wrong. Her arc is the arc of principle: she must choose, in Act 3, between her sect (her mother, her lineage, her duty) and her principles (the protagonist, the Cangli Riverlands, the law). She chooses principle — but the choice costs her the sect. In the "found a lineage" ending, she leaves the Azure Sword Sect and co-founds the Wang lineage school with the protagonist (not as a disciple — she is senior — but as a co-teacher). In the "cross the tribulation" ending, she stays in the mortal world and continues the protagonist's work. In the "final death" ending, she is the one who reports the protagonist's death to Wang Family Bend.
   282→
   283→### 9.4 Why three and not more
   284→
   285→Three companions is the threshold at which the protagonist's social world feels populated without becoming a party-based RPG. The genre (xianxia) is fundamentally solitary; the protagonist's long life is, in the end, alone. Three companions — one lineage (Wang Meili), one peer (Liang Zhu), one romance (Leng Qingxue) — is enough to witness the life without diluting the solitude. More would turn the game into a party-RPG; fewer would make the life uninhabitable. The precedent is Baldur's Gate 3's companion count (party of four, three companions) for the social-density threshold, and Disco Elysium's lone protagonist for the solitude ceiling. This game sits between: three companions, but the protagonist is always the point of view.

   289→## 10. Romance
   290→
   291→**In scope. One love interest. One courtship verb.**
   292→
   293→### 10.1 The love interest: Leng Qingxue (冷清雪)
   294→
   295→See §9.3. Leng Qingxue is the romance option. She is the protagonist's political opposite, the sect dissident, the Core Formation cultivator who chooses principle over lineage. The romance is not a reward; it is a relationship that develops over hundreds of sessions, through conflict and shared grief, and that becomes load-bearing in Act 3 (she is the one who gives the protagonist the Azure Sword Sect's intelligence on Pei Liang; she is the one who must choose between sect and protagonist).
   296→
   297→### 10.2 The courtship verb: the Exchange of Cuts (交劍, jiaojian)
   298→
   299→**The mechanic:** Two cultivators spar. The loser owes the winner a truthful answer to one question. The questions cannot be about combat; they must be about the self. Three bouts (three questions, three answers) = courtship acknowledged. Seven bouts = betrothal. Either party may decline a bout; declining three times ends the courtship.
   300→
   301→**The thematic load:** The Exchange of Cuts makes courtship a kind of cultivation. You learn the other's qi through their sword. You learn the other's self through their answers. The truth-debt mechanic enforces honesty — you cannot lie in the answer, because the answer is owed. The courtship is, in this way, the opposite of the antagonist's crime: Pei Liang harvests anchors without consent; the Exchange of Cuts exchanges truth with consent. The courtship verb and the antagonist's crime are the two poles of the game's moral spectrum.
   302→
   303→**The precedent:** The Exchange of Cuts is calibrated against Bushido Blade's (1997) one-cut dueling (one exchange resolves the bout; there is no health bar) and against the courtship-by-duel conventions of wuxia literature (Jin Yong's *The Return of the Condor Heroes* — the duel as love-language). It is not a "dating sim" mechanic; it is a combat-and-dialogue hybrid that uses the combat grammar (doc 13) and the dialogue system (doc 22) together. The truth-debt is enforced by the simulation: a lie in an answer registers as a karmic trace (doc 24 §2.1) and is perceivable, at Core Formation, by the wronged party.
   304→
   305→**The arc:** The first bout (session ~300, after the Cangli spirit vein dispute) is antagonistic — Leng Qingxue wins, asks the protagonist "Why did you join the Cangwu Sect?", the protagonist must answer truthfully. The second bout (session ~400) is competitive — the protagonist wins, asks "Why do you oppose your mother?". The third bout (session ~500, after Core Formation) is the courtship acknowledgment — they spar to a draw, exchange no questions, and sit in silence. The subsequent bouts (sessions ~700-1200) develop the relationship toward betrothal, culminating in the seventh bout before the final confrontation with Pei Liang. The seventh bout's question, asked by whichever party the player is not, is: "Will you come back?" The answer, whatever it is, is the truth.

   307→### 10.3 Why Leng Qingxue and not Wang Meili
   308→
   309→Wang Meili is the protagonist's cousin. Romance with her is out of scope (the Cangli Riverlands culture, per doc 00 §4, follows late-imperial Jiangnan exogamy norms; cousin marriage within the patrilineal clan is forbidden). More importantly, Wang Meili's narrative function is the lineage bridge (§9.1), not the romance. Leng Qingxue's function is the political and personal opposite who becomes the partner. The two functions are distinct; conflating them would weaken both.
