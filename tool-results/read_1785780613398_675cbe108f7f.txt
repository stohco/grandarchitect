Sect master **Patriarch Iron-Fang (鐵牙上人)**, Core Formation, age 610, Matriarch Ironbone's son and the Confederacy's hereditary War-Chair.    102→- **The Stone Lantern Sect (石燈宗)** — Xi's frontier sect, on the continent's eastern coast, guarding the approaches to the Western Spirit Wilds' Law Reaches (doc 36 §9.2: Xi's Spirit Wilds are where the most Law Reaches are found). Sect master **Patriarch Stone-Lantern (石燈上人)**, Core Formation, age 540. Specialty: formation arrays (the sect's name comes from the spirit-stone lanterns it maintains along the Western Ocean's coast, marking the safe harbors and warning of Qi-storms). The Stone Lantern Sect's relationship to the Skyfire Holy Land is independent — the Stone Lantern is the only Xi great sect that does not tithe to the Skyfire, on the grounds that its mission (frontier defense) is a Courts-delegated responsibility. The Reaches are not on the Mortal Stratum's surface — they are in the Precelestial Stratum above Xi — but their resonance is felt in the mortal continent's metal-phase qi (the sharp, brittle quality is the Reaches' resonance bleeding down). The protagonist at Core Formation can perceive the Reach-resonance; at Spirit Severance, the protagonist could enter a Reach (the Western Spirit Wilds' maintained roads begin at the Stone Lantern Sect's anchor-shrine). This is the late-game's spatial structure: the protagonist's path from the Cangli Riverlands to a Law Reach runs through Zhong's western interior, across the Western Ocean, across Xi, and up into the Precelestial above Xi.

   110→## 4. Nan Continent (南洲) — the southern continent
   111→
   112→### 4.1 Geography and qi-climate
   113→
   114→Nan Continent (南洲) is tropical, archipelagic, monsoon-rotated (doc 36 §2.5, §9.2: fire-phase, intensity, conflict, transformation; qi is wood-dominant and water-dominant). The continent's coast is dense with mortal settlement; the interior is dense primary jungle, broken by the central highlands' dry grasslands (the only place on Nan where ruins preserve well, due to the dry air). The spirit veins run shallow and wood-dominant along the coasts; the central highlands' deep veins are earth-wood dual-phase. The continent is the Mortal Stratum's most yao-dense region — the Pillar Dynasty's yao-civilization flourished here (doc 37 §6.1), and the yao who survived the dynasty's fall dispersed into the jungle interior, where their descendants still live.
   115→
   116→### 4.2 The Monsoon Cities and the jungle interior
   117→
   118→Nan has no single empire. The coastal civilization is the **Monsoon Cities (季風諸城)**, a network of seventeen trading city-states linked by the monsoon-cycle sail routes. The Cities' loose confederation is governed by the **Monsoon Council (季風議會)**, which meets annually at the city of **Nanhai (南海)**, the largest port. The Council's chair rotates among the Cities' merchant-princes. The jungle interior is governed by no mortal authority; it is the yao-territory of the **Pillar Descendants (柱裔)**, the scattered yao-civilizations descended from the Pillar Dynasty's dispersed population. The Pillar Descendants are not a single polity; they are an archipelago of yao-villages, yao-groves, and the occasional yao-city, each governed by a yao-elder (typically a Foundation Establishment yao of a tree- or stone-species). The Monsoon Cities and the Pillar Descendants trade at the city of **Linwang (林王)**, on the jungle's northern edge — the only place on Nan where yao and humans meet regularly as peers.

   120→### 4.3 The Verdant Pillar Holy Land (青柱聖地) — preview
   121→
   122→Nan's — and the cultivation world's only yao-co-founded — holy land (full spec in doc 43). Patriarch: **Daoist Pillar Canopy (柱冠真人)**, Nascent Soul, age 1,700, a human cultivator who co-founded the holy land with the tree-yao **Old Pillar (老柱)**, a Foundation Establishment yao (the youngest holy land patriarch's partner is the cultivation world's oldest non-Mahayana yao, at ~3,200 years). The Verdant Pillar's compound is the **Verdant Pillar Grotto-Heaven (青柱洞天)**, anchored on the central highlands' great earth-wood vein, whose interior contains the surviving Pillar Cities' ruins and bark-strip libraries. Specialty: yao cultivation, wood-phase beast-taming, Pillar Dynasty lore. The Verdant Pillar is the only holy land independent of the Courts of Heaven (its yao-foundation places it outside the Courts' lineage-jurisdiction; the Courts respect this and the Verdant Pillar, in turn, does not petition the Courts). The Verdant Pillar's relationship to the Jade Void Holy Land is warm — the Jade Void holds 7 of the 12 volumes of the Pillar Dynasty Annals, and the Verdant Pillar holds the other 5; the two holy lands maintain a 1,500-year scholarly correspondence.
   123→
   124→### 4.4 The Pillar Dynasty's ruins
   125→
   126→The Pillar Cities (柱城群, doc 37 §6.1) are the ruins of the yao-civilization that flourished 22,000-30,000 years ago on Nan's central highlands. The Cities are stone — fused by the yao's wood-phase qi, grown like wood in forms they designed — partially overgrown, partially intact, with bark-strip libraries preserved by the highlands' dry air. The Azure Sword Sect's intelligence hall has a file on a Pillar-City ruin 300 li from the sect's territory, in the western Cangwu range's deep extension (doc 37 §6.1) — this is a transplanted ruin, established by Pillar Dynasty yao-explorers who reached the Cangwu foothills (the Cangwu granitic arc is the easternmost extension of the same fault system as the Nan central highlands, doc 37 §9.2). The Verdant Pillar Holy Land controls access to the seven intact Cities on Nan; the bark-strip libraries within hold the Pillar Dynasty's qi-economy records, wood-phase cultivation manuals, and the Annals that the Jade Void and the Verdant Pillar hold in divided custody.

The Sea-Fan Sect's relationship to the Pearl-Tide Sect (Zhong) is the southern maritime trade route's protective alliance — the two sects' patrol routes meet at the southern edge of the Eastern Sea. The Sea-Fan's relationship to the Whale-Rider Sect (Dong) is cooperative; the two share deep-water spirit-herb beds.

Sect master **Patriarch Redwood (紅冠上人)**, Core Formation, age 530, a half-yao cultivator (his mother was a Pillar Descendant tree-yao, his father a Monsoon City merchant). Specialty: wood-phase cultivation, yao-human diplomacy, the Linwang trade-fair's neutral ground enforcement. The Redwood Canopy's relationship to the Verdant Pillar Holy Land is the closest of any great sect to any holy land — the Redwood Canopy's outer disciples train in the Verdant Pillar's grotto-heaven, and the Verdant Pillar's elders consider the Redwood Canopy their preferred successor-institution. The Redwood Canopy's relationship to the Sea-Fan Sect is the Monsoon Council's internal balance: the two sects jointly enforce the Linwang trade-fair's neutrality.

   135→## 5. Bei Continent (北洲) — the northern continent
   136→
   137→### 5.1 Geography and qi-climate
   138→
   139→Bei Continent (北洲) is cold, subarctic, and mostly uninhabitable by wet-rice agriculture (doc 36 §2.5, §9.2: water-phase, stillness, storage, depth; qi is water-dominant and yin-dominant). The continent's coast is taiga; the interior is tundra and steppe, broken by glacial mountain ranges. The spirit veins run cold and deep; the qi is dense but slow (the yin-dominant climate makes cultivation harder for fire-phase cultivators and easier for water-phase and yin-phase). The continent's mortal population is nomadic and pastoral — reindeer-herders, horse-riders, mammoth-bone-dwellers — and culturally distinct from the wet-rice civilizations of the other continents. The northern ancestral courts (the Precelestial's oldest, per doc 36 §9.2) are above Bei; their resonance is felt in the continent's deep cold.

   141→### 5.2 The Black-Ice Khanate (黑冰汗國)
   142→
   143→Bei's largest political structure is the Black-Ice Khanate, a nomadic-pastoral confederation that has unified the continent's central steppe for 600 years under the Black-Ice Borjigin clan. The reigning Khan is **Khan Tengri-Iron (天鐵汗)**, age 90, a Foundation Establishment cultivator (the Khans' cultivation is water-phase, mounted, and the Khanate's elite cavalry are all Qi Condensation). The Khanate's capital is **the Ordu (斡魯朵)**, a movable camp of 30,000 tents that follows the seasonal pastures; in winter, the Ordu winters at the city of **Beicheng (北城)**, the Khanate's only permanent settlement, on the continent's southern coast. The Khanate's relationship to the Great Yan Dynasty is the long-running northern border war (the Iron-Horse Sect's century-long patrol, §1.7); the two have fought eleven wars in 247 years, none decisive. The Khanate's relationship to Bei's holy land (the NorthSea) is the Khanate's foundation myth: the first Khan was a NorthSea Holy Land outer disciple who left to unite the steppe.
   144→
   145→### 5.3 The NorthSea Holy Land (北海聖地) — preview
   146→
   147→Bei's — and the cultivation world's oldest-patriarch — holy land (full spec in doc 43). Patriarch: **Matriarch NorthSea (北海道君)**, Nascent Soul, age 3,000, the cultivation world's oldest non-Mahayana being (after the pantheon itself). The NorthSea's compound is the **NorthSea Grotto-Heaven (北海洞天)**, anchored at the glacial peak of the continent's central range, on a water-yin vein so cold that the grotto's interior is a permanent ice-field. Specialty: water-phase ice-cultivation, ancestral-court preservation (the NorthSea holds the oldest accessible ancestral-court records on the Mortal Stratum; the Precelestial's ancestral courts are older but harder to reach). The NorthSea is independent of the Courts of Heaven — its ancestral-court records predate the Courts' founding (Year ~40,000), and the NorthSea considers itself peer-to-the-Courts in matters of ancestral record. The Courts tolerate this (the NorthSea does not challenge the Courts' lineage-jurisdiction; it merely maintains its own older records). The NorthSea's relationship to the Black-Ice Khanate is the holy land's only institutional commitment on Bei: the NorthSea trains the Khanate's Foundation Establishment Khans and considers the Khanate's unity the continent's stabilizing force.

The Frostfang Yao's elder-council meets every century at the glacial peak adjacent to the NorthSea Holy Land's anchor; the council's chair is **Old Frost-Fang (老霜牙)**, a mammoth-yao at Foundation Establishment, age 1,400. The Frostfang Yao's relationship to the Khanate is a 600-year cold peace: the Khanate's herders do not enter the deep tundra, the Frostfang Yao do not raid the Khanate's herds. The Frostfang Yao's relationship to the NorthSea Holy Land is the continent's deepest alliance — the yao-council and the holy land's elders consult on all matters concerning the continent's spirit-vein ecology.

The Snowstrider's relationship to the Iron-Horse Sect (Zhong) is the long border war — the two sects' outer disciples skirmish along the frontier every spring. The Snowstrider's relationship to the NorthSea Holy Land is a near-vassalage (the Snowstrider's sect master is always a NorthSea-trained cultivator).

   160→## 6. The Eastern Sea (東海)
   161→
   162→### 6.1 The Pearl Archipelago (珠島群)
   163→
   164→The Eastern Sea, between Zhong and Dong, is broken at its midpoint by the Pearl Archipelago — a chain of 340 cultivator-inhabited islands, the largest of which (**Great Pearl Island, 大珠島**) is 80 li across. The Archipelago is governed by the **Pearl Archipelago Compact (珠島約)**, a 1,400-year-old treaty between the Pearl-Tide Sect (Zhong), the Whale-Rider Sect (Dong), and the Crystal-Tide Sect (the undersea sect, §6.3). The Compact designates the Archipelago as neutral ground; the three sects jointly maintain the spirit-beacon lighthouses that mark the Vermilion Pearl Route (§6.4) through the Archipelago's channels. Great Pearl Island's harbor town, **Zhubu (珠埠)**, is the route's mid-way port and the cultivation world's largest neutral market (the only place where cultivators from all five continents regularly meet). The protagonist, at Foundation Establishment, could travel to Zhubu by sea (a 40-day voyage from Haimen); at Core Formation, by flight (a 6-hour flight, island-hopping).
   165→
   166→### 6.2 Sea-beasts and the abyssal qi
   167→
   168→The Eastern Sea's deep waters are the habitat of the sea-beasts — leviathans, qi-turtles, abyssal cultivators, and the Whale-Rider Sect's cultivated qi-whales. The sea-beast tiers follow doc 14's spirit-beast ecology; the leviathans (the largest class, low- to mid-tier) are the route's main hazard, and the Vermilion Pearl Route's patrol cultivators kill or drive off perhaps twenty leviathans per year. The abyssal cultivators are a separate category — deep-water yao (octopus-yao, eel-yao, the rare abyssal-serpent-yao) who govern the deep waters below the route's patrol depth. The abyssal yao do not threaten surface shipping; they are, however, the Crystal-Tide Sect's primary diplomatic partners, and the Crystal-Tide's sect master is the only surface cultivator who meets with the abyssal yao's elder-council.
   169→
   170→### 6.3 The Crystal-Tide Sect (晶潮宗)
   171→
   172→The Eastern Sea's only undersea great sect, headquartered at the **Crystal Palace (晶宮)**, a Foundation-Establishment-built compound at 600-li depth on the Eastern Sea's floor, anchored at the Crystal-Tide Grotto-Heaven (晶潮洞天, a water-phase pocket-world). Sect master **Patriarch Crystal-Tide (晶潮上人)**, Core Formation late-stage, age 850, is a water-phase cultivator who has not surfaced in three centuries. Specialty: water-phase deep-cultivation, abyssal yao-diplomacy, deep-water spirit-herb gathering (the sect's divers harvest the abyssal herb-beds that no surface cultivator can reach). The Crystal-Tide's relationship to the Pearl-Tide Sect is tense — the two contest the Eastern Sea's spirit-herb beds. The Crystal-Tide's relationship to the Whale-Rider Sect is cooperative — the Whale-Rider's qi-whales and the Crystal-Tide's divers share the deep-water ecology. The Crystal-Tide's relationship to the Pearl Archipelago Compact is the Compact's most fragile clause: the Crystal-Tide maintains the Archipelago's undersea defenses but is not allowed to surface-claim any of the islands.
   173→
   174→The Eastern Sea's abyssal layer — the waters below 1,000-li depth, where the light fails and the qi grows cold and dense — is the domain of the **Abyssal Yao Council (深妖議會)**, a confederation of octopus-yao, eel-yao, and the rare abyssal-serpent-yao. The Council's chair is **Old Many-Arm (老多腕)**, an octopus-yao at Core Formation, age 1,100, who has met with the Crystal-Tide's patriarch nine times in three centuries. The Council's relationship to surface civilization is purely through the Crystal-Tide; the abyssal yao do not surface, do not trade, and do not threaten surface shipping. Their concern is the abyssal herb-beds' ecology, which the Crystal-Tide's divers harvest under a 700-year quota-treaty that the Council renews every century. The protagonist at Core Formation could, in principle, visit the Crystal Palace; the visit is optional content and requires the Crystal-Tide's permission.
   175→
   176→### 6.4 The Vermilion Pearl Route (赤珠航路)
   177→
   178→The cultivator shipping lane between Zhong and Dong, the Eastern Sea's most important travel-route. The Route runs from Haimen (Zhong's eastern port) east through the Pearl Archipelago's channels, then east across the open Eastern Sea to Qingyang (Dong's western port). Total length: ~30,000 li. Travel time: 60 days by mortal sail (the traditional merchant fleet, ~20 ships per year), 30 days by Qi-Condensation-piloted spirit-junk, 8 days by Foundation-Establishment flight with island rest-stops, 1 day by Core Formation flight. The Route is marked by 47 spirit-beacon lighthouses (maintained by the Pearl-Tide, Whale-Rider, and Crystal-Tide sects jointly), warded against sea-beasts by a chain of qi-barrier anchors, and patrolled by Core Formation cultivators from the three Compact sects. The Route is the protagonist's most likely Act 3 cross-continental travel-path — the journey from the Cangli Riverlands to the Jade Void Holy Land on Dong runs through Haimen, along the Vermilion Pearl Route, to Qingyang, then overland to Voidjade Peak.

   182→## 7. Travel between continents
   183→
   184→### 7.1 The travel tiers
   185→
   186→A Core Formation cultivator crosses the Central-to-Eastern-Continent gap (~30,000 li) in one day of sustained flight (doc 36 §6.3, §10.5). A Foundation Establishment cultivator cannot cross the open ocean in a single flight (qi-reservoir depletion after a few hours); they must island-hop, resting at the Pearl Archipelago's spirit-beacon stations, and the journey takes 8-12 days. A Qi Condensation cultivator cannot cross the open ocean by footwork (light footwork does not work on water); they must travel by spirit-junk, a 30-day voyage. A mortal travels by sail, 60 days, with perhaps one ship in forty lost to sea-beasts or qi-storms. A Nascent Soul cultivator crosses in an hour; a Spirit Severance cultivator, in minutes; a Void Amalgamation cultivator, instantaneously, if both endpoints are bonded. The protagonist's first cross-continental journey (Cangli to Jade Void, session ~1,000-1,100, at Nascent Soul) is therefore a single hour of sustained flight, but the protagonist will likely travel by spirit-junk (the Jade Void's invitation is for a Foundation-Establishment-quality journey, not a Nascent-Soul-quick one — the Jade Void's elders want the protagonist to perceive the Eastern Sea, the Pearl Archipelago, and Dong's coast as a traveler, not a projectile).
   187→
   188→### 7.2 The Vermilion Pearl Route in detail
   189→
   190→The Route's three sections:
   191→- **The Haimen Reach (海門段)** — from Haimen east to the Pearl Archipelago's western anchor (Great Pearl Island's harbor at Zhubu). 12,000 li. Patrolled by the Pearl-Tide Sect. Eight spirit-beacon lighthouses.
   192→- **The Archipelago Crossing (珠島段)** — through the Archipelago's channels, from Zhubu east to the Archipelago's eastern anchor (the island of **Little Pearl, 小珠島**). 4,000 li. Patrolled jointly by all three Compact sects. Twelve spirit-beacon lighthouses. The Archipelago Crossing is the Route's safest section; the channels are narrow, the islands are inhabited, and the sea-beasts avoid the lighthouse network.
   193→- **The Qingyang Reach (青陽段)** — from Little Pearl east across the open Eastern Sea to Qingyang. 14,000 li. Patrolled by the Whale-Rider Sect. Twenty-seven spirit-beacon lighthouses. The Qingyang Reach is the Route's most dangerous section — the open water is leviathan-territory, and the Whale-Rider's patrol cultivators kill or drive off perhaps fifteen leviathans per year on this reach alone.
   194→
   195→### 7.3 The dangers
   196→
   197→The Eastern Sea's hazards, in descending order of lethality:
   198→- **Qi-storms (氣暴)** — sudden concentrations of phase-conflicted qi that can flip a spirit-junk, disorient a flying cultivator, or, in the worst cases, tear the qi-reservoir out of a Foundation Establishment cultivator's meridians. Qi-storms are most common in the Qingyang Reach's open water. The lighthouse network's beacons include qi-storm warnings; a Foundation Establishment cultivator who heeds the warnings can wait out a storm at a lighthouse-station in 2-3 days.
   199→- **Leviathans (海獸)** — the sea-beast ecology's largest tier, low- to mid-tier beasts 30-300 li long. Leviathans are not yao (they lack anchors); they are spirit beasts of the deep. They are territorial and predatory; a Core Formation cultivator can drive one off, a Foundation Establishment cultivator can evade one, a Qi Condensation cultivator on a spirit-junk is in danger. The patrol cultivators kill the most aggressive leviathans; the rest are warded by the lighthouse network's qi-barriers.
   200→- **The Outer Margin's dread (外涯之懼)** — beyond the last charted island, the Eastern Sea's qi begins to thin and the compass-needle wanders (doc 36 §1.4, §2.5). A Core Formation cultivator who flies too far east (past Dong Continent, into the uncharted ocean) perceives the sky lowering and the air stilling. The dread is the perception of the Outer Margin approaching. Cultivators who do not turn back are not recorded. The Vermilion Pearl Route does not approach the Margin; the easternmost lighthouse (Qingyang Reach's last beacon) is 2,000 li west of Dong's coast, and Dong's coast is 12,000 li west of the Mortal Stratum's eastern shore. The Margin is far past that.
   201→
   202→### 7.4 The cultivator shipping lane
   203→
   204→The Vermilion Pearl Route is the cultivation world's most-traveled inter-continental lane, but it is not the only one. The four other inter-continental routes:
   205→- **The Western Ocean Crossing (西海航路)** — Yumen (Zhong) to the Stone Lantern Sect's coast (Xi), 25,000 li of open water, three ships per decade, no Pearl-Archipelago equivalent. Foundation-Establishment cultivators can cross in 18 days of sustained flight, resting on the rare spirit-beacon buoys the Stone Lantern Sect maintains. The Crossing is the Sand-Veiled Sect's western anchor and the Stone Lantern Sect's eastern anchor.
   206→- **The Southern Monsoon Run (南季風航路)** — Haimen (Zhong) to Nanhai (Nan), 18,000 li of monsoon-driven open water, two monsoon-windows per year (the spring north-east monsoon and the autumn south-west monsoon). The Run is the Sea-Fan Sect's northern anchor and the Pearl-Tide Sect's southern anchor; the two sects' patrols meet at the Run's mid-point shrine.
   207→- **The Northern Ice Lane (北冰航路)** — Beiguan (Zhong) to Beicheng (Bei), 14,000 li of subarctic ocean, ice-choked for eight months of the year. The Iron-Horse Sect and the Snowstrider Sect jointly maintain the Lane's ice-breaker spirit-junks. The Lane is the only inter-continental route where Qi-Condensation cultivators are routinely required to fly ahead and break ice for the merchant fleet.
   208→- **The Xi-Nan Sandalwood Run (西南香航路)** — Xi's western coast to Nan's western coast, 12,000 li of open tropical water. Two ships per year, carrying Xi's worked metal for Nan's spirit herbs. The Stone Lantern and Sea-Fan sects maintain the Run's beacons.
   209→
   210→No cultivator shipping lane crosses the inter-hemispheric open ocean between Nan and Bei or between Dong and Bei — those crossings exceed Core Formation flight range without island-hop rest, and no mortal fleet has attempted them. The five continents are, in practice, four trade-clusters: Zhong-Dong (the Vermilion Pearl Route, dense), Zhong-Xi (the Western Ocean Crossing, sparse), Zhong-Nan (the Southern Monsoon Run, seasonal), Zhong-Bei (the Northern Ice Lane, ice-locked), Xi-Nan (the Sandalwood Run, minimal). Zhong is the trade-hub of every cluster. This is the cosmological center's economic expression (doc 36 §9.1).

   215→
   216→### 8.1 Location on Dong Continent
   217→

   220→### 8.2 The patriarch: Daoist Jade Void (玉虛真人)
   221→
   222→Daoist Jade Void (玉虛真人), Nascent Soul, age 2,100, appears 60. The founder of the holy land; retired patriarch (the holy land's current acting head is **Elder Mu Yunfeng (穆雲峰)**, Nascent Soul early-stage, age 800, the founder's 17th-generation successor). Daoist Jade Void is the cultivation world's oldest non-Mahayana being-by-choice — he has declined Tribulation Crossing three times, on the grounds that his law (the holy land's ancestral-veneration practice) is more stable at Nascent Soul than it would be at Mahayana, and that his continuing presence sustains the holy land's rotating-shrine rite in the Cangli Riverlands. His personality: patient, withdrawn, grieving (he has outlived nineteen successor-generations of disciples; his grief is the grief of a patriarch who has become, in his own perception, more institution than person). His specialty: divination (the holy land's foundational practice, derived from the Silent Hexagram School's fragment, doc 37 §6.3) and the ancestral-veneration rite that maintains his bond to his Cangli birthplace across 2,100 years. His relationship to the Azure Sword Sect is suzerain-to-vassal (the sect pays him nominal homage; he extends the sect his implicit protection). His relationship to Daoist Yellow Court (the Yellow Court Holy Land's patriarch, his nearest peer) is a 2,000-year polite rivalry.
   223→
   224→### 8.3 The compound and the peak grotto-heaven
   225→
   226→The Jade Void's compound is the **Three-Courtyard Seat (三院)**: the **Outer Courtyard (外院)** for outer disciples and visiting cultivators (1,800 of the 3,000 disciples are outer; the Outer Courtyard's dormitories and lecture halls can house 600 visitors); the **Inner Courtyard (內院)** for inner disciples and elders (1,200 inner disciples, 47 Foundation Establishment elders, 3 Nascent Soul elders including the acting head); the **Founder's Courtyard (祖院)**, a small compound at the peak's summit where Daoist Jade Void resides in near-solitude, attended by three senior inner disciples. The compound's architecture is the Yunwu range's native grey granite, with wood-phase qi-trellises covering every courtyard (the trellises are 1,500 years old, planted by the founder's first generation of disciples; their wood-phase qi sustains the compound's ambient qi-climate). The peak grotto-heaven, the **Jade Void Grotto-Heaven (玉虛洞天)**, is anchored at the Founder's Courtyard's central shrine; its interior is a wood-phase mountain-garden (time-rate 1:5; one day inside = five days outside), where the holy land's inner disciples cultivate and where Daoist Jade Void spends most of his time. The grotto's interior contains the holy land's library (the next subsection).
   227→
   228→### 8.4 The library and its holdings
   229→
   230→The Jade Void's library is the cultivation world's most comprehensive non-Courts repository. Notable holdings:
   231→- **The Pillar Dynasty Annals (柱朝紀)** — 7 of 12 volumes (the Verdant Pillar Holy Land holds the other 5). A Nascent-Soul-tier text; access requires the acting head's permission.
   232→- **The Crimson Vow Code (赤誓律)** — ~60% of the original alliance's legal code, in fragmentary form. The most complete extant copy; access requires Daoist Jade Void's personal permission.
   233→- **The Silent Hexagram Method (默卦法)** — a Foundation-Establishment-tier fragment of the Silent Hexagram School's divination tradition; the holy land's foundational practice is derived from this fragment.
   234→- **The Precelestial Institutions Compendium (先天諸司匯考)** — a Core-Formation-tier text on the Courts of Heaven (the same text the Azure Sword Sect's Scripture Hall holds a copy of, doc 37 §8.3). The Jade Void's copy is the original; the Azure Sword's is a 600-year-old transcription.
   235→- **The Eastern Sea Atlas (東海圖志)** — a Foundation-Establishment-tier atlas of the Eastern Sea, the Pearl Archipelago, and the Vermilion Pearl Route, with bathymetric qi-charts and sea-beast territorial maps. The Pearl-Tide and Whale-Rider sects hold copies; the Jade Void's is the master.
   236→
   237→### 8.5 The rotating shrine in the Cangli Riverlands
   238→
   239→The Jade Void maintains a small shrine (staffed by two Foundation Establishment elders on rotating six-year assignments) at the site of the founder's birth village, somewhere in the central Cangwu foothills within 30 li of Wang Family Bend (doc 31 §2.9). The shrine's exact location has been lost — the founder's birth village was abandoned in a flood 1,400 years ago and the site has shifted. The rotating elders search for the shrine's true location quietly, on their assignments, and have not found it. The current rotating elders are **Elder Xu Yunfeng (許雲峰)**, Core Formation, age 200 (the same Xu Yunfeng who corresponds with Leng Qingxue, doc 34 §166), and **Elder Lin Qingzhou (林青舟)**, Foundation Establishment, age 150. The shrine's current nominal seat is a small hermitage 12 li west of Wang Family Bend, maintained as a "search-base" by the two elders. The protagonist, at Foundation Establishment (session ~120-150), may meet Xu Yunfeng at the hermitage; at Core Formation, may be invited to assist in the search; at Nascent Soul, may meet Daoist Jade Void in person at Voidjade Peak and learn the founder's full story (including, potentially, a connection between the founder's mortal lineage and the Wang lineage, if the investigation arc is pursued).
   240→
   241→The search is not archaeological. The Jade Void's intelligence hall has, over 1,400 years, narrowed the birth-village's site to a 30-li by 30-li region of the central Cangwu foothills; the rotating elders' task is to walk the region in slow loops, perceiving the qi-residue of a 2,100-year-old mortal habitation. The residue is faint (mortal habitation leaves little qi-trace); the search requires a Core Formation cultivator's anchor-perception, sustained for years. The protagonist's Residual Error Sense (doc 00 §5), which is unusually sharp for the protagonist's realm, is the search's best asset; this is why Xu Yunfeng is in correspondence with Leng Qingxue (to recruit the protagonist, when the protagonist reaches Core Formation, as the search's most perceptive participant).
   242→
   243→### 8.6 Relationship to the Azure Sword Sect (suzerain)
   244→
   245→The Azure Sword Sect is the Jade Void's formal vassal (one mid-grade spirit stone per decade; the Jade Void's implicit protection in return, per doc 31 §2.9). The arrangement is unwritten and fragile. The Azure Sword's expansionist faction (Leng Wushuang's, doc 31 §2.5) risks the Jade Void's goodwill; the Azure Sword's Isolationist faction (Elder Jing You's) considers the Jade Void's goodwill the sect's long-term strategic anchor. The Jade Void's rotating elders, in the Cangli foothills, observe the Azure Sword's expansion and report to Voidjade Peak; the Jade Void has not yet intervened, but the founder's birthplace-claim to the region gives the holy land a standing the Azure Sword cannot ignore. The protagonist's intervention in the Cangli spirit vein dispute (session ~300, doc 26 §4.2) — specifically, the option to bring in the Jade Void as a third party — is the Act 2 political verb that activates this relationship.
   246→
   247→### 8.7 Relationship to the Courts of Heaven (independent but deferential)
   248→
   249→The Jade Void is, like all five holy lands, below Tribulation Crossing (doc 38 §... : "the holy lands' patriarchs are below Tribulation Crossing; they cannot enter the courts as peers"). The Jade Void's relationship to the Courts is therefore mediated: the holy land petitions the Courts through the Courts' Acquired-stratum intermediaries (the Records department's regional magistrates, doc 38 §2.1). The Jade Void's petitions are mostly concerned with the holy land's ancestral-veneration rite (the founder's birthplace-claim is technically a Courts-registered ancestral-veneration, with the founder's anchor in bardo as the rite's subject). The Jade Void's relationship to Scribe Yan (the corrupted Records official, doc 26 §2.3) is, ironically, the same as every other holy land's — the Jade Void's petitions pass through Scribe Yan's office. The Jade Void does not know of Scribe Yan's corruption (the corruption is below the holy land's notice; the holy land's petitions are routine and Scribe Yan has not interfered with them).
   250→
   251→### 8.8 Relationship to the other holy lands
   252→
   253→- **The Yellow Court Holy Land (Zhong)** — a 2,000-year polite rivalry. The two holy lands contest influence over Zhong's middle reaches (the Yellow Court considers the Cangli Riverlands within its natural sphere; the Jade Void's ancestral-veneration claim contests this). The two patriarchs correspond once per century. The rivalry is non-violent; both holy lands are below the threshold where direct conflict would be productive.
   254→- **The Skyfire Holy Land (Xi)** — distant and cool. The Jade Void's wood-phase tradition and the Skyfire's metal-phase tradition are metaphysically opposed (metal cuts wood). The two holy lands have no direct conflict and no direct alliance.
   255→- **The Verdant Pillar Holy Land (Nan)** — warm scholarly collaboration. The two holy lands hold divided custody of the Pillar Dynasty Annals and have maintained a 1,500-year correspondence on the Annals' interpretation.
   256→- **The NorthSea Holy Land (Bei)** — respectful distance. The NorthSea's patriarch is older than the Jade Void's by 900 years; the Jade Void defers to the NorthSea on ancestral-court matters, and the NorthSea defers to the Jade Void on divination matters.
   257→
   258→### 8.9 Act 2-3 narrative role
   259→
   260→Act 2 (Foundation Establishment → Core Formation): The protagonist encounters the Jade Void indirectly — through Xu Yunfeng's correspondence with Leng Qingxue (the regional-compact proposal, doc 34 §166), through the rotating-shrine hermitage 12 li west of Wang Family Bend (a Foundation-Establishment-quality encounter), and through the Azure Sword Sect's Isolationist faction's arguments (which cite the Jade Void's goodwill). The protagonist does not visit Dong Continent in Act 2.
   261→
   262→Act 3 (Nascent Soul → Mahayana): The protagonist, at Nascent Soul, accepts (or seeks) an invitation to Voidjade Peak. The journey is the protagonist's first cross-continental travel (the Vermilion Pearl Route, by spirit-junk, 30 days, session ~1,000-1,030). At Voidjade Peak, the protagonist meets Daoist Jade Void, accesses the library (the Pillar Dynasty Annals, the Crimson Vow Code fragments, the Silent Hexagram Method), and learns the founder's Cangli birth-origin and the connection between the Cangli spirit vein and the Pillar Root Vein (doc 37 §9.2). The protagonist may, if the investigation arc is pursued, discover the founder's mortal lineage and its connection (if any) to the Wang lineage. The Jade Void's intelligence hall briefs the protagonist on the Crimson Vow Remnant (the connection to the Zhou Wugui incursion, doc 37 §9.4). The Jade Void becomes the protagonist's primary Act 3 institutional ally — the holy land's library, intelligence network, and Daoist Jade Void's personal counsel are the protagonist's resources for the Pei Liang confrontation and (in the Transcend path) the Mahayana law-authorship.

   299→## 11. What this document does not decide
   300→
   301→- **The specific geography of each continent beyond the named sites.** The Yueling Dynasty's provinces, the Ironbone Confederacy's forty-three citadels, the Monsoon Cities' seventeen members, and the Black-Ice Khanate's tribal structure are procedural content, generated against the seed and the continent-specific geography generators (future plugins).
   302→- **The protagonist's specific cross-continental encounters.** This document specifies which institutions are where; it does not specify which the protagonist will visit, in what order, or with what consequence. The Act 3 narrative arc (doc 26 §4.3) commits only to the Jade Void visit; the other holy-land visits are optional content.
   303→- **The full specifications of the four other holy lands.** The Yellow Court, Skyfire, Verdant Pillar, and NorthSea holy lands are previewed here; their grotto-heavens, sect masters, spirit veins, court-relationships, and narrative roles are specified in doc 43.
   304→- **The full roster of great sects per continent.** This document names 13 great sects across the five continents; doc 20 §1.3 specifies 5-10 per region, implying 25-50 great sects across the five continents. The 13 named are the politically-central ones; the rest are procedural content.
   305→- **The Pearl Archipelago's 340 islands.** Great Pearl Island and Little Pearl Island are named; the other 338 are procedural content. The Compact's three signatory sects are committed; the islands' individual inhabitants are not.
   306→- **The protagonist's specific Mahayana endgame geography.** The Transcend path's Law Reach venue (doc 26 §4.3, doc 37 §6.3) is in the deep Precelestial above Xi Continent (the Western Spirit Wilds are the most Reach-rich region, doc 36 §9.2). The specific Reach the protagonist authors their law in is endgame content, not geographic commitment.
   307→
   308→These are deferred details, not deferred forks. The decisions this document makes — the five continents' populations, the Eastern Sea's geography and travel-grammar, the Jade Void Holy Land's full specification, the four other holy lands' previews, the 13 great sects' specifications, and the political map of the cultivation world's upper tier — are committed. If they prove wrong in prototype, the prototype's evidence overrides this document, and this document is revised — not appended with a candidate alternative.
