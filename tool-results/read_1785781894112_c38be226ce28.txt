   100→### 2.1 Precelestial-Acquired trade
   101→
   102→**Does trade happen?** Yes, but restricted. The courts' Roads department (per doc 38 §2.4) manages stratum-crossing travel; every stratum-crossing transit requires a Roads department permit. The permits are issued for: official courts business (Ministers' travel, Foreign Affairs yao-court negotiations, Defense border-garrison rotations), lineage-rite travel (the ancestral court retrieval rite's transit, the lineage's senior cultivators' consultations), and (rarely) commercial transit.
   103→
   104→**Commercial transit.** Commercial stratum-crossing transit is restricted to a small number of licensed carriers (twelve, by the Roads department's current roster). The carriers are mostly lineage-affiliated (the Yan clan operates two; the Builder's Lineage operates three; the Garden Lineage operates one; the remaining six are independent, licensed by the Roads department under the Rogue Outreach Bloc's "incorporation rather than marginalization" program, per doc 38 §3.4). The carriers transport: cosmic-tier herbs from the peak grotto-heavens to the Acquired-stratum holy lands (the herbs are grown in the grotto-heavens, which are Precelestial-affixed; the herbs' transit to the Acquired stratum is the carriers' most common run); law-fragments from the substrate to the Mahayana councilors' Reach-libraries (rare; only the licensed carriers' three most senior carriers are authorized); veneration-pulses from the Acquired-stratum lineages to their Precelestial ancestral courts (the pulses are bundled by the lineage's senior cultivator at a Roads-department stratum-crossing gate and conveyed by the carrier to the court's threshold).
   105→
   106→**What flows down (Precelestial to Acquired).** Cosmic-tier herbs (the peak grotto-heavens' production); law-inscribed treasures (the Builder's Court's architectural qi-inscriptions, commissioned by Acquired-stratum holy lands); ancestor-interventions (rare; the persistence-budget's cost); Mahayana counsel (rarer still; the time-debt's cost). The downward flow's volume is, by the Roads department's annual report, approximately equivalent to a moderate holy land's annual revenue — a small fraction of the Acquired stratum's total economic activity, but a disproportionate fraction of the Acquired stratum's cosmic-tier resource supply.
   107→
   108→**What flows up (Acquired to Precelestial).** Veneration-pulses (the lineages' daily and seasonal rites); Acquired-stratum goods for the courts' maintenance (the courts' Roads department's commission, paid in spirit stones by the Acquired-stratum holy lands whose sects use the maintained roads); raw herbs and beast parts for the Precelestial's alchemists (the Precelestial's alchemists are mostly persistent ancestors whose persistence-budget allows the alchemical work; the raw materials come from the Acquired stratum). The upward flow's volume is larger than the downward flow's (the Acquired stratum is, in qi-density terms, the larger economy), but the upward flow's value-per-unit is lower (the Acquired stratum's resources are mortal-tier; the Precelestial's needs are cosmic-tier).
   109→
   110→### 2.2 The cosmic currencies
   111→
   112→**Spirit stones (the subsidiary currency).** Spirit stones remain the cosmic economy's subsidiary currency — used for transactions below the Nascent Soul tier, for the courts' Roads-department commissions, and for the licensed carriers' fees. The exchange rate (10-50 taels of silver per spirit stone, per doc 24 §3.1) holds across the Acquired stratum; in the Precelestial, the exchange rate is denominated in veneration-pulses (1 spirit stone ≈ a Qi Condensation cultivator's daily veneration-pulse; 100 spirit stones ≈ a Foundation Establishment cultivator's daily veneration-pulse; 10,000 spirit stones ≈ a Core Formation cultivator's daily veneration-pulse). A Nascent Soul cultivator's daily veneration-pulse is, in spirit-stone equivalent, approximately 1,000,000 stones — a sum no Acquired-stratum institution can pay in mortal currency. The exchange rate is therefore theoretical above Nascent Soul; in practice, Nascent Soul and above transactions are denominated in the cosmic currencies below.
   113→
   114→**Qi-authority (the reserve currency).** Qi-authority — the substrate's measurable qi-density, in a cultivator's anchor or in a court's reservoir — is the cosmic economy's reserve currency. Qi-authority is finite (per doc 00 §1, the cosmos is non-regenerating) and non-substitutable (every cosmic-scale transaction consumes qi-authority, directly or indirectly). The Thinning is the decline of the Acquired stratum's total qi-authority; the pantheon's deadlock (per doc 39 §6) is, at root, a disagreement about how to allocate the declining qi-authority. Qi-authority is not traded directly (it is too fundamental to be transferable in discrete units); it is conserved, spent, or wasted, and the conservation is the cosmic economy's central practice.
   115→
   116→**Veneration (the ancestral court currency).** Veneration (per doc 46 §5) is the qi-pulse a lineage's living cultivators generate by the veneration rite. Veneration is the cosmic economy's lineage-currency — it flows from the living to the dead, sustains the ancestral courts, and is the medium in which the courts' interventions are denominated. Veneration is, in principle, denominated in spirit-stone equivalent (see §2.2 above), but the equivalence is theoretical (a lineage cannot sell its veneration-pulses to another lineage; the veneration is bound to the lineage's qi-connection to its court). Veneration is therefore a non-transferable currency, except in the restricted form of inter-court donations (the Garden Court to the Mourner's Court, per §1.4).
   117→
   118→**Law-fragments (the high-tier currency).** Law-fragments (per §1.6) are the cosmic economy's high-tier currency — the medium in which Mahayana-tier transactions are denominated. A law-fragment's value is, by the pantheon's collective assessment, approximately equivalent to a Tribulation Crossing cultivator's persistence-budget (a Tribulation Crossing cultivator would, in principle, trade their entire remaining budget for a law-fragment of a relevant predecessor's law). The trade is rare (the pantheon's nine Mahayana hold, between them, perhaps fifty law-fragments worth studying; the fragments are mostly hoarded); the trade's protocol is the pantheon's Council of Nine (per doc 39 §7), where a Mahayana may offer a fragment for a favor, a vote, or a coalition.
   119→
   120→**Time (the constraint, not the currency).** The time-debt (per §1.5) is the cosmic economy's master constraint, not a currency. Time cannot be traded (the 1:365 ratio is lawful and non-negotiable, per doc 15 §7.1); time can only be spent, and the spending's cost is the mortal world's aging. Every cosmic-scale transaction is, in part, a time-cost calculation; the calculation is the cosmic economy's tragic engine.
   121→
   122→### 2.3 The grotto-heaven economy
   123→
   124→A grotto-heaven (per doc 24 §2.7, doc 37 §6.2) is a Precelestial-affixed cultivation zone sustained by an Acquired spirit vein's main current. The grotto-heaven's qi is denser than the Acquired stratum's ambient; the grotto-heaven's time-rate is faster than the Acquired stratum's (the Crimson Vow City's 1:3, the Garden Grotto's 1:5, the Jade Void's 1:10). The grotto-heavens are the cosmic economy's primary production zones — the places where cosmic-tier herbs are grown, cosmic-tier beasts are raised, and cosmic-tier alchemy is performed.
   125→
   126→The cosmos's grotto-heavens number approximately one hundred and forty, by Tu Nan's map (per doc 39 §2.6). The distribution is uneven: the eastern continent holds the most (fifty-two, including the Jade Void Grotto and the Garden Grotto); the central continent holds forty-one (including the Azure Sword Sect's minor grotto); the southern continent holds thirty-nine (mostly wood-phase grottos, sustained by the central highlands' vein system); the northern continent holds eight (the northern continent's qi-density is lower; the Thinning is most advanced there). The Crimson Vow City's former grotto (collapsed, Year ~43,000) is not in the count; the Silent Hexagram Grotto-Heaven (sealed, Year ~45,500) is in the count but inaccessible. The count has declined by approximately ten grotto-heavens over the last three thousand years (the Thinning's effect; some grottos have failed as their spirit veins declined below the sustenance threshold).
   127→
   128→**Who controls the grotto-heavens.** Each grotto-heaven is controlled by the holy land or sect that holds its anchor (the surface trace in the Acquired stratum, per doc 24 §2.7). The Jade Void Holy Land holds the Jade Void Grotto (the eastern continent's largest peak grotto-heaven); the Garden Holy Land holds the Garden Grotto; the Azure Sword Sect holds a minor grotto-heaven in the Cangwu Mountains (a 1:2 time-rate grotto, sustaining 500-year herbs, the sect's primary cosmic-tier asset). The courts do not control any grotto-heaven (the courts' jurisdiction does not extend to the grotto-heavens, which are private holdings; the lineage-jurisdiction clause, jurisdiction 5).
   129→
   130→**What the holy lands charge for grotto-heaven access.** A holy land leases access to its grotto-heaven to allied sects and cultivators. The lease's terms vary; the Jade Void Holy Land's standard lease (per the holy land's published schedule, accessible at Nascent Soul) charges: 1,000 spirit stones per Acquired-stratum day of access for a Core Formation cultivator; 100 spirit stones per day for a Nascent Soul cultivator (Nascent Soul cultivators are valuable enough to the holy land that the lease is subsidized); free access for Tribulation Crossing cultivators who agree to perform a maintenance service (grotto-heaven law-inscription repair, a 1-day service in exchange for 30 days of access). The Azure Sword Sect's grotto-heaven lease is restricted to the sect's own disciples and to the sect's two allied sects (the Cangwu Sect, the protagonist's first sect, is not among them); the protagonist's first grotto-heaven access is, in Act 2, the Azure Sword Sect's grotto, accessed by a personal favor from the sect master Leng Wushuang (per doc 26, the protagonist's relationship with the Azure Sword Sect).
   131→
   132→**The grotto-heaven economy's tragic engine.** The grotto-heavens are sustained by Acquired spirit veins; the spirit veins are declining (the Thinning, per §1.7); the grotto-heavens are, in the long run, a wasting asset. The Jade Void Grotto's qi-feed has declined approximately 5% in the last five thousand years (the Jade Void patriarch's measurement, recorded in the holy land's archive); the decline is, for now, sustainable (the grotto's production is still sufficient for the holy land's needs), but the trajectory is the cosmic economy's master scarcity. In eight to ten thousand years (per doc 39 §6's timeline), the grotto-heavens will fail; the cosmic-tier herbs' production will cease; the cosmic economy's primary production zone will be gone. The pantheon's Builders faction (per doc 39 §6) wants to author a qi-conservation law to slow the decline; the Tenders faction wants to stop authoring and let the decline proceed gracefully; the deadlock is, in economic terms, a deadlock over the grotto-heaven economy's future.
   133→
   134→### 2.4 The ancestral court economy
   135→
   136→Per doc 46 §5, the ancestral court's qi-economy is: veneration flows up (from living to dead), and the court's persistence-budget flows down (in interventions, blessings, counsel). The ancestral court economy is the cosmic economy's most vertical sector — the resource flows are unidirectional (up) and the reciprocal flows (down) are constrained by the persistence-budget's finitude.
   137→
   138→**What flows up.** Veneration (per §2.2). The veneration-pulse's magnitude depends on the lineage's living cultivators' number, realm, and rite-efficiency. A lineage with a Nascent Soul elder, a dozen Core Formation cultivators, and a hundred Foundation Establishment cultivators (the Yan clan's approximate Acquired-stratum remnant) generates a daily veneration-pulse approximately equivalent to 5,000,000 spirit stones — a sum that, in the cosmic economy, is denominated not in spirit stones but in the court's qi-reservoir's daily increment. The court's qi-reservoir, in turn, sustains the persistent ancestors' coherence.
   139→
   140→**What flows down.** Counsel (the consultation rite's primary product, per doc 15 §3.3); interventions (rare, per doc 15 §3.4, denominated in persistence-budget); blessings (the most common down-flow, a small qi-authority investment that elevates a living descendant's capability for a short period); curses (rare, expensive, and the form most likely to attract the courts' attention). The down-flows are constrained by the persistence-budget's finitude — every down-flow diminishes the ancestor, and the ancestor's counsel about whether to down-flow is, in part, the ancestor's calculation of whether the down-flow is worth the cost.
   141→
   142→**The court's economic relationship to its living lineage.** The court is, in economic terms, the lineage's pension fund — a reservoir the lineage fills during its productive years (when the lineage's cultivators are generating veneration) and draws from during its crisis years (when the lineage's cultivators need counsel, blessings, or interventions). A lineage with a well-fed court (the Yan clan, the Builder's Lineage) has a strategic reserve no Acquired-stratum wealth can buy; a lineage with a starving court (the Mourner's Lineage, the Pillar Dynasty) has a reserve that is, in cosmic terms, a wasting asset. The Wang lineage (the protagonist's lineage, courtless, per doc 46 §3) has no reserve at all — the protagonist's grief for Wang Xiaodi is, in economic terms, the grief of a being whose lineage cannot afford the persistence the cosmos's other lineages take for granted.

   144→### 2.5 The underworld market
   145→
   146→The underworld market (per doc 26 §9.2, doc 38 §9) is the cosmic economy's unregulated sector — the trade in goods the courts' Roads department does not permit through the licensed carriers, conducted in the Acquired stratum's wild edges and the Spirit Wilds' border zones. The market is not a single institution; it is a network of brokers, carriers, and fences (Liang Zhu, the protagonist's companion, is one of the brokers) operating across the eastern and central continents' wild paths.
   147→
   148→**What the underworld market trades.** Wild herbs (per §1.2) that the courts' Foreign Affairs department's yao-court trade does not acquire; wild beast parts (per §1.3) that the Wild Hunters' lineages do not sell through the licensed carriers; law-fragments (per §1.6) that the pantheon's Mahayana have not hoarded; veneration-pulses (per §2.2) stolen from courts whose retrieval rites have been corrupted (Scribe Yan's bardo-harvest, per doc 26 §2.3, is the named instance of veneration-pulse theft — the harvested anchors' qi-authority is, in effect, a stolen veneration-pulse, redirected from the lawful bardo-processing to the harvester's personal use). The underworld market's prices are denominated in spirit stones (for the lower-tier goods), law-fragments (for the higher-tier goods), and qi-authority (for the highest-tier goods, denominated in the buyer's personal qi-authority, transferred to the seller in a controlled burn).
   149→
   150→**Who trades in the underworld market.** Rogue cultivators (the market's primary constituents), corrupt courts officials (Scribe Yan, before his exposure), demonic cultivators (the Crimson Vow Remnant, per doc 37 §6.2, trades fire-phase fragments through the market), and (rarely) holy land cultivators seeking resources the courts' licensed carriers cannot supply. The courts' Roads department considers the market a nuisance; the Foreign Affairs department considers the market a source of intelligence (Liang Zhu's gossip is, in part, the market's gossip); the Defense department considers the market a security risk (the market's wild-edge locations are Spirit Wilds breach-points). The protagonist's companion Liang Zhu is the protagonist's primary access to the market; the protagonist's pursuit of Pei Liang (per doc 26 §3) requires the market's intelligence, and the protagonist's first cosmic-tier resource purchases (a wild herb for a Core Formation breakthrough preparation; a law-fragment for a Nascent Soul inheritance-study) are made through Liang Zhu's broker-network.
   151→
   152→**The market's economic function.** The underworld market is, in cosmic-economic terms, the cosmic economy's black market — the sector that exists because the courts' regulated sector cannot supply all the cosmic-tier resources the cosmos's cultivators demand. The market's existence is a structural feature of the cosmos's economic regulation (the courts' licensing of carriers restricts supply; the unmet demand creates the market); the market's persistence is a structural feature of the cosmos's political fragmentation (the courts cannot enforce their jurisdiction in the wild edges; the market operates where the courts' law does not reach). The market's function is, in the cosmic economy, the function of a pressure valve — the market absorbs the demand the regulated sector cannot supply, and the absorption prevents the regulated sector's breakdown. The Reform Bloc's "incorporate the rogues" program (per doc 38 §3.4) is, in economic terms, a proposal to formalize the market's function (bring the brokers into the licensed carriers' roster); the Conservative Bloc's opposition is, in economic terms, a defense of the regulated sector's purity (the market's existence is a moral failing, not an economic necessity). The protagonist's relationship to the market (through Liang Zhu) is, in cosmic-economic terms, the protagonist's first encounter with the cosmic economy's structural imperfection.
   153→
   154→### 2.6 The mortal economy's interface with the cosmic economy
   155→
   156→The mortal economy (doc 18) and the cosmic economy are not separate systems; they are two layers of a single economic substrate, with the mortal economy as the lower layer and the cosmic economy as the upper layer. The interface between the layers is the spirit-stone exchange rate (10-50 taels of silver per spirit stone, per doc 24 §3.1) and the licensed carriers' stratum-crossing fees (per §2.1).
   157→
   158→**Where the mortal economy ends and the cosmic economy begins.** The boundary is, in practice, at the Core Formation tier. A Qi Condensation cultivator operates in the mortal economy (earning spirit stones, buying mortal-tier resources); a Core Formation cultivator operates in both economies simultaneously (earning spirit stones but also encountering cosmic-tier resources they cannot access); a Nascent Soul cultivator operates primarily in the cosmic economy (the spirit-stone economy is, for a Nascent Soul cultivator, irrelevant; the cosmic-currency economy is the Nascent Soul cultivator's actual constraint). The protagonist's economic arc (§4 below) traces the protagonist's passage through this boundary — the passage is the protagonist's central economic transformation, and the passage's cost (the protagonist's mortal kin aging past recognition, per doc 15 §7.2) is the genre's central tragic engine.
   159→
   160→**The interface's leakage.** The interface is not perfectly sealed. Cosmic-tier resources occasionally leak into the mortal economy — a wild herb, harvested by a mortal herb-gatherer in the Cangwu Mountains' deep interior, sold at the Qinghe Market Town for silver (the gatherer does not know the herb's cosmic-tier properties; the buyer, if the buyer is a rogue cultivator with the right perception, profits enormously). The leakage is the mortal economy's occasional encounter with the cosmic economy's multiplier, felt as a windfall rather than perceived as a structural feature. The protagonist's first encounter with cosmic-tier resources, in Act 2 (per §4.4 below), is a leakage-encounter — the Pillar Dynasty bark-strip text in the Azure Sword Sect's Scripture Hall is a cosmic-tier resource that has leaked, via a rogue cultivator's trade, into a Core Formation-tier sect's archive. The leakage is the cosmic economy's mortal-world footprint, and the footprint is the protagonist's first perception of the cosmic economy's existence.

   164→## 3. What Mahayana cultivators fight over
   165→
   166→The pantheon's nine (per doc 39 §1) are the cosmos's only Mahayana cultivators. They are, by the pantheon's collective assessment, the cosmic economy's only beings whose transactions are denominated in law-authorship. This section specifies what they fight over.
   167→
   168→### 3.1 Law Reaches
   169→
   170→Per doc 15 §5, the Law Reaches are the Precelestial's regions where the substrate-law has thinned (the Sundering's scars, per doc 37 §5, doc 38 §5). The Reaches are the only venue for Reach-authorship (per doc 24 §1.1, Reach-authorship creates new local substrate where no law previously held). The Reaches are finite; the pantheon has not counted them precisely (the Reaches are not stable; they shift with the substrate-law's slow erosion), but the Cartographer Tu Nan's map (per doc 39 §2.6) identifies approximately **nine major Reaches** and a tail of thirty-seven minor Reaches.
   171→
   172→The nine major Reaches, in Tu Nan's map's order (which follows the Precelestial's geography clockwise from the Reach of the Sundered Stratum): (1) the **Reach of the Sundered Stratum** (斷層法域, the largest, holding the Fourth-Stratum Attempt's sealed residue, per doc 37 §7.1); (2) **Mo Shen's Reach** (the silent Reach, per doc 39 §2.7, the pantheon's neutral ground for the Council of Nine); (3) **Lie Fa's Reach** (the ruins, per doc 39 §2.9, holding the partial law-inscription); (4) the **Reach of the Caged Sky** (籠天法域, holding the sealed Sky-Devouring Beast, per doc 37 §7.2); (5) the **Reach of the Western Mirror** (西鏡法域, unclaimed, the Builders' preferred qi-conservation-law venue); (6) the **Reach of the Northern Wind** (北風法域, unclaimed, the Tenders' preferred restraint-law venue); (7) the **Reach of the Eastern Tide** (東潮法域, unclaimed, contested between the Builders and the Tenders); (8) the **Reach of the Southern Stem** (南莖法域, unclaimed, considered too unstable for authorship by most of the pantheon); (9) the **Reach of the Central Quiet** (中寂法域, unclaimed, Mo Shen's adjacent Reach, considered by the pantheon to be Mo Shen's extended holding despite Mo Shen's refusal to claim it).
   173→
   174→**Who controls the Reaches.** No one. The Reaches are explicitly outside the courts' jurisdiction (per doc 38 §7.4); the courts maintain watchposts but do not cross the boundary. The Reaches are, by the pantheon's collective assessment, common ground — any Mahayana cultivator may enter any Reach and attempt Reach-authorship. The Reaches' common-ground status is, however, complicated by the pantheon's personal holdings: Mo Shen's Reach (per doc 39 §2.7) is Mo Shen's silent abode; Lie Fa's Reach (per doc 39 §2.9) is Lie Fa's ruins. These two Reaches are, by pantheon custom, not available for other Mahayana's authorship. The remaining seven major Reaches are, in principle, available; in practice, the pantheon has not authored a new Reach-law in eight hundred years (the deadlock, per doc 39 §6), so the question of who would author where has been moot.
   175→
   176→**Is there competition for unclaimed Reaches?** Yes, latently. The deadlock's breaking (the protagonist's amendment, per doc 39 §6) will reopen the question. The Builders faction (Fan Xuan, Tu Nan, Lu Shen) has, in private, identified three of the seven unclaimed major Reaches as candidate venues for a qi-conservation law (the Builders' preferred post-deadlock project). The Tenders faction (Yin Mu, Bei Huai, Mo Shen) has identified the same three Reaches as candidates for a different project — a law of restraint that would, in effect, codify the Tenders' position (no new authorship) into cosmic law. The two factions' candidate-Reach lists overlap; if the deadlock breaks and both factions attempt authorship in the same Reach, the Reach's substrate may not sustain two simultaneous authorships (the substrate's law-paradox perception, doc 30 Station 10, would flag the conflict). The conflict would be the pantheon's first Reach-authorship contest in eight hundred years.
   177→
   178→### 3.2 The Thinning
   179→
   180→Per doc 39 §6, the Thinning is the pantheon's deadlock: should the pantheon author new laws to slow the Thinning (the Builders' position), stop authoring and let the Thinning proceed gracefully (the Tenders' position), or approach the Origin to perceive whether the Origin's generative function can be renewed (the Wanderers' position)? The deadlock has held for two thousand years; the Thinning has accelerated in that time.
   181→
   182→**Do Mahayana cultivators compete for the remaining qi?** No — and this is the deadlock's specific texture. The Mahayana cultivators are not, in their personal practice, competing for qi (a Mahayana cultivator's qi-metabolism is sustained by the Precelestial's undifferentiated qi, which is sustained by the Origin's generative function, which is not subject to the Thinning). The Thinning is, for the Mahayana personally, a slow dimming of the Acquired stratum they observe but do not depend on. The deadlock is not about the Mahayana's personal qi-supply; the deadlock is about the Acquired stratum's qi-supply, which is the supply that sustains mortal cultivation, mortal lineages, and the ancestral courts' veneration. The Mahayana's deadlock is, in economic terms, a deadlock over the cosmic economy's substrate — not over the Mahayana's personal resources.
   183→
   184→**Do they cooperate to conserve it?** Latently. The Tenders' position (stop authoring) is a cooperation position — the Tenders are willing to forgo new law-authorship to conserve the qi-authorship that new inscriptions would consume. The Builders' position (author conservation laws) is also a cooperation position — the Builders want to author laws that would, in effect, tax the Acquired stratum's qi-authority to fund the grotto-heavens' maintenance. The two positions are both cooperative in intent; the deadlock is over which cooperation to pursue. The Wanderers' position (approach the Origin) is, in economic terms, a speculation position — the Wanderers want to perceive whether the Origin's generative function can be renewed, which would, if successful, end the Thinning; the speculation's cost is the Mahayana's risk (Lie Fa's fall, per doc 39 §2.9, is the cautionary tale).
   185→
   186→**What the protagonist's law does, economically.** The protagonist's bardo-harvest-loophole amendment (Transcend path, per doc 39 §5.2, §6) is, in economic terms, a qi-conservation law — it closes a qi-theft (harvested anchors are qi-authority diverted from the lawful bardo-processing to the harvester's personal use). The amendment conserves, by the pantheon's preliminary modeling, approximately 0.01% of the Acquired stratum's annual qi-authority — a small fraction, but the first conservation the pantheon has been able to enact in eight hundred years. The amendment gives the Builders a precedent (qi-conservation is possible); the Tenders a concession (the conservation was achieved by amendment, not by new authorship); the Wanderers nothing (the amendment does not approach the Origin). The deadlock does not break cleanly along the amendment's lines; the deadlock's breaking is, in economic terms, the beginning of a longer negotiation the protagonist's post-game work is to tend.
   187→
   188→### 3.3 Predecessors' laws
   189→
   190→Per doc 24 §1.1, Acquired-authorship can amend existing Acquired law within a scope; the amendment requires, by the pantheon's procedural law, either the original law's author's ratification or the author's dispersal. The procedural law is the source of the courts' current deadlock (the bardo-window law's author, Gu Wu / Qiu Wuhen, has neither ratified nor dispersed, per doc 38 §8).
   191→
   192→**Can a Mahayana cultivator amend another's law?** Yes, with the author's ratification or dispersal. The ratification is the author's consent; the dispersal is the author's death. The procedural law is, in this respect, the cosmic economy's authorship-property law — a Mahayana's law is the Mahayana's property, and the property's transfer requires the owner's consent or death.
   193→
   194→**What if the predecessor is dead?** If the predecessor has dispersed (final death, 寂滅), the law is, in procedural terms, ownerless — the law persists (laws outlast their authors, per doc 39 §8), but no Mahayana's ratification is required for amendment. The amendment, in this case, requires only the Council's vote (a simple majority of those present, per doc 38 §4). The ownerless-law amendment is the easiest amendment procedurally; it is also the rarest (most of the cosmos's active laws are still held by their authors, because most authors have not yet dispersed).
   195→
   196→**What if the predecessor is Gu Wu, who is dying?** This is the protagonist's specific case (Transcend path, per doc 39 §5.2). Gu Wu is alive (not dispersed) but dying (within the century, per doc 39 §2.1). The protagonist's amendment to Gu Wu's bardo-window law requires either Gu Wu's ratification (Gu Wu has not decided, per doc 38 §8) or Gu Wu's dispersal (Gu Wu has not chosen). The protagonist's path, in the Transcend ending, is to force the issue: the protagonist authors the amendment as an Acquired-authorship act (which the procedural law does not explicitly forbid — the law requires ratification for the amendment to take effect, but the authorship itself is the Mahayana's verb). The amendment, taking effect, is the fait accompli the Council must ratify or contest (per doc 38 §8); the Conservative Bloc's contest (if it comes) is the civil-war risk; the protagonist's survival of the contest (or failure to survive) determines whether the amendment holds. Gu Wu, perceiving the amendment's effect, disperses — the dispersal is the procedural law's resolution (the predecessor's death frees the amendment to proceed without ratification). The economic consequence: the bardo-window law's authorship-property transfers, in cosmic-economy terms, from Gu Wu to the protagonist (the protagonist becomes the law's maintainer, the way Gu Wu was); the transfer is the protagonist's inheritance and the protagonist's burden.
   197→
   198→**Can a Mahayana amend a dispersed predecessor's law without the Council's vote?** No. The procedural law is clear (the Council's vote is required for ownerless-law amendments). A Mahayana who attempts an amendment without the Council's vote is, in cosmic-economy terms, attempting a property-transfer without the property-court's approval — the attempt is unlawful, and the courts' Adjudication department would hear the case (the Mahayana's law-tribulation, in this case, would include an Adjudication-department component). The procedural law is, in this respect, the cosmic economy's contract law — the law that makes Mahayana authorship a lawful practice rather than a personal power.
