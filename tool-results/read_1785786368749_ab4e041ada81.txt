     9→## Station 6: Nascent Soul (元嬰, yuanying)
    10→
    11→### Essence
    12→The anchor can act without the body. The upper dantian becomes the seat of a self-perceiving spirit (元嬰) that can project, retrieve the dead from bardo, possess, and survive the body's death if a refuge is prepared. The 煉神還虛 station of the internal-alchemy chain (doc 24 §2.3): spirit refined into the void, the anchor independent of flesh.
    13→
    14→### Lifespan
    15→~1000 years. Aging near-halts; a 500-year-old Nascent Soul cultivator appears ~40. Capability envelope: physical lift ~4000-6000 jin; sprint ~40-60 m/s; survive falls from ~150m; qi throughput roughly 10× Core Formation, sustainable for hours of combat; perception range extends to bardo (~50 li) and lineage-resonance (continental, faint); authority extends to others' heart-minds (projectable emotional state, criminal without consent).
    16→
    17→### New perceptions
    18→- **Bardo perception.** The cultivator perceives the bardo state directly — the anchors of the recently dead within a ~50 li radius, their coherence, their attachment-type, their remaining window. This is a new modality: not qi-quality (Qi Condensation), not anchor-presence (Core Formation), but the perception of a being-in-transition, half-disembodied, half-held by its anchor-point.
    19→- **Projection perception.** While the spirit is projected, perception is seated at the projection, not the body. The body lies in trance; the projection sees, hears, and qi-perceives at a distance. Not "qi-perception at range" (a deepening of Core Formation) — a relocated seat of perception.
    20→- **Lineage-resonance perception.** The cultivator perceives the anchors of their own descendants (in the metaphysical sense — all whom they have transmitted practice to) — faintly across continents, clearly within their domain. The cultivator knows when a descendant forms a core, breaks an oath, or dies.
    21→- **Karmic-trace perception (others').** Per doc 24 §2.1, the cultivator now perceives others' karmic ledgers (Core Formation grants own-trace only). The cultivator sees, on another's anchor, the lawful record of their acts — heavy or light, balanced or unsustainable. The perception that makes the demonic cultivator unmistakable (doc 25 §5.6).
    22→
    23→### New verbs
    24→- **Project spirit (出神, chushen).** The anchor leaves the body and acts at a distance — perceives, speaks in qi-modulations, leaves traces, performs rites. Genuinely new because the ACTOR is the anchor itself, not qi routed through a body. Range ~50-200 li initially, growing with practice; duration hours to days; the body in trance is undefended.
    25→- **Possess a body (奪舍, duoshe — CRIMINAL).** The cultivator's anchor displaces another's anchor from a living body and inserts itself. The displaced anchor enters bardo; the cultivator's original body becomes an empty vessel (走尸, zoushi). Genuinely new because the verb's target is another being's anchor, not their qi or body. Consensual possession is a different verb (借舍, jieshe — body-borrowing by consent, bounded task, original anchor retained in a side-state). Both are new act-classes; the criminal/non-criminal distinction is one of consent, not mechanics.
    26→- **Survive bodily death (保命, baoming).** When the body is killed, the anchor flees to a prepared refuge — a sanctum, an inscribed statue, a willing student's dantian, an ancestral court's threshold. Genuinely new because the verb's precondition is bodily death; no prior station survives it. The refuge must be prepared in advance; an unprepared Nascent Soul cultivator whose body dies disperses.
    27→- **Found a lineage (立宗, lizong).** Establish a multi-generational cultivation institution whose persistence is metaphysically sustained by the founder's anchor. Distinct from 立派/立支 (sect founding at Core Formation, sociological) because the lineage's first ancestral court seeds from the founder's anchor at this station; the lineage's dead can be retrieved by the founder's living projection; the founder's qi-signature is the lineage's qi-signature.
    28→- **Retrieve a bardo anchor (取陰, quyin).** Fetch a specific anchor from bardo before the window closes. Distinct from ghost-dispersal (Core Formation can disperse an anchor-fragment) because retrieval preserves the anchor and returns it to processing — for a lineage rite, for an ancestral court, for a sect soul-rite. Requires the retriever's projection to enter bardo; bardo is hostile to projected anchors.
    29→- **Project heart-mind influence (傳心, chuanxin).** Per doc 27 §7.3. The cultivator intentionally projects an emotional state — calm, fear, awe, grief — outward, affecting those around them. Distinct from 調心 (regulate own emotional state, Qi Condensation) because the target is another's heart-mind, not the cultivator's own. Criminal without consent in most jurisdictions.
    30→
    31→### New responsibilities
    32→- **Bardo duties.** Perform soul-rites for the dead in the territory — retrieve lost anchors, lay the unquiet dead to rest, exorcise the possessed. Refusal is permitted but frowned upon.
    33→- **Lineage stewardship.** The cultivator's descendants (metaphysical sense — all whom they have transmitted practice to) are now the cultivator's responsibility. Their breakthroughs, deviations, deaths are the cultivator's concern.
    34→- **Karma accountability.** With karmic-trace perception comes the obligation to act on it. A Nascent Soul cultivator who perceives a demonic cultivator's heavy trace and does not act is complicit.
    35→- **Ancestral-court duty (prospective).** The cultivator is now eligible (after Void Amalgamation) for ancestral-court retrieval after bodily death; they are expected to prepare the conditions for their lineage's eventual retrieval rite.
    36→
    37→### New constraints
    38→- **Projection leaves the body undefended.** The body in trance is a mortal object; a single sword-cut to the throat ends the body (though not, if a refuge is prepared, the cultivator). Station guardians or wards before projecting.
    39→- **Bardo is hostile.** A projected anchor in bardo is a foreign object; bardo's natural momentum pulls at it toward rebirth. Prolonged bardo stays risk the cultivator's own anchor being drawn into the rebirth path.
    40→- **Possession is criminal.** Duoshe is the cosmology's second-most-prosecuted crime (after anchor-harvesting). Even jieshe requires registration with the local court or sect.
    41→- **Karma perception is involuntary.** The cultivator cannot turn it off; they perceive others' traces whenever they perceive the anchor. In places dense with violence (battlefields, demonic-cultivator sites, old execution grounds), overwhelming.
    42→- **Anchor-lock.** The anchor can leave the body, but it can also be slow to return. A projection interrupted — by trap, by tribulation, by enemy action — may leave the body alive but the anchor unable to return for hours, days, or (in extreme cases) ever.
    43→
    44→### Failure modes
    45→- **Projection capture.** A stronger cultivator (Spirit Severance or higher) traps the projected anchor — binds it in a formation, seals it in a treasure, draws it into a domain. The body lives; the anchor is held; the cultivator is imprisoned in two places at once.
    46→- **Possession backlash.** The displaced anchor's kin take revenge; the displaced anchor's lineage pursues; the courts prosecute. Successful duoshe produces a karmic trace that the next tribulation will test.
    47→- **Lineage collapse.** The cultivator's descendants fail to cultivate, deviate, die, or turn against the founder. A lineage whose founder is alive can be re-founded; one whose founder has dispersed cannot.
    48→- **Anchor-lock (described above).** The body ages and dies while the anchor is held elsewhere; the cultivator must then attempt bardo retrieval of their own anchor — a paradox that usually ends in dispersal.
    49→- **Bardo-pull.** A projection that overstays is drawn toward rebirth; the cultivator perceives their own anchor beginning to dissolve into the rebirth path. Recovery requires a prepared refuge and a powerful retrieval rite; many do not recover.
    50→- **Heart-mind overload.** The karmic-trace perception, unfiltered, produces a specific 心魔: the cultivator becomes unable to perceive beings as anything but their karmic ledgers. Everyone is an accounting; every conversation an audit. The "karma-only" 心魔, escalation of Core Formation's "anchor-only" 心魔.
    51→
    52→### What it feels like
    53→The first time the spirit leaves the body, the vertigo is total. You — the you that perceives, that wills, that remembers — rise out of the body's seat and look down at it. The body lies in trance, breathing slowly, eyes closed. It is yours and it is not you. You are seeing it from outside. The room is the same room; the qi is the same qi; but the seat of perception has moved, and with it the seat of self. The body feels like a garment left on a chair — yours, familiar, but not where you are.
    54→
    55→The bardo perception comes next, and it is the heaviest perception in cultivation. You walk through a village and see, layered over the living, the recently dead — their anchors still coherent, still hanging in the places where they died, fading as the bardo window closes. A mother who died in childbirth, anchored to the household where her infant sleeps. A traveler murdered at the crossroads, anchored by hatred. An old man who died peacefully, his anchor already thinning, already drawn toward rebirth. The dead are not silent; they are merely not heard by the living. You hear them now. You will hear them for the rest of your very long life.
    56→
    57→The lineage-resonance perception is gentler. You feel your students — their anchors' faint signatures, registered in your perception like the signatures of your own breath. You know when one forms a core. You know when one breaks through. You know, with a cold clarity, when one dies. The death is a small absence in a fabric you did not know you were weaving. You will attend many such absences.
    58→
    59→The first time you perceive another's karmic trace, you understand why the courts trust Nascent Soul cultivators as investigators. A man stands before you and you see, on his anchor, the lawful record of his acts — a faint shading for the small harms, a heavier band for the great ones, a luminous thread for the healings and kindnesses. You can read him in a way he cannot read himself. This is the perception that makes demonic cultivators unmistakable: their anchors are not shaded but blackened, mottled with the dispersed residue of those they have harvested (doc 25 §5.6). You perceive one and you know, before they speak, what they are. You cannot unknow it.
    60→
    61→The body becomes a garment. The world becomes a place with a bardo layer and a karmic layer you cannot unsee. You are powerful — your projection can act at a distance, your retrieval can save the unretrieved, your lineage can persist across your bodily death — but you are burdened in a way no prior station's cultivator is. The dead speak to you. The living are open books of their own acts. Your descendants are threads in a fabric you wove. The body lies in trance, undefended. The world is larger than it was, and you are larger in it, and the weight of the larger world is on your shoulders.

    65→## Station 7: Spirit Severance (化神, huashen)
    66→
    67→### Essence
    68→The cultivator externalizes their upper dantian as a domain — a bounded region of the Acquired Stratum where their will is partially authoritative, partially lawful. The 煉虛合道 station of the internal-alchemy chain: the void (the cultivator's internal law-pattern) refined into the Dao (the world's law, partially authored). The threshold of "great power."
    69→
    70→### Lifespan
    71→~2000 years. Aging nearly halts; a 1000-year-old Spirit Severance cultivator appears ~40. Capability envelope: physical lift ~10000-15000 jin within domain; sprint ~80-120 m/s within domain; survivability reinforced by domain (only a stronger domain or a tribulation can pierce); qi throughput equal to the domain's total ambient qi (vastly more than a Nascent Soul's bodily reservoir); perception range equal to the domain's extent (~10-100 li radius, growing with practice); authority: within the domain, the cultivator's will is partially lawful — local law responds to the cultivator as a co-author.
    72→
    73→### New perceptions
    74→- **Domain perception.** The cultivator perceives the interior of the domain as an extension of self — every qi-flow, every being, every law-resonance within the domain is felt the way one feels one's own body. A leaf falling at the domain's far edge is felt the way a hair on one's arm is felt. Not perception AT a distance (Nascent Soul projection); perception AS the place.
    75→- **Law-structure perception.** The cultivator perceives local laws not as rules but as objects — inscribable, contestable, modifiable structures with joints and load-bearing walls. A Core Formation cultivator perceives that laws exist; a Spirit Severance cultivator perceives their architecture. The perception that makes law-amendment possible.
    76→- **Tribulation-precursor perception.** The cultivator perceives tribulation gathering hours to days before it strikes — the lawful pressure building at the stratum boundary. A temporal perception (the future of the stratum's lawful state), not a spatial one.
    77→- **Domain-edge perception.** The cultivator perceives the boundary of their domain as a living edge — where their will holds and where it thins. A breach is felt immediately; a contest by another domain is felt as a pressure at the edge.
    78→
    79→### New verbs
    80→- **Open a domain (開域, kaiyu).** The cultivator's upper dantian externalizes as a bounded region. The dantian was an internal center; it is now a place. Genuinely new because the verb's effect is ontological — the cultivator's interior becomes exterior; the world gains a region that is, partially, the cultivator. Distinct from Nascent Soul projection (the anchor leaves the body but the body remains the cultivator's center) because the domain IS the cultivator's center now.
    81→- **Enforce a local law (制法, zhifa).** Within the domain, write a rule the LOCAL WORLD enforces. Distinct from Core Formation's 立法 (author a minor law, which the cultivator's own qi enforces within a small scope like their body or sect hall) on three axes: (a) the enforcer is the local world, not the cultivator's qi; (b) the scope is the domain (10-100 li), not the cultivator's body; (c) the law is partially lawful — the world treats it as a real, enforceable rule, not a cultivator's technique. The law's compatibility with deeper Acquired law is tested at the next tribulation.
    82→- **Contest another's domain (破域, poyu).** Enter an enemy's domain and destabilize its law-structure, forcing the enemy's will to compete with yours. Distinct from attacking the enemy because the target is the domain (the enemy's externalized dantian), not the enemy's body. A successful poyu collapses the enemy's domain — a catastrophic injury to a Spirit Severance cultivator, often fatal.
    83→- **Sever an attachment (斬緣, zhan yuan).** Permanently release a specific emotional bond — love for a person, grief for a loss, fear of a threat, hatred for an enemy. Distinct from Foundation Establishment's 合 (integrate a disturbance, which transforms the bond into a stable form) because severance destroys the bond. The cultivator becomes different — less, but also differently constituted. This is the verb that gives Spirit Severance its name.
    84→- **Hold the domain against tribulation (守域, shouyu).** When tribulation strikes (the domain's opening triggers tribulation, per doc 15 §6.2), the domain itself is the test surface. Distinct from prior stations' tribulations (which test the cultivator's body, core, or anchor) because the test is whether the domain can sustain its law under the Precelestial's lawful pressure.
    85→- **Anchor a law in the heart-mind (印心, yinxin).** Inscribe the domain's law in your own heart-mind so enforcement flows through your will rather than through ambient qi. Distinct from 制法 because the enforcer becomes the cultivator's attention; faster, more powerful, more dangerous (the cultivator bears the law's backlash directly if the law is contested).
    86→
    87→### New responsibilities
    88→- **Domain stewardship.** The beings in the domain — mortals, cultivators, beasts, place-spirits — are your responsibility. Their suffering under the domain's law is your fault if the law is wrong. You cannot escape this; the domain is your exteriorized self.
    89→- **Law-accountability.** The laws you author have consequences you bear. A law that produces unintended harm — "no fire shall burn" producing famine when the harvest cannot be dried — is your harm. You cannot disclaim it.
    90→- **Stratum-accountability.** A domain's law, if it touches a stratum boundary, is felt in the Precelestial; the courts notice. A domain that harbors a demonic cultivator draws the courts' attention to the domain's author.
    91→- **Lineage duty (elevated).** A Spirit Severance cultivator is a lineage's strongest living asset; the lineage's ancestral court expects consultation, the living cultivators expect protection, the students expect the breakthrough transmission.
    92→
    93→### New constraints
    94→- **Domain is place-bound.** You cannot move the domain. You can leave it (a Spirit Severance cultivator is mobile, unlike a Void Amalgamation cultivator), but while absent, the domain's law weakens and the domain's edge is contestable. Long absences invite rivals.
    95→- **Severing attachments is irreversible.** A severed bond cannot be re-severed (it is already gone); it cannot be re-forged (the heart-mind's structure has changed). The cultivator who severs their love for a spouse becomes a being who does not love that spouse; the spouse experiences this as abandonment; the cultivator experiences this as... nothing, which is the horror of it.
    96→- **Law-paradox.** A law that contradicts a deeper Acquired law fails, often catastrophically. You must, before inscribing, model the law's compatibility against the known deeper laws — a study that can take years.
    97→- **Tribulation risk.** The domain's opening triggers tribulation; each subsequent law-inscription may trigger a smaller law-tribulation (form 3). You cannot inscribe laws casually; each is a lawful event the Precelestial notices.
    98→- **The body is now secondary but not absent.** You can survive the body's death (the domain persists; the anchor is in the domain), but the body is still your primary seat for action outside the domain. A Spirit Severance cultivator whose body is killed becomes a domain-bound spirit — a ghost of themselves, weakened, easily contested.
    99→
   100→### Failure modes
   101→- **Domain collapse.** A stronger cultivator's 破域, a tribulation that exceeds the domain's law, or a paradox in the inscribed law collapses the domain. The externalized dantian shatters; the body is overwhelmed; the anchor is often dispersed. The most common Spirit Severance death.
   102→- **Severance regret.** You sever an attachment you later realize you needed — the love that motivated you, the grief that drove your practice, the fear that kept you cautious. The severance is irreversible. You continue, but diminished in a way you cannot repair. The "less" the practice produces; sometimes the less is too much.
   103→- **Law-backlash.** An inscribed law produces unintended consequences that destroy what you were trying to protect. The "no fire" law produces famine; the "violence-backlash" law produces a community unable to defend itself. You bear the harm.
   104→- **Domain-stagnation.** A stagnant domain is a brittle domain; the cultivator who does not deliberately develop the domain's law finds, after centuries, that the domain is no longer a living exterior of the self but a fossil of the self the cultivator was when they opened it.
   105→- **Domain-identification 心魔.** The cultivator, perceiving the domain as self, treats the domain's beings as internal — the way one treats one's own thoughts and organs. A mortal in the domain who defies the law is perceived as a mutiny in the cultivator's own body; the response is correspondingly violent. The "tyrant of the domain" 心魔 — the specific madness of externalized selfhood.
   106→- **Tribulation death.** The domain's opening tribulation, or a subsequent law's tribulation, exceeds capacity. The domain collapses; the cultivator dies. Final death is common at this station.
   107→
   108→### What it feels like
   109→The first time you open a domain, you externalize your upper dantian into a place. The dantian was a center in your body; it is now a room, a clearing, a hilltop — the bounded region where your will holds. The feeling is sovereignty. Within this space, what you say, is. The first law you inscribe — even a small one, "no fire shall burn within this grove" — is the most vertiginous verb in cultivation short of Mahayana authorship. You speak, and the world hears you, and the world obeys. The fire, lit, goes out. You did not put it out; the world put it out, on your behalf, because you said so. The feeling is not power — power was already familiar. The feeling is *legitimacy*. The world treats your will as lawful, and you understand, for the first time, what law is: it is the world's habit of obedience, extended now to you.
   110→
   111→The domain-perception comes with the opening. You feel the domain as yourself. The leaves on the trees at the domain's far edge are felt the way you feel the hairs on your arm — not perceived at a distance, but *as self*. A mortal walking through the domain is felt the way you feel a thought moving through your mind. The boundary is the limit of your self; beyond it, the world is other again.
   112→
   113→The severance is the verb that gives the station its name, and it is the most disorienting practice in cultivation. You can, with great effort, cut away a piece of yourself: your love for a person, your grief for a loss, your fear of a threat, your hatred for an enemy. The piece is gone. You are different. You are also less. The first time you sever — say, the grief over a parent long dead — you wake the next morning and the grief is not there. The parent is still dead; you still remember them; but the grief, the specific gravity of their absence, is gone. You are lighter. You are also lessened in a way you cannot articulate. The grief was a part of you, and now it is not, and you are the being who remains. The cultivators who survive this practice are the ones who sever rarely and with great deliberation. The ones who do not survive it are the ones who sever often — who cut away every attachment that burdens them until they are beings of pure will, uncontaminated by love or grief or fear, and they are also alone in a way no mortal loneliness approaches.
   114→
   115→The law-structure perception lets you see laws as objects. You walk through a region and perceive, layered over the qi-topography, the laws that govern it — the law that says water flows downhill, the law that says fire burns, the law that says a mortal's bardo window is forty-nine days (a Mahayana inscription, you now perceive; not the original law). These are not rules; they are structures, with joints and load-bearing walls, and you can see where they could be amended, contested, severed. This is the perception that prepares you for Mahayana, where amendment becomes authorship.
   116→
   117→The first time another cultivator contests your domain — presses at the edge with their will, testing your law — you feel it as a pressure at the boundary of your self. It is not metaphorical. Your domain is you, externally; a contest is an assault. You respond with your full attention, because the contest is for your self. A successful defense is felt as the rival's will bending away from your edge, like a hand pushed back from a door. A failed defense is felt as your edge folding inward, your law thinning, your self shrinking. The cultivator who loses a domain-contest loses themselves, partially, and may not recover.
   118→
   119→You are powerful. You are sovereign within your domain. You are also burdened with the beings in your domain, the laws you have inscribed, the attachments you have severed, the tribulations that gather at your edge. The world treats you as a co-author of its law, and the responsibility of co-authorship is the weight you carry for the rest of your very long life.

   123→## Station 8: Void Amalgamation (合虛, hexu)
   124→
   125→### Essence
   126→The cultivator bonds to a place — a grotto-heaven, a sacred mountain, a region — and becomes place-anchored. The bonded place is the cultivator's body, now; the flesh-body is one seat among many within it. The "earth immortal" (地仙, dixian) station. Per doc 24 §1.10, this station does NOT trigger tribulation: the bond is internal re-architecture within the Acquired Stratum, not a boundary crossing.
   127→
   128→### Lifespan
   129→~5000 years. Aging nearly halts; a 2000-year-old Void Amalgamation cultivator appears ~40 (when embodied; the bonded place has no appearance). Capability envelope: physical output is the bonded place's physicality (a mountain's mass, a river's flow) — orders above a Spirit Severance domain; speed is the place's natural qi-flow pace (slow by bodily standards, but the flesh-body can still move at Spirit Severance speeds within the place); survivability is the bonded place's durability — only by destroying the place itself; qi throughput is the bonded place's total ambient qi (vastly more than a Spirit Severance domain, less than a Precelestial stratum); perception range is the bonded place's full extent; authority is near-absolute within the bonded place.
   130→
   131→### New perceptions
   132→- **Place-resonance perception.** The cultivator perceives the bonded place as self — not as domain-perception (Spirit Severance, where the cultivator perceives the domain as an extension of self) but as identity. The mountain's streams are the cultivator's circulation; the forests are the cultivator's breath; the creatures are the cultivator's microflora. Different from domain-perception because it is not bounded by a contested edge — the bonded place has no contested boundary; it is the cultivator's body, and the cultivator's body cannot be contested from outside without killing the cultivator.
   133→- **Place-history perception (識境 — see Verbs).** The cultivator perceives the bonded place's full qi-history — every event the place has witnessed, every being that has lived in it, every residue it has accumulated. A temporal-depth perception, not a spatial one. The genuinely new modality the prior summary's reskin-verb concealed.
   134→- **Stratum-edge perception.** The cultivator perceives the boundary between the Acquired and Precelestial strata directly. The bonded place, if it includes a grotto-heaven or a sacred mountain, often touches this boundary; the cultivator perceives the boundary's lawful texture — where it is thin, where it is reinforced, where tribulation will cross it. The perception that prepares the cultivator for Tribulation Crossing.
   135→- **Ancestral-court handle perception.** The cultivator perceives, in the bonded place's qi-connection to the Acquired Stratum, the "handle" by which the lineage's ancestral court can retrieve the cultivator's anchor after bodily death (doc 24 §1.9). The perception that confirms Void Amalgamation as the ancestral-court eligibility threshold.
   136→
   137→### New verbs
   138→- **Bond to a place (合地, hedi).** The cultivator bonds to a grotto-heaven, a sacred mountain, or a region. The bond is permanent and mutual: the place becomes the cultivator's body; the cultivator becomes the place's will. Distinct from Spirit Severance's 開域 (open a domain) because the domain is the cultivator's externalized dantian (a created place); the bond is with a place that already exists (a grotto-heaven, a mountain, a region with its own qi-history and ecology). The cultivator does not create the bonded place; they join it. This is why Void Amalgamation does NOT trigger tribulation (doc 24 §1.10) — the bond is internal re-architecture, not a boundary crossing.
   139→- **Know the place (識境, shi jing).** Perceive the bonded place's full qi-history. Distinct from 讀殘 (read residue, Qi Condensation) on three axes: (a) scope is the place's entire history (centuries to millennia), not a single recent residue; (b) the perception is temporal-depth, not spatial; (c) the cultivator perceives events they did not witness and could not have witnessed, by reading the place's qi-archive. This is the perception-verb that the critics identified as the missing genuinely-new verb at this station.
   140→- **Turn the place (轉境, zhuan jing).** Rotate the bonded place's phase-affinity — a water-phase place toward wood, a yin-dominant place toward yang. Distinct from 運氣 (route qi, Qi Condensation) on three axes: (a) the target is the place's law-structure (its phase-affinity is a lawful property), not its qi; (b) the effect is permanent, not temporary; (c) the cultivator does not move qi through their body to effect the change — the change is at the place's law-level, mediated by the bond. The verb that lets a Void Amalgamation cultivator remediate a contaminated or imbalanced bonded place over decades.
   141→- **Sustain the place (持境, chi jing).** Actively maintain the bonded place's qi-coherence against contamination — grief-residue from deaths, violence-residue from battles, phase-imbalance from over-tapping, law-residue from a Spirit Severance domain that overlapped. Distinct from 洩氣 (vent into a sink, Qi Condensation) on three axes: (a) the target is the place, not the cultivator's body; (b) the contamination is often lawful (the grief-residue of the place's dead is a lawful record, not waste) and cannot simply be vented — it must be honored and integrated; (c) the practice is continuous, not episodic.
   142→- **Open a grotto-heaven (開洞天, kai dongtian).** Create or claim a pocket world within the Acquired Stratum, accessible through a geographic anchor. Distinct from 合地 because opening a grotto-heaven is the act of creating a new bonded place — typically the cultivator's second, after their first bond. Subsequent bonds are secondary and weaker.
   143→- **Fall when the place falls (同盡, tongjin).** The cultivator's life is linked to the bonded place; if the place is destroyed, the cultivator dies. Not a verb the cultivator performs; the lawful consequence of the bond, accepted at bonding. Listed here because the cultivator's acceptance is the verb — the consent that completes the bond. Distinct from 保命 (survive bodily death, Nascent Soul) because survival is conditional on the place's survival, not on a prepared refuge.
   144→
   145→**[Retired verb: 用境 (draw on place-qi). The critics correctly identified this as a reskin of 運氣 (route qi): "route qi through the body to do work, with the qi coming from a bonded place instead of ambient" — same act-class. The three replacement verbs above — 識境, 轉境, 持境 — are genuinely new act-classes: temporal-depth perception, law-structure amendment, ecological maintenance. None is a renamed routing verb.]**
   146→
   147→### New responsibilities
   148→- **Place-stewardship.** The bonded place's ecology and inhabitants are your body, now. A drought is your illness; a pestilence is your infection; a war is your wound. You cannot ignore the place's health the way a Spirit Severance cultivator can ignore their domain's beings.
   149→- **Ancestral-court readiness.** You are now eligible for ancestral-court retrieval after bodily death. The lineage expects you to prepare the bonded place's "handle" and to participate in the court's consultations during life.
   150→- **Stratum-edge guardianship.** You perceive the stratum boundary directly; you are expected to guard it where it touches the bonded place. A grotto-heaven's anchor is a place where the boundary is thin; a careless cultivator at the anchor can let Precelestial qi leak or Acquired qi escape.
   151→- **Tribulation-shelter.** Your bonded place is a refuge for lower-realm cultivators facing tribulation — the place's qi-density buffers the tribulation's energy. You are expected to shelter your lineage's cultivators (and, by courtesy, allied lineages') during tribulations, at the cost of the place's coherence.
   152→
   153→### New constraints
   154→- **Place-bound.** You cannot leave the bonded place for long without weakening. A short absence (days to weeks) is sustainable; a long absence (months to years) weakens the bond, the place, and the cultivator. A century of absence dissolves the bond; you revert to Spirit Severance, often lethally.
   155→- **Place-death is your death.** The bonded place's destruction kills you. There is no refuge, no prepared sanctum, no escape; the bond is your body, and the body's death is your death. The most severe constraint in cultivation short of Tribulation Crossing.
   156→- **No tribulation, no tribulation-shelter for yourself.** Void Amalgamation does NOT trigger tribulation (doc 24 §1.10), which means you cannot use tribulation-farming techniques. The bonded place is also not a tribulation-proof shelter for you personally; a tribulation triggered by your prior actions (e.g., a Spirit Severance law-amendment before bonding) still finds you.
   157→- **Slowness.** Your perception, when identified with the place, runs at the place's pace. A mortal lifetime passes while you notice the seasons. This makes you useless for mortal-timescale events — a war that finishes in five years is, to you, a momentary disturbance.
   158→- **The flesh-body is now one seat among many.** You can still act through your flesh-body (at Spirit Severance-level capability within the bonded place), but it is no longer your primary seat. A Void Amalgamation cultivator who insists on acting primarily through the flesh-body finds the bond weakening, the place sickening, and their cultivation regressing.
   159→
   160→### Failure modes
   161→- **Place-destruction.** An enemy (a Spirit Severance cultivator with a stronger domain; a Tribulation Crossing cultivator; a coalition) destroys the bonded place. You die. The most common Void Amalgamation death.
   162→- **Place-corruption.** The bonded place is contaminated — by grief-residue from a massacre, by violence-residue from a war, by phase-imbalance from over-tapping, by law-residue from a contested domain. You sicken with the place. Recovery requires 持境 over decades, or a 轉境 that shifts the place's phase-affinity away from the contamination.
   163→- **Place-boredom (the specific 心魔).** Millennia in one place produce a specific 心魔: the cultivator becomes a feature of the landscape, losing volition. The mountain sleeps; the cultivator sleeps with it. The cultivator's will, no longer exercised, atrophies; the bonded place's coherence, no longer actively sustained, drifts; the cultivator and the place become a sleeping thing that was once a person. The "earth immortal" 心魔 — the genre's "the old immortal who has forgotten they were ever a person" trope, made mechanical.
   164→- **Bond-attenuation.** Long absence weakens the bond. A century of absence dissolves the bond; the cultivator reverts to Spirit Severance, often lethally.
   165→- **Ancestral-court corruption (inherited).** If your lineage's ancestral court is corrupted (doc 15 §3.5), your consultations are corrupted too. The counsel is confident, commanding, present — and wrong. Following corrupted counsel into a place-bonding error (bonding to a place the corrupted ancestor recommends, for reasons you do not perceive are the ancestor's drift) leaves you bound to a place that does not suit you, with no easy escape.
   166→- **Stratum-edge breach.** A careless cultivator at the bonded place's stratum-edge anchor can let Precelestial qi leak into the Acquired Stratum (producing tribulation in the surrounding region) or let Acquired qi escape into the Precelestial (weakening the bonded place's qi-density). You are the steward; a breach is your fault.
   167→
   168→### What it feels like
   169→You bond to the mountain and you become the mountain. The first morning after the bond, you wake — not in your flesh-body (which lies in a hut on the mountain's lower slope, breathing slowly) — but as the mountain. The streams are your circulation; you feel them as you feel your blood. The forests are your breath; you feel the trees' respiration as you feel your lungs. The creatures — the foxes, the pheasants, the small spirits in the groves — are your microflora; you feel them as you feel the bacteria in your gut. The mountain's granite is your bone. The mountain's soil is your flesh. You are vast and slow. A mortal lifetime passes while you notice the seasons.
   170→
   171→The 識境 (know the place) perception is the deepest perception in cultivation short of law-source perception (Mahayana). You read the mountain's history in its qi — every event the mountain has witnessed, every being that has lived on it, every residue it has accumulated. You perceive the hunting party that camped on the eastern ridge three hundred years ago. You perceive the place-spirit that cohered above the mountain's wood-phase vein six hundred years ago and was destroyed when a Qi Condensation cultivator shattered its anchor-object two hundred years ago. You perceive the dragon-vein that underlies the mountain and feeds its surface trace. The mountain is a temporal archive, and you read it the way a Core Formation cultivator reads a qi-residue — but at a temporal depth no Core Formation cultivator could approach.
   172→
   173→The 轉境 (turn the place) verb is the most consequential act of cultivation short of Mahayana law-authorship. You rotate the mountain's phase-affinity — a wood-phase mountain turned toward water, say, to support a different ecology. The change takes years of sustained attention. You feel the mountain's law-structure shift under your will; you feel the streams' phase-signature change; you feel the forests' qi-quality alter. The mountain's ecology responds, slowly, over the years of the turning. The foxes that depended on wood-phase qi migrate or die; the water-phase spirits that could not cohere before begin to form. You are reshaping a place's law, and the place is reshaping its beings, and the beings' suffering is your doing. This is the responsibility of place-bonding at its heaviest.
   174→
   175→The 持境 (sustain the place) verb is the daily practice of the earth immortal. The mountain accumulates contamination — grief-residue from the death of a hunter on its slopes; violence-residue from a skirmish between two Spirit Severance domains that overlapped the mountain's edge; phase-imbalance from a sect that tapped the mountain's vein without courtesy. You clean this. You honor the grief-residue (it is a lawful record of a real death; you cannot simply vent it), integrate it into the mountain's qi-archive, and let it fade naturally. You vent the violence-residue into a sink (the mountain's deep earth-phase vein absorbs it). You re-balance the phase-imbalance by routing the sect's tapping through a courtesy-channel that returns a portion of the tapped qi to the mountain. Without this, the mountain sickens, and you sicken with it. With it, the mountain flourishes, and you flourish with it.
   176→
   177→The slowness is the temptation. The mountain's tempo is the tempo of seasons; the cultivator identified with the mountain drifts toward that tempo. A mortal war that finishes in five years is, to the mountain, a momentary disturbance. A mortal dynasty that rises and falls in three hundred years is, to the mountain, a single growing-season. The cultivator who lets themselves drift into the mountain's tempo becomes a feature of the landscape — a sleeping mountain, dreaming of being a person. The cultivators who resist this drift are the ones who keep one human attachment: a student, a rival, a lineage, a feud. Something that forces them to remain a person and not just a place. The student visits; the cultivator must wake. The rival contests the edge; the cultivator must respond. The lineage asks for consultation; the cultivator must speak. The attachment is the tether that keeps the cultivator a cultivator. Without it, they are a mountain. With it, they are a person who is also a mountain. The difference is everything.
   178→
   179→You are vast. You are slow. You are nearly unkillable (only by destroying the place itself). You are also nearly immobile (the bonded place is your body; you cannot move it). Your power is the power of a place — patient, durable, contextual. Your vulnerability is the vulnerability of a place — patient, durable, contextual. You will outlive every mortal you have ever known. You will outlive most cultivators. You will perceive the rise and fall of dynasties the way a mortal perceives the change of weather. The world will change around you, and you will be the mountain that does not change, and the cultivators of future generations will come to your slopes and seek your counsel, and you will give it from the deep temporal archive of your bonded place, and they will leave, and you will sleep again, and the seasons will pass, and you will be the mountain.

   183→## Station 9: Tribulation Crossing (渡劫, dujie)
   184→
   185→### Essence
   186→The cultivator crosses a tribulation to ascend from the Acquired Stratum to the Precelestial. Most die. Those who succeed become heavenly immortals (天仙, tianxian) — beings of the Precelestial, subject to its slow time and its fallible institutions. Per doc 15 §8, there is no middle outcome: the cultivator either crosses or dies.
   187→
   188→### Lifespan
   189→~10000 years. Aging nearly halts; a 5000-year-old Tribulation Crossing cultivator appears ~35 (in flesh-body; the heavenly immortal's true form is a qi-body sustained by Precelestial qi). Capability envelope: physical output bounded by the Precelestial's substrate-density (orders above Void Amalgamation, but expressed through the flesh-body which is now a "garment"); speed can include stratum-travel (minutes to hours between strata); survivability reinforced by Precelestial qi-metabolism (can survive in qi-dead Acquired zones, Spirit Wilds, and Law Reaches); qi throughput is undifferentiated Precelestial qi (vastly more than Void Amalgamation's place-qi); perception range is stratum-spanning (the Precelestial's full extent, the Acquired's full extent, and the boundary between them); authority is partial peerage in the Precelestial courts.
   190→
   191→### New perceptions
   192→- **Stratum perception.** The cultivator perceives the Precelestial directly — its qi-density, its law-texture, its inhabitants, its institutions. Genuinely new because the Precelestial was, before this station, perceived only indirectly (through tribulation-precursors at Spirit Severance, through stratum-edge perception at Void Amalgamation). The cultivator now sees the stratum above as a place, with geography and inhabitants and politics.
   193→- **Tribulation-structure perception.** The cultivator perceives the tribulation as a lawful process, not a random storm. The lightning's phase-resonance, the heart-demon's specific attachment, the law-tribulation's compatibility-probe, the karmic-tribulation's ledger-reading — each is perceived as a structured test, not an attack. The perception that lets a Tribulation Crossing cultivator prepare for a tribulation's specific form, when prior stations only perceived its gathering.
   194→- **Precelestial-law perception.** The cultivator perceives the Precelestial's substrate-law directly — the law that governs how undifferentiated qi becomes differentiated, how the strata relate, how tribulation is triggered. The perception that prepares the cultivator for Mahayana law-authorship; without it, authoring a law is blind inscription.
   195→- **Time-debt perception.** The cultivator perceives the 1:365 time-ratio directly — the Precelestial's slow time, the Acquired's fast time, and the lawful relationship between them. The perception of a temporal lawful structure, not the perception of time passing (which all beings have).
   196→
   197→### New verbs
   198→- **Cross a tribulation (渡劫, dujie).** The single most dangerous verb in cultivation. At the peak of Void Amalgamation, deliberately enter the tribulation that tests whether your internal architecture can sustain the Precelestial's qi-density. Success is permanent ascension; failure is final death (the anchor disperses). No middle outcome. Distinct from prior stations' tribulations (which test a realm-transition) because this tribulation tests a STRATUM-transition; the proof is whether the cultivator's being can sustain a different law-substrate.
   199→- **Travel between strata (昇降, shengjiang).** The heavenly immortal moves between Acquired and Precelestial at will, through specific stratum-crossings (maintained roads, grotto-heaven anchors, the courts' sanctioned gates). Distinct from 出神 (project spirit, Nascent Soul) because the traveler is the whole being (body included), not a projected anchor; the travel is between strata, not within one. The verb's cost is the time-debt: every Precelestial day costs an Acquired year.
   200→- **Enter the Precelestial courts as a peer (朝天, chaotian).** The heavenly immortal presents themselves to the Courts of Heaven as a peer — not a supplicant, not a petitioner, but a being of the Precelestial with the right to be heard. Distinct from 受職 (bear an oath of office, Foundation Establishment) because the office is in the Precelestial; the courts' jurisdiction is stratum-spanning; the oath binds the cultivator to the courts' politics.
   201→- **Consume undifferentiated Precelestial qi (食元, shiyuan).** The heavenly immortal admits undifferentiated Precelestial qi and either lets it pass through unchanged or, with effort, condenses it into a specific phase for emission. Distinct from 自養 (sustain a qi field without ambient qi, Core Formation) because the qi-source is the Precelestial (a different stratum's substrate), not the cultivator's internal reservoir. The verb that frees the heavenly immortal from Acquired-qi dependence.
   202→- **Bear the time-debt (承時, chengshi).** The heavenly immortal accepts the Precelestial's slow time as their condition. Every Precelestial day costs an Acquired year. The cultivator can return to the Acquired briefly, but every return costs Precelestial time. The lawful condition that makes the Precelestial a place the cultivator cannot live in without cost. Distinct from prior stations' time-perception because the cultivator now ACTS under the time-debt, not merely perceives it.
   203→
   204→### New responsibilities
   205→- **Stratum-stewardship.** You are now a being of the Precelestial; your actions affect both strata. A law authored in a Law Reach propagates to the Acquired; a tribulation triggered in the Acquired is felt in the Precelestial. You are accountable to both strata's lawful processes.
   206→- **Courts' jurisdiction.** You are now subject to the Courts of Heaven's jurisdiction — their records, their enforcement, their politics. A crime in the Precelestial is prosecuted by the courts; a crime in the Acquired that affects the Precelestial is also prosecuted. You cannot escape the courts by ascending; ascending enters the courts' world.
   207→- **Ancestral-court duty (elevated).** Having crossed, you are now a persistent ancestor of your lineage's ancestral court (upon bodily death, the anchor persists in the Precelestial; doc 15 §3.2). You are expected to give counsel, to intervene in the lineage's existential crises (at the cost of persistence-budget), and to refrain from corrupted drift.
   208→- **Time-debt stewardship.** Your every Precelestial action costs Acquired time. You are expected to be deliberate — every visit to the Precelestial, every court appearance, every Law Reach exploration costs the Acquired world time you cannot get back. Frivolous Precelestial residence is, in effect, frivolous Acquired-world abandonment.
   209→
   210→### New constraints
   211→- **Time-debt is absolute.** Every Precelestial day = 1 Acquired year. A month in the Precelestial = 30 Acquired years. A year in the Precelestial = 365 Acquired years. You cannot escape this; ascending makes it your permanent condition.
   212→- **Tribulation-accumulation.** Each subsequent tribulation is harder. A Tribulation Crossing cultivator who lingers faces escalating tribulations until they cross a subsequent boundary (Mahayana) or die. The Precelestial does not permit stasis; the cultivator who does not advance is ground down.
   213→- **Stratum-shock.** The Precelestial's qi-density is overwhelming to a newly-crossed immortal. Acclimatization takes decades (in Precelestial time, which is centuries in Acquired time). A cultivator who attempts to act in the Precelestial before acclimatization is crushed — the qi-density itself is the danger, not any enemy.
   214→- **The flesh-body is now a garment.** You can still manifest a flesh-body in the Acquired (and must, to act there for any duration), but it is no longer your primary being. Your primary being is the qi-body sustained by Precelestial qi. The flesh-body can be killed without killing you, but the killing is a wound — you must spend Precelestial time to re-grow it.
   215→- **Final death is always possible.** A Tribulation Crossing cultivator can be killed by: a stronger Precelestial being, a Law Reach collapse, a subsequent tribulation's failure, the ancestral-court's persistence-budget exhaustion. The ~10000-year lifespan is the natural span; it is not immortality.
   216→
   217→### Failure modes
   218→- **Tribulation death.** The most common failure at this station. The tribulation's specific form probes a specific weakness; the architecture does not hold; the anchor disperses. No second attempt.
   219→- **Stratum-shock.** A newly-crossed immortal, unacclimated, attempts to act in the Precelestial. The qi-density crushes them; the qi-body destabilizes; without rapid retreat to the Acquired (costing Acquired time), the cultivator disperses.
   220→- **Time-loss.** The cultivator returns from a Precelestial visit to find a century has passed and everyone they knew is dead. Not a metaphysical failure but a personal one; grief-stricken, the cultivator may regress (a heart-mind collapse that, in extreme cases, produces a 心魔 of obsessive attachment to the past).
   221→- **Subsequent-tribulation escalation.** The cultivator lingers, faces escalating tribulations, and is ground down. Each tribulation is harder; eventually one exceeds capacity; the anchor disperses.
   222→- **Courts' prosecution.** The cultivator commits a crime in the Precelestial (unauthorized Law Reach entry, unlawful ancestor intervention, court-record falsification) and is prosecuted. Punishments include persistence-budget confiscation, office-stripping, anchor-binding, and (for the gravest crimes) final dispersal.
   223→- **Ancestral-court drift (inherited).** The cultivator, now an eligible ancestor, consults with their lineage's ancestral court and receives corrupted counsel (doc 15 §3.5). Lacking the perspective to recognize the drift, follows it into a Precelestial error — an unlawful Law Reach entry, a contested law, an office-usurpation — that the courts prosecute.
   224→- **Time-debt 心魔.** The cultivator, perceiving the Acquired world aging past them every Precelestial day, develops a specific 心魔: the cultivator becomes unable to engage with the Acquired world at all, perceiving it as a frantic, mayfly place not worth their attention. The cultivator retreats into the Precelestial and ceases to care about the Acquired world — including the lineage they founded, the places they bonded, the students they taught. The "immortal who has forgotten the world" 心魔; the specific madness of the time-debt.
   225→
   226→### What it feels like
   227→The tribulation gathers and you understand, for the first time, that the world is judging you — not morally, but lawfully, the way a river judges a dam. Either you are strong enough to hold, or you break. The lightning comes first, phase-resonant, probing your meridians' weakest junctions. You hold. The heart-demon comes next — your specific unresolved attachments, surfaced and confronted. You integrate (or you do not, and you break). The karmic tribulation comes (if your ledger is heavy) — the weight of your acts, sustained in the new state. You bear it (or you do not, and you break). The law-tribulation comes (if you have authored law) — the law you wrote, tested for compatibility with the Precelestial's substrate. The law holds (or it does not, and you break).
   228→
   229→The crossing itself is not a fight. It is a proof. You prove that your integrated system can sustain the Precelestial's density. The Precelestial's qi enters you — undifferentiated, dense, lawful — and either your architecture channels it (and you are transformed) or your architecture ruptures (and you disperse). There is no middle outcome. The proof takes seconds; the preparation took centuries; the outcome is permanent.
   230→
   231→If you cross, the world transforms. The Acquired world's time, which you have lived inside your entire life, suddenly feels fast — a rushing river you have stepped out of. The Precelestial's time feels thick — a medium you can move through with weight and intention. You are, for the first time, in the world the laws come from. You are also, for the first time, accountable to that world's slow, fallible, beautiful, corrupt institutions. You are no longer a cultivator. You are an immortal. The difference is the difference between a being in the world and a being of it.
   232→
   233→The first time you return to the Acquired — to visit your lineage, to consult a student, to attend a funeral — you perceive the time-debt directly. A month in the Precelestial has cost thirty Acquired years. The student you left at Foundation Establishment is now Nascent Soul, or dead, or has children and grandchildren who are themselves cultivators. The lineage's hall has new tablets; the old tablets are dust. The mountain you bonded (if you were Void Amalgamation) has a new place-spirit (the old one faded, was destroyed, or was replaced). The dynasty has changed. You walk through a world that is your world and is not your world. You are a stranger in the place you came from.
   234→
   235→The courts are the next shock. You present yourself (朝天) and you are received — not as a supplicant but as a peer. The courts' officials are cultivators who crossed centuries or millennia ago; they speak slowly (the Precelestial's tempo); they are patient, formal, and (often) corrupt (doc 15 §2.4). You are now one of them. You may take office. You may go rogue. You may join an ancestral court. You may retreat to the Spirit Wilds. You may explore the Law Reaches (where Mahayana authorship is possible). The Precelestial is vast and you are now a being in it.
   236→
   237→The first time you perceive the Acquired world from the Precelestial, you understand the tragic engine. The Acquired world is fast — its beings born, age, die in what is, to you, the blink of an eye. A mortal lifetime is a Precelestial afternoon. A cultivator's career is a Precelestial season. You perceive, from above, the frenetic mayfly urgency of the Acquired world, and you understand — finally, completely — why the Precelestial's institutions are slow, formal, patient. They have to be. They operate at a tempo where a mortal dynasty is a weather-pattern.
   238→
   239→You are powerful. You are nearly immortal (but not — final death is always possible). You are accountable to the Precelestial's institutions. You are subject to the time-debt, which is the genre's central tragic engine made permanent. Every visit to the Acquired costs Precelestial time; every residence in the Precelestial costs Acquired time. There is no escape from this. The cultivators who survive Tribulation Crossing with their heart-mind intact are the ones who maintain a deliberate relationship with the Acquired world — who return, briefly and purposefully, to attend the funerals of their descendants' descendants, to consult the lineage's living cultivators, to perceive (one more time) the village they came from. The cultivators who do not survive it as persons are the ones who let the Precelestial's tempo claim them entirely, who cease to return, who become slow formal patient beings of the courts with no memory of why they crossed.

   243→## Station 10: Mahayana (大乘, dacheng)
   244→
   245→### Essence
   246→The cultivator can author scoped world-laws. Per doc 24 §1.1, BOTH Acquired-authorship (amend existing Acquired law within a scope) AND Reach-authorship (create a new local substrate in the Precelestial's Law Reaches) are possible as distinct verbs. This is the endgame: the cultivator becomes a world-law author, with all the responsibility and constraint that entails. Not apotheosis — the Nameless Origin is untouched; new strata cannot be created; final death is always possible.
   247→
   248→### Lifespan
   249→~10000 years (not immortal; the same as Tribulation Crossing, but extended by the law-author's deeper integration with the stratum's law). Capability envelope: physical output is irrelevant (the cultivator can manifest a body but the body is a garment; the cultivator's effective capability is the law they author); speed includes Law Reach traversal and stratum-travel; survivability reinforced by the law-author's inscribed law (the law protects its author as one of its provisions, if the author chose to inscribe that); qi throughput is the stratum's qi (Acquired or Precelestial at need); perception range is stratum-spanning law-source perception; authority is the authority of a law-author — partial, scoped, consequential.
   250→
   251→### New perceptions
   252→- **Law-source perception.** The cultivator perceives the Nameless Origin's influence on the Acquired Stratum — not the Origin itself (which no one perceives), but its generative function as it manifests in the stratum's law-substrate. Genuinely new because the perception is of the stratum's source-condition, not its current state. The cultivator sees where the law comes from, not just where it is.
   253→- **Law-legacy perception.** The cultivator perceives the laws previous Mahayana cultivators authored — the layers of accumulated law-authorship that shape the current Acquired Stratum. Some of these laws are millennia old; their authors are dispersed, but their laws persist. The cultivator perceives them as one perceives a city's architecture — the layers of construction and demolition and reconstruction that produced the current cityscape.
   254→- **Law-paradox perception.** The cultivator perceives, before inscribing a law, whether the law would contradict a deeper law. The paradox is perceived as a structural stress — a law that would fit poorly into the existing law-architecture, producing unintended consequences. The perception that lets a Mahayana cultivator avoid the most catastrophic authorship errors.
   255→- **Authorship-venue perception.** The cultivator perceives the difference between an Acquired-authorship venue (where law already holds and can be amended) and a Reach-authorship venue (where law does not yet hold and can be created). The two venues feel different — the Acquired is dense with existing law; the Reach is sparse, silent, awaiting inscription.
   256→
   257→### New verbs
   258→- **Author an Acquired law (立法, lifa).** Write a rule that amends existing Acquired law within a bounded scope. The scope can be a region (the Cangli Riverlands), a class of beings (all mortals within the cultivator's reach), a class of acts (all anchor-harvesting), or a class of phenomena (all tribulation-farming). The law, if compatible with deeper Acquired law (perception-checked before inscription, then tribulation-tested after), is enforced by the Acquired Stratum itself. Distinct from Core Formation's 立法 (author a minor law) on three axes: (a) the enforcer is the Acquired Stratum itself, not the cultivator's qi; (b) the scope is stratum-wide (within the law's definition), not body-bounded; (c) the law persists after the cultivator's death — it is a stratum-level inscription, not a qi-technique. The verb that closes the bardo-harvest loophole in the Transcend ending (doc 26 §4.3).
   259→- **Author a Reach law (創法, chuangfa).** Write a rule that creates a new local substrate in the Precelestial's Law Reaches — a region where no law previously held. Distinct from 立法 (Acquired-authorship) on three axes: (a) the target is a law-less region, not a law-bearing one; (b) the act is creation, not amendment; (c) the law created is the region's first law, and the region becomes a place with the cultivator's law as its own — a mini-cosmos within the Precelestial. Distinct from 開洞天 (open a grotto-heaven, Void Amalgamation) because the grotto-heaven is within the Acquired Stratum's existing law; the Reach-law creates a new local law where none was. The verb that is the endgame's furthest reach.
   260→- **Ratify a law (批法, pifa).** Formally adopt a law authored by another Mahayana cultivator, adding your authority to it. A law with multiple ratifiers is harder to contest and easier to enforce. Distinct from 立法 because you are not the author; you are a co-sponsor. The verb by which a Mahayana cultivator builds a law-coalition.
   261→- **Contest a law (破法, pofa).** Destabilize and overturn an existing law — one you authored, one another authored, or (rarely and dangerously) one that is part of the deeper Acquired law. Distinct from Spirit Severance's 破域 (contest another's domain) because: (a) the target is a stratum-level law, not a domain-level one; (b) the contest is lawful (the Precelestial recognizes law-contestation as a lawful process), not an assault; (c) the contest's outcome is determined by the law's compatibility with deeper law and by the relative authority of the contesting cultivators, not by raw power.
   262→- **Bear the consequences of a law (承法, chengfa).** Accept the lawful consequences of the law you authored — its effects, its unintended consequences, its contests, its eventual fate. Not a verb you perform; the lawful condition of authorship, accepted at inscription. Listed here because your acceptance is the verb — the consent that completes the inscription. You bear the law's consequences forever (or until the law is overturned); the consequences include the law's effects on every being in its scope, the law's contests by other Mahayana cultivators, and the law's eventual decay (laws are not eternal; they erode over millennia unless maintained).
   263→- **Inscribe a law's maintenance (修法, xiufa).** Actively maintain a law you authored, reinforcing its inscription against erosion. Distinct from 持境 (sustain the place, Void Amalgamation) because the target is a stratum-level law, not a place-bond; the maintenance is at the law-substrate level, not the qi-coherence level. Without 修法, an authored law erodes over millennia; with it, the law persists as long as the cultivator maintains it.
   264→
   265→### New responsibilities
   266→- **Law-stewardship.** The laws you author affect everyone in their scope, forever, until overturned. You are responsible for the law's effects — including the unintended ones, including the ones that emerge only after centuries, including the ones that emerge only after your death (if the law persists).
   267→- **Stratum-accountability.** You are now a being whose actions shape the world itself. The Acquired Stratum's law-substrate is, in part, your inscription; you are accountable to the stratum's lawful processes (which include the Precelestial's tribulation system, the courts' law-enforcement, and other Mahayana cultivators' contestations).
   268→- **Coalition-duty.** A Mahayana cultivator who authors alone is fragile (their law is easily contested); one who authors in coalition is robust (the coalition's ratifiers share the law's authority). You are expected to participate in coalitions — to ratify allies' laws, to seek ratification for your own, to refrain from frivolous contests.
   269→- **Reach-stewardship (if Reach-authorship is performed).** A Reach whose law you authored is your mini-cosmos; its beings (if any develop) are your responsibility; its law's consequences are yours; its collapse (if it collapses) is your catastrophe.
   270→
   271→### New constraints
   272→- **Scope limit.** You cannot author laws in the Precelestial's main territory (only in the Law Reaches). You cannot rewrite the Nameless Origin (no one can). You cannot create new strata. The scope of Acquired-authorship is the Acquired Stratum; the scope of Reach-authorship is a single Reach at a time.
   273→- **Law-paradox.** A law that contradicts a deeper law fails, often catastrophically. The deeper law's resistance is the law-tribulation (form 3, doc 15 §6.3); you must survive the resistance and the paradox's consequences. A paradox you cannot sustain kills you.
   274→- **Endgame rivalry.** Other Mahayana cultivators may contest your laws, and you theirs. The contests are lawful but dangerous; a lost contest can kill (the law's collapse can disperse the author's anchor).
   275→- **Law-erosion.** Authored laws are not eternal; they erode over millennia unless maintained (修法). A Mahayana cultivator who authors many laws and does not maintain them finds, after centuries, that their laws have decayed into partial inscriptions — still lawful, still enforceable, but with gaps and ambiguities that produce unintended consequences.
   276→- **Final death is always possible.** A Mahayana cultivator can be killed by: a successful law-contest that disperses the author's anchor; a Law Reach collapse; a subsequent tribulation's failure; the cumulative consequence of an authored law's paradox. The ~10000-year lifespan is the natural span; it is not immortality. The genre's "the immortal falls" trope is ratable here.
   277→- **The bardo-harvest loophole itself (per doc 26).** The Transcend ending's law closes the loophole; the Mahayana cultivator who authors that law bears its consequences, which include the dispersal of every harvested anchor in the law's scope (including, possibly, the cultivator's own lineage's harvested anchors, if any), and the prosecution of every demonic cultivator in the scope. The cultivator who authors this law becomes the enemy of every demonic cultivator in the Acquired Stratum.
   278→
   279→### Failure modes
   280→- **Law-backlash.** An authored law has unintended consequences that destroy what you were trying to protect. The bardo-harvest-loophole law, for instance, may (if poorly scoped) also prevent lawful lineage retrieval of anchors from bardo — a consequence you did not intend and must live with (or contest your own law to amend).
   281→- **Law-contest loss.** A rival Mahayana cultivator contests your law and wins. The law collapses; your anchor, bound to the law's inscription, is dispersed. The most common Mahayana death.
   282→- **Endgame 心魔 (the specific madness of limitless power).** The cultivator becomes unable to distinguish their will from the world's law. They perceive the law as their own inscription (which it partially is), and they perceive the world as their own creation (which it partially is), and they lose the capacity to distinguish between the two. The cultivator becomes a being for whom the world is their image, and they are alone in it. The specific 心魔 of Mahayana; the genre's "the god who has forgotten they were ever a person" trope.
   283→- **Reach-collapse.** A Reach-law authored in a Law Reach destabilizes the Reach itself. The Reach collapses inward; the cultivator (if present) is taken with it. Rare (the law-paradox perception usually catches the instability before inscription) but not impossible.
   284→- **Authorship-isolation.** The cultivator, perceiving the law-source and the law-legacy, becomes unable to engage with beings who do not perceive them. Mortals are mayflies; cultivators are children; even other Mahayana cultivators are peers in a small and lonely club. The cultivator retreats into the Law Reaches and ceases to engage with the world they have shaped.
   285→- **Self-contestation.** Having authored many laws, you find that the laws contradict each other (a law against violence and a law requiring the protection of the weak, in a world where protection requires violence). The contradictions produce a law-paradox that you must resolve by contesting and overturning one of your own laws — a painful, costly, and (if the contradiction is deep) dangerous process.
   286→- **The cumulated consequence.** A Mahayana cultivator's laws, maintained over millennia, accumulate consequences the cultivator did not foresee. The bardo-harvest-loophole law, maintained for ten thousand years, has shaped the Acquired Stratum's bardo-processing in ways the cultivator could not have predicted. You perceive the consequences and bear them, because they are yours.
   287→
   288→### What it feels like
   289→You can now write in the world's book, and the world will read what you wrote and obey. The first law you author — even a small one, "no fire shall burn within this grove" (Acquired-authorship, scoped to a single grove) — is the most vertiginous verb in cultivation. You write the law (in qi-inscription, in a state of focused attention that takes hours to days), and the law takes. The grove's fires, lit, go out. You did not put them out; the Acquired Stratum put them out, on your behalf, because you wrote that they should. The feeling is not power — power was already familiar from Spirit Severance's domain. The feeling is *authorship*. You are no longer a being in the world; you are a being who shapes the world. The world's law is, in part, your law.
   290→
   291→The Acquired-authorship verb (立法) is the verb the Transcend ending's bardo-harvest-loophole law uses. You inscribe the law — "anchor-harvesting is unlawful in the Cangli Riverlands, and by precedent in the broader Acquired Stratum" — and the law, if it survives its tribulation (a law-tribulation, form 3, testing compatibility with deeper Acquired law), is enforced by the Acquired Stratum itself. Every demonic cultivator in the law's scope perceives the law taking effect — their harvest-techniques fail; their stored anchors begin to disperse lawfully, going to their next rebirths. Among them, Wang Xiaodi's anchor disperses. You perceive it disperse. You recognize, in the dispersal, the residue of the infant you practiced near decades (or, in your time-frame, centuries) ago. This is the deepest form of the first awe: not "the world is larger than I knew" but "the world's law is the world's love, and I have written a small part of it."
   292→
   293→The Reach-authorship verb (創法) is the verb that is the endgame's furthest reach. You travel to a Law Reach — a region of the Precelestial where the law has decayed or not formed, where the substrate is present but the inscription is absent. You stand in the Reach and you perceive the silence — not the absence of sound but the absence of law. The qi is present, undifferentiated; the substrate is present; the inscription is not. You prepare the inscription (years of preparation; the law-paradox perception checks compatibility; the coalition-duty brings ratifiers if you seek them). You inscribe. The Reach, where there was no law, now has your law. The region becomes a place with your law as its own — a mini-cosmos within the Precelestial, with its own internal logic, its own consequences, its own beings (if any develop). You are the author of a world. The vertigo is total. The Nameless Origin is untouched (you did not create the substrate; you only inscribed the law). New strata are not created (the Reach is within the Precelestial). But within the Reach, you are the law's author, and the Reach's law is yours.
   294→
   295→The law-legacy perception lets you see the layers of law-authorship that produced the current Acquired Stratum. You perceive the laws of previous Mahayana cultivators — the law that says a mortal's bardo window is forty-nine days (authored by a Mahayana cultivator four thousand years ago; the natural window was variable before); the law that says tribulation is triggered by specific boundary conditions (authored by a coalition of Mahayana cultivators two thousand years ago; before that, tribulation was less predictable); the law that says place-spirits' jurisdiction is bounded to their place (authored by a Mahayana cultivator sixteen hundred years ago; before that, place-spirits could extend their jurisdiction with veneration). The Acquired Stratum is, in part, a constructed artifact — the accumulated authorship of millennia of Mahayana cultivators, each inscribing their small piece, each bearing their consequences. You are now one of them. Your law will be one of the layers future Mahayana cultivators perceive.
   296→
   297→The law-paradox perception is the perception that lets you avoid the most catastrophic authorship errors. You perceive, before inscribing, where the law would fit poorly into the existing law-architecture. A law against all violence, for instance, would fit poorly (it contradicts the law that says place-spirits may enforce boundaries; it contradicts the law that says tribulation is lawful boundary-enforcement; it contradicts the deeper law that says beings act). The paradox is perceived as a structural stress. You do not inscribe that law. You inscribe a more carefully scoped law — "unlawful violence, defined as violence against a being who has not consented to violence and who is not themselves in violation of law, produces a qi-backlash proportional to the violence" — and the paradox perception shows this law fits more cleanly. You inscribe. The tribulation tests the law's compatibility. If the law holds, it is now part of the Acquired Stratum's law-substrate.
   298→
   299→The Mahayana cultivators who do not go mad are the ones who author few laws, slowly, with immense care, and who bear the consequences without flinching. The ones who go mad are the ones who author many laws, quickly, to remake the world in their image. The world does not resist; it obeys. And then the world is their image, and they are alone in it. This is the specific 心魔 of Mahayana: the madness of limitless power, the cultivator who has become a god in all but name and who has forgotten that the world was not always their image and does not, in fact, belong to them. The Nameless Origin is untouched. The new strata are not created. But within the cultivator's scope, the world is the cultivator's law, and the cultivator is alone.
   300→
   301→You are at the endgame. You can shape the world's law. You cannot own the world. You cannot become the Origin. You cannot create new strata. You can inscribe your small piece in the world's book, and bear the consequences, and (if you maintain the law) the piece persists after your death as one of the layers future Mahayana cultivators perceive. This is the endgame: not apotheosis, but authorship. Not omnipotence, but scoped, consequential, lawful, fallible authorship. The world is larger than you, and you are larger in it than you were, and the weight of the larger world is on your shoulders, and the weight is the law you wrote.
