     1→# 42 — The Mortal World's Continents
     2→
     3→**Status:** Candidate canon. The horizontal geography of the Mortal Stratum's planet, in fulfilment of doc 36 §2.5 (five continents named but four blank) and doc 20 §1.3 (great powers per region). Compliant with 24_RECONCILIATION_AND_DECISIONS §4 (Great Yan Dynasty, Cangli Riverlands, the named-places table), 31_NAMED_INSTITUTIONS_AND_HEXAGRAMS §2.9 (the Jade Void Holy Land's Cangli birth-origin and Azure Sword suzerainty), 37_COSMIC_HISTORY §6 (the Pillar Dynasty on Nan, the Crimson Vow Alliance on Dong), 38_THE_COURTS_OF_HEAVEN §5 (holy lands independent but deferential), 39_THE_MAHAYANA_PANTHEON §2.5 (Yin Mu's Cangwu grotto-heaven). The companion document 43_HOLY_LANDS_AND_GREAT_SECTS fully specifies the institutions named here.
     4→**Date:** 2026-08-03
     5→**Conventions:** Hanzi in parentheses on first use. No pinyin tone marks. Distances use the li at mortal scale and the heaven-stride (天步, doc 36 §6.1, ~10,000 li) at cosmic scale. Every named institution has a personality, a specialty, and a relationship to at least one other named institution.

     9→## 0. Why this document exists
    10→
    11→Doc 36 §2.5 names the five continents but populates only the Central Continent (中洲) — and only the Cangli Riverlands' tiny corner of it. Doc 20 §1.3 specifies that the cultivation world has 3-5 holy lands per continent and 5-10 great sects per region, but the corpus named only one holy land (the Jade Void, on another continent) and two sects (the Cangwu Sect, a small sect; the Azure Sword Sect, a great sect). Doc 37 adds two fallen civilizations — the Pillar Dynasty on the southern continent, the Crimson Vow Alliance on the eastern continent — but leaves the living civilizations undescribed. This document populates the four blank continents and the rest of the central one, names the other four holy lands (with full specs deferred to doc 43), and commits the geography that Act 2's and Act 3's cross-continental travel will traverse.
    12→
    13→The Cangli Riverlands is the protagonist's home; the Mortal World is the protagonist's horizon. Both are real.

    17→## 1. Zhong Continent (中洲) — the starting continent
    18→
    19→### 1.1 The Great Yan Dynasty (大燕朝)
    20→
    21→The largest continent's largest empire, founded 247 years ago (per doc 24 §4.1). The reigning emperor is Yan Sizong (燕嗣宗), 14th emperor, in the 14th year of the Jianhe reign (建和十四年). The imperial seat is **Yanjing (燕京)**, 5,400 li northwest of Wang Family Bend, in the middle reaches of the Yan River (燕江). Yanjing is a mortal city of two million; the **Heavenly Pivot Sect (天樞宗)**, the empire's favored great sect, maintains a compound inside the inner wall. The dynasty is in middle decline — bloated bureaucracy, stretched military, the eunuch faction (Grand Eunuch Wei Zhongxian) versus the literati faction (Chief Grand Secretary Li Tingyun), and an aging emperor with no designated heir. The succession crisis is the empire's open secret. The Azure Sword Sect, 500 li north of the Cangli Riverlands, is too peripheral to matter at court; the Heavenly Pivot Sect, two provinces away, is the sect the emperor calls on when a Core Formation problem needs solving.
    22→
    23→### 1.2 The Cangli Riverlands (沧篱江乡)
    24→
    25→The protagonist's home region, on Zhong's eastern coast in Qingzhou Prefecture (青州府), Jiangnan Province (江南省). The Cangli River (沧篱江) flows east into the Eastern Sea. The region is a backwater: low qi-density (the Green Mirror Vein is a minor vein, doc 31 §1.4), one small sect (the Cangwu Sect), one great sect in reach (the Azure Sword Sect). The named places (Wang Family Bend, Li Family Creek, Qinghe Market Town, Cangli County Seat, Qingzhou Prefectural Seat) are per doc 24 §4.3. The Cangwu Mountains (蒼梧山脈) back the plain to the south and west; the Azure Sword Sect sits in their northern extension. The region's cosmic-history distinction (doc 37 §9.5) — the Jade Void Holy Land's founder was born here 2,100 years ago — is invisible to the protagonist at game start.
    26→
    27→### 1.3 The Azure Sword Sect (碧劍宗)
    28→
    29→The regional hegemon of the central and northern Cangwu range (doc 31 §2). Sect master: **Leng Wushuang (冷無霜)**, Core Formation, age 340, appears 50. 400 disciples, three spirit veins, one grotto-heaven (the Jade Edge Grotto-Heaven, 玉涯洞天, in the western Cangwu's deep extension). Specialty: sword cultivation (碧劍, the jade-green sword). Personality: proud, inward, grieving — the sect's expansion toward the Cangli Riverlands (the latent spirit vein dispute, doc 31 §2.4) is, per Leng Qingxue's dissent (doc 34 §166), her mother's 80-year act of mourning for her surveyor husband, hardened into policy. The Azure Sword Sect is a formal vassal of the Jade Void Holy Land (one mid-grade spirit stone per decade; the Jade Void's implicit protection in return). The sect's Isolationist faction (Elder Jing You, doc 31 §2.5) quietly opposes the expansion, fearing it will lose the Jade Void's goodwill.
    30→
    31→### 1.4 The Cangwu Sect (蒼梧派)
    32→
    33→The small local sect, 100 li west of Wang Family Bend (doc 31 §1). Sect master: **Wu Changqing (吳長青)**, Qi Condensation peak, age 52. 30 disciples. One minor spirit vein (the Green Mirror Vein). Specialty: water-wood dual-phase practice (the Cangwu range is water-dominant, the foothills wood-dominant). Personality: poor, cautious, dignified — Wu Changqing knows his sect is a vassal-in-waiting and is betting everything on the protagonist. The Cangwu Sect's compound (the Green Mirror Valley, the Founder's Hall, the Five Phases Pavilion) is described in doc 31 §1.1. The sect is the protagonist's first sect (session ~120, doc 26 §4.2).
    34→
    35→### 1.5 The Western Interior — the Yellow Court range and the Sand-Veiled Sect
    36→
    37→Zhong's western interior is dry, mountainous, and sparsely populated — the great overland trade route to the Western Ocean begins at the oasis city of **Yumen (玉門)**, 7,000 li west of Yanjing, where the desert gives way to the Western Ocean's coast. The **Yellow Court Holy Land (黃庭聖地)** holds the western interior's central range, with its patriarch's seat in the **Yellow Court Grotto-Heaven (黃庭洞天)** above the great earth-phase vein that underlies the desert. The Yellow Court's patriarch, **Daoist Yellow Court (黃庭真人)**, is Nascent Soul late-stage, age 3,400; the holy land's specialty is alchemy (earth-phase refinement) and the Yellow Court is the Great Yan Dynasty's traditional alchemical supplier. The Yellow Court is the only holy land on Zhong Continent. The **Sand-Veiled Sect (沙幕宗)** is the western interior's leading great sect, allied with the Yellow Court; its sect master **Patriarch Dune (沙丘老人)**, Core Formation, age 600, runs the desert trade route's protection and the Yumen oasis's spirit-stone market. The Sand-Veiled Sect's relationship to the Azure Sword Sect is cold — both are vassals of a holy land (Yellow Court and Jade Void respectively), and the two holy lands' long-standing rivalry for influence over Zhong's middle reaches bleeds down through their vassals.
    38→
    39→The western interior's other notable feature is the **Sand-Veiled Caravansary (沙幕驛)**, the cultivation world's largest neutral trade-post, at Yumen itself. The Caravansary is a Foundation-Establishment-warded compound inside Yumen's old city, where cultivators from Zhong, Xi (via the Western Ocean crossing), and the occasional Nan Sea-Fan envoy meet to trade. The Caravansary is governed by a Sand-Veiled-appointed steward and protected by a Foundation-Establishment-tier qi-barrier; the Sand-Veiled Sect's standing rule is that no combat is permitted inside the compound, on pain of the steward's displeasure (which, in practice, means the offender is barred from the Caravansary for a decade — a serious penalty for any cultivator who depends on the western trade).
    40→
    41→### 1.6 The Pearl-Tide Coast — the eastern maritime province
    42→
    43→Zhong's eastern coast, from the Cangli Riverlands' mouth north to the great port of **Haimen (海門)**, 1,200 li north of Wang Family Bend, is the empire's maritime frontier. The **Pearl-Tide Sect (珠潮宗)**, headquartered at Haimen, is the eastern coast's leading great sect; its sect master **Matriarch Tide Pearl (潮珠道君)**, Core Formation, age 480, commands a fleet of twelve qi-sailed junks and holds the **Pearl-Tide Grotto-Heaven (珠潮洞天)**, a water-phase pocket-world anchored at the harbor's mouth. The Pearl-Tide Sect maintains the Zhong-side anchor of the **Vermilion Pearl Route (赤珠航路, §6.4 below)** — the cultivator shipping lane that runs east to Dong Continent. The Pearl-Tide Sect's relationship to the Azure Sword Sect is distant but cordial; the Azure Sword's sword-cultivators and the Pearl-Tide's maritime cultivators have little overlap, and the Pearl-Tide considers the Cangli Riverlands too small to notice. The Pearl-Tide's relationship to the Jade Void Holy Land (Dong Continent) is the eastern coast's most important alliance — the Pearl-Tide is the Jade Void's preferred Zhong-side intermediary, and Pearl-Tide's outer disciples often travel to the Jade Void's compound on Dong Continent for advanced training.
    44→
    45→### 1.7 The Northern Marches — the Iron-Horse Sect
    46→
    47→Zhong's northern frontier, beyond the Yan River's middle reaches, is a steppe-grassland border with the cold subarctic that runs north to Bei Continent. The **Iron-Horse Sect (鐵馬宗)**, headquartered at the garrison city of **Beiguan (北關)**, 4,000 li north of Yanjing, is the empire's northern frontier great sect. Sect master **Patriarch Iron-Horse (鐵馬上人)**, Core Formation late-stage, age 720, commands the empire's northern cavalry reserve and the spirit-beast mounted scouts who patrol the steppe. Specialty: beast-mounted cavalry cultivation (metal-water dual-phase, the cavalry's sabers are metal-phase, the steppe's qi is water-dominant). The Iron-Horse Sect's relationship to the Azure Sword Sect is warm — the two sects share a sword-cultivation root, and Iron-Horse's founder was an Azure Sword outer disciple four centuries ago. The Iron-Horse's relationship to the Black-Ice Khanate on Bei Continent (§5.2 below) is the empire's longest-running cold conflict: a border war every generation, neither side strong enough to settle.
    48→
    49→The Northern Marches are also the empire's coldest cultivation frontier — the steppe's water-yin qi is inhospitable to fire-phase cultivators, and the Iron-Horse Sect's metal-water dual-phase practice is the only tradition that flourishes. The sect's signature technique is the **Northern Slash (北斬)**, a saber-art that draws the steppe's cold qi into a metal-phase blade-edge; the technique's founder was the Azure Sword defector who founded the Iron-Horse, and the Azure Sword's Scripture Hall still preserves the founder's departure-record ("left in good standing, with the sect master's blessing and a saber-blank of Cangwu jade").

    53→## 2. Dong Continent (東洲) — the eastern continent
    54→
    55→### 2.1 Geography and qi-climate
    56→
    57→Dong Continent (東洲) lies across the Eastern Sea, ~30,000 li east of Zhong's coast (doc 36 §6.2: two months by mortal sail; a day by Core Formation flight; an hour by Nascent Soul). The continent is smaller than Zhong but its Acquired-stratum qi-density is higher (doc 36 §2.5); wood-phase qi is dominant. The land is wetter, lusher, and the spirit veins run shallower — wood-phase veins surface frequently, producing the dense spirit-herb ecology that makes Dong the cultivation world's alchemical center. The mortal population is smaller than Zhong's (the continent's interior is dense primary forest, not easily settled), but its cultivator-to-mortal ratio is the highest of the five continents. The Eastern Sea's floor between Zhong and Dong is, in Acquired-stratum terms, the shallowest continental shelf; this is why the Vermilion Pearl Route runs here, rather than between any other pair of continents.
    58→
    59→### 2.2 The Yueling Dynasty (月菱朝)
    60→
    61→Dong's largest mortal empire, founded 1,100 years ago on the central lowlands' alluvial plain. The reigning monarch is **Yueling Lingxi (月菱菱汐)**, the 23rd monarch, in the 60th year of the Qingxiao reign (清嘯六十年) — a 130-year-old Foundation Establishment cultivator (one of the few mortals who ascends to a sect-supported throne). The Yueling Dynasty's structure is a hybrid: the throne is mortal-hereditary but the monarch must be Foundation Establishment (the sects provide the cultivation training for the heir-apparent), and the imperial council includes the three leading great sects' sect masters as voting peers. The Yueling's capital is **Qingyuan (青苑)**, on the central lowlands' great river. The Yueling's relationship to the Great Yan Dynasty is distant — the two empires trade through the Vermilion Pearl Route (Dong exports spirit herbs, jade, and sea-silk; imports bronze, iron, and mortal crafts) but have never been to war. The Yueling's relationship to the Jade Void Holy Land is the continent's defining political fact: the Jade Void is the Yueling's traditional protector, and the Yueling throne's Foundation Establishment requirement is enforced, in practice, by Jade Void elders.
    62→
    63→### 2.3 The Jade Void Holy Land (玉虛聖地) — preview
    64→
    65→Dong's — and the cultivation world's — most influential holy land (full spec in §8 below). Patriarch: **Daoist Jade Void (玉虛真人)**, Nascent Soul, age 2,100, founder and retired patriarch. 3,000 disciples. Peak grotto-heaven (the Jade Void Grotto-Heaven, 玉虛洞天). Specialty: ancestral veneration, divination, library. The Jade Void's compound sits on Dong's western coast, 400 li inland from the port of **Qingyang (青陽)**, on a wood-phase peak called **Voidjade Peak (虛玉峰)**. The Jade Void is the Azure Sword Sect's suzerain, the Yueling Dynasty's protector, the Crimson Vow Remnant's most determined enemy (the holy land holds 60% of the Crimson Vow Code and considers the Remnant's corrupted fire-phase tradition a heresy against the original alliance's oath-form). The Jade Void's founder was, per the holy land's own records, born in the Cangli Riverlands (§8.5).
    66→
    67→### 2.4 The Crimson Vow Alliance's ruins and the Remnant
    68→
    69→The Crimson Vow Alliance (赤誓盟, doc 37 §6.2) flourished on Dong's central lowlands 5,000 years ago — 14 great sects, 7 holy lands, ~120 lesser sects, with the Crimson Vow City grotto-heaven as capital. The alliance fell when its guarantor, the Mahayana Daojun Chiri (赤日道君), dispersed in a failed law-amendment tribulation in Year ~43,000. The Crimson Vow City's grotto-heaven was sealed with 8,000 cultivators trapped inside. The collapsed anchor — the cave on Dong's shore where the grotto's anchor once opened — is now a qi-residue site, faintly warm, that the Jade Void's intelligence hall monitors. The **Crimson Vow Remnant (赤誓餘孽)** is a hidden network of demonic cultivators who claim descent from the alliance's dissolved membership and practice a corrupted form of the alliance's fire-phase tradition. The Remnant's recruiter was responsible for the Zhou Wugui incursion in the Cangli Riverlands 95 years ago (doc 37 §9.4). The Remnant's current leadership is unknown; the Jade Void's intelligence hall believes the network operates from Dong Continent's southern marshes and has perhaps two hundred members, including three Core Formation cultivators.
    70→
    71→### 2.5 Dong's great sects
    72→
    73→The three great sects on Dong, in descending order of influence:
    74→
    75→- **The Emerald Pagoda Sect (翠塔宗)** — Dong's leading alchemy sect, allied with the Jade Void. Sect master **Patriarch Emerald Furnace (翠爐上人)**, Core Formation late-stage, age 590, runs the sect's nine-tier alchemy pagoda at **Cuiping (翠屏)**, 300 li south of Qingyuan. Specialty: wood-phase alchemy (Root-Developing Pill, Body-Tempering Pill, the rare Wood-Marowing Pill). The Emerald Pagoda's relationship to the Jade Void is a near-vassalage; the Jade Void's library and the Emerald Pagoda's furnace-gardens are mutually dependent (the Jade Void supplies the manuals, the Pagoda supplies the pills). The Emerald Pagoda's relationship to Patriarch Mu (Azure Sword Sect's alchemist, doc 35 §3.9) is distant professional respect — the two correspond on alchemical problems across the Eastern Sea.
    76→- **The Whale-Rider Sect (鯨馭宗)** — Dong's coastal beast-taming sect, headquartered at the port of **Haiqing (海青)** on Dong's eastern coast. Sect master **Matriarch Whale-Song (鯨吟道君)**, Core Formation, age 410, commands a pod of seventeen cultivated qi-whales (a sea-beast lineage the sect has bred for nine centuries). Specialty: sea-beast taming, oceanic spirit-herb gathering, deep-water exploration. The Whale-Rider Sect maintains the Dong-side anchor of the Vermilion Pearl Route and operates the route's deep-water patrol. The Whale-Rider's relationship to the Pearl-Tide Sect (Zhong) is a maritime alliance — the two sects' patrol routes meet at the Pearl Archipelago's mid-route shrine. The Whale-Rider's relationship to the Crystal-Tide Sect (the undersea sect, §6.3 below) is tense — the two contest the deep-water spirit-herb beds.
    77→- **The Azuremist Sect (青嵐宗)** — Dong's mountain-interior sect, in the central range's cloud forests. Sect master **Patriarch Azure-Mist (青嵐上人)**, Core Formation, age 380. Specialty: wood-phase sword cultivation (the Azuremist's sword-tradition is Dong's counterpart to the Azure Sword Sect's, derived from the same pre-Crimson-Vow root). The Azuremist's relationship to the Azure Sword Sect is the cultivation world's longest-running cross-continental friendship: the two sects exchange outer disciples every sixty years and consider each other sword-lineage cousins. The Azuremist's relationship to the Jade Void is cool — the Azuremist considers the Jade Void's divination-tradition a distraction from the sword, and the Jade Void considers the Azuremist's sword-asceticism a useful but limited path.
    78→
    79→### 2.6 Relationship to Zhong
    80→
    81→Dong and Zhong trade through the Vermilion Pearl Route (§6.4). Dong exports spirit herbs (especially the wood-phase herbs that grow only in Dong's primary forest), jade (Dong's western mountains are jade-rich), and sea-silk (a qi-conductive fiber woven by the Whale-Rider Sect's qi-whales from deep-water spirit-silk kelp). Zhong exports bronze, iron, mortal crafts (paper, lacquerware, porcelain), and grain (Dong's interior cannot support wet-rice agriculture at Zhong's scale). The two empires have never been to war — the Eastern Sea is wide enough that the cost of projection exceeds the value of conquest — but the cultivation world's balance is delicate: the Jade Void Holy Land's influence extends across both continents (its suzerainty over the Azure Sword Sect on Zhong, its protectorate over the Yueling Dynasty on Dong), and a Jade Void withdrawal from either side would shift the balance.

    85→## 3. Xi Continent (西洲) — the western continent
    86→
    87→### 3.1 Geography and qi-climate
    88→
    89→Xi Continent (西洲) lies across the Western Ocean, beyond Zhong's western deserts. The continent is dry, mountainous, and metal-dominant (doc 36 §2.5, §9.2: metal-phase, sharpness, cutting, endings). The interior is a high plateau of granite and iron-veined ranges; the coast is a narrow strip of Mediterranean-climate city-states. The spirit veins run deep and metal-dominant; the qi is sharp, cold, and (in the cultivation world's idiom) "brittle" — powerful in bursts, hard to sustain. Few Zhong mortals have heard of Xi; fewer have traveled there. The Western Ocean crossing is ~25,000 li of open water, with no Pearl-Archipelago equivalent to break the journey; only Core Formation cultivators and the rare mortal fleet (the Yumen-to-Xi sandalwood-and-jade run, three ships in the last century) make the crossing.
    90→
    91→### 3.2 The Ironbone Confederacy (鐵骨盟)
    92→
    93→Xi has no unified empire. The continent's political structure is the Ironbone Confederacy — a federation of forty-three mountain-clan citadels, each ruled by a Core Formation patriarch or matriarch, who meet in council every twelve years at the citadel of **Tiegufeng (鐵骨峰)**, the Confederacy's nominal capital. The current Council Chair is **Matriarch Ironbone (鐵骨道君)**, Core Formation late-stage, age 890, the citadel's hereditary ruler. The Confederacy's law is the **Ironbone Compact (鐵骨約)**, a 2,400-year-old mutual-defense oath that has held the confederacy together through three continental-scale crises. The Compact's central clause: an attack on one citadel is an attack on all; the Council elects a War-Chair for the duration of the crisis. The Ironbone Confederacy's relationship to the Great Yan Dynasty is nonexistent (the two are too distant); its relationship to the Yueling Dynasty is a single trade route (Xi's western coast to Dong's southern coast, ~12,000 li of open water, two ships per year, carrying Xi's worked metal for Dong's spirit herbs).
    94→
    95→### 3.3 The Skyfire Holy Land (天火聖地) — preview
    96→
    97→Xi's — and the metal-phase world's — leading holy land (full spec in doc 43). Patriarch: **Patriarch Iron Sage (鐵聖上人)**, Nascent Soul, age 2,800, a taciturn smith-cultivator who has not spoken in council for three centuries. The Skyfire's compound is the **Skyfire Crucible (天火爐)**, a great grotto-heaven anchored at the peak of the Ironbone Range's central spire. Specialty: metal-phase smithing, weapon-craft, formation arrays. The Skyfire Holy Land is the Courts of Heaven's traditional weapon-supplier; the courts' Defense department (doc 38 §2.5) purchases its enforcement-cultivators' swords from the Skyfire's forge-elders. The Skyfire's relationship to the Courts is therefore the closest of the five holy lands; the Skyfire's patriarch is the only holy land patriarch who visits the Courts' Roads department (doc 38 §2.4) in person. The Skyfire's relationship to the Ironbone Confederacy is suzerain-to-vassal: the Confederacy's citadels pay an annual tithe of refined metal to the Skyfire, and the Skyfire's forge-elders train the Confederacy's Core Formation smiths.
    98→
    99→### 3.4 Xi's great sects
   100→
   101→- **The Ironbone Sect (鐵骨宗)** — the Confederacy's leading great sect, headquartered at Tiegufeng itself. Sect master **Patriarch Iron-Fang (鐵牙上人)**, Core Formation, age 610, Matriarch Ironbone's son and the Confederacy's hereditary War-Chair. Specialty: metal-phase body-tempering and the Ironbone Clan's ancestral saber technique. The Ironbone Sect's relationship to the Skyfire Holy Land is a near-vassalage; the Skyfire's forge-elders refine the Ironbone Sect's saber-blanks, and the Ironbone Sect's outer disciples serve the Skyfire's forges as apprentices.
   102→- **The Stone Lantern Sect (石燈宗)** — Xi's frontier sect, on the continent's eastern coast, guarding the approaches to the Western Spirit Wilds' Law Reaches (doc 36 §9.2: Xi's Spirit Wilds are where the most Law Reaches are found). Sect master **Patriarch Stone-Lantern (石燈上人)**, Core Formation, age 540. Specialty: formation arrays (the sect's name comes from the spirit-stone lanterns it maintains along the Western Ocean's coast, marking the safe harbors and warning of Qi-storms). The Stone Lantern Sect's relationship to the Skyfire Holy Land is independent — the Stone Lantern is the only Xi great sect that does not tithe to the Skyfire, on the grounds that its mission (frontier defense) is a Courts-delegated responsibility. The Stone Lantern's relationship to the Sand-Veiled Sect (Zhong's western interior) is the western overland trade route's protective alliance; the two sects' outer disciples meet at the Yumen oasis every autumn.
   103→
   104→### 3.5 The Law Reaches of the Western Spirit Wilds
   105→
   106→Xi Continent's Acquired-stratum Spirit Wilds are the cosmos's most Reach-rich region (doc 36 §9.2: "the most Law Reaches are found" in the Western Spirit Wilds). The Reaches are not on the Mortal Stratum's surface — they are in the Precelestial Stratum above Xi — but their resonance is felt in the mortal continent's metal-phase qi (the sharp, brittle quality is the Reaches' resonance bleeding down). The Stone Lantern Sect's frontier mission includes monitoring this resonance; the sect's intelligence hall has mapped eleven Reach-locations in the Western Spirit Wilds and reports to the Skyfire Holy Land's forge-elders, who in turn report to the Courts of Heaven. The protagonist at Core Formation can perceive the Reach-resonance; at Spirit Severance, the protagonist could enter a Reach (the Western Spirit Wilds' maintained roads begin at the Stone Lantern Sect's anchor-shrine). This is the late-game's spatial structure: the protagonist's path from the Cangli Riverlands to a Law Reach runs through Zhong's western interior, across the Western Ocean, across Xi, and up into the Precelestial above Xi.

   110→## 4. Nan Continent (南洲) — the southern continent
   111→
   112→### 4.1 Geography and qi-climate
   113→
   114→Nan Continent (南洲) is tropical, archipelagic, monsoon-rotated (doc 36 §2.5, §9.2: fire-phase, intensity, conflict, transformation; qi is wood-dominant and water-dominant). The continent's coast is dense with mortal settlement; the interior is dense primary jungle, broken by the central highlands' dry grasslands (the only place on Nan where ruins preserve well, due to the dry air). The spirit veins run shallow and wood-dominant along the coasts; the central highlands' deep veins are earth-wood dual-phase. The continent is the Mortal Stratum's most yao-dense region — the Pillar Dynasty's yao-civilization flourished here (doc 37 §6.1), and the yao who survived the dynasty's fall dispersed into the jungle interior, where their descendants still live.
   115→
   116→### 4.2 The Monsoon Cities and the jungle interior
   117→
   118→Nan has no single empire. The coastal civilization is the **Monsoon Cities (季風諸城)**, a network of seventeen trading city-states linked by the monsoon-cycle sail routes. The Cities' loose confederation is governed by the **Monsoon Council (季風議會)**, which meets annually at the city of **Nanhai (南海)**, the largest port. The Council's chair rotates among the Cities' merchant-princes. The jungle interior is governed by no mortal authority; it is the yao-territory of the **Pillar Descendants (柱裔)**, the scattered yao-civilizations descended from the Pillar Dynasty's dispersed population. The Pillar Descendants are not a single polity; they are an archipelago of yao-villages, yao-groves, and the occasional yao-city, each governed by a yao-elder (typically a Foundation Establishment yao of a tree- or stone-species). The Monsoon Cities and the Pillar Descendants trade at the city of **Linwang (林王)**, on the jungle's northern edge — the only place on Nan where yao and humans meet regularly as peers.
   119→
   120→### 4.3 The Verdant Pillar Holy Land (青柱聖地) — preview
   121→
   122→Nan's — and the cultivation world's only yao-co-founded — holy land (full spec in doc 43). Patriarch: **Daoist Pillar Canopy (柱冠真人)**, Nascent Soul, age 1,700, a human cultivator who co-founded the holy land with the tree-yao **Old Pillar (老柱)**, a Foundation Establishment yao (the youngest holy land patriarch's partner is the cultivation world's oldest non-Mahayana yao, at ~3,200 years). The Verdant Pillar's compound is the **Verdant Pillar Grotto-Heaven (青柱洞天)**, anchored on the central highlands' great earth-wood vein, whose interior contains the surviving Pillar Cities' ruins and bark-strip libraries. Specialty: yao cultivation, wood-phase beast-taming, Pillar Dynasty lore. The Verdant Pillar is the only holy land independent of the Courts of Heaven (its yao-foundation places it outside the Courts' lineage-jurisdiction; the Courts respect this and the Verdant Pillar, in turn, does not petition the Courts). The Verdant Pillar's relationship to the Jade Void Holy Land is warm — the Jade Void holds 7 of the 12 volumes of the Pillar Dynasty Annals, and the Verdant Pillar holds the other 5; the two holy lands maintain a 1,500-year scholarly correspondence.
   123→
   124→### 4.4 The Pillar Dynasty's ruins
   125→
   126→The Pillar Cities (柱城群, doc 37 §6.1) are the ruins of the yao-civilization that flourished 22,000-30,000 years ago on Nan's central highlands. The Cities are stone — fused by the yao's wood-phase qi, grown like wood in forms they designed — partially overgrown, partially intact, with bark-strip libraries preserved by the highlands' dry air. The Azure Sword Sect's intelligence hall has a file on a Pillar-City ruin 300 li from the sect's territory, in the western Cangwu range's deep extension (doc 37 §6.1) — this is a transplanted ruin, established by Pillar Dynasty yao-explorers who reached the Cangwu foothills (the Cangwu granitic arc is the easternmost extension of the same fault system as the Nan central highlands, doc 37 §9.2). The Verdant Pillar Holy Land controls access to the seven intact Cities on Nan; the bark-strip libraries within hold the Pillar Dynasty's qi-economy records, wood-phase cultivation manuals, and the Annals that the Jade Void and the Verdant Pillar hold in divided custody.

   128→### 4.5 Nan's great sects

Specialty: water-phase maritime cultivation and the Monsoon Council's enforcement arm. The Sea-Fan Sect's relationship to the Pearl-Tide Sect (Zhong) is the southern maritime trade route's protective alliance — the two sects' patrol routes meet at the southern edge of the Eastern Sea. The Sea-Fan's relationship to the Whale-Rider Sect (Dong) is cooperative; the two share deep-water spirit-herb beds.
   131→- **The Redwood Canopy Sect (紅冠宗)** — Nan's jungle-interior sect, at the city of Linwang. Sect master **Patriarch Redwood (紅冠上人)**, Core Formation, age 530, a half-yao cultivator (his mother was a Pillar Descendant tree-yao, his father a Monsoon City merchant). Specialty: wood-phase cultivation, yao-human diplomacy, the Linwang trade-fair's neutral ground enforcement. The Redwood Canopy's relationship to the Verdant Pillar Holy Land is the closest of any great sect to any holy land — the Redwood Canopy's outer disciples train in the Verdant Pillar's grotto-heaven, and the Verdant Pillar's elders consider the Redwood Canopy their preferred successor-institution. The Redwood Canopy's relationship to the Sea-Fan Sect is the Monsoon Council's internal balance: the two sects jointly enforce the Linwang trade-fair's neutrality.

   135→## 5. Bei Continent (北洲) — the northern continent
   136→
   137→### 5.1 Geography and qi-climate
   138→
   139→Bei Continent (北洲) is cold, subarctic, and mostly uninhabitable by wet-rice agriculture (doc 36 §2.5, §9.2: water-phase, stillness, storage, depth; qi is water-dominant and yin-dominant). The continent's coast is taiga; the interior is tundra and steppe, broken by glacial mountain ranges. The spirit veins run cold and deep; the qi is dense but slow (the yin-dominant climate makes cultivation harder for fire-phase cultivators and easier for water-phase and yin-phase). The continent's mortal population is nomadic and pastoral — reindeer-herders, horse-riders, mammoth-bone-dwellers — and culturally distinct from the wet-rice civilizations of the other continents. The northern ancestral courts (the Precelestial's oldest, per doc 36 §9.2) are above Bei; their resonance is felt in the continent's deep cold.
   140→
   141→### 5.2 The Black-Ice Khanate (黑冰汗國)
   142→
   143→Bei's largest political structure is the Black-Ice Khanate, a nomadic-pastoral confederation that has unified the continent's central steppe for 600 years under the Black-Ice Borjigin clan. The reigning Khan is **Khan Tengri-Iron (天鐵汗)**, age 90, a Foundation Establishment cultivator (the Khans' cultivation is water-phase, mounted, and the Khanate's elite cavalry are all Qi Condensation). The Khanate's capital is **the Ordu (斡魯朵)**, a movable camp of 30,000 tents that follows the seasonal pastures; in winter, the Ordu winters at the city of **Beicheng (北城)**, the Khanate's only permanent settlement, on the continent's southern coast. The Khanate's relationship to the Great Yan Dynasty is the long-running northern border war (the Iron-Horse Sect's century-long patrol, §1.7); the two have fought eleven wars in 247 years, none decisive. The Khanate's relationship to Bei's holy land (the NorthSea) is the Khanate's foundation myth: the first Khan was a NorthSea Holy Land outer disciple who left to unite the steppe.
   144→
   145→### 5.3 The NorthSea Holy Land (北海聖地) — preview
   146→
   147→Bei's — and the cultivation world's oldest-patriarch — holy land (full spec in doc 43). Patriarch: **Matriarch NorthSea (北海道君)**, Nascent Soul, age 3,000, the cultivation world's oldest non-Mahayana being (after the pantheon itself). The NorthSea's compound is the **NorthSea Grotto-Heaven (北海洞天)**, anchored at the glacial peak of the continent's central range, on a water-yin vein so cold that the grotto's interior is a permanent ice-field. Specialty: water-phase ice-cultivation, ancestral-court preservation (the NorthSea holds the oldest accessible ancestral-court records on the Mortal Stratum; the Precelestial's ancestral courts are older but harder to reach). The NorthSea is independent of the Courts of Heaven — its ancestral-court records predate the Courts' founding (Year ~40,000), and the NorthSea considers itself peer-to-the-Courts in matters of ancestral record. The Courts tolerate this (the NorthSea does not challenge the Courts' lineage-jurisdiction; it merely maintains its own older records). The NorthSea's relationship to the Black-Ice Khanate is the holy land's only institutional commitment on Bei: the NorthSea trains the Khanate's Foundation Establishment Khans and considers the Khanate's unity the continent's stabilizing force.
   148→
   149→### 5.4 Bei's beast civilizations
   150→
   151→Bei's megafauna include mammoths (a spirit-beast lineage at peak-tier), snow-tigers (high-tier), ice-bears (high-tier), and the great reindeer-herds (mid-tier, the Khanate's pastoral base). The yao of these species are the continent's other civilization — the **Frostfang Yao (霜牙妖)**, a confederation of mammoth-yao, tiger-yao, and bear-yao who govern the deep tundra the Khanate does not enter. The Frostfang Yao's elder-council meets every century at the glacial peak adjacent to the NorthSea Holy Land's anchor; the council's chair is **Old Frost-Fang (老霜牙)**, a mammoth-yao at Foundation Establishment, age 1,400. The Frostfang Yao's relationship to the Khanate is a 600-year cold peace: the Khanate's herders do not enter the deep tundra, the Frostfang Yao do not raid the Khanate's herds. The Frostfang Yao's relationship to the NorthSea Holy Land is the continent's deepest alliance — the yao-council and the holy land's elders consult on all matters concerning the continent's spirit-vein ecology.
   152→
   153→### 5.5 Bei's great sects
   154→
   155→- **The Snowstrider Sect (雪行宗)** — the Khanate's leading great sect, headquartered at Beicheng. Sect master **Patriarch Snowstrider (雪行上人)**, Core Formation, age 590, the Khan's cultivation-teacher. Specialty: water-phase mounted cavalry cultivation, the Khanate's cavalry spirit-beast training (the sect breeds the Khanate's war-horses from a spirit-beast lineage). The Snowstrider's relationship to the Iron-Horse Sect (Zhong) is the long border war — the two sects' outer disciples skirmish along the frontier every spring. The Snowstrider's relationship to the NorthSea Holy Land is a near-vassalage (the Snowstrider's sect master is always a NorthSea-trained cultivator).
   156→- **The Mammoth-Bone Sect (巨骨宗)** — Bei's yao-allied great sect, at the deep-tundra's edge. Sect master **Patriarch Mammoth-Bone (巨骨上人)**, Core Formation, age 720, a human cultivator who married into the Frostfang Yao (his wife is a tiger-yao). Specialty: beast-mounted cultivation (the sect's cultivators ride mammoth-yao), yao-human diplomacy, the deep-tundra patrol. The Mammoth-Bone's relationship to the Frostfang Yao is the sect's foundation — the Yao elder-council approves the sect's disciple roster. The Mammoth-Bone's relationship to the Redwood Canopy Sect (Nan) is the cross-continental yao-allied sects' correspondence; the two sects exchange outer-disciples every sixty years.
