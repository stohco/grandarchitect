     1→# Identity, Death, Soul, Memory & Reincarnation Models — Research Draft
     2→
     3→**Package:** Cultivation Foundations & Mortal Reality v0.1  
     4→**Status:** comparative design research; no option is canon  
     5→**Date:** 2026-08-02  
     6→**Purpose:** make death, survival, duplication, possession, ancestorhood, resurrection, and reincarnation executable without reducing personhood to one meter or turning death into a reload screen
     7→
     8→---
     9→
    10→## 0. Locked constraints
    11→
    12→1. A person is not assumed to be identical to a body, memory file, soul token, name, legal record, or reputation until the selected ontology says so.
    13→2. Numerical identity, psychological continuity, bodily continuity, causal continuity, social recognition, legal standing, oath liability, inheritance, and cultivation continuity are separate questions.
    14→3. “Unknown” may describe what characters know; the simulation still retains a governed ontic answer or explicitly bounded unresolved state.
    15→4. Death has bodily, relational, economic, legal, ritual, historical, and cultivation consequences.
    16→5. Any surviving or created consciousness receives personhood analysis. A duplicate is not disposable inventory merely because it was copied.
    17→6. Resurrection cannot silently duplicate matter, qi, memories, authority, cultivation bases, unique artifacts, debts, names, or legal claims.
    18→7. Save/load is not an in-world immortality power unless a later ratified mechanism explicitly makes it one.
    19→8. The player is the protagonist but receives no hidden exemption from identity, death, possession, reincarnation, or continuity law.
    20→9. Familiar xianxia vocabulary may remain where appropriate. Niche romanizations are translated or glossed for seasoned English-speaking readers.
    21→10. Historical and religious models are research lenses, never proof that the original universe must adopt them.
    22→11. Disability, unconsciousness, aphasia, memory loss, atypical cognition, transformed embodiment, or inability to demonstrate agency never by themselves remove personhood. Cultures and institutions may misjudge these states; canonical systems do not encode that prejudice as fact.

    26→## 1. Research controls
    27→
    28→### 1.1 Do not invent a universal Chinese “soul system”
    29→
    30→K. E. Brashier’s study of early Chinese sources disputes the common modern simplification that *hun* and *po* formed one uniformly popular dual-soul doctrine. The sources show competing scholastic, medical, funerary, bodily, and ancestral models. This is a warning against converting a debated history into “three heavenly souls and seven earthly souls” as universal game physics ([Cambridge, *Han Thanatology and the Division of “Souls”*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/early-china/article/abs/han-thanatology-and-the-division-of-souls/512C858E97ACE63791498AA28CA63DE3)).
    31→
    32→Research consequence:
    33→
    34→- retain `hun`/`po` only inside exact historical or fictional context;
    35→- do not make them default project-native player terms;
    36→- do not assume every species or culture partitions persons identically;
    37→- distinguish scholarly model, textual claim, lived practice, in-world belief, and canonical truth; and
    38→- treat funerary care, ancestral presence, bodily integrity, and spirit mobility as separable design variables.
    39→
    40→### 1.2 Ritual continuity is not identical to numerical identity
    41→
    42→Studies of ancestor ritual show that care, food, remembrance, effigies, tablets, graves, lineage, and ritual labor can sustain relationships with the dead without providing one uncontested metaphysical theory. Some accounts describe life as qi aggregation and death as dispersion while still treating ritual ancestor presence as meaningful ([MDPI, “Why Ancestor Is Possible”](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/16/8/1025)). Ethnographic work also emphasizes ritual care and the danger of neglected dead becoming socially anonymous or hostile, but those observations are historically and regionally situated rather than universal ([Cambridge, “Care as Critique of Care”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/china-quarterly/article/care-as-critique-of-care-public-services-social-security-and-ritual-responsiveness/262AF52AC3C8C6A1B8DB43338CBCF293)).
    43→
    44→Design consequence: ancestorhood can be a relationship, office, sustained manifestation, interpretive claim, or transformation without automatically answering whether the ancestor is numerically the deceased person.
    45→
    46→### 1.3 Rebirth need not transport a permanent soul object
    47→
    48→Buddhist philosophical traditions contain sophisticated disputes about persons, no-self, causal continuity, karma, and rebirth. A causally connected stream can model continuity without positing an unchanging substance that travels between bodies ([Stanford, *Mind in Indian Buddhist Philosophy*](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mind-indian-buddhism/); [Stanford, *Buddha*](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buddha/)). The project must not collapse these traditions into “karma points move a soul to a new avatar.”
    49→
    50→Design consequence: a reborn being may be numerically identical, a causal successor, an inheritor, a new person carrying imprints, or a contested case depending on the selected original ontology.
    51→
    52→### 1.4 Memory alone fails under gaps and branching
    53→
    54→Contemporary personal-identity research distinguishes bodily, psychological, and soul-based criteria and demonstrates problems involving amnesia, copying, and fission. Psychological continuity can branch to two successors even though numerical identity cannot simply become one-to-two ([Stanford, *Personal Identity*](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/identity-personal/); [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *Personal Identity*](https://iep.utm.edu/person-i/)).
    55→
    56→Design consequence: memory copying never silently copies identity. Every split, merge, replacement, reincarnation, and possession event creates explicit continuity and claim records.
    57→
    58→### 1.5 Research boundary
    59→
    60→These sources supply questions and distinctions, not canon answers. The original universe must choose its own executable model and disclose where cultures misunderstand it. No historical religion becomes a skin for a loot or morality system.

    64→## 2. Minimum executable identity ontology
    65→
    66→The following node families are required regardless of which metaphysical option is selected.
    67→
    68→| Node | Meaning |
    69→|---|---|
    70→| `PersonContinuant` | Stable subject used to gather identity claims and history; existence does not itself prove any identity theory. |
    71→| `Embodiment` | Time-scoped relation between a continuant and a body, construct, projection, domain, or other vehicle. |
    72→| `BodyInstance` | Living, dying, dead, transformed, divided, repaired, occupied, or reconstructed physical organization. |
    73→| `AgencyProcess` | Ongoing capacity to perceive, intend, choose, learn, and act. |
    74→| `SpiritComponent` | Optional ontology-specific nonbodily component with source, functions, continuity, divisibility, mobility, decay, and counters. |
    75→| `MemoryTrace` | Content, perspective, encoding, provenance, confidence, accessibility, corruption, and causal source. |
    76→| `CharacterDisposition` | Habits, attachments, values, fears, commitments, and learned response tendencies. |
    77→| `CultivationContinuity` | Body, qi, spirit/mind, Dao-orientation, technique, injury, deviation, and authority carried through a transition. |
    78→| `NameBearer` | Relation among an entity, name revision, language, audience, naming authority, and valid time. |
    79→| `LegalIdentity` | Jurisdiction-specific rights, liabilities, status, records, and recognition. |
    80→| `SocialRecognition` | Which people or institutions recognize a claimant as someone, and why. |
    81→| `RelationshipContinuity` | Kinship, affection, apprenticeship, office, ownership, oath, debt, enmity, care, and grief after transformation. |
    82→| `CausalLineage` | Directed provenance from earlier to later body, memory, agency, spirit, technique, or person claims. |
    83→| `CopyLineage` | Exact event and method by which information or structure was duplicated. |
    84→| `DeathEvent` | Time, body state, agency cessation/change, causes, witnesses, remains, records, and disputed interpretations. |
    85→| `Remains` | Corpse, ash, bone, relic, residue, dispersed material, memorial object, or missing body, with custody and provenance. |
    86→| `PostmortemManifestation` | Ghost, echo, ancestor presence, dream contact, record intelligence, remnant will, projection, or fraud. |
    87→| `RebirthEvent` | Causal transition into a new life, including what crossed, what did not, latency, selection, and knowledge. |
    88→| `ResurrectionEvent` | Attempt to restore a prior person, body, agency, or relationship after death. |
    89→| `PossessionEvent` | Time-scoped access/control contest among body, agency, memory, and authority. |
    90→| `IdentityClaim` | A claimant’s proposition that two stages or entities are the same person or legitimate successor. |
    91→| `OnticResolution` | Canonical output of the selected metaphysical law for a typed continuity question; it has rule version, inputs, truth status, and physical observables but no social deciding authority. |
    92→| `ContinuityJudgment` | Domain-specific decision with rule, evidence, authority, dissent, consequences, and appeal. |
    93→| `FinalityState` | What forms of return remain physically, metaphysically, legally, or informationally possible. |
    94→
    95→### 2.1 Identity is a family of typed questions
    96→
    97→Never answer all of these with one `identity_score`:
    98→
    99→```text
   100→NUMERICALLY_SAME_PERSON?
   101→SAME_LIVING_ORGANISM?
   102→SAME_AGENCY_PROCESS?
   103→PSYCHOLOGICALLY_CONTINUOUS?
   104→SAME_SPIRIT_CONTINUANT?
   105→CAUSALLY_DESCENDED_FROM?
   106→LEGALLY_THE_SAME?
   107→SOCIALLY_RECOGNIZED_AS?
   108→INHERITS_THIS_RELATIONSHIP?
   109→BEARS_THIS_OATH_OR_DEBT?
   110→OWNS_THIS_ITEM?
   111→CONTINUES_THIS_CULTIVATION?
   112→MAY_EXERCISE_THIS_OFFICE_OR_AUTHORITY?
   113→MAY BE BLAMED, PUNISHED, REWARDED, OR MOURNED AS?
   114→```
   115→
   116→An `OnticResolution` pins:
   117→
   118→- ontology and law revision;
   119→- world time;
   120→- candidate entities/stages;
   121→- applicable continuity rule;
   122→- direct evidence and missing evidence;
   123→- branch/merge history;
   124→- canonical answer: yes, no, indeterminate, or inapplicable;
   125→- entailed physical/metaphysical effects; and
   126→- observable/falsifying conditions.
   127→
   128→A separate legal, social, religious, cultivation, or relationship `ContinuityJudgment` pins:
   129→
   130→- jurisdiction, domain, and rule revision;
   131→- evidence available to that audience rather than omniscient state;
   132→- answer: yes, no, indeterminate, contested, or inapplicable;
   133→- deciding authority and competence where that domain has one;
   134→- dissenting claims;
   135→- downstream effects; and
   136→- reopening conditions.
   137→
   138→An authority can misjudge, suppress, or refuse to recognize an ontic fact; it cannot author numerical identity merely by decree. Conversely, ontic sameness does not automatically settle inheritance, guilt, office, marriage, consent, ownership, or mourning.
   139→
   140→### 2.2 Numerical identity remains one-to-one
   141→
   142→If one process branches into two independently acting persons, both may be descendants and both may retain relationships or claims. They cannot both be numerically identical to the same earlier person under ordinary one-to-one identity. The selected ontology must choose among:
   143→
   144→- an anchored branch remains the original and others are new;
   145→- branching ends the original and creates multiple successors;
   146→- the original was already a temporally extended branching structure;
   147→- numerical identity becomes indeterminate while practical succession is adjudicated; or
   148→- branching is metaphysically impossible and attempts create damage, suppression, fraud, or partial copies.
   149→
   150→The answer cannot be “whichever one the player controls.”
   151→
   152→### 2.3 Ontic × judgment fixture — `ONTIC-JUDGMENT-SPLIT-v0.1`
   153→
   154→This fixture proves that metaphysical truth and institutional recognition have separate writers and effects.
   155→
   156→**Pinned input manifest**
   157→
   158→```text
   159→PriorStageId: PERSON-BEFORE-01
   160→ClaimantStageId: PERSON-CLAIMANT-01
   161→TransitionId: RESTORE-FIXTURE-01
   162→BodyInstanceIds: BODY-OLD-01, BODY-NEW-01
   163→OntologyRevision: ID-MODEL-FIXTURE-01
   164→WorldInstant: 100000
   165→LegalJurisdiction: LAW-FIXTURE-01
   166→SocialAudience: HOUSEHOLD-FIXTURE-01
   167→ReligiousCommunity: RITE-FIXTURE-01
   168→EvidenceIds: EV-BODY, EV-MEMORY, EV-CAUSAL, EV-SPIRIT-IF-APPLICABLE, EV-WITNESS
   169→```
   170→
   171→Three ontology variants pin the selected resolver's inputs so its expected `OnticResolution` is respectively `YES`, `NO`, or `INDETERMINATE`. For each ontic variant, each domain `D ∈ {LEGAL, SOCIAL, RELIGIOUS}` is tested with evidence/authority packets producing `J ∈ {YES, NO, CONTESTED}`. This is the complete 3×3 cross-product per domain, not a narrative sample.
   172→
   173→| Ontic `O` | Domain judgment `J` | Required invariant and expected writes |
   174→|---|---|---|
   175→| YES | YES | ontic record remains YES; domain writes recognition plus only its own downstream rights/relationships/rites |
   176→| YES | NO | ontic record remains YES; domain writes denial, reasons, authority and appeal; denial cannot end the person or rewrite numerical identity |
   177→| YES | CONTESTED | ontic record remains YES; domain writes competing claims, provisional effects and reopening conditions |
   178→| NO | YES | ontic record remains NO; domain may recognize a legal successor, social continuation or ritual representative, but cannot label that recognition ontic sameness |
   179→| NO | NO | ontic record remains NO; domain denial has its normal scoped effects only |
   180→| NO | CONTESTED | ontic record remains NO; disagreement and appeal remain possible without changing the resolver |
   181→| INDETERMINATE | YES | ontology remains indeterminate; domain may apply a disclosed presumption and scoped effects, never promote the presumption to cosmic fact |
   182→| INDETERMINATE | NO | ontology remains indeterminate; domain denial records the evidence standard and reopening conditions |
   183→| INDETERMINATE | CONTESTED | ontology remains indeterminate; competing judgments and provisional safeguards persist |
   184→
   185→**Writer separation**
   186→
   187→- `OntologyResolver` alone writes `OnticResolution {rule, inputs, O, observables, falsifiers}`.
   188→- A domain authority/audience writes `ContinuityJudgment {domain, evidence_seen, J, competence, dissent, scoped_effects, appeal, reopen}`.
   189→- Appeals create a new judgment revision. They never overwrite the old judgment or the ontic record.
   190→- New metaphysical evidence schedules resolver reopening only where the selected ontology permits it; a changed ontic revision does not retroactively erase good-faith legal/social/religious history.
   191→
   192→The executable oracle asserts exact read/write sets, zero unauthorized cross-writes, and preservation of all 27 combinations across save/load and S4→S0→S4. `NormalizedHash = SHA-256(SEM-NORM-compatible canonical serialization of input manifest + ordered expected events)` must match the always-S4 control for each variant. A deliberate misjudgment, corrupt authority, appeal, and later reopening are mandatory event variants.

   196→## 3. Death as a multi-domain transition
   197→
   198→Death is an event bundle, not a Boolean hit-point state.
   199→
   200→### 3.1 Required death facets
   201→
   202→```text
   203→biological organization
   204→agency and perception
   205→qi circulation and reservoirs
   206→spirit/mind components if any
   207→memory accessibility
   208→Dao/cultivation continuity
   209→body and material custody
   210→ownership and inheritance
   211→oaths, debts, offices, and crimes
   212→household labor and dependents
   213→funerary and religious duties
   214→records, testimony, and reputation
   215→ecological and public-health consequences
   216→postmortem manifestations
   217→return possibilities and finality
   218→```
   219→
   220→### 3.2 State family, not universal ladder
   221→
   222→The exact model remains open, but every body/person combination must express applicable states such as:
   223→
   224→- alive and integrated;
   225→- dying/recoverable;
   226→- agency absent but body maintained;
   227→- bodily death with unresolved spirit/agency status;
   228→- separated or displaced component;
   229→- remains present, transformed, dispersed, consumed, sealed, or missing;
   230→- postmortem remnant or manifestation;
   231→- ancestor or institutionalized dead;
   232→- resurrection/reconstruction in progress;
   233→- reincarnated or causally succeeded;
   234→- copied, split, merged, or contested;
   235→- dispersed beyond ordinary recovery; and
   236→- irreversibly final under the current laws.
   237→
   238→Species, paths, techniques, environments, and world laws determine which states exist. Human meridians or one human funeral grammar never become universal anatomy.
   239→
   240→“Agency absent” is an observed functional state, not a declaration of nonpersonhood or irreversible death. Sleep, unconsciousness, paralysis, sensory difference, dissociation, possession, sealed awareness, and communication barriers require distinct predicates, diagnostic uncertainty, and safeguards against premature burial, organ/material harvesting, abandonment, or identity seizure.
   241→
   242→### 3.3 Consequence scheduler
   243→
   244→One death may schedule:
   245→
   246→1. bodily change, decay, contamination, scavenging, preservation, or environmental transformation;
   247→2. household shock: lost labor, care, food, rent, debt, guardianship, grief, and knowledge;
   248→3. relationship transitions: widowhood, orphanhood, succession, apprenticeship, enmity, mourning, and unfinished promises;
   249→4. custody disputes over remains, property, techniques, artifacts, names, offices, and records;
   250→5. investigation, testimony, legal process, vengeance, concealment, or memorialization;
   251→6. ritual choices with material labor, specialists, expense, disagreement, and possible efficacy;
   252→7. spirit/remnant processes only if admitted by ontology and conditions;
   253→8. ecological and political consequences; and
   254→9. future discovery by the player, including wrong or fraudulent explanations.
   255→
   256→At S0–S2 simulation-resolution tiers, named identity, causal order, custody, unique objects, obligations, scheduled rites, succession, and bounded demographic/economic effects remain protected invariants. Rendering LOD is a separate axis.

   260→## 4. Four original ontology candidates
   261→
   262→These are mutually distinguishable starting points. Later synthesis may select one, hybridize them only with explicit conflict resolution, or reject all four.
   263→
   264→### Model A — Anchored Spirit Continuant
   265→
   266→**Thesis:** A person has one noncopyable spirit anchor that can change embodiment while maintaining numerical identity.
   267→
   268→```text
   269→living body + active agency + spirit anchor
   270→death → anchor separation/damage/retention
   271→resurrection → same anchor lawfully rejoins or forms an embodiment
   272→reincarnation → same anchor begins a new embodiment under memory constraints
   273→copy → new person; structure/memory may match, anchor does not
   274→```
   275→
   276→**Strengths**
   277→
   278→- Clear player intuition and strong soul-threat gameplay.
   279→- Possession, sealing, resurrection, reincarnation, and spirit injuries have concrete targets.
   280→- Numerical identity remains one-to-one under copying.
   281→
   282→**Necessary limits**
   283→
   284→- The anchor is not a free indestructible save file.
   285→- It has finite integrity, access, mobility, perception, memory bandwidth, and compatibility.
   286→- A stolen anchor does not automatically grant the thief its cultivation, consent, relationships, or legal standing.
   287→- A reconstructed body requires matter, organization, qi, time, knowledge, and authority.
   288→
   289→**Risks**
   290→
   291→- Can trivialize Buddhist-inspired traditions by implying a permanent self.
   292→- Can make death cheap unless dispersion/finality is possible.
   293→- Can turn soul capture into inventory slavery.
   294→- Can reduce identity disputes to scanning an object.
   295→
   296→**Critical tests:** anchor duplication, forged anchor signature, partial fracture, two bodies claiming one anchor, one body hosting several anchors, anchor/body incompatibility, amnesia with same anchor, total personality replacement, final dispersion.
   297→
   298→### Model B — Braided Continuity
   299→
   300→**Thesis:** A person is a maintained braid of bodily continuity, agency, memory, dispositions, qi/spirit organization, names, relationships, and causal history. No single strand is always sufficient.
   301→
   302→```text
   303→PersonContinuant = governed continuity relation across component histories
   304→death = braid rupture with possible surviving strands
   305→resurrection = reweaving that may succeed, fail, or create a successor
   306→reincarnation = new braid causally inheriting selected strands
   307→copy = branch; numerical identity follows the world's nonbranching rule
   308→```
   309→
   310→**Strengths**
   311→
   312→- Supports rich, genuinely difficult identity cases.
   313→- Makes families, teachers, enemies, institutions, and self-understanding mechanically relevant.
   314→- Allows different legal/religious/cultivation traditions to disagree without all being metaphysically correct.
   315→- Makes restoration a process rather than a consumable.
   316→
   317→**Necessary limits**
   318→
   319→- No opaque weighted identity percentage.
   320→- Each judgment uses typed necessary/sufficient rules and preserves dissent.
   321→- Numerical identity, relationship inheritance, liability, and cultivation continuity remain separate.
   322→
   323→**Risks**
   324→
   325→- Harder to explain and validate.
   326→- May feel arbitrary if the player cannot investigate the deciding strands.
   327→- Can permit institutions to deny obvious personhood unless metaphysical and legal judgments remain distinct.
   328→
   329→**Critical tests:** amnesia, body replacement, gradual transformation, duplicate memories, fission, merge, possession, forged social recognition, dead name, oath inheritance, cultivation deviation replacing dispositions.
   330→
   331→### Model C — Causal Flame / Successor Stream
   332→
   333→**Thesis:** No permanent soul substance persists. Momentary body/mind/qi processes cause later processes. What survives is a directed causal stream and its consequences, not a transferable self-object.
   334→
   335→```text
   336→life = causally connected process stream
   337→death = termination or transformation of one organized stream
   338→rebirth = caused successor stream without a migrating substance
   339→memory return = new causal access, not proof of numerical identity
   340→resurrection = restoration of a process only if causal continuity was never completely severed
   341→```
   342→
   343→**Strengths**
   344→
   345→- Deeply resists soul-as-loot and immortality-as-backup-file.
   346→- Makes actions, habits, teachings, relationships, and consequences more important than essence collection.
   347→- Rebirth can matter without giving the player perfect autobiographical continuity.
   348→- Creates distinctive Dao-heart questions without importing a permanent ego.
   349→
   350→**Necessary limits**
   351→
   352→- This is an original world rule, not a claim that it accurately represents all Buddhism.
   353→- Moral/legal succession needs explicit rules because numerical identity may not carry.
   354→- Players need legible causal traces, not philosophical vagueness.
   355→
   356→**Risks**
   357→
   358→- Some players may feel that reincarnation is simply a different person.
   359→- Personal endgame immortality becomes conceptually difficult.
   360→- Responsibility across lives can become unjust if imposed without knowledge or agency.
   361→
   362→**Critical tests:** successor with no memories, perfect copy without causal descent, weak causal descent with strong copied memory, two successors, interrupted stream, retrocausal influence, inherited consequences without inherited blame.
   363→
   364→### Model D — Relational Ancestor Ecology
   365→
   366→**Thesis:** An individual has embodied agency while alive, but postmortem continuation depends on an ecology of remains, place, descendants, records, names, offerings, institutions, memories, qi residues, and possibly responsive manifestations.
   367→
   368→**Classification correction:** as presently written, this is a postmortem manifestation and relationship ecology, not a complete rival rule for numerical identity. It may layer over A, B, or C. It cannot be selected as the sole identity ontology unless a later revision supplies unique outputs for copying, fission, amnesia, resurrection, reincarnation, possession, and merging.
   369→
   370→```text
   371→living person = embodied center in a relational network
   372→death = loss of embodied center and redistribution of continuities
   373→ancestor = sustained postmortem relation/manifestation under defined supports
   374→neglected dead = decay, silence, transformation, dispersal, or dangerous remnant
   375→return = reconstitution from sufficient lawful relations, never memory download alone
   376→```
   377→
   378→**Strengths**
   379→
   380→- Makes mortal families, funerals, graves, temples, archives, food, place, and memory persist into high-realm play.
   381→- Creates systemic reasons to protect or reform institutions.
   382→- Allows afterlife gameplay without assuming every manifestation is the full deceased person.
   383→
   384→**Necessary limits**
   385→
   386→- People without descendants, wealth, institutions, or approved lineage are not ontologically lesser.
   387→- Alternative care networks and self-chosen memorial forms must be possible.
   388→- Ritual labor has material and emotional meaning even when supernatural efficacy fails.
   389→- Institutional recognition cannot manufacture numerical identity.
   390→
   391→**Risks**
   392→
   393→- Can reproduce patriarchal lineage hierarchies or punish marginalized dead.
   394→- Can turn mourning into resource upkeep.
   395→- May make the dead dependent on NPC schedules in frustrating ways.
   396→
   397→**Critical tests:** anonymous death, destroyed grave, false tablet, adopted family, hostile descendants, extinct lineage, competing memorials, ancestor office without person continuity, mass casualty, displaced community, culturally mixed funeral.

   431→| new life with previous-life memories | same only if the unique anchor crossed; memory alone is insufficient | default causal successor, not numerically same, unless a future stricter braid rule says otherwise | causal successor, not a transported identical self | records inherited relations/manifestations; cannot declare same person alone |
   432→| possession without merge | host and possessor remain distinct persons with typed access/control | distinct braids remain unless an explicit merge changes them | distinct processes remain while causally interacting | determines support, recognition, and postmortem consequences for each |
   433→| irreversible merge of two persons | two anchors cannot become one identical predecessor; outcome is coexistence, destruction, or a new person under an explicit anchor rule | both predecessors end and a successor begins unless the contract preserves them as co-agencies | contributing streams end or continue only as specified; a merged successor is not numerically both | distributes memorial, relational, and institutional continuities; no sameness result alone |

   473→### 6.1 Resurrection
   474→
   475→A resurrection claim decomposes into independent predicates:
   476→
   477→- body repaired, replaced, regrown, or reconstructed;
   478→- biological life restarted;
   479→- agency present;
   480→- memories causally restored;
   481→- dispositions continuous;
   482→- spirit component continuous if applicable;
   483→- cultivation dimensions restored or relearned;
   484→- numerical identity judgment;
   485→- relationship and legal recognition;
   486→- liabilities and ownership adjudicated; and
   487→- competing continuants excluded or acknowledged.
   488→
   489→“Revives the dead” is prohibited unless the contract names which of these it guarantees and in what scope.
   490→
   491→### 6.2 Reincarnation
   492→
   493→Define separately:
   494→
   495→- successor selection or formation;
   496→- time gap and clock domain;
   497→- causal carrier: none, process, imprint, spirit, vow, law, place, or other;
   498→- memory access: absent, latent, fragmentary, triggered, trained, stolen, or fabricated;
   499→- temperament/skill/qi/Dao carryover;
   500→- bodily and species change;
   501→- previous-life relationship visibility;
   502→- debt, oath, karma, law, and ownership rules where those modules exist;
   503→- numerical identity and legal succession; and
   504→- whether return can branch or repeat.
   505→
   506→Previous-life memory is evidence with provenance, not automatic omniscience or automatic identity.
   507→
   508→### 6.3 Possession
   509→
   510→Possession separates:
   511→
   512→- sensory access;
   513→- motor access;
   514→- speech access;
   515→- attention and memory read/write;
   516→- qi and technique access;
   517→- identity presentation;
   518→- consent;
   519→- resistance and counterplay;
   520→- shared or exclusive control;
   521→- responsibility for acts;
   522→- body harm and adaptation;
   523→- detection and evidence; and
   524→- termination/aftermath.
   525→
   526→“Controls the target” is not one primitive.
   527→
   528→### 6.4 Copying and fission
   529→
   530→Copy contracts specify:
   531→
   532→- copied structure and fidelity;
   533→- whether source changes;
   534→- perspective and memory cutoff;
   535→- new stable identity allocation;
   536→- causal/copy lineage;
   537→- unique-item and authority nonduplication;
   538→- relationship claims;
   539→- legal status and personhood from first agency;

   543→### 6.5 Merging
   544→
   545→A merge cannot be a stat addition. It specifies:
   546→
   547→- participants and consent;
   548→- whether persons survive, end, coexist, alternate, or create a successor;
   549→- conflict resolution for memories, values, injuries, techniques, bodies, qi, and Dao orientations;
   550→- conservation and loss;
   551→- responsibility and debt;
   552→- ownership and relationship outcomes;
   553→- ability to reverse; and
   554→- treatment of dissenting internal agencies.

   561→
   562→- confirm breathing, warmth, responsiveness, and culturally known signs;
   563→- call family, healer, official, priest, undertaker, teacher, or witness;

   565→- notify dependents, negotiate debt, arrange labor, mourn, sit vigil, prepare food, dig, cremate, bury, record, petition, inherit, contest, or investigate;

   567→- refuse, reform, combine, or create funerary practices within social consequences; and
   568→- care for forgotten dead without requiring supernatural reward.
   569→
   570→Diagnostic actions expose uncertainty and cultural knowledge rather than a magical binary “dead/alive” indicator. A mortal may be mistaken; healers and cultivators gain better sensors only through trained, bounded perception with known false positives and failure modes.

   573→
   574→- perceive or conceal qi/spirit residues;
   575→- stabilize a dying integration without guaranteeing survival;
   576→- prevent hostile possession or memory alteration;

   578→- adjudicate an identity claim with sect, family, court, ancestor, or world-law evidence;
   579→- trace causal lineage across reincarnation or copying;
   580→- sever unlawful control without erasing the hosted person;
   581→- restore, reweave, or release within realm and authority limits; and
   582→- accept finality when intervention would create a different person or violate consent.
   583→

   585→

   595→## 8. Exploit and tragedy gauntlet
   596→
   597→### Resource exploits
   598→
   599→- Duplicate a cultivator and harvest both cultivation bases.
   600→- Reincarnate repeatedly to reroll spiritual roots, bodies, talent, fate, or tribulations.
   601→- Resurrect livestock, rare species, herbs, or cultivators for infinite matter/qi.
   602→- Split before paying a cost; merge after receiving two rewards.
   603→- Transfer debt, deviation, injury, or cooldown to an abandoned copy.
   604→- Use corpse ownership to claim carried soul, memory, artifact, or technique.
   605→- Destroy records to manufacture legal death while preserving the actor.
   606→- Create ancestor offices or worship loops that generate unbounded authority.
   607→
   608→### Identity exploits
   609→
   610→- Perfect memory copy claims all relationships and property.
   611→- Amnesiac original is displaced by a persuasive copy.
   612→- Two heirs each pass a different institutional test.
   613→- Possessor commits crimes through a host and shifts blame.
   614→- Teacher reincarnates as student and manipulates lineage seniority.
   615→- A sect declares inconvenient members “different persons” after deviation.
   616→- A powerful being calls a created consciousness merely an echo to deny personhood.
   617→- The protagonist receives favorable continuity judgments unavailable to isomorphic NPCs.
   618→
   619→### Persistence exploits
   620→
   621→- Save during death/resurrection prepare and reload after resource spend.
   622→- Unload the area to halt decay, ritual deadlines, possession, or spirit dispersal.
   623→- Migrate an old save across an identity-law revision and duplicate/remap continuants.
   624→- Demote to S0 so a named death loses witnesses, unique remains, debt, or scheduled funeral.
   625→- Cross a world boundary to shed identity, oath, or liability without an explicit compatibility rule.
   626→
   627→### Human tragedy tests
   628→
   629→- A poor household cannot afford the prestigious funeral.
   630→- An unidentified traveler dies far from home.
   631→- A child, elder, disabled person, stigmatized practitioner, criminal, enemy soldier, nonhuman person, and culturally mixed family each receive non-caricatured options.
   632→- A resurrection produces a loving, psychologically continuous successor whom the law rejects.
   633→- A returned person rejects the role and relationships others impose.
   634→- An ancestor manifestation is real but not numerically the deceased.
   635→- The only safe intervention preserves the world while allowing a beloved person’s final death.
   636→
   637→Passing requires systemic consequences, multiple defensible actions, preserved grief, and no hidden “correct morality” meter.

   641→## 9. Golden scenario fixtures
   642→
   643→1. **The winter bedside:** A mortal household must decide how to allocate fuel, food, healer fees, care labor, and debt while an elder declines. No cultivation spectacle is required for meaningful play.
   644→2. **The unclaimed body:** A stranger dies near the village with an unusual scar, inconsistent papers, and a hidden relationship. Burial, investigation, theft, fear, hospitality, law, and ritual compete.
   645→3. **The teacher’s remnant:** After a first cultivation lesson, the teacher dies. A memory-bearing remnant offers guidance, but its identity, accuracy, motive, and personhood are uncertain.
   646→4. **The copied confession:** Two beings remember committing the same act. Only one body was present; both carry genuine guilt. Legal and metaphysical judgments diverge.
   647→5. **The failed return:** A costly resurrection restores body and memories but not the original agency continuity under the selected model. The result is a person, not a defective item.
   648→6. **The rival reincarnation claims:** Two children display different causally valid traces of one dead cultivator. Fission rules, family grief, inheritance, sect politics, and the children’s autonomy matter.
   649→7. **World-law border:** A high-realm traveler crosses into a world where their identity carrier does not exist. Compatibility must preserve personhood or refuse the crossing honestly.
