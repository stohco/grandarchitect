
## 20. Failure, consequence, and recovery atlas

### 20.1 Failure doctrine

Failure should be:

- **legible:** the player can reconstruct most contributing causes;
- **proportional:** not every mistake destroys a life;
- **persistent:** consequences become history rather than disappearing;
- **socially interpreted:** people judge intention, warning, obligation, and honesty, not only outcome;
- **recoverable through changed play:** the player often continues with altered resources, body, residence, livelihood, or relationships;
- **non-teleological:** recovery need not restore the old state.

### 20.2 Failure and recovery matrix

| Setback | Immediate consequence | Secondary risk | Recovery routes | Persistent memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Poor or lost harvest | food/seed/rent shortfall | debt, sale of assets, hunger, migration | ration, substitute food, wage work, seed aid, rent relief, collective purchase, partial relocation | changed crop mix, creditor relation, thinner livestock, story of who shared |
| Tool/boat/loom failure | work stops; repair cost | missed window/order, injury, default | improvise, borrow/rent, repair labor exchange, salvage, subcontract, switch task | repaired object, obligation to lender, reputation of maker |
| Injury or illness | pain, lost function, care need | lost income, debt, neglect of dependents | rest/care, adaptation, task reassignment, specialist, mutual aid, new livelihood | scar/impairment, learned practice, caregiver bond or grievance |
| Caregiver exhaustion | mistakes, conflict, neglect | illness, household fracture | respite exchange, simplify obligations, recruit kin/neighbor, paid help, boundary setting | changed division of labor, recognition or resentment |
| Debt default | demand, shame, collateral/service risk | tenancy loss, coercion, countersuit, flight | early renegotiation, guarantor, refinance, work plan, collective relief, legal challenge, surrender limited asset | long creditor memory, altered trust and status |
| Tenancy/land-use loss | displacement, crop claim dispute | homelessness, job loss, household split | customary-right claim, compensation, new tenancy, service/lodging, migration, reclamation | abandoned house/plot, rival claim, diaspora tie |
| Theft/fraud accusation | lost trust, seizure, confrontation | violence, exclusion, official case | evidence, restitution, apology, mediation, trial, counterproof, protective relocation | rumor variants, record, faction alignment |
| Fire | shelter/store/tool loss, burns | winter exposure, debt, blame, spreading damage | bucket line, communal shelter, salvage, rebuilding compact, relief, investigation | rebuilt material, fire rules, memorial, liability |
| Flood/drought/storm | crop, route, house, animal damage | prices, disease, migration, conflict | preparedness, rescue, public works, relief, crop switch, debt moratorium, resettlement | altered channels/fields, graves, new institution |
| Death | grief, care/funeral labor, role vacancy | debt, inheritance, guardianship, household split | mutual aid, adaptive rite, leadership negotiation, adoption/guardianship, livelihood transfer | grave/tablet/story, anniversary, unresolved promise |
| Public humiliation or broken promise | reputation loss | credit/work/marriage exclusion | candid account, restitution, demonstrated reliability, mediator, new network/location | relationship-specific caution; not a global permanent meter |
| Failed apprenticeship/enterprise | sunk time/material, conflict | debt, identity loss | narrower specialty, different teacher, wage work, salvage stock, admit mismatch | practical knowledge retained, former-master relationship |

### 20.3 Recovery resources

Recovery capacity comes from diversified stores, skills, tools, health, kin and non-kin ties, mutual aid, reputation, legal claims, mobility, ecological knowledge, and institutions. It must not collapse into money.

**[Design candidate]** Maintain a “recovery graph” invisible as a score but inspectable through the world:

- people who might provide food, shelter, testimony, work, care, transport, or mediation;
- institutions with criteria, limited stores, schedules, and political motives;
- assets that can be repaired, pawned, divided, entrusted, or relocated;
- skills usable under the protagonist's current body and access conditions;
- claims that can be asserted;
- destinations reachable before reserves run out.

### 20.4 No automatic misery spiral

Compounding risk is realistic; hopeless deterministic decline is not. The system should generate:

- early warnings and partial interventions;
- small reciprocities that matter;
- creditors who prefer workable renegotiation to destruction;
- institutions and people who sometimes help without secret malice;
- new livelihoods emerging from crisis work;
- migration as a complex strategy, not automatic punishment;
- joy, humor, love, play, and dignity during hardship.

Conversely, the player cannot solve structural vulnerability solely by winning minigames. Recovery may require changing a rule, building a coalition, accepting dependence, leaving, or living differently.

## 21. The playable mortal year

### 21.1 Year shape

The first complete year should be generated around a **household or survival-network strategy under uncertainty**, not a main quest chain. A household may contain one person; a traveler, displaced person, hermit, lodger, institutional resident, or person without stable housing still depends on explicit care, exchange, shelter, and obligation networks rather than being treated as a contentless solitary avatar.

At year start, the player knows:

- household members and visible roles, but not every secret or future desire;
- residence, immediate stores, tools, access rights, debts/claims, and next deadlines;
- their own embodied competence and a few learned routines;
- the local route to water, fields/workplace, neighbors, and the next market;
- several conflicting accounts of the coming weather, prices, institutions, and strange phenomena.

The year provides:

- one main ecological cycle with at least two hard labor windows;
- recurring market/institution cycles;
- a household negotiation that cannot be solved by obedience alone;
- one care demand;
- one meaningful opportunity to learn, specialize, migrate, or build enterprise;
- one dispute with more than one legitimate forum;
- ritual/festival/funeral possibilities based on generated events, not mandatory checklisting;
- weather variance with observable signs;
- at least one setback whose recovery changes the player's path;
- subtle, ambiguous contact with the larger xianxia cosmos only if later cosmology research authorizes it.

### 21.2 Example phase gates, not scripted chapters

| Gate | What the player should understand by then | Example emergent proof |
|---|---|---|
| First several days | household and bodily time; food/water/fuel routes; who can ask whom | player rearranges one day's work and sees who bears the cost |
| First local cycle | market/information/credit network | player prepares goods or service, travels, returns with changed information and obligations |
| First hard window | ecological skill and labor coordination | weather forces a choice among field/work, care, paid obligation, and repair |
| First public event | reputation is contextual; institutions have process | player participates in a festival, dispute, work levy, funeral, or relief effort |
| First major setback | failure changes play rather than ending it | injury, crop damage, debt, conflict, or loss opens recovery routes |
| End of cycle | memory and continuity | stores, landscape, debts, relationships, skills, bodies, households, and stories all differ visibly from the start |

### 21.3 Mortal agency without power fantasy

The player can become consequential by:

- noticing earlier;
- learning whose knowledge applies;
- coordinating people fairly or persuasively;
- maintaining tools and relations;
- carrying trustworthy information;
- creating records and procedures;
- taking risk on behalf of someone;
- making, repairing, healing, teaching, hosting, mediating, or organizing;
- discovering exploitation and choosing whether and how to contest it;
- preserving future options under uncertainty.

This makes later personal power emotionally measurable. The player knows what it once cost to move a sack, heal a fracture, cross a flooded river, keep grain dry, send a letter, or defy a creditor.

## 22. Mortal-year gauntlet and review criteria

Before the project may treat the mortal foundation as designed, a prototype specification or paper simulation should survive the following tests. These are review gates, not implementation instructions.

### 22.1 Continuity gauntlet

- Follow one water vessel from source to drinking, cooking, washing, craft use, wastewater, and downstream effect.
- Follow one unit of grain from seed claim through labor, weather, harvest, processing, rent/debt, storage, cooking, and allocation.
- Follow one garment from fiber through production, wear, repair, pawn, inheritance, and reuse.
- Follow one rumor from witness through market, household, institution, contradiction, and memory.
- Follow one injury through lost work, care labor, debt, adaptation, altered skill use, and social interpretation.

**Pass condition:** no resource, fact, or consequence teleports between systems.

### 22.2 One-year household gauntlet

Simulate at least three starting households in the same settlement:

1. a resource-thin tenant or service household;
2. a middling mixed-livelihood household with tools but labor/care pressure;
3. a locally powerful creditor, landlord, merchant, institutional, or office-linked household.

Then simulate the same weather and market year without privileging the protagonist.

**Pass condition:** the year produces unequal but causally understandable outcomes; weaker households retain agency; powerful households face coordination, legitimacy, and concentration risks; no household is a stereotype.

### 22.3 Perspective gauntlet

Replay or audit key events from:

- different genders and ages;
- a disabled or chronically ill person;
- a hired/dependent worker;
- a migrant or linguistic outsider;
- the creditor and debtor;
- upstream and downstream water users;
- believer, skeptic, and ritual worker;
- literate scribe and non-literate but numerate trader.

**Pass condition:** different lives reveal different constraints, verbs, knowledge, and leverage—not merely altered dialogue or difficulty.

### 22.4 Chore gauntlet

Audit every recurring action after the first few repetitions.

**Pass condition:** it is summarized, delegated, conditional, socially interesting, materially variable, or voluntarily embodied. No mandatory low-decision animation remains solely to advertise realism.

### 22.5 Recovery gauntlet

Force crop failure, injury, fire, debt default, bereavement, and wrongful accusation separately and in two plausible combinations.

**Pass condition:** each has multiple recovery routes, at least one non-monetary route, persistent consequences, and no guaranteed optimal answer. At least one route leads to a genuinely different viable life rather than restoration.

### 22.6 Cultural coherence gauntlet

For every generated custom, object, building, institution, or festival, reviewers must be able to answer:

- what ecology/material supply supports it;
- what labor and knowledge reproduce it;
- who pays and who benefits;
- how it varies by household/status/body;
- whether it is sourced mechanism, authored invention, or canon fact;
- what historical source limits apply;
- whether it copies a living or historical culture too directly.

**Pass condition:** no feature survives on “because xianxia” alone.

### 22.7 Fun gauntlet

- Does the player form specific hopes for tomorrow and next season?
- Are there at least three compelling goals that do not involve violence, wealth maximization, or entering cultivation?
- Can skilled play consist of judgment, care, craft, persuasion, memory, logistics, or courage?
- Do NPC initiatives regularly surprise the player while remaining explainable?
- Can a quiet evening, successful repair, shared meal, letter, game, or resolved water turn feel like a real reward?
- Does the year contain breathing room and beauty, not only pressure?

**Pass condition:** the later signed playtest threshold for voluntary continuation in mortal life is met even when a credible cultivation path is available; feedback shows the choice arises from attachment to particular people, work, places, and unresolved possibilities rather than confusion about how to progress.

## 23. Open research questions before specification

### 23.1 Highest-priority gaps

1. **Comparative household evidence beyond late-imperial Han-majority records.** Commission focused research on several regions, ethnic communities, frontier conditions, and earlier periods, with living-culture sensitivity review.
2. **Foodways by ecology and class.** Build sourced seasonal food baskets, preservation methods, kitchen fuel budgets, infant/elder foods, feast foods, famine substitutions, and food-safety risks without turning modern regional cuisines into timeless facts.
3. **Water and sanitation at household scale.** Wells, latrines, wastewater, bathing, laundry, menstrual care, infant care, animal proximity, and seasonal contamination need precise regional studies.
4. **Fuel and woodland regimes.** The present design logic is strong but the China-specific evidence base here is thin. Research commons, forest tenure, charcoal, crop residues, dung, transport radius, and indoor smoke across several ecologies.
5. **Childhood and adolescence.** Research play, work, schooling, discipline, sibling care, adoption/fostering, coming-of-age, and child perspectives using sources that do not only depict elite boys.
6. **Leisure and performance.** The current section is intentionally conservative. Research storytelling, opera/theater circuits, games, gambling, sports, songs, toys, courtship, fairs, and rest by class, gender, age, and region.
7. **Fisheries, pastoralism, mining, salt, and mountain livelihoods.** These require their own ecological and labor calendars rather than being extensions of farming.
8. **Pregnancy, childbirth, postpartum care, contraception, infant feeding, and reproductive loss.** Use medical-history and gender-history specialists; build robust content controls.
9. **Disability history.** Expand beyond Qing household-care framing to material aids, work, law, poverty relief, communication, intellectual disability, mental distress, and first-person evidence where available.
10. **Violence and safety.** Research rural policing, watches, domestic safety, refuge, banditry, feud, collective violence, enslavement, trafficking, and legal redress with ethical review.
11. **Measures, money, prices, and accounting.** Avoid false precision across eras. Establish original-world standards and local variation from research on historical metrology and bimetallic/commodity exchange.
12. **Ritual diversity and religious institutions.** Separate local cult, household rite, Buddhism, Daoist traditions, state cult, spirit-medium practice, Islam, Christianity, indigenous/community traditions, and skeptic positions where historically relevant; do not merge them into a fantasy soup.
13. **Climate baselines and disaster distributions.** The fictional world's own orbital, geological, and magical laws must come first; historical events should inform response systems, not be copied as an annual event table.
14. **Language and translation.** Determine how dialect, register, multilingual trade, literacy, names, technical vocabulary, gesture, and misunderstanding function in the original universe.
15. **Aesthetic material study.** Architecture, vessels, tools, clothing, fields, roads, boats, lighting, wear, repair, dirt, storage, and soundscapes should be specified only after the causal settlement bundles are approved.

### 23.2 Questions for the later cosmology team

- Does mortal biology follow ordinary nutrition, infection, aging, pregnancy, disability, and death until cultivation changes it? Which changes are gradual, categorical, heritable, or socially supplied?
- What is objectively detectable before formal cultivation, and with what false-positive rate?
- Can supernatural forces alter weather, soil, water, disease, craft materials, memory, or contracts? If so, why have mortal institutions not reorganized around reliable use?
- What costs prevent cultivators from solving every harvest, transport, medical, or disaster problem?
- How do mortal law, taxation, religion, inheritance, and markets classify rare supernatural events without universal knowledge?
- Which mortal skills remain valuable after cultivation and why?

No answer should be inserted into the mortal simulation until it is authorized as cosmological canon.

## 25. Provisional design decisions for review

These are **recommended non-canon decisions** distilled from the research. They require approval before entering a PRD or lore bible.

1. Make the household or explicit survival/care network—including one-person and non-co-resident forms—the smallest complete survival economy, not an unsupported solitary avatar.
2. Generate settlements from compatible ecology-livelihood-institution bundles.
3. Replace “week” with locally authored overlapping cycles while preserving interface accessibility.
4. Treat agricultural expertise as observation and local judgment, not recipe unlocking.
5. Represent food, water, fuel, cloth, care, and storage as linked processes with competing uses.
6. Make rent, tax, debt, wages, service, public works, and reciprocity distinct obligation types.
7. Model literacy as domain capabilities and information as physically transmitted belief.
8. Make disability and care alter actions, schedules, spaces, and relationships—not simply statistics.
9. Separate metaphysical truth, personal belief, and public practice.
10. Let norms pressure behavior while allowing historically and socially coherent variation.
11. Give every recurring task a summarization, delegation, conditional, social, or voluntary-embodiment path.
12. Design failure to produce recoverable life changes and lasting world memory.
13. Require at least a full mortal-year paper simulation before cultivation progression is specified.
14. Use the mortal-year gauntlet as a standing review suite for every later system; supernatural scale must not erase mortal continuity without a canonically justified mechanism.

## 26. Definition of research readiness

This atlas is ready to become an implementation design input only when:

- high-priority research gaps have assigned owners and evidence standards;
- at least three mutually distinct ecological complexes have complete food/water/fuel/labor/market/care calendars;
- household and life-course models have been reviewed for gender, disability, class, labor coercion, and living-culture sensitivity;
- the original world's mortal biology, climate, geography, political institutions, and metaphysical leakage are separately authorized;
- every proposed content bundle carries source scope, authored invention label, compatibility rules, and prohibited stereotypes;
- a non-code paper simulation passes the continuity, household, perspective, chore, recovery, cultural-coherence, and fun gauntlets;
- canon custodians explicitly promote selected design candidates. Silence does not do so.

Until those conditions are met, this remains a research scaffold—not the mortal world's final truth.
