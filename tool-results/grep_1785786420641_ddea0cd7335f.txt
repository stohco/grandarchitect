20:### 1.1 The compound and its valley
21-
22-The Cangwu Mountains (蒼梧山脈, doc 24 §4.2) are a long granitic arc running roughly southwest to northeast through southern Qingzhou Prefecture. Wang Family Bend sits on the plain below their southern foothills; the Cangwu Sect sits in their central spine, one hundred li west of the bend, in a hanging valley called **Cangwu Valley** (蒼梧谷) at an elevation of roughly 1,400 meters. The valley is roughly two li long and half a li wide, oriented east-west, walled on three sides by granite ridges and open on the east to a steep descent into the foothills. A single paved mule-track — the sect's only road — switchbacks up the eastern wall and through the **Stone Gate** (石門), a natural cleft between two house-sized boulders that is the sect's formal threshold and its only easy access. Above the Stone Gate, painted in fading cinnabar, is the sect's seal: a stylized wutong tree (梧桐) under three short strokes representing the three peaks of the valley's western wall.
23-
24:Through the valley's center runs **Stone Gate Creek** (石門溪), a fast, cold stream that drops out of a spring on the western wall, crosses the valley floor in a series of small cascades, and exits east through a crack beneath the Stone Gate itself. The creek is the sect's water source, its laundry, and (in summer, when the children are let out of the scripture hall) its swimming hole. Its qi is faintly water-phase — cool, dense, downward-tending — and the sect's outer disciples learn to perceive qi, in their first months, by attending to the creek's surface.
25-
26:On the valley's north wall, three hundred meters above the creek, a granite shelf juts out: **Holding Sun Cliff** (抱陽崖), so named because it faces due south and catches the full arc of the winter sun. The cliff is where the sect's scripture hall, the sect master's house, and the inner disciples' dormitories are built. A wooden stair of 211 steps climbs from the valley floor to the cliff; the stair is steep and old, and the third step from the top has cracked along its grain, so every disciple in the sect has learned to skip it without thinking. At the top of the stair sits the **Founder's Hall** (祖師堂), a single-room timber building with a tile roof, three ancestor tablets on the altar, and — behind the altar, in a sandalwood box — the founder's token: a thumb-sized jade bead, faintly green, qi-bound to the spirit vein. The bead is the sect master's authority; without it, the elder council can convene but cannot ratify.
27-
28:The compound's other buildings: the **Merit Hall** (功德堂), where tasks are posted on a board and qi-stone stipends are counted out; the **Scripture Pavilion** (藏經閣), a two-story stone building with a warded door and a single Foundation-locked inner chamber; the **Pill Hall** (丹房), really a kitchen-and-workshop with three small furnaces, two of which work; the **Punishment Shed** (刑房), unused in seven years and currently used to store kindling; and the outer disciples' **Long Dormitory** (長舍), a single building of packed earth and timber with forty cots in two rows. The Long Dormitory is overcapacity at thirty (the sect's inner disciples, twelve of them, sleep in smaller rooms on the cliff); the surplus cots hold stored grain and the corpse of a Foundation Establishment manual so damaged the Scripture Pavilion will not shelve it.
29-
30:Behind the compound, on the western wall, the spirit vein breaks the surface: a **spring** (青鏡泉, the Green Mirror Spring) in a small cave behind a curtain of moss, where the qi is dense enough to taste — cool, faintly sweet, water-phase dominant with a wood-phase undertone that smells like wet bark. The spring feeds the creek. The sect's main cultivation practice happens in a half-circle of flat stones around the spring's mouth, where outer disciples sit in pre-dawn rotation.

32:### 1.2 The sect master: Wu Changqing (吳長清)
33-
34:Wu Changqing is fifty-two years old and looks sixty. He is tall, thin, slightly stooped, with a sparse beard he allows to grow because his wife (dead nine years, in the same fever season that took two of the outer disciples) once said she liked it. He wears the dark blue robe of his office most days, patched at both elbows, and a pair of straw sandals he replaces himself every three months. His dantian, perceived by a Qi Condensation cultivator, is a low, steady warmth — the warmth of a banked fire, not a burning one. His meridians, perceived at the same resolution, carry a faint wrongness in the central conduit: a scar, a kink, the residue of the failed Foundation Establishment integration thirty-one years ago.
35-
36-He attempted Foundation Establishment at twenty-one, on the advice and with the support of his own teacher (the previous sect master, Daoist Cangwu, who died at seventy-eight of pure old age — the only peaceful death in the sect's recorded line). The integration began well and collapsed at the seventh meridian — the heart-conduit — when Wu Changqing's wood-phase dominant qi met an unprocessed grief (his elder brother had died in a hunting accident six weeks earlier; Wu Changqing had not mourned him; he had gone straight back to practice). The collapse nearly killed him. He survived by aborting the integration at the cost of permanent meridian damage: the heart-conduit narrowed, and the Qi Condensation reservoir has been leaky ever since. He cannot attempt Foundation Establishment again. The narrow conduit would not survive a second integration.
37-
38:His grief is twofold. The first grief is the brother — unmourned, then turned to grief for his own collapse, then turned to grief for the brother again. The second grief is the sect. He inherited a sect of forty-two disciples and a vein producing a third again its current output; he has presided over the decline of both. The vein is thinning (the Azure Sword Sect's deep-tap upstream draws, he believes without proof, more than the tribute records show); the disciple cohort is shrinking (the last recruitment tour, two years ago, found only three children with spiritual roots in nine villages). He has not said this aloud to anyone. He writes it, in private, in a small notebook he keeps in the founder's hall, behind the altar, in a box that once held a different jade bead.
39-
40-His ambition is also twofold, and the two strands knot against each other. The first ambition is for the sect: to leave it stronger than he found it — which means, concretely, to find a successor who can break through to Foundation Establishment, which he cannot, and to break or transform the vassalage to the Azure Sword Sect, which he cannot do alone. The second ambition is for himself: to die whole. He has watched Old Chen (he knows Old Chen; they correspond, twice a year, by letter carried through Qinghe Market Town) reach the same Qi Condensation peak and grow old without bitterness. He cannot. He is bitter. He does not know if this is a flaw in him or a flaw in his circumstance, and he writes about this in the notebook too, in passages that have grown longer over the years.

44:### 1.3 The thirty disciples
45-
46:The sect has thirty disciples on the rolls at the protagonist's arrival. Twelve are inner disciples (Qi Condensation, mid-to-peak); eighteen are outer (Qi Induction to early Qi Condensation). The eight specified below are the ones whose presence the simulation must track from session ~120 onward, because each will, at some point in Acts 2 or 3, do something the protagonist cannot ignore.
47-
48:**Wang Meili (王美麗)** — the protagonist's cousin (father's brother's daughter), twenty-two at the protagonist's arrival, early Qi Condensation, healer-track. She entered the sect at fourteen against her father's wishes (her father, the protagonist's uncle Wang Shouye, distrusts Old Chen and by extension all retired cultivators; he sent Meili to the Cangwu Sect because it was a sect, which is to say an institution, which he understands, rather than a hermit, which he does not). She is small, watchful, precise, with calloused hands from the herb garden and a quiet steadiness the sect's other outer disciples find either reassuring or unnerving depending on their own temperaments. She has, by the time the protagonist arrives, been the sect's effective healer for two years — the previous healer, an old inner disciple named Lin Shu, died of a stroke the previous winter and Wu Changqing has not replaced her. Meili's practice is wood-phase (good for healing, good for growth) with a water-phase secondary. She is the protagonist's first ally in the sect and, eventually (doc 26 §9.1), the lineage bridge.
49-
50:**Yan Boyan (嚴伯言)** — twenty-eight, late Qi Condensation, the protagonist's first rival in the sect. Yan is the son of the headman of Cangwu Sect's largest tenant village, sent to the sect at eight on a sect-recruitment tour. He is broad-shouldered, handsome in a hard-jawed way, meticulous about his appearance, and ambitious in a way that has curdled over twenty years of being the most talented disciple in a sect too small to reward him. He attempted Foundation Establishment at twenty-five and failed — not as catastrophically as Wu Changqing (his meridians are intact) but badly enough that he has not tried again. He believes (without proof) that Wu Changqing has held back a key technique from him. He believes (with some proof) that the Azure Sword Sect would treat him better. He is the leader of what the inner disciples quietly call the **Northern Faction** — those who favor accepting Azure Sword Sect patronage. When the protagonist arrives — a Foundation Establishment cultivator who will, by sect custom, be ranked above Yan despite having done nothing for the sect — Yan's resentment finds a new object. The rivalry is professional at first (spirit-stone allocations, mission assignments, scripture-hall access), then personal, then (by Act 3, depending on player choices) factional.
51-
52:**Zhao Wujiu (趙五九)** — twenty-four, mid Qi Condensation, the protagonist's first friend in the sect. "Wujiu" is a childhood name (Fifth-Nine, from his birth order in his father's lineage and his mother's) that he never replaced with a courtesy name because, he says, he likes being numbered. He is tall, soft-voiced, ugly in a kind way (a long nose, a crooked smile), and the son of a herdboy from a tenant village. He entered the sect at twelve (a late recruit — the sect found him when he was helping drive cattle past the Stone Gate and a senior disciple noticed he could perceive the creek's qi without instruction). His practice is earth-phase, slow and steady, and his cultivation has been the slowest in his cohort — he is twenty-four and only mid Qi Condensation, where Yan Boyan is twenty-eight and late Qi Condensation. Zhao Wujiu does not mind. He likes the sect. He likes the routine. He likes Wu Changqing. He will become the protagonist's first friend in the sect because he is the only inner disciple who, on the protagonist's first day, talks to the protagonist without calculating.
53-
54:**Su Wenli (蘇文理)** — thirty-one, Qi Condensation peak, the betrayer. Su is the son of a county-school teacher from Cangli County Seat who paid the sect a silver fee to take his son as an outer disciple at fifteen (a practice the sect accepts when the treasury is low, which is most years). Su is intelligent, well-read, articulate, and possessed of a self-regard that he has learned to disguise as humility. He is the sect's effective Scripture Hall steward (the actual hall head, an elderly inner disciple named Old Liu, is in early-stage cognitive decline and lets Su run the hall). Su is also, as the protagonist will discover over Act 2, in quiet correspondence with Elder Rong Zhaoyuan of the Azure Sword Sect (the Merit Hall head and leader of the Azure Sword expansionists, §2.5). The correspondence began four years ago, after the Azure Sword Sect's last tribute-collection visit, when Rong Zhaoyuan complimented Su on a memo he had written about the Cangwu Sect's vein output. Su does not think of himself as a traitor. He thinks of himself as a realist. The Cangwu Sect is dying; the Azure Sword Sect is the future; the protagonist's arrival is a complication in an arrangement he has spent four years building. He will betray the sect in Act 2 by leaking the protagonist's Foundation Establishment status and the vein's deep-tap location to Rong Zhaoyuan — information that accelerates the Azure Sword Sect's formal claim at session ~300.
55-
56:**Old Liu (劉師兄, Liu Shixiong)** — forty-seven, mid Qi Condensation, the sect's Scripture Hall head in name and the outer disciples' de facto father in practice. Old Liu entered the sect at seven (the youngest recruit in sect history) and has not left the valley in thirty-eight years. He is short, round, perpetually amused, and unable to read anything more complex than the sect's basic manuals — which is why he let Su Wenli take over the Scripture Hall's administrative work. He teaches the outer disciples' morning scripture class himself (from memory; he has the foundational manual memorized in its entirety, with all Wu Changqing's annotations) and he is the one who, when an outer disciple is crying in the dormitory at two in the morning, gets up and makes them tea. He is, in his way, the sect's actual heart. He will not betray the sect, will not rise in it, and will die (in Act 3, of a stroke, at sixty-one, in the Scripture Hall with a cup of tea in his hand) having never perceived his own anchor.
57-
58:**Ma Yuening (馬月寧)** — nineteen, early Qi Condensation (broke through three months before the protagonist's arrival; the breakthrough was the sect's social event of the year), the youngest inner disciple. She is from a fishing family on the Eastern Sea coast — recruited on the same tour that produced Yan Boyan — and she is, in temperament, the opposite of Yan: open, excitable, generous with her opinions, prone to crushes (on Yan at sixteen, on Su at eighteen, on Zhao Wujiu at nineteen — the last of which Zhao has not noticed). Her practice is water-phase (matching the vein), and she is the most talented pure cultivator the sect has produced in two generations. Wu Changqing, privately, believes she might be the one — the disciple who reaches Foundation Establishment legitimately. He has not told her this; he is afraid of putting the same pressure on her that broke him.
59-
60:**The Cao Twins — Cao Shan (曹山) and Cao Hai (曹海)** — both thirty, late Qi Condensation, sons of a deceased inner disciple (Cao Ping, who died in a beast hunt twelve years ago) and raised in the sect. They are not identical — Cao Shan is the older by half an hour, taller, slower, kinder; Cao Hai is shorter, quicker, sharper — but they have spent thirty years finishing each other's sentences and the sect has stopped trying to tell them apart except by height. They are Yan Boyan's wingmen in the Northern Faction, more out of lifelong habit than conviction. They will, if the protagonist befriends Yan, follow Yan; if the protagonist befriends them, they will (reluctantly, then with relief) follow the protagonist. They are the sect's swing vote in any inner-disciple dispute.
61-
62:**Peng Li (彭力)** — thirty-five, mid Qi Condensation, the sect's woodworker and the dormitory's eldest occupant. He is the only disciple who entered the sect as an adult (he was a carpenter in Qinghe Market Town; he perceived qi at twenty-eight after a long illness and walked to the Stone Gate on foot; Wu Changqing admitted him on the spot). He is quiet, slow-speaking, large-handed, and the only disciple in the sect who can repair a warded door or rebuild a qi-channel in the spirit-vein tap. He is indispensable and knows it. He is also, like Zhao Wujiu, fundamentally content — he wants nothing from the sect except to keep working wood and practicing in the spring's half-circle. He is the protagonist's unlikely ally in Act 2: the practical man who, when the politics gets thick, asks the question that deflates it ("Will it still need a door?").
63-
64:**Tong Sijing (童思敬)** — fourteen, early Qi Induction, the sect's youngest disciple and, by Wu Changqing's quiet assessment, its most unusual. Tong Sijing was found by the last recruitment tour at age eleven in a charcoal-burner's hut in the southern foothills, the daughter of a charcoal-burner and a deaf-mute mother who had walked out of the mountains years before and would not say from where. She did not speak Cangli dialect; she spoke a mountain dialect no one in the sect recognized, and she has, in three years, learned Cangli dialect with an accent the other outer disciples tease her about (gently; she is small and watchful and they are mostly kind). She has, however, an inexplicable talent for the founder's water-phase primary route — she picked it up in two weeks where most outer disciples take three months — and a perception of qi-residue that Wu Changqing has, in private, called "Core Formation-clear at Qi Induction," a thing he has never seen in fifty years of cultivation and does not know how to explain.
65-
66:Tong Sijing does not know how to explain it either. She says, when asked, that the qi "has always been there, the same way the creek has always been there," and that she does not understand why this is remarkable. Wu Changqing has not told anyone else about her residue-perception; he is afraid of what it might mean and afraid of what the Azure Sword Sect would do if they knew. He has written, in his notebook, in the small careful hand he uses for the entries that frighten him: "If the vein has produced a perception-talent, the vein is deeper than we know." Tong Sijing, for her part, is too young to have an arc; she has, instead, a trajectory — a line the simulation must track from her arrival to her late teens, when her talent will either flower into something the sect cannot hide or be quietly suppressed by a sect master who knows what the Azure Sword Sect does to perception-talents it cannot recruit. The protagonist, on arrival, notices her in the first week (she is the only disciple younger than the protagonist, and the only one who watches the protagonist practice without calculation). The protagonist's relationship with Tong Sijing, across Act 2, is the relationship of a senior cultivator who sees a junior talent and must decide what to do about it — teach her, hide her, or, hardest, let her find her own way.

68:### 1.4 The spirit vein: the Green Mirror Vein (青鏡脈)
69-
70:The sect's spirit vein is the **Green Mirror Vein** (青鏡脈), a yin-dominant, water-phase-primary vein with a wood-phase secondary, running northeast-southwest through the granite of the central Cangwu range. Its surface trace is the Green Mirror Spring (§1.1); its main current lies roughly 240 meters below the valley floor, flowing slowly northeast at an estimated rate of (the sect's last measurement, fifteen years ago) enough to sustain thirty-five to forty Qi Condensation cultivators at full practice. The vein's history: it was discovered 240 years ago by a wandering Qi Condensation cultivator named Daoist Cangwu (the founder's courtesy name; his personal name is not recorded), who settled the valley, built the Founder's Hall, and slowly attracted the first disciples. The sect was formally constituted 180 years ago (doc 24 §4.4), at which point the vein's output was nearly double its current level. The decline is real (Wu Changqing's notebook records it; the Azure Sword Sect's tribute-collectors know it); the cause is contested. Wu Changqing believes the Azure Sword Sect's deep-tap upstream — at the Jade Edge Grotto (§2.3) — is drawing more than the tribute records state. The Azure Sword Sect maintains the decline is geological, the vein aging naturally. The vein's phase-affinity shapes the sect's entire practice tradition: the founder's manual is a water-phase primary circulation with wood-phase secondary integration, and every disciple the sect has ever produced has practiced it. This is why the sect's cultivators tend toward healer and diviner paths and away from combat — water and wood are gentle phases. It is also why the Azure Sword Sect (metal-phase) considers the Cangwu Sect's tradition soft and the Cangwu Sect's territory, by extension, an underdeveloped resource.

72:### 1.5 The relationship to the Azure Sword Sect: vassalage
73-
74:The Cangwu Sect is, in everything but name, a vassal of the Azure Sword Sect. The arrangement is not formalized in a treaty (the Azure Sword Sect does not treaty with Qi Condensation sects); it is a generational fact sustained by three things. First, **tribute**: the Cangwu Sect sends, every autumn, a chest of six low-grade spirit stones and four bundles of wood-phase spirit herbs (the sect's only export-grade crop) to the Azure Sword Sect, delivered by outer disciple escort to the foothills and there handed over to an Azure Sword Sect outer disciple who comes down to meet them. The tribute is roughly a quarter of the sect's annual surplus; Wu Changqing pays it because not paying it would invite a visit. Second, **protection**: in theory, the Azure Sword Sect defends the Cangwu Sect from external threats. In practice, the last Azure Sword Sect intervention on the Cangwu Sect's behalf was twelve years ago, when a Foundation Establishment beast (a jade-scaled python from the southern foothills) threatened the tenant villages and Wu Changqing, whose own combat ability is limited, requested help. The Azure Sword Sect sent a single Foundation Establishment elder, who killed the python in an afternoon and stayed the night in the Founder's Hall and complimented the tea. Third, **non-competition**: the Cangwu Sect does not recruit in villages the Azure Sword Sect claims; the Azure Sword Sect does not, in principle, recruit in the Cangwu Sect's territory. In practice, the Azure Sword Sect's definition of "its territory" extends to the entire central Cangwu range, and the Cangwu Sect's effective recruitment zone is six villages within thirty li of the Stone Gate.
75-
76:The relationship is resented. Every disciple in the sect knows the tribute is excessive; every inner disciple knows the protection is theoretical; Yan Boyan and Su Wenli know, in addition, that the arrangement is structurally unchangeable from below. Wu Changqing's resentment is the most private and the most pointed: his teacher (the previous sect master) was friends, in his youth, with the Azure Sword Sect's previous sect master (the Zhou Wugui-killer, doc 24 §4.4) — they had hunted together once, in the foothills, as young cultivators. That friendship was the reason the tribute, in the previous sect master's time, was three stones and two bundles. After the previous Azure Sword sect master died and Leng Wushuang succeeded him, the tribute was "reassessed" upward. Wu Changqing has never forgiven this. He has also never mentioned it to anyone except Old Liu, who is the only one who remembers the old amount.

78:### 1.6 Internal politics: three factions and the succession question
79-
80-The sect's internal politics are organized, at the protagonist's arrival, into three informal factions. None is named; all are real.
81-
82:The **Old Mountain faction** is led by Old Liu (in his amiable, distracted way) and includes Peng Li, Ma Yuening, and most of the outer disciples by default. Its position: the sect's tradition is correct, the vassalage is regrettable but endurable, and the path forward is patient cultivation and the hope of a Foundation Establishment successor. The Old Mountain faction is the largest and the quietest; it does not meet as a faction, it does not strategize, it is simply the disposition of the sect's contented majority.
83-
84:The **Northern faction** (Yan Boyan, Su Wenli, the Cao twins, and two other inner disciples) favors accepting formal Azure Sword Sect patronage. The argument: the Cangwu Sect cannot survive another generation at its current trajectory; the Azure Sword Sect offers resources, recruitment access, and a path for ambitious disciples (Yan) into the larger sect; the sect's independence is, in any case, a fiction. The Northern faction is the most politically active; it meets, in effect, whenever Yan and Su walk together in the valley, which is most evenings. The Northern faction's internal tension: Su Wenli is in correspondence with the Azure Sword Sect (§1.3); Yan Boyan does not know this. Yan thinks the Northern faction's strategy is to negotiate from a position of internal consensus. Su knows the negotiation is, in fact, already happening, and the question is only what terms the Cangwu Sect will accept.
85-
86-The **Independence faction** is Wu Changqing alone, with the protagonist (on arrival) as its only potential ally. Its position: the vassalage must be broken, the vein must be defended, the Azure Sword Sect is a predator, and the path forward is to find allies outside the central Cangwu range — the Jade Void Holy Land (distant, but historically interested in the Cangli Riverlands), the rogue cultivator network (Old Chen's connections, which the protagonist inherits), or the Wang lineage itself (which is, after the protagonist's Foundation Establishment, technically a cultivation-lineage-in-waiting). The Independence faction is the smallest and the most desperate; Wu Changqing knows he cannot win a confrontation with the Azure Sword Sect and is betting everything on the protagonist.
87-
88:The succession question: Wu Changqing has no children and no designated heir. The elder council, in the sect's history, has convened twice to discuss succession — once when Wu Changqing was forty (he fought off a quiet movement to designate Yan Boyan as heir-apparent) and once when he was forty-eight (after a chest infection that winter that frightened everyone, including him). The council is, in practice, the inner disciples plus Old Liu, and the question is who will be sect master when Wu Changqing ages out or dies. The candidates, in descending order of likelihood at the protagonist's arrival: Yan Boyan (the senior inner disciple, ambitious, the Northern faction's choice); the protagonist (Foundation Establishment, the only cultivator in the sect qualified by realm, but a newcomer with no faction); Su Wenli (Qi Condensation peak, intelligent, but scarier to the Old Mountain faction than Yan); Ma Yuening (talented, young, but three years a Qi Condensation cultivator and not yet ready). Wu Changqing's private preference, which he has not stated and will not state until forced: the protagonist, with Ma Yuening as a future successor after Foundation Establishment. This preference is the engine of the sect's Act 2 internal tension.

90:### 1.7 The protagonist's daily life as an outer disciple
91-
92:The protagonist, despite being Foundation Establishment, enters the sect as an outer disciple — a deliberate choice by Wu Changqing, endorsed by Old Chen's letter, on the grounds that the protagonist has no sect training and must learn the sect's tradition from the bottom. The arrangement is unusual and the sect knows it; the outer disciples, on the protagonist's first week, watch the protagonist carry water and weed the herb garden with a mixture of awe and unease. The protagonist's daily schedule, from session ~120 onward:
93-
94-**Dawn (寅時, roughly 4-6 a.m.):** silent practice at the Green Mirror Spring's half-circle. The protagonist has a reserved seat (Wu Changqing's order; Yan Boyan, who had the seat, was moved one stone to the left). Practice runs 90 minutes. The protagonist practices the sect's water-phase primary route (different from Old Chen's wood-phase route; the protagonist is learning it for the first time) alongside the other dawn-rotation cultivators.
95-
96:**Early morning (卯時, 6-8 a.m.):** communal breakfast in the Pill Hall — millet porridge with pickled vegetables, with a small bowl of spirit-herb broth twice a week. After breakfast, the protagonist's outer-disciple duties: three mornings a week, herb-garden weeding with Wang Meili (this is how the protagonist and Meili rebuild their cousin-friendship); two mornings, scripture-hall maintenance with Old Liu (sweeping, sanding the warded door, copying damaged manuals); one morning, spirit-vein tap inspection with Peng Li (the protagonist's Foundation Establishment perception is useful here — Peng Li asks, in the protagonist's second week, whether the vein "feels right" at depth, a question Peng Li has wanted to ask a Foundation Establishment cultivator for years).
97-
98:**Mid-morning to midday (辰時-午時, 8 a.m.-12 p.m.):** sect missions or free practice. Missions are posted on the Merit Hall board; the protagonist, as Foundation Establishment, is offered higher-tier missions (beast suppression in the foothills, escort to Qinghe Market Town, investigation of qi-anomalies in the tenant villages) that pay more spirit stones than the outer-disciple standard. The protagonist may take these or decline; declining is allowed but noted by Su Wenli, who tracks the Merit Hall's books.
99-
100-**Midday meal:** rice with whatever the kitchen has, eaten in the Pill Hall or, on warm days, on the steps of the Founder's Hall.
101-
--
104:**Evening (酉時, 5-7 p.m.):** evening meal, then the outer-disciple scripture lecture in the scripture hall. Old Liu teaches the foundational manual; the protagonist attends (Wu Changqing's order, again) and sits at the back. The protagonist already knows the material at a level Old Liu cannot teach, but Wu Changqing's instruction is that the protagonist must be seen to learn the sect's tradition in front of the sect's younger disciples. This is political theater; it works. By the protagonist's third month, the outer disciples who were uneasy about a Foundation Establishment cultivator in their dormitory have decided the protagonist is, in some undefinable way, one of them.
105-
106-**Night (戌時-亥時, 7-11 p.m.):** private practice in the Long Dormitory (the protagonist has a corner cot, separated from the next by a hanging cloth) or, by the second month, in a small practice room Wu Changqing has had cleared in the Founder's Hall. Sleep by 11.
107-
108:**Social dynamics:** the protagonist, as Foundation Establishment, is socially anomalous — too powerful to be a true outer disciple, too junior (in sect standing) to be an inner disciple. Yan Boyan treats the protagonist with cold courtesy. Zhao Wujiu treats the protagonist as a friend, which the protagonist, after the long solitude of Old Chen's hermitage, finds almost unbearably welcome. Wang Meili treats the protagonist as a cousin, which means she is both warmer and ruder than anyone else. Su Wenli treats the protagonist as a curiosity, asks careful questions, and writes the answers in a small notebook the protagonist does not see. Ma Yuening develops, in the protagonist's first month, a hero-worship that the protagonist must (gently, carefully) deflect. The Cao twins watch the protagonist for two months before deciding, on the basis of an afternoon the protagonist spent helping them repair a warded door, that the protagonist is acceptable. Peng Li offers the protagonist a piece of carved wood — a small horse, badly done — as a house-warming gift the first week, and never mentions it again. Old Liu, on the protagonist's second night, sits on the cot next to the protagonist's and says, "Welcome home, little cultivator. The tea is in the brown pot. Don't drink the green one, it's Old Chen's leftovers from his visit three years ago and it's gone off." The protagonist, who has not slept in a room with nine other people since childhood, sleeps deeply for the first time in years.

110:### 1.8 The founder's manual and the practice tradition
111-
112:The Cangwu Sect's founder (Daoist Cangwu, §1.4) left a single manual — the **Green Mirror Water-Wood Scripture** (青鏡水木經) — stored in the Scripture Pavilion's Foundation-locked inner chamber. The manual is short: 4,200 characters across three scrolls, hand-copied onto paper now yellowed and brittle, the founder's own brushwork (a small, precise, slightly cramped hand that Wu Changqing has spent thirty years studying and still finds, in places, opaque). The first scroll describes the water-phase primary circulation route (the route the outer disciples practice at the spring's half-circle at dawn). The second scroll describes the wood-phase secondary integration (the route the inner disciples add at mid Qi Condensation). The third scroll describes the Foundation Establishment integration theory — the route Wu Changqing attempted at twenty-one and could not complete.
113-
114:The third scroll is the sect's central tension. The first two scrolls are sufficient for Qi Condensation; any disciple with patience can practice them safely. The third scroll is the route to Foundation Establishment, and it is the route that broke Wu Changqing. The route's difficulty is not in its qi-circulation (the route is, by all accounts, elegant and well-designed) but in its psychospiritual demand: the water-wood integration requires the cultivator to hold a grief-stable heart-mind at the moment of integration, because water-phase (associated with grief in the five-phase emotion correspondence) and wood-phase (associated with anger) must be balanced against each other under pressure. A cultivator with unprocessed grief cannot balance them. Wu Changqing had unprocessed grief (his brother's death) and the integration collapsed. Old Chen, who studied the same manual at the Cangwu Sect sixty years ago (Old Chen was, briefly, a Cangwu Sect disciple before he left in his dispute with the previous-previous sect master), also failed — for the same reason, in his case the grief of a different kind.
115-
116:The protagonist arrives at the Cangwu Sect with two advantages Wu Changqing and Old Chen lacked. First, the protagonist has already achieved Foundation Establishment (via Old Chen's teaching, which was not the Cangwu manual but a wood-phase primary route Old Chen developed independently). The protagonist's Foundation Establishment is therefore not at risk; the third scroll is, for the protagonist, a text to study rather than a route to attempt. Second, the protagonist has (per Scene 4 and Scene 5) already processed the grief of Wang Xiaodi — the protagonist's own water-phase heart-mind is stable. This means the protagonist can, in principle, teach the third scroll's route to a Cangwu Sect disciple who has not yet attempted Foundation Establishment. Ma Yuening is the obvious candidate. Wu Changqing's private hope (§1.6) is, in concrete terms, the hope that the protagonist will teach Ma Yuening the third scroll's route and that Ma Yuening will succeed where Wu Changqing failed.

118:### 1.9 The seasonal rhythm and the ritual year
119-
120:The Cangwu Sect's year is organized around the twenty-four solar terms (二十四節氣, doc 00 §4), with four days of particular sect-weight. **The Spring Founding Day** (立春後第三日, the third day after the Beginning of Spring) commemorates the founder's arrival in the valley; the sect observes it with a recitation of the manual's first scroll in the Founder's Hall and a small feast (rice with pickled fish, a luxury the sect cannot really afford). **The Summer Tribute Day** (夏至前五日, five days before the Summer Solstice) is when the autumn tribute is prepared: the spirit stones are counted out, the herb bundles are tied, and the outer-disciple escort is chosen (the protagonist, by their second year, is the obvious choice; the Azure Sword Sect's receiving disciple is, by then, used to seeing them). **The Autumn Recruitment Tour** (秋分前後, around the Autumn Equinox) sends Wu Changqing and one inner disciple (Yan Boyan, by tradition, though Wu Changqing would prefer Zhao Wujiu) through the six tenant villages and three further-flung villages to test children for spiritual roots. The tour is the sect's lifeblood; a year with no recruits is a year the sect's future dims. **The Winter Solstice Vigil** (冬至守夜) is the sect's only night-cultivation: the entire sect sits in the spring's half-circle from sundown to sunrise, the one night the outer disciples are excused from their dormitory curfew. The vigil is cold, long, and quietly beloved. Old Liu makes tea every hour.
121-
122:The protagonist's first year in the sect is structured by these four days. The first Recruitment Tour the protagonist attends (autumn of the protagonist's first year, age 23 in mortal time but the sect does not track mortal ages) is the tour where the protagonist, accompanying Wu Changqing, returns to Wang Family Bend for the first time since Foundation Establishment — the first return (doc 26 §5.3, §8, the viral moment). The protagonist, walking into the village with Wu Changqing and Yan Boyan behind them, perceives the qi of the place and weeps. Wu Changqing, perceiving the protagonist's state with Qi Condensation perception (he cannot perceive the grief's content, only its disturbance), says nothing. Yan Boyan, perceiving nothing he can name, says nothing either. The tour, that year, finds two children with spiritual roots in Wang Family Bend's cousin-branch. One of them, a girl of seven named Wang Yanrong (王燕容, distant cousin), will, in Act 3, become the protagonist's second disciple in the "found a lineage" ending. This is the structural reason the Recruitment Tour scene is load-bearing: it is where the lineage's future is seeded, even as the protagonist is grieving the lineage's past.

208:The Jade Edge Grotto's six-month rotations are the Azure Sword Sect's most contested internal resource. The grotto's slow ecological decline is, for the Isolationists, an argument against expansion (the sect cannot manage what it has); for the Expansionists, an argument for it (the sect needs a second grotto-heaven to relieve the first).

215-
216:The Azure Sword Sect is the regional hegemon of the central and northern Cangwu range, but it is not the regional hegemon of the broader Cangli watershed. That position belongs, in cultivation-world accounting, to the **Jade Void Holy Land** (玉虛聖地, doc 24 §4.3) — a Nascent Soul+ holy land on another continent, with three thousand disciples and a peak grotto-heaven, whose founder (Daoist Jade Void, two thousand one hundred years old) was, per the holy land's own records, born in the Cangli Riverlands. 218-The Azure Sword Sect's relationship to the Jade Void is therefore careful. The sect pays nominal homage to the holy land (a small tribute of one mid-grade spirit stone per decade, delivered by a Foundation Establishment elder to the Jade Void's rotating shrine staff); the Jade Void, in return, extends the sect its implicit protection against other holy lands. The arrangement is unwritten and is, in the cultivation world's accounting, fragile. If the Azure Sword Sect's expansion into the Cangli Riverlands is perceived by the Jade Void as a violation of the region's ancestral-veneration status, the holy land's protection could withdraw — and a holy land's withdrawn protection is, in the cultivation world's brutal accounting, an invitation to other powers to contest the now-unprotected region. This is the Isolationist faction's strongest argument (§2.5): the expansion risks the Jade Void's goodwill, and the Jade Void's goodwill is the sect's long-term strategic anchor.
