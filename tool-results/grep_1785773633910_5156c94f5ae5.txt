/home/z/my-project/corpus-extension/29_CULTIVATION_PRIMARY_SOURCES.md:227:This passage grounds the **heart-mind cultivation system's Chan Buddhist layer** (doc 24 §1.2's 心魔 taxonomy and the heart-mind mechanics introduced in passage 2A above). Huineng's three refutations — not attaching to mind (不著心), not attaching to purity (不著淨), not non-movement (不是不動) — are the canonical warnings against three cultivation errors that map directly to three 心魔 types. The cultivator who "attaches to mind" (tries to concentrate the mind as an object) falls into obsession-type 心魔: the mind is phantom, and attaching to it produces a feedback loop. The cultivator who "attaches to purity" (tries to maintain a pure mind-state) falls into compulsion-type 心魔: the purity-state is reified as an object, and the cultivator is "bound by purity" (被淨縛). The cultivator who equates meditation with physical stillness (不動) falls into the body-stillness trap that the Zhuangzi 心齋 passage also warns against: meditation is not non-movement but a specific internal relation to external states. For the RPG, this is the textual anchor for the heart-mind cultivation minigame: the player does not press inputs to "concentrate" or "purify"; they release inputs and respond to arising states without attachment. The line "外離相為禪；內不亂為定" is the canonical definition of the meditative state — externally, forms are not grasped; internally, the mind is not scattered — and it provides the design vocabulary for the cultivation layer's mental-state display: the player's internal state is shown as a degree of "scattering" (亂) and the external state as a degree of "form-grasping" (著相), and the meditative state is the region where both are low.
