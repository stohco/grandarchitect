     1→# 47 — Cosmic Resources and Economy
     2→
     3→**Status:** Candidate canon. Specifies the cosmic-scale economy — what Spirit Severance, Void Amalgamation, Tribulation Crossing, and Mahayana cultivators fight over, trade in, and conserve. Extends doc 18 (the mortal economy) upward through the realm ladder; compliant with 15_PRECELESTIAL_AND_TRIBULATION §1-5 (Precelestial qi, courts, ancestral courts, Spirit Wilds, Law Reaches); 37_COSMIC_HISTORY §5-7 (the Sundering, fallen civilizations, sealed evils, the Thinning); 38_THE_COURTS_OF_HEAVEN §2 (the six departments, including Roads for stratum-crossing trade); 39_THE_MAHAYANA_PANTHEON §2, §6 (the nine Mahayana, their holdings, the deadlock over the Thinning); 46_ANCESTRAL_COURTS_AND_LINEAGES §5 (the ancestral court qi-economy, veneration as currency); 24_RECONCILIATION_AND_DECISIONS §3.1 (spirit-stone value: 10-50 taels); 18_ECONOMY_SYSTEM (the mortal economy's substrate). No pinyin tone marks appear in this document. Hanzi appear in parentheses on first mention.

     8→## 0. Why this document exists
     9→
    10→Doc 18 specified the mortal economy: copper, silver, spirit stones, grain; the household debt cycle; the sect's revenue and expenses; the genre's central economic fact (one month of Qi Condensation work ≈ 18-55 years of mortal labor, per doc 24 §3.1). The specification stopped at the Qi Condensation to Core Formation tier. The cosmic economy — what a Spirit Severance cultivator trades in, what a Mahayana cultivator conserves, what the pantheon fights over when the pantheon is finite and the cosmos is dying — was unspecified. Doc 39 §6 named the Thinning as the pantheon's deadlock (the cosmos's qi is non-regenerating; the pantheon cannot agree on whether to author new laws to slow the Thinning or to stop authoring and let the cosmos end gracefully) but did not specify the economic substrate the Thinning operates on. Doc 37 named the fallen civilizations (the Pillar Dynasty, the Crimson Vow Alliance, the Silent Hexagram School) and the sealed evils but did not specify how the ruins and the seals function as cosmic resources. Doc 15 §3-5 named the ancestral courts, the Spirit Wilds, and the Law Reaches but did not specify their economies. Doc 46 §5 specified the ancestral court's qi-economy (veneration as a finite, lineage-bound currency) but did not specify the broader cosmic economy in which veneration is one currency among several.
    11→
    12→This document fills those deferrals. It specifies: the cosmic-scale resources that exist above the mortal tier (ancient inheritances, cosmic-tier herbs and beasts, the ancestral court persistence-budget, the time-debt itself, law-fragments, the Thinning as scarcity); the cosmic economy (Precelestial-Acquired trade, the cosmic currencies, the grotto-heaven economy, the ancestral court economy); what Mahayana cultivators fight over (Law Reaches, the Thinning, predecessors' laws); and the protagonist's economic arc from mortal poverty to cosmic-scale responsibility. Every resource is named; every price (or equivalent) is specified; every controller is identified.
    13→
    14→The finiteness is the point. A cosmic economy without scarcity is a cosmic economy without tragedy; a cosmic economy with specified scarcity is the engine of the endgame's stakes.

    18→## 1. Cosmic-scale resources
    19→
    20→The mortal economy's resources (grain, cash, silver, spirit stones, mortal-tier herbs and beasts) are the substrate of the lower realm ladder. Above Core Formation, the mortal economy's resources are insufficient — a Nascent Soul cultivator can buy mortal resources in such quantity that the purchase is meaningless, and the cultivator's actual constraints (qi-authority, time, law-residue, veneration) are not priced in mortal currency. This section specifies the cosmic-scale resources that exist above the mortal tier.
    21→
    22→### 1.1 Ancient inheritances
    23→
    24→Ancient inheritances are the ruins and residues of past cultivators, lineages, and civilizations. They are the cosmic economy's primary treasure-class resource — the equivalent, at cosmic scale, of mortal inheritance manuals (doc 18 §2.3). The categories:
    25→
    26→**Dispersed Mahayana anchor-residues.** When a Mahayana cultivator disperses (final death, 寂滅), the anchor's law-inscription does not fully unwrite; a residue persists in the substrate, fading over millennia. The residue, for a Tribulation Crossing cultivator with the law-legacy perception (doc 30 Station 10), is readable — the residue contains fragments of the Mahayana's authored law, the Mahayana's personal qi-signature, and (rarely) the Mahayana's lucid final perception. The residue is the only way, below Mahayana, to study a Mahayana's law-inscription directly. The residues are tracked by the Records Department (the Register of Dispersed Mahayana, 散尊錄) and are, by the founding compact's jurisdiction 6, the property of the cosmos (not of the lineage — the lineage-jurisdiction clause, jurisdiction 5, does not extend to dispersed anchors). The residues are, in practice, accessed by the pantheon's permission; the permission is rare and costly.
    27→
    28→**Law-fragments of failed Reach-authorship.** When a Reach-authorship fails (per doc 24 §1.1, the Reach can collapse; per doc 39 §2.9, Lie Fa's Reach collapsed three thousand years ago), the law-inscription that almost held persists in the Reach's ruins. The fragment is, in principle, studyable — the law-paradox perception (doc 30 Station 10) can read the fragment's structure and perceive where the paradox destabilized it. The fragment is, in practice, dangerous — to read it is to begin to perceive the recursion that broke the author (per doc 39 §2.9, the recursion is contagious). Lie Fa's fragment (the only known surviving Reach-collapse fragment) is the cosmos's most dangerous text; access is restricted to Mahayana, and most of the pantheon has read it. The fragment's economic value is, paradoxically, low (it cannot be reused; it cannot be repaired; it can only be studied as a caution) but its strategic value is high (the Mahayana who studies it most deeply is the Mahayana most likely to perceive, in advance, the next Reach-authorship's paradox-risk).
    29→
    30→**The ruins of fallen civilizations.** The three named fallen civilizations (the Pillar Dynasty, the Crimson Vow Alliance, the Silent Hexagram School, per doc 37 §6) each left ruins. The ruins contain: bark-strip libraries (the Pillar Cities, doc 37 §6.1), partial Code-survivals (the Crimson Vow Code, 60% held by the Jade Void Holy Land), sealed grotto-heaven libraries (the Silent Hexagram Grotto-Heaven, presumed intact). The ruins' economic value is in the texts (cultivation manuals, law-commentaries, lineage histories) and the residual qi-tools (the Pillar Dynasty's fused-stone architectural elements retain wood-phase qi that can be harvested; the Crimson Vow's fire-phase formation-fragments retain small qi-charges). Access to the ruins is restricted by the ruins' dangers (the Pillar Cities are in contested wilds; the Crimson Vow Grotto-Heaven's collapsed anchor is a qi-residue site; the Silent Hexagram Grotto-Heaven is sealed by Jing Wuyin's dispersal-method, presumed lost). The Jade Void Holy Land's library, which holds the Pillar Dynasty Annals (7 of 12 volumes) and 60% of the Crimson Vow Code, is the cosmos's most accessible ancient-inheritance archive; access is granted to Nascent Soul cultivators by petition, and the petition's approval rate is approximately 5%.
    31→
    32→**The inheritance-economy's structural asymmetry.** The ancient-inheritance economy is structurally asymmetric: the inheritances are produced by past civilizations' collapses (the Pillar Dynasty's vein-exhaustion, the Crimson Vow Alliance's guarantor-dispersal, the Silent Hexagram School's ancestor-corruption), but the inheritances are accessed by present civilizations' cultivators (the Jade Void Holy Land's library, the Azure Sword Sect's Scripture Hall, the protagonist's first cosmic-tier encounters). The asymmetry is the cosmic economy's central inter-generational feature: the past's catastrophes are the present's resources, and the present's catastrophes (the Thinning; the pantheon's deadlock; the courts' corruption) will be the future's inheritances. The protagonist's eventual dispersal (in the far future, in the Transcend-path variant) will, if the protagonist's law is maintained after the protagonist's death, leave a law-fragment for some future cultivator to study — the protagonist's contribution to the inheritance-economy's asymmetric flow. The inheritance-economy is, in this respect, the cosmic economy's most poignant sector: every inheritance is a grave, and every grave is a treasure.

    34→### 1.2 Cosmic-tier spirit herbs
    35→
    36→Mortal-tier spirit herbs (100-year ginseng, 500-year lingzhi, etc., per doc 18 §3.2) are the substrate of mortal alchemy. Cosmic-tier spirit herbs are the herbs that grow only in the peak grotto-heavens or in the Spirit Wilds, and that have properties no mortal-tier herb can replicate.
    37→
    38→**10,000-year herbs from peak grotto-heavens.** A peak grotto-heaven (the Jade Void Holy Land's grotto, the Garden Grotto, the Crimson Vow City's former grotto) sustains herbs at a time-rate that allows 10,000-year maturation in 1,000 to 3,000 Acquired-stratum years (the Jade Void's grotto has a 1:10 time-rate; the Garden Grotto has 1:5; the Crimson Vow City had 1:3 before its collapse). A 10,000-year herb (the Garden Grotto's 10,000-year wood-phase spirit peach; the Jade Void's 10,000-year metal-phase spirit lotus) is the alchemical equivalent of a Mahayana-tier treasure — it can sustain a Tribulation Crossing cultivator's breakthrough attempt, repair a Spirit Severance cultivator's anchor-damage, or (rarely) extend a Mahayana cultivator's natural span by a century. The herbs are not sold (no mortal currency can price them); they are gifted, traded for law-fragments or veneration-pulses, or hoarded. The Jade Void Holy Land's 10,000-year spirit lotus is the holy land's most closely guarded treasure; the patriarch has authorized its use twice in 2,100 years.
    39→
    40→**Precelestial herbs from the Spirit Wilds.** The Spirit Wilds' plants are alien (per doc 15 §4.3) — they have no phase-affinity, and their effects on an Acquired cultivator's body are unpredictable. A wild herb, gathered by a Spirit Severance cultivator with the skill to identify and harvest it, can produce effects no Acquired herb can replicate: a forced transformation (a Qi Condensation cultivator's qi-state forcibly re-tuned to a different phase-affinity), an accelerated cultivation (a Foundation Establishment cultivator's meridian-integration compressed from years to hours), or a forced dispersion (the wild herb's undifferentiated qi overwhelms the cultivator's anchor and disperses it). The wild herbs are traded in the underworld market (doc 26 §9.2) at prices denominated in law-fragments, veneration-pulses, or (rarely) a Spirit Severance cultivator's qi-authority. The protagonist's companion Liang Zhu knows the underworld market's herb-trade; the trade is one of Liang Zhu's established expertise.
    41→
    42→### 1.3 Cosmic-tier spirit beasts
    43→
    44→Mortal-tier spirit beasts (100-year beasts, peak beasts with cores worth 10-100 spirit stones, per doc 18 §3.2) are the substrate of mortal hunting. Cosmic-tier beasts are the beings that exist only in the Spirit Wilds or in the deep Precelestial, and that do not follow the five-phase model.
    45→
    46→**Spirit Wilds native beasts.** The wilds' beasts (per doc 15 §4.3) have metabolisms that operate on undifferentiated qi directly. A wild beast does not have a phase-affinity; its attacks do not have phase-signatures; its core (if it has one — some wild beasts do not have anchors and therefore do not have cores in the Acquired sense) is a knot of undifferentiated qi that can be refined, with great difficulty, into a Precelestial-tier pill. The wild beasts' body parts (hide, bone, organs) have properties no Acquired beast's parts have — a wild beast's hide, properly tanned, can serve as a Spirit Severance cultivator's armor; a wild beast's bone, properly carved, can serve as a Tribulation Crossing cultivator's formation-substrate. Wild beasts are hunted by Spirit Severance cultivators (the minimum realm at which the hunt is not suicide); the hunters are organized in small lineages (the Wild Hunters, three small lineages in the eastern continent's wild-edge) that trade the parts through the underworld market.
    47→
    48→**Substrate-predators.** The Sky-Devouring Beast (per doc 37 §7.2) is the named instance of a substrate-predator — a being whose nature is to consume law-inscriptions. The Beast is sealed; if the seal breaks, the Beast would consume six to ten grotto-heavens before a coalition could re-seal it. Substrate-predators are not hunted (the hunt is, by the pantheon's collective assessment, suicide for any coalition below three Mahayana); they are sealed, and the sealing is the cosmic economy's most expensive verb. The sealing's cost is denominated in Mahayana law-authority (one Mahayana dispersal per sealing, by historical average) and in Tribulation Crossing cultivators' persistence-budgets (the two surviving lineages' Tribulation Crossing cultivators are aging; the seal's maintenance is consuming their budgets).
    49→
    50→**Beings that don't follow the five-phase model.** Some wild beings — the Feathered Court's feathered serpents, the Stone-Singing Court's stone-singers, the Serpentine Court's serpentine yao (per doc 38 §7.3) — are foreign powers, not beasts. They are not hunted; they are negotiated with. The Foreign Affairs department's standing commission to the three yao courts (the longest-running foreign-relations commission in the courts' history) trades Acquired-stratum goods (spirit stones, herbs, pills — the yao courts' cultivators can use Acquired resources) for Wild-stratum resources (wild herbs, wild beast parts, law-residue access in the wilds' interior). The trade is the courts' most reliable Precelestial-Acquired-Wild exchange; the trade's annual volume is, by the Foreign Affairs department's annual report, approximately equivalent to a small holy land's annual revenue.
    51→
    52→**The yao-courts' trade-goods.** The three yao courts' specific trade-goods are: the Feathered Court's feathers (each feather is a qi-conduit that can be refined into a wind-phase treasure the Acquired-stratum's phase-model does not produce); the Stone-Singing Court's resonant stones (each stone is a law-residue substrate that can host a minor law-inscription the way a grotto-heaven's anchor hosts the grotto's law); the Serpentine Court's shed skins (each skin is a qi-resonant fabric that can serve as a Spirit Severance cultivator's robe, with properties no Acquired-stratum textile can replicate). The trade-goods' exchange rate is denominated in spirit stones (for the feathers and stones, which the yao courts consider tradeable) and in law-fragments (for the shed skins, which the Serpentine Court considers sacred and trades only for high-tier cosmic currency). The Foreign Affairs department's standing commission negotiates the rates annually; the rates have been stable for the last five hundred years (the yao courts' and the courts' mutual suspicion is, by long practice, resolved into a stable trading relationship).
    53→
    54→### 1.4 The ancestral court persistence-budget as a resource
    55→
    56→Per doc 46 §5, the ancestral court's qi-reservoir is sustained by veneration, and each persistent ancestor has a persistence-budget (the qi-authority the anchor holds, finite, non-regenerating). The persistence-budget is a cosmic resource — it is the currency in which the ancestor's interventions are denominated. A lineage with a well-fed court and a senior ancestor with a full persistence-budget (the Yan Court, the Builder's Court) has a strategic resource no Acquired-stratum wealth can buy; a lineage with a starving court and a fading ancestor (the Mourner's Court, the Pillar Court) has a resource that is, in cosmic terms, a wasting asset.
    57→
    58→The persistence-budget is traded, in restricted form, between allied courts. The Garden Court's veneration-pulse donation to the Mourner's Court (per doc 46 §5, six hundred years ago) is the named instance: the Garden Court's senior ancestor (Muyuan) invested a portion of her persistence-budget in a veneration-pulse that traveled along the Garden Lineage's qi-connection to the Garden Court, was bundled by the court's law-inscriptions into a transferable packet, and was conveyed (by a Tribulation Crossing cultivator's personal transit, over three Precelestial days = three Acquired years) to the Mourner's Court. The donation slowed the Mourner's Court's decline but did not reverse it; the donation's cost to Muyuan was approximately one percent of her remaining budget (a noticeable but not existential cost). The transfer is the cosmic economy's analogue of a mortal wealthy household's charitable donation to a poorer village's lineage hall — the same gesture, at a scale where the gesture's qi-cost is felt by the donor.
    59→
    60→### 1.5 The time-debt itself as a resource
    61→
    62→The Precelestial-Acquired time-debt (1 Precelestial day = 1 Acquired year, per doc 15 §7.1) is the cosmos's central tragic engine. It is also, in a restricted sense, a cosmic resource — a trade-opportunity between strata.
    63→
    64→**The trade.** A Precelestial being (a heavenly immortal, a Mahayana cultivator, a persistent ancestor) can, in principle, perform Acquired-stratum work for an Acquired-stratum patron, and the work's Precelestial-time cost is 1/365 of the work's Acquired-time value. A Mahayana cultivator who spends a Precelestial day (one Acquired year) inscribing a law-amendment for an Acquired-stratum holy land's benefit has, in effect, traded one Precelestial day for one Acquired year of work the holy land would have had to perform itself. The trade is, in pure efficiency terms, a 365x multiplier — the Precelestial being's labor is worth 365 Acquired-stratum labor-units.
    65→
    66→**Why the trade is restricted.** The trade is restricted by three constraints. First, the time-debt is symmetric: the Precelestial being's Precelestial day is one Acquired year of mortal-world time, and the mortal world the Precelestial being was trying to protect ages past recognition (per doc 15 §7.2). The trade is therefore not free — the Precelestial being pays, in mortal-world time, for the multiplier. Second, the Precelestial being's intervention in the Acquired stratum is bound by Acquired-time during the intervention (per doc 15 §7.4); the Precelestial being, while in the Acquired stratum, is not 365x faster — they are 1x, on Acquired time. The multiplier applies only to the planning and the law-authorship, not to the in-stratum action. Third, the courts' Roads department regulates stratum-crossing travel (per doc 38 §2.4); a Precelestial being's Acquired-stratum visit requires a permit, and the permit's issuance is governed by the courts' political blocs. The Lineage Bloc supports lineage-ancestral interventions; the Conservative Bloc restricts them; the Reform Bloc wants to formalize the permit-system's criteria.
    67→
    68→**The trade's actual volume.** The trade's actual volume is small. The pantheon's nine Mahayana collectively visit the Acquired stratum, on average, perhaps once per Precelestial year (= 365 Acquired years); the visits are typically brief (a few Acquired days) and focused (a specific death to attend, a specific law to inspect, a specific lineage to consult). The persistent ancestors' interventions are rarer (per doc 46 §5, the persistence-budget's cost). The trade is therefore not a market in the conventional sense; it is a restricted, high-stakes, low-volume exchange between specific beings for specific purposes. The protagonist's meeting with Bei Huai in a mortal village (per doc 39 §5.1) is an instance of the trade: Bei Huai, visiting the Acquired stratum to attend a mortal's death, spends Acquired-time (a few hours) on a brief consultation with the protagonist. The consultation's value to the protagonist is enormous (the protagonist's path to the Transcend ending is, in part, the consultation's consequence); the consultation's cost to Bei Huai is the time not spent attending other deaths during the visit.

    70→### 1.6 Law-fragments
    71→
    72→Law-fragments are the residue of failed law-authorship (per §1.1 above) and the residue of contested law-authorship (when a Mahayana's law is amended, the original law's residue persists in the substrate, fading over millennia). Law-fragments are the cosmic economy's highest-tier currency — they are the only resource that can be used, at Mahayana, to study another Mahayana's law-authorship directly.
    73→
    74→**What law-fragments are good for.** A law-fragment can be studied (by a Mahayana cultivator with the law-legacy perception, doc 30 Station 10) for: the original author's law-authorship technique (the specific qi-inscription method, useful as a reference for the student's own authorship), the original law's full text (useful when the law has been amended and the original text is no longer in force), and the original author's lucid final perception (rare; only fragments from authors who dispersed by natural span retain lucid perception). Law-fragments cannot be reused (the law-authorship verb, 創法, requires the author's living anchor; a fragment is a residue, not a living inscription); they cannot be repaired (the substrate's law-paradox perception does not allow partial inscriptions to be completed); they can only be studied.
    75→
    76→**Law-fragment degradation.** Law-fragments are not permanent. A fragment fades over millennia, the way a mortal inscription fades on weathered stone. The fade-rate depends on the fragment's substrate-depth (deeper fragments, inscribed in the Precelestial's deeper substrate, fade slower; shallower fragments fade faster) and the fragment's author's realm at dispersal (Mahayana-authored fragments fade slowest; Tribulation Crossing-authored fragments fade faster; Spirit Severance-authored fragments fade fastest). The Records Department's Register of Law-Fragments tracks each known fragment's fade-rate; the register is, in cosmic-economic terms, the cosmic economy's inventory of a depreciating asset. A law-fragment studied in the present decade yields more insight than the same fragment studied in a century; the depreciation is the law-fragment's time-cost, and the time-cost is, in cosmic-economic terms, the cosmic economy's most elegant scarcity mechanism — even the highest-tier currency decays.
    77→
    78→**Who controls the law-fragments.** The courts' Records Department tracks the law-fragments (the Register of Law-Fragments, 法殘錄) but does not control access (the fragments are in the substrate, not in the courts' archive). The Mahayana who can perceive a fragment (typically the Mahayana whose law the fragment is from, or the Mahayana's successor) has de facto control. The protagonist's amendment to Gu Wu's bardo-window law (Transcend path) will, when Gu Wu disperses, leave a law-fragment in the substrate — the protagonist's amendment's residue plus Gu Wu's original inscription's residue, fused. The protagonist, as Gu Wu's successor, will perceive this fragment more clearly than any other Mahayana; the fragment will be, for the protagonist, a personal inheritance (the closest thing to Gu Wu's continued presence the cosmos allows).

    80→### 1.7 The Thinning itself as a scarcity driver
    81→
    82→The Thinning (per doc 39 §6, doc 37 §1.2) is the Acquired Stratum's slow qi-density decline. The Thinning is the cosmic economy's master scarcity — every other resource is, in the long run, denominated in the qi-authority the Thinning is consuming.
    83→
    84→**What the Thinning means for resource availability.** Spirit veins produce less qi per year than they did a millennium ago (the decline is approximately 0.1% per century, per the Records Department's measurements). Spirit herbs take longer to mature (a 100-year ginseng now takes 110 years). Spirit beasts' cores are smaller (a peak beast's core, a millennium ago, was approximately 10% larger than today's). The Thinning is, for mortal and Core Formation cultivators, imperceptible (the decline is too slow to perceive in a cultivator's lifetime); for Nascent Soul cultivators (who live centuries), the decline is faintly perceptible; for Spirit Severance and above, the decline is unmistakable.
    85→
    86→**What the Thinning means for cosmic-tier resources.** The cosmic-tier resources are more directly affected. A 10,000-year herb's maturation, in a peak grotto-heaven, now takes 11,000 years (the grotto-heaven's qi-feed is declining with the spirit vein's). The wilds' beasts' cores are smaller (the wilds' undifferentiated qi is, contrary to the Acquired-stratum trend, *not* declining — the wilds' substrate is the Precelestial's own interior, which is sustained by the Origin's generative function, not by the Acquired stratum's qi-density; but the wilds' beasts' metabolisms are slower in the Thinning's edge zones, where the wilds border the Acquired stratum). The ancestral courts' veneration-pulses are weaker (the lineage's living cultivators' qi-authority is declining with the Acquired stratum's qi-density, and the veneration-pulses are proportional to the living cultivators' qi-authority). The First Court's qi-reservoir is declining (Gu Wu's law-maintenance draws qi from the law-substrate, which is thinning); the Mourner's Court's qi-reservoir is declining faster (no Mahayana resident, no law-maintenance, only the thinning veneration).
    87→
    88→**The Thinning's timeline.** Per doc 39 §6, the Thinning's trajectory (if unbroken) is: in 3,000-5,000 years, the Acquired stratum's qi-density falls below the Foundation Establishment threshold; in 8,000-10,000 years, the qi-density falls below the Tribulation Crossing sustenance threshold; in 12,000-15,000 years, the substrate itself fails and the pantheon's laws begin to cascade. The cosmic economy's resources are, in this timeline, a wasting asset — every cosmic-tier resource is declining, slowly, and the decline is the background condition of every cosmic-scale transaction.
    89→
    90→**The Thinning's asymmetry across strata.** The Thinning's effects are not uniform across the three strata. The Mortal Stratum (the diffuse ambient, per doc 24 §2.6) is most affected (the Mortal Stratum's qi-density was already the lowest; the decline pushes more mortal regions below the cultivation threshold; mortal cultivation becomes harder in the Mortal Stratum's edge regions, and the mortal population's cultivation-rate declines). The Acquired Stratum is moderately affected (the spirit veins' decline is measurable but, for now, sustainable; the grotto-heavens' production declines but does not cease). The Precelestial is, in a strict sense, *not* affected by the Acquired Stratum's Thinning — the Precelestial's substrate is sustained by the Origin's generative function, which is not subject to the Acquired Stratum's decline. However, the Precelestial's institutions (the courts, the ancestral courts, the pantheon's maintained laws) are affected, because those institutions depend on the Acquired Stratum's qi-authority (the courts' Roads department's commission, the ancestral courts' veneration, the pantheon's law-maintenance's qi-feed). The Precelestial's institutions are, in cosmic-economic terms, subsidized by the Acquired Stratum; the subsidy's decline is the Thinning's Precelestial manifestation. The asymmetry is the cosmic economy's central irony: the stratum that is, in itself, least affected by the Thinning (the Precelestial) is the stratum whose institutions are most vulnerable to the Thinning's Acquired-stratum effects, because those institutions are powered by the Acquired stratum's qi-authority.
    91→
    92→**The cosmic economy's historicity.** The cosmic economy is not static. Per doc 37 §2, the cosmos has passed through six epochs across 48,000 years, and each epoch's economy was different. In the Generative Epoch (Year 0 to Year ~20,000), there was no economy — no cultivators, no resources, no transactions; the Origin's generative function condensed the Precelestial substrate without exchange. In the Primordial Substrate Epoch (Year ~20,000 to Year ~38,000), the first cultivators developed the realm ladder from Qi Induction to Void Amalgamation, and the cosmic economy's first transactions (in qi-authority and time) emerged. In the First Law Epoch (Year ~38,000 to Year ~40,000), Gu Wu invented law-authorship, and the cosmic economy's highest-tier currency (law-fragments) emerged. In the Sundering Epoch (Year ~40,000 to Year ~40,500), the cosmic economy crashed (the bardo-overwhelm killed billions of anchors; the substrate-law destabilized; the Law Reaches were created) and reorganized (the Courts of Heaven were founded; the licensed-carrier system's precursor was established). In the Long Peace Epoch (Year ~40,500 to Year 47,753), the cosmic economy reached its peak complexity (the grotto-heaven economy's expansion; the ancestral court economy's maturation; the pantheon's fragment-trade's elaboration) and began its decline (the Thinning's first perceptibility; the Silent Hexagram Court's corruption and sealing; the Crimson Vow Alliance's collapse). In the Current Epoch (Year 47,753 to present, the Epoch of the Fading Law), the cosmic economy is in decline — the Thinning is unmistakable, the grotto-heavens are failing, the pantheon is deadlocked, and the protagonist's lifetime is the decline's acceleration point. The cosmic economy the protagonist encounters is the cosmic economy at its most complex and most fragile — the economy's peak and the economy's decline are simultaneous, and the simultaneity is the protagonist's endgame's central condition.
