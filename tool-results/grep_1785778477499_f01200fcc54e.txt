8:## 0. What this document is and why it exists
10:The critics found that only 5 of the 31 households in Wang Family Bend are named (doc 04 §1.2) and that the village has no non-Wang social actors with agency. A village of 180 people cannot be a diorama of five named households and twenty-six anonymous ones; the player must perceive the village as a society of recognizable, interlocked people, most of whom are not Wang and most of whom have their own plans. This document names ten more households (bringing the total to 15 of 31), gives full character bibles for the three companions (Wang Meili, Liang Zhu, Leng Qingxue) and the antagonist (Pei Liang), and specifies the death schedule for every named NPC who dies across the game.

147:### 2.2 Liang Zhu (梁柱) — the first peer
155:- **Why he travels with the protagonist:** he meets the protagonist at Qinghe Market Town at session ~250. The protagonist, ~50 (Foundation Establishment, appears ~30), is investigating Pei Liang's raid on the Cangwu Sect. Liang Zhu has heard of the raid (the rogue cultivator network is efficient). He has perceived that signature before: his sect master's qi, in the months before exposure, was similarly stained. He knows what Pei Liang is. He travels with the protagonist because Pei Liang is the second sect master, and Liang Zhu will not be silent a second time.

He teaches the rogue cultivator's skills: how to read a sect's politics from its outer hall, how to find safe lodging in a strange town, how to spot a hustler. He plays the xiao at the hermitage on the evenings when the protagonist is weary. He tells the protagonist, drunk, at session ~350, about A-Qing. The protagonist does not know what to say. Liang Zhu does not expect them to.
158:  - **Act 3 (sessions 500-1500+):** joins the pursuit of Pei Liang. He knows he will not survive it (Foundation Establishment peak against a Nascent Soul antagonist). He joins because he will not be silent a second time. At session ~700 he identifies one of Pei Liang's harvest-stores by its qi-signature — the same stained residue as his sect master's. At session ~750 he tells the protagonist, soberly, that he does not expect to see the end of the pursuit. The protagonist does not accept this. Liang Zhu does not argue.
159:- **His death:** at session ~800, the protagonist and companions are ambushed by Pei Liang at a cave system in the Cangwu foothills (one of Pei Liang's harvest-stores). Pei Liang, now Nascent Soul (approaching Spirit Severance), outclasses them all. The protagonist is wounded; Leng Qingxue holds the line; Meili drags the protagonist toward the cave mouth. Liang Zhu stays behind to delay Pei Liang. He plays a single note on the xiao — a qi-residue technique he developed, leaving his signature in the qi of the cave. The note is the same tune he has played for A-Qing every year for 30 years. He plays it now for the protagonist — a final teaching, in the form of a sound. Pei Liang strikes him down. His anchor disperses (Pei Liang's harvest-technique, applied at the moment of death, prevents the anchor from entering bardo — the dispersal is immediate and irreversible). The protagonist, retreating, perceives the dispersal. The protagonist screams — the only time in the game they do.
162:### 2.3 Leng Qingxue (冷清雪) — the sect dissident and romance
171:- **Why she travels with the protagonist:** she is sent by the Azure Sword Sect at session ~200 to investigate Pei Liang's raid on the Cangwu Sect. The sect's official reason: assess the threat to Azure Sword interests in the region. Her private reason: assess the protagonist (the Azure Sword has heard of the Wang lineage's first cultivator; Leng Wushuang wants to know if the protagonist is potential ally, pawn, or obstacle). She travels with the protagonist initially as an observer. She stays because she finds, in the protagonist, the first person in 100 years who does not want anything from her — the protagonist is not intimidated by her sect, not interested in her mother, not trying to court her. The protagonist is, in her assessment, the only honest cultivator she has met since Xu Yunfeng.
172:- **The courtship verb — the Exchange of Cuts (交劍, jiaojian):** two cultivators spar. The loser owes the winner a truthful answer to one question. The questions cannot be about combat; they must be about the self. Three bouts (three questions, three answers) = courtship acknowledged. Seven bouts = betrothal. Either party may decline a bout; declining three times ends the courtship. The Exchange of Cuts makes courtship a kind of cultivation: you learn the other's qi through their sword, the other's self through their answers. The truth-debt is enforced by the simulation — a lie in an answer registers as a karmic trace (doc 24 §2.1) and is perceivable, at Core Formation, by the wronged party.
175:  - Third bout (session ~500, after Core Formation): they spar to a draw. No questions. They sit in silence. This is the courtship acknowledgment.
177:  - Seventh bout (before the final confrontation with Pei Liang): the question, asked by whichever party the player is not, is "Will you come back?" The answer, whatever it is, is the truth.
179:  - **Act 2 (sessions 200-500):** introduced as the sect investigator. Initially cold, then cautiously warm, then — by session ~500, after the third bout — openly allied with the protagonist. Provides the Azure Sword Sect's intelligence on Pei Liang. Does not, in Act 2, break with her sect. Her dissent is still private.
180:  - **Act 3 (sessions 500-1500+):** the break. At session ~700, when Liang Zhu identifies one of Pei Liang's harvest-stores, Leng Qingxue recognizes the harvest-technique's signature — it is the same technique her mother's late husband was investigating when he died 80 years ago. The realization: Pei Liang's technique is descended from the same source as the technique Leng Wushuang's husband died investigating. The Cangwu Mountains have been a harvest-ground for demonic cultivators for centuries. Leng Wushuang's expansion is, in part, an 80-year war against this legacy — but the war has hardened into territorial ambition. Leng Qingxue confronts her mother at session ~900 (a scene at the Azure Sword Sect's main hall). Her mother disowns her. Leng Qingxue leaves the sect. She rejoins the protagonist as a full companion, not an observer.
182:  - In the **Confront path**, if the protagonist loses the final confrontation with Pei Liang, Leng Qingxue (present) is killed trying to protect the protagonist's retreat. Her anchor disperses. The protagonist, if they survive, returns to Wang Family Bend and reports her death to her mother — a scene in which Leng Wushuang, for the first time in 80 years, weeps.
183:  - In the **Accommodate path**, if the protagonist accepts Pei Liang's offer, Leng Qingxue refuses to join. She attempts to leave with Wang Meili. Pei Liang, perceiving her as a threat, kills her. Her anchor is harvested. The protagonist perceives this and does nothing — the moment that defines the Accommodate path's moral cost.
184:  - In the **Transcend path**, she survives. She is the one who, in the "cross the tribulation" ending, stays in the mortal world and continues the protagonist's work. She is the one who, in the "final death" ending (if the protagonist fails the Tribulation Crossing), reports the protagonist's death to Wang Family Bend. She lives, in the Transcend path, to ~500 (Nascent Soul lifespan), and dies at her mother's grave, having buried her 160 years before.

215:## Part 3: The Antagonist — Pei Liang (裴良)
217:### 3.1 Background and realm progression
219:Pei Liang (裴良) was born 180 years before game start, in a small town 200 li west of the Cangwu Mountains. His father was a country doctor; his mother died in childbirth. He perceived qi at 12 and was admitted to the Iron Bamboo Sect (鐵竹派, a small sect 50 li from his hometown) as a low-ranking disciple. His sect master was Daoist Iron Bamboo (鐵竹道人, Foundation Establishment, deceased at 110 when Pei Liang was 60). The Iron Bamboo Sect had no political ambition and no enemies; it was dissolved 80 years ago after Daoist Iron Bamboo's death, its disciples scattered. Pei Liang was the sect's most talented disciple, but not its heir — he left at 70 to pursue Core Formation as a rogue.
221:At 85 (95 years before game start), while traveling home to visit his dying father, Pei Liang encountered the aftermath of Zhou Wugui's massacre (doc 24 §4.4 — Zhou Wugui killed three villages in the Cangwu foothills, harvesting their anchors to fuel a breakthrough attempt; the Azure Sword Sect's previous sect master pursued and killed him). Pei Liang arrived two weeks after the killings, helped bury the dead, and in one of the villages found a fragment of Zhou Wugui's harvest-manual — a single page, hidden in a floorboard, describing the anchor-harvest technique in incomplete but workable detail. He kept the page. He did not know why.
223:He spent 30 years (85 to 115) reconstructing the technique from the single page. He tested on animals first, then on dying mortals (consensual — he asked, they agreed, in exchange for qi-healing for their families), then on the freshly dead. He did not harvest a living mortal's anchor until 140, when Scribe Yan (閻, the corrupt Precelestial official, doc 26 §2.3) contacted him through an intermediary and offered "extended window" anchors — the anchors of the recently dead whose bardo dispersal Scribe Yan had unlawfully delayed. Pei Liang's technique, combined with Scribe Yan's corruption, made harvesting lawful-seeming: the anchors were "already past their window," technically, even though Scribe Yan had artificially extended the window. Pei Liang has been harvesting for 40 years. He has harvested approximately 200 anchors.
227:### 3.2 Personality, secret, grief, motivation
230:- **Secret:** he has harvested the anchor of his own son. His son, Pei An (裴安), was a Foundation Establishment cultivator who died in a law tribulation 50 years ago (when Pei Liang was 130). The tribulation tested a minor Acquired-law amendment Pei An was attempting; he failed. Pei An's anchor, by lawful process, should have entered bardo and dispersed. Pei Liang, in the moment of his son's death, harvested the anchor — the first time he harvested an anchor he loved. He has kept it in his harvest-store for 50 years. He speaks to it, in private, every night. He believes his son's anchor is still conscious, still him, still Pei An. He is wrong (an anchor in a harvest-store is not conscious; it is fuel, slowly drained). He has not been able to verify this because the harvest-technique, by design, prevents the cultivator from perceiving the harvested anchor's interiority — to perceive it would be to perceive its suffering, and the technique's continued use requires the cultivator not to perceive. The secret is: he is, at the deepest level, harvesting anchors to keep his son's anchor company. The 200 anchors are, in his grief-addled heart, companions for Pei An.
231:- **Grief:** Pei An. His only child. His wife (Lady Mu, 木氏) died 80 years ago in a beast attack, before Pei An's tribulation. Pei An was the only person Pei Liang had left. When Pei An died in the tribulation, Pei Liang's grief broke something in him — the part that distinguished between "I will not let my son go" and "I will undo what the law has done." He has not been able to distinguish the two since.
232:- **Motivation (why he harvests anchors):** Pei Liang wants Spirit Severance because Spirit Severance is the realm of externalized law (化神, doc 03 Station 7). He wants to author a domain where anchor-harvesting is lawful, where his son's anchor can be restored to consciousness, where the 200 anchors in his store can be returned to their bodies. He believes this is possible. It is not — the harvest is irreversible; the drained anchors cannot be reconstituted; Spirit Severance authorship is bounded and cannot undo the harvest. His motivation is not power. It is grief. He wants his son back. The 200 harvested anchors are, in his mind, the cost of a grief-undoing law he believes he can write at Spirit Severance. He is wrong about the law. He is wrong about his son (Pei An's anchor is not conscious). He is right about one thing: the law, as it stands, is cruel. The bardo window is bounded, and the bound is a grief the cosmos does not acknowledge. His dissent from the law is, at root, a dissent from a cosmos that took his son and called it lawful.
234:### 3.3 The dark mirror
236:The protagonist and Pei Liang have both lost someone (Wang Xiaodi / Pei An). Both want them back. Both have, at some level, considered breaking the law to do so (the protagonist's Accommodate path is exactly this — joining Pei Liang). The difference is that the protagonist chose to mourn (Scene 5, the successful Foundation Establishment, requires mourning first); Pei Liang chose not to. The dark mirror: Pei Liang is what the protagonist would have become if they had not mourned. This is the load-bearing thematic parallel. When the protagonist perceives Wang Xiaodi's anchor in Pei Liang's harvest-store (session ~550), they perceive, in the same store, Pei An's anchor — and recognize, in the recognition of Pei An, the recognition of their own grief in Pei Liang's. This is the moment the personal conflict and the antagonist merge (doc 26 §4.3).
238:### 3.4 Arc across the three acts
240:- **When the player first hears of him (Act 2, session ~200):** the raid on the Cangwu Sect. The protagonist, visiting the Cangwu Sect on sect business, survives because Pei Liang does not consider a Foundation Establishment cultivator worth killing (doc 26 §3.3). The protagonist perceives Pei Liang's qi-signature: stained, corrupted by the residue of dozens of harvested anchors. The protagonist does not yet know his name. The Cangwu Sect's records, which Pei Liang stole, contained the anchor-signatures of every mortal who had died in the region for 50 years — the catalog Pei Liang uses to select harvest targets. The protagonist does not yet know this.
241:- **When the player first meets him (Act 2, session ~350):** the protagonist, investigating the raid, tracks Pei Liang to a village 200 li west of the Cangwu Mountains — Pei Liang's hometown, which he visits once a year on the anniversary of his father's death. The protagonist perceives him across a market square. Pei Liang perceives the protagonist. They do not speak. Pei Liang, recognizing the protagonist from the raid, chooses not to kill them — too low-realm to be a threat, and he is, in his own way, courteous. He nods. The protagonist, in the same moment, perceives Pei Liang's anchored grief — a faint resonance, in Pei Liang's qi, of a held anchor the protagonist cannot yet identify. The protagonist does not know what they are perceiving. They will remember it at Core Formation.
242:- **The revelation and first fight (Act 2/3 boundary, session ~550):** the protagonist, now Core Formation, pursues Pei Liang to one of his harvest-stores in the Cangwu foothills. They fight. The protagonist is outmatched (Core Formation vs. Nascent Soul). During the fight, the protagonist perceives Wang Xiaodi's anchor in the store — the Residual Error Sense, extended at Core Formation to perceive others' anchors, confirms the signature: it matches the residue-traces Wang Xiaodi left in the protagonist's own practice decades ago (the protagonist, as a Qi Condensation cultivator, practiced near the infant Wang Xiaodi; the infant's anchor left faint traces in the protagonist's residue). The fight ends with the protagonist wounded and Pei Liang gone (Pei Liang does not kill the protagonist; he is courteous; he perceives the protagonist's grief and recognizes, in it, his own). This is the moment the personal conflict and the antagonist merge.
243:- **The pursuit (Act 3, sessions ~600-1000):** the protagonist, with companions, pursues Pei Liang. Liang Zhu dies in this arc (session ~800). The protagonist breaks through to Nascent Soul (sessions ~1000-1200).
244:- **The final confrontation (Act 3, session ~1200-1500):** the protagonist, at Nascent Soul or Spirit Severance, tracks Pei Liang to the Cangwu Mountains' latent spirit vein — the site of Pei Liang's attempted Spirit Severance breakthrough.
246:### 3.5 The three possible fates
248:- **Confront (the protagonist kills him):** Pei Liang, in the moment of death, perceives his son's anchor in his own harvest-store one last time — and, in perceiving it, finally perceives that it is not conscious. He has been keeping a held grief, not a held son. His last words (the protagonist perceives them via Residual Error Sense) are: "He is not there. He was never there." The harvested anchors, freed, enter bardo — but the bardo windows have closed. The anchors disperse lawfully. The protagonist perceives Wang Xiaodi's anchor disperse, finally and lawfully, into the cosmos's law-substrate. This is the teaching's resolution: Wang Xiaodi is gone, finally and lawfully, and the protagonist lets him go.
249:- **Accommodate (the protagonist joins him):** the protagonist accepts Pei Liang's offer — shares Wang Xiaodi's anchor, becomes complicit in the harvest, gains Pei Liang's protection and a share of his Spirit Severance breakthrough. The protagonist's karmic trace becomes heavy. At the next tribulation (karmic tribulation, doc 24 §2.1), the protagonist cannot sustain the ledger. Their anchor disperses. In the moment before dispersal, the protagonist perceives — for the first time — that Pei An's anchor in Pei Liang's store is not conscious. The protagonist perceives, in this, the futility of the accommodation: the harvest cannot undo grief; it can only compound it. The protagonist dies knowing this. Pei Liang, surviving, continues the harvest. (Per doc 26 §7.3, this is ending 3, the corrupted variant.)
250:- **Transcend (the protagonist transcends him):** the protagonist, at Mahayana (having crossed Tribulation and authored the law that closes the bardo-harvest loophole), does not kill Pei Liang directly. The law, taking effect, disperses Pei Liang's harvest-store lawfully — including Pei An's anchor. Pei Liang, in the moment of his son's anchor's dispersal, perceives (the protagonist's law, by design, makes the harvested anchors' interiority perceivable in their final moment) that Pei An is not there. Pei Liang, broken by the perception, does not resist. The protagonist does not kill him; Pei Liang, anchor-less (his own anchor, sustained by the harvest, collapses when the harvest is gone), disperses. It is not a killing. It is a dying. The protagonist perceives, in Pei Liang's last residue, a recognition — Pei Liang perceiving, finally, that his son was not in the store. The protagonist weeps. This is the Transcend path's deepest cost: the antagonist is not defeated; he is shown, and the showing kills him.
252:### 3.6 The Zhou Wugui inheritance and the third-generation line
254:Pei Liang is, per doc 26 §15, "the third-generation inheritor of Zhou Wugui's anchor-harvesting technique." The line runs as follows. **Zhou Wugui** (周無鬼), the first-generation demonic cultivator, was killed 95 years ago by the Azure Sword Sect's previous sect master after harvesting three Cangwu foothills villages (doc 24 §4.4). His technique died with him — except for the single page Pei Liang found. **The unnamed second-generation inheritor** was a rogue cultivator who, 70 years ago, acquired fragments of Zhou Wugui's manual through the underworld market and attempted a smaller harvest in a coastal village 100 li east. The Azure Sword Sect's outer hall investigated, killed the cultivator, and destroyed the fragments they found — but did not find all of them. The Azure Sword's records of this second case are what Leng Wushuang's late husband was investigating when he died in the Cangwu Mountains 80 years ago; this is the connection Leng Qingxue discovers at session ~700 (§2.3). **Pei Liang** is the third-generation inheritor, the one who reconstructed the technique from the single page and, with Scribe Yan's corruption, made it scalable. He is the most successful of the three — the only one to reach Nascent Soul on harvested fuel, the only one to attempt Spirit Severance. He is also the last. The protagonist's law (Transcend path) closes the loophole that made all three possible; the protagonist's sword (Confront path) closes the line by killing its last inheritor; the protagonist's complicity (Accommodate path) continues the line by becoming its fourth.
256:The line is also a mirror of the Wang lineage. The Wang lineage is three generations of mortal farmers (Wang Tianlu, Wang Shouzheng, the protagonist) producing one cultivator (the protagonist) who founds a teaching lineage. The Zhou Wugui line is three generations of demonic cultivators (Zhou Wugui, the unnamed second, Pei Liang) producing one law-breaker (Pei Liang) who attempts to found a domain. The two lines run parallel through the same region for 95 years. They meet at the Cangwu Mountains' latent spirit vein, where the protagonist's lineage-founding and Pei Liang's domain-founding both attempt to root. The meeting is the final confrontation. The protagonist's choice (Confront, Accommodate, Transcend) is the choice of which line continues.

332:## 5. What this document enables
338:### 5.1 What remains unnamed
340:The remaining 16 households of Wang Family Bend (households 16-31) are generator-facing. They are produced lawfully from the household model (doc 04 §2.3) when the player enters them, with the 15 named households as the social spine. The generator draws surnames from the regional distribution (Wang, Chen, Li, Lin, Zhao, Sun, Hu, He, Wu, Yang, Pan, Zhou, Tang, Yu, Han, Ma, Qin, Bai, Xu — the surnames already established in the corpus), assigns occupations from the village's occupational distribution (farmer, tenant, day laborer, weaver, fisher, carpenter, butcher, tofu-maker, charcoal-burner, ferryman, midwife, merchant, teacher, soldier, gravedigger, herb-gatherer), and binds each generated household to at least one of the 15 named households via kinship, trade, or obligation. The generator must produce, for each generated household, at least one connection to one of the three current tensions (doc 28 §2) — either as a participant (the household is affected) or as a witness (the household saw something). No household in Wang Family Bend is a spectator to the village's life.
344:### 5.2 Reviewer voices (per the doctrine: "Exhibit reviewer voices; do not self-certify")
346:**Reviewer A (the genre traditionalist):** "The antagonist's grief-as-motivation is a Western psychological framing imposed on a xianxia antagonist.
