     1→# Terminology and Cultivation Foundation — Research Draft v0.1
     2→
     3→**Project:** Grand Architect Xianxia Sandbox  
     4→**Status:** research-gate draft; not canon  
     5→**Date:** 2026-08-02  
     6→**Scope:** seasoned-reader terminology and a realm-neutral cultivation ontology  
     7→**Explicit non-scope:** no realm ladder, no protagonist advantage selection, no production code, no real-world cultivation or medical instructions
     8→
     9→---
    10→
    11→## 0. Executive finding
    12→
    13→The project should speak fluent xianxia English without turning Chinese history, Daoism, Buddhism, medicine, or modern web fiction into one invented “ancient system.” The safest and strongest vocabulary policy is:
    14→
    15→1. keep terms that seasoned English-language xianxia readers already process as ordinary genre language—**qi, Dao, cultivation, cultivator, Qi Condensation, meridians, spiritual roots, dantian, yin–yang, breakthrough, tribulation, sect**;
    16→2. translate or briefly gloss terms whose romanization would make prose feel like an untranslated database—**Five Phases**, **inner alchemy**, **heart-mind**, **inner nature**, **vital essence**, **pattern/coherence**;
    17→3. show Hanzi and tonal pinyin in research notes, codex entries, scholarly tooltips, or a first-use cultural note, not in every line of play;
    18→4. distinguish an expression's historical uses from its later religious uses, its modern genre uses, and this project's original definition; and
    19→5. define cultivation as a transformation of body, qi, heart-mind/spirit, practice, environment, relationships, and Dao orientation—not as one rising number.
    20→
    21→The historical record does not supply one timeless realm ladder. Religious Daoism is internally diverse; early philosophical texts, later ritual lineages, medical channel theory, internal alchemy, and modern cultivation fiction are related but not interchangeable. The [Stanford entry on Religious Daoism](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2024/entries/daoism-religion/) explicitly describes Daoism as heterogeneous and tracks distinct ritual, institutional, meditative, alchemical, and soteriological formations. Zhange Ni's study of modern *xiuzhen* fantasy describes the genre as a reinvention of alchemy and other Chinese religious and cultural materials rather than a transparent survival of one historical system ([Ni 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/11/1/25)).
    22→
    23→The original game ontology proposed below therefore treats history as a set of **design constraints and conceptual provocations**, modern xianxia as a set of **genre expectations**, and canon as a future **original decision**.

    27→## 1. Evidence labels used in this draft
    28→
    29→Every claim or term belongs to one or more evidence layers:
    30→
    31→| Label | Meaning | Permitted use |
    32→|---|---|---|
    33→| **EARLY TEXT** | Attested in an early Chinese text or early-philosophy scholarship. | Conceptual history; never proof of a later unified religion or game mechanic. |
    34→| **HISTORICAL/RELIGIOUS** | Attested in scholarship on a particular period, lineage, ritual, medical tradition, or practice. | Contextual inspiration with period and tradition named. |
    35→| **GENRE CONVENTION** | Recurrent in modern cultivation fiction, translation, or games. | Reader-facing vocabulary and comparative design pressure; not historical fact. |
    36→| **PROJECT ORIGINAL** | A proposed definition or rule for this game. | Candidate design only until separately ratified. |
    37→| **UNRESOLVED** | Plausible but not yet supported by an adequate source set. | Research question, not lore. |
    38→
    39→Two further controls apply:
    40→
    41→- A word can exist historically while its familiar game meaning is modern. **Spiritual root** is a good example: the expression has older literary/religious uses, but the standardized aptitude-and-affinity organ familiar to cultivation readers is a modern genre construction and varies by work.
    42→- Similar English translations do not prove identical concepts. **Spirit**, **soul**, **mind**, **essence**, **fate**, **mandate**, and **life** must not be collapsed because a translator once used the same English noun for different source terms.

    46→## 2. Historical map: what is related, and what must remain distinct
    47→
    48→### 2.1 Early cultivation discourse
    49→
    50→The received *Neiye* (內業, *Nèiyè*, “Inward Training”) brings bodily regulation, breath/qi, vital essence, attention, tranquility, and the heart-mind into one field of self-cultivation. Harold D. Roth's scholarly translation and study treats it as an early text of breath meditation and inner cultivation, while also making a specific interpretive argument about early Daoism—not an uncontested universal scheme ([Columbia University Press](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/original-tao/9780231115650/); primary-text discovery copy at [Chinese Text Project](https://ctext.org/guanzi/nei-ye)).
    51→
    52→The *Daodejing* and *Zhuangzi* offer multiple ways to think about *dao* as path/way, natural possibility, changing guidance, skill, perspective, and transformation. The current [Stanford Daoism entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/daoism/) emphasizes that *dao* is not simply a magical force or a Western-style statutory law. This is a valuable guardrail: the project may create gameable “world laws,” but should not claim that this is the only or literal historical meaning of Dao.
    53→
    54→### 2.2 Correlative cosmology
    55→
    56→Yin–yang and the Five Phases became important ways of classifying and describing cyclic transformation. The [Stanford Chinese metaphysics entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chinese-metaphysics/) describes generating and overcoming relations among the Five Phases and also records historical criticism of such correlations. Therefore:
    57→
    58→- yin and yang are relational and context-dependent, not good and evil;
    59→- *wuxing* should normally be translated **Five Phases**, not “five elements,” because the emphasis is process and relation rather than five inert substances; and
    60→- a project affinity system must remain an original game rule, not be marketed as an authoritative reconstruction of Chinese metaphysics.
    61→
    62→### 2.3 Medical channels and the body
    63→
    64→Chinese medical channel theory has a long and changing history. Early excavated manuscripts describe eleven *mai* vessels; later received medical systems prominently organize twelve regular channels alongside collaterals and other networks. “Meridian” is a familiar English umbrella term, but the historical vocabulary is finer-grained: 經脈/经脉 *jīngmài* for main channels or conduit vessels and 經絡/经络 *jīngluò* for channels-and-collaterals as a network. Useful overviews include [NCBI MeSH](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/mesh/68016740), a historical discussion of early channel texts ([PMC 9209873](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9209873/)), and a review noting the lack of consensus about a dedicated modern anatomical structure ([PMC 2694206](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2694206/)).
    65→
    66→For the game, **meridians are real fictional structures under the project's canon**. That is a fantasy decision, not a biomedical claim. The project must not present in-world diagrams or medicine as real clinical advice. The US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health documents mixed evidence and meaningful safety concerns around real traditional-medicine products and procedures ([NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/traditional-chinese-medicine-what-you-need-to-know)).
    67→
    68→### 2.4 Religious Daoism and alchemy
    69→
    70→Historical Daoism includes ritual communities, priesthoods, temples, registers, revelations, lineages, monastic and non-monastic forms, meditation, external alchemy, internal alchemy, longevity practices, and multiple models of transcendence. These cannot be reduced to solitary energy training. The [Stanford Religious Daoism entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2024/entries/daoism-religion/) is especially useful on four points:
    71→
    72→- teaching and lineage are mutually implicated rather than separable flavor;
    73→- external alchemy (*waidan*) was a ritual and technical sequence, not merely “craft a potion”;
    74→- internal alchemy (*neidan*) combined multiple roots and did not simply move an external laboratory into the body; and
    75→- immortality could mean extraordinary longevity, bodily transcendence, ascent, celestial office, union/return, or the generation of a deathless transformed person, depending on source and tradition.
    76→
    77→Its summary of *jing*, *qi*, and *shen* as Essence, Breath, and Spirit is important historical context, but not permission to make every school in the game use one “three resource bars” model.
    78→
    79→### 2.5 Heart-mind, spirit, body
    80→
    81→心 *xīn* is frequently rendered **heart-mind** because it encompasses cognitive, affective, intentional, and evaluative functions. The [Stanford heart-mind entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chinese-mind/) also reports real scholarly disagreement about how holistic or dualistic early Chinese views were. The project should draw one practical lesson: do not begin with a hard imported split between “rational mind,” “emotional heart,” “material body,” and “immaterial soul,” and do not claim that all Chinese traditions deny every functional distinction.
    82→
    83→神 *shén* can mean spirit, numinous efficacy, a god/spirit, or a cultivated aspect of a person depending on context. It is not a universal synonym for a detachable RPG soul.
    84→
    85→### 2.6 Landscape and cultivation
    86→
    87→Scholarship can support a model in which bodies and environments are porous and mutually conditioning rather than isolated. James Miller's *China's Green Religion* highlights qi across landscapes and bodies, transformative power associated with specific topographies, and flourishing as ecological relation ([Oxford/Columbia abstract](https://academic.oup.com/columbia-scholarship-online/book/13601)). This supports an environmental design pressure, not a claim that every Daoist historically accepted one “ambient qi density” map.
    88→
    89→### 2.7 Modern cultivation fiction and games
    90→
    91→Modern *xiuzhen/xiuxian/xianxia* works transform alchemical, religious, literary, martial, medical, mythic, technological, and game-like materials into progression fantasy. Ni argues that *xiuzhen* fantasy built generic conventions around a reimagined Daoist alchemy and made cultivation society's organizing center ([Ni 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/11/1/25)). A recent cultivation-game study distinguishes *xiuzhen*, *xiuxian*, and *xianxia*, then describes the transfer of level structures, alchemical crafts, spiritual roots, Five-Phase relationships, and stretched time into game mechanics ([Hao 2026](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20594364251364733)).
    92→
    93→This layer gives us reader expectations such as sects, breakthroughs, bottlenecks, body/qi/spirit practices, alchemy, artifacts, spiritual roots, and tribulations. It does **not** give us a single canonical ladder. Even closely related works rename, merge, split, reorder, or redefine realms.

    97→## 3. Seasoned-reader terminology register
    98→
    99→### 3.1 Keep these established terms in ordinary player-facing English
   100→
   101→| Preferred term | Hanzi and pinyin where supported | Evidence layer | Project usage rule |
   102→|---|---|---|---|
   103→| **qi** | 氣/气, *qì* | EARLY TEXT; HISTORICAL/RELIGIOUS; GENRE | Keep **qi**, not “chi,” “mana,” or a universal gloss such as “energy.” Lowercase in ordinary use; capitalize only in a proper label such as **Qi Condensation**. Canon must define what project qi can carry, do, conserve, and fail to do. |
   104→| **Dao** / **a dao** | 道, *dào* | EARLY TEXT; HISTORICAL/RELIGIOUS; GENRE | Keep **Dao** for the ultimate or cosmological sense selected by canon and **a dao** for a particular way/path only when context is clear. Never use Dao as an unexplained synonym for spell school, physical energy, statute, fate, or moral goodness. |
   105→| **cultivation** | 修煉/修炼, *xiūliàn*, as a broad modern expression; related terms vary | HISTORICAL/RELIGIOUS; GENRE | Keep **cultivation** as the umbrella English verb and process. Its project definition is whole-person, relational transformation, not XP accumulation. |
   106→| **cultivator** | 修士 *xiūshì*, 修行者 *xiūxíngzhě*, 修真者 *xiūzhēnzhě*, and others vary by context | GENRE with older constituent vocabulary | Keep **cultivator**. Do not randomly alternate practitioner, immortalist, truth-cultivator, and Daoist as if they were exact synonyms. A cultivator need not be a Daoist priest. |
   107→| **Qi Condensation** | No single universal source label should be asserted yet; 凝氣/凝气 *níngqì* and 煉氣/炼气 *liànqì* are not automatically identical | GENRE; PROJECT LOCKED TERM | Keep the established English label. Do not retroactively assign one Hanzi form until the project's chosen Chinese naming layer is researched. Its position and mechanics remain undecided; the existing capability constraint still bars personal huge-mountain splitting. |
   108→| **meridians** | 經脈/经脉 *jīngmài*; broader 經絡/经络 *jīngluò* | HISTORICAL/MEDICAL; GENRE | Keep **meridians** in play. Specialist codex text may distinguish main channels, collaterals, and other pathways. They are fictional canonical structures, not modern anatomy claims. |
   109→| **spiritual roots** | 靈根/灵根, *línggēn* | older expression; standardized aptitude system is primarily GENRE | Keep **spiritual roots** because seasoned readers know it. Treat every rule—inheritance, testability, affinity, purity, mutability, rarity—as an original decision. Do not call the common webnovel model an ancient medical fact. |
   110→| **dantian** | 丹田, *dāntián*, “cinnabar/elixir field” | HISTORICAL/RELIGIOUS; GENRE | Keep **dantian** after a first-use gloss such as “inner elixir field.” Historical sources can describe one or three fields and differing locations/functions; canon must choose or deliberately allow disagreement. Do not call it a literal biomedical organ. |
   111→| **yin–yang** | 陰陽/阴阳, *yīnyáng* | EARLY/HISTORICAL; GENRE | Keep **yin–yang**. Use as contextual polarity, mutual implication, alternation, and transformation—not gender essence, moral binary, or two universal damage colors. |
   112→| **breakthrough** | multiple Chinese expressions by work | GENRE | Keep **breakthrough** for a qualitative state transition. It must produce changed capacities, risks, needs, perception, and social facts, not merely a stat popup. |
   113→| **bottleneck** | multiple Chinese expressions by work | GENRE | Keep **bottleneck** for the concrete limiting relation preventing a transition. Every bottleneck needs a type and cause; it is not a random progress lock. |
   114→| **tribulation** / **heavenly tribulation** | 劫 *jié*; 天劫 *tiānjié*; “cross/pass a tribulation” 渡劫 *dùjié* | broader religious/literary calamity concepts; modern breakthrough usage is GENRE | Keep the English terms. Do not assume every breakthrough receives lightning or that Heaven is one judging person. Project cause, target selection, jurisdiction, escalation, aftermath, and exploit limits must be original. |
   115→| **qi deviation** | 走火入魔 *zǒuhuǒ rùmó* has a wider historical and modern semantic history | HISTORICAL/RELIGIOUS/MEDICAL; WUXIA/XIANXIA GENRE | Keep **qi deviation** as a readable umbrella only when the specific in-world failure is also named. The Taiwanese Ministry of Education dictionary records bodily and delusional senses ([MOE](https://dict.revised.moe.edu.tw/dictView.jsp?ID=138853&la=0&powerMode=0)); the game must not turn real distress into spectacle or advice. |
   116→| **sect** / **lineage** | 宗, 派, 門/门, lineage names and institutional forms vary | HISTORICAL/RELIGIOUS; GENRE | Keep both, but do not treat them as synonyms. A sect is an organization; a lineage is a claimed chain of authorization/transmission; they may overlap, split, or be fabricated. |
   117→| **cultivation method** / **technique** | 功法 *gōngfǎ* is common in genre; historical categories vary | GENRE | Prefer fluent English in ordinary prose. Use *gongfa* only in a linguistic or in-world historical note. A method is a structured transformative curriculum; a technique is a bounded operation or skill. |
   118→
   119→### 3.2 Translate or gloss these rather than flooding prose with romanization
   120→
   121→| Source term | Default English | Context rule |
   122→|---|---|---|
   123→| 五行 *wǔxíng* | **Five Phases** | Avoid “Five Elements” unless quoting a source or representing an in-world mistranslation. The phases are relational processes/categories. |
   124→| 精 *jīng* | **essence** or **vital essence** | Do not give one universal substance definition. Historical uses include refined/material and reproductive associations; project mechanics require their own definition. |
   125→| 神 *shén* | **spirit**, **numinous spirit**, **conscious spirit**, or **deity** | Translate by referent. Never assume all *shen* is a detachable human soul. |
   126→| 心 *xīn* | **heart-mind** | Use when cognition, affect, desire, intention, and attention are jointly relevant. “Mind” or “heart” alone may be correct in a narrow context. |
   127→| 性 *xìng* | **inner nature** or **nature** | In internal-alchemy scholarship it can pair with *ming*; elsewhere it has different philosophical uses. Do not reduce it to personality alignment. |
   128→| 命 *mìng* | **life**, **existence**, **lifespan**, **allotment**, **command**, **mandate**, or **destiny** | Translate by context. The [Religious Daoism entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2024/entries/daoism-religion/) explicitly notes this semantic range. Never collapse lifespan, fate, and political mandate into one meter. |
   129→| 內丹/内丹 *nèidān* | **inner alchemy** | Gloss at first scholarly/codex use. It names a diverse historical field, not a single historical level list. |
   130→| 外丹 *wàidān* | **external alchemy** | Use for historically situated alchemical traditions. In-world pill and material arts may be inspired by the design pressure while remaining original. |
   131→| 金丹 *jīndān* | **golden elixir** | Prefer this historical/alchemical gloss in research. **Golden Core** is an established genre rendering only if the future ladder deliberately selects it; this draft does not. |
   132→| 自然 *zìrán* | **naturalness**, **spontaneity**, or “self-so” | Translate by argument. Do not turn it into an elemental “nature affinity.” |
   133→| 德 *dé* | **potency**, **virtuosity**, **excellence**, or **virtue** | Translate by context. Do not default to a moral-goodness stat. |
   134→| 理 *lǐ* | **pattern**, **coherence**, or **principle** | Avoid using it as a generic synonym for Dao or statutory law. The relationship of *li* and qi differs across thinkers ([Chinese metaphysics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chinese-metaphysics/)). |
   135→| 修真 *xiūzhēn* | **cultivation toward perfection/transcendence**; retain *xiuzhen* as a genre/research label | Do not put unexplained *xiuzhen* into routine player prose. Modern scholarly usage identifies it as a cultivation-fantasy label while also tracing older religious resonances. |
   136→| 修仙 *xiūxiān* | **cultivation toward immortality**; retain *xiuxian* as a genre/research label | Use the Chinese term when distinguishing modern genre communities, not as decorative synonym variation. |
   137→| 仙俠/仙侠 *xiānxiá* | **xianxia** | Keep the genre name. A literal “immortal heroes” gloss is introductory, not a complete definition. |
   138→| 仙 *xiān* | **immortal**, **transcendent**, or **xian** in a specialist note | The historical/religious category is diverse. Canon must decide what an in-world immortal is rather than inherit a universal checklist. |
   139→| 元神 *yuánshén* | **original spirit** | Translate in player prose unless a school deliberately preserves the term. It is not automatically “Nascent Soul.” |
   140→| 經絡/经络 *jīngluò* | **channel-and-collateral network** | Use in specialist anatomy; ordinary dialogue can say meridians. |
   141→| 經脈/经脉 *jīngmài* | **main channels** or **conduit vessels** | Use when distinction from collaterals matters. |
   142→| 丹田 *dāntián* | **dantian (inner elixir field)** | Retain after first gloss because seasoned readers know it. |
   143→| 天 *tiān* | **Heaven**, **sky**, or **nature** | Translate by speaker and doctrine. Never assume a single anthropomorphic heavenly ruler. |
   144→| 法 *fǎ* | **method**, **model**, **standard**, or **law** | Do not translate every *fa* as cosmic physics. |
   145→
   146→### 3.3 Terms that need explicit separation in project canon
   147→
   148→The following may all exist, but they may not silently share one number or resolver:
   149→
   150→| Terms to separate | Why |
   151→|---|---|
   152→| **Dao / world law / statute / technique rule** | A way or cosmic pattern is not automatically an enacted rule, a local jurisdiction, or an algorithmic condition. |
   153→| **karma / causality / moral reputation / social consequence** | Buddhist karmic action and fruition, ordinary causation, institutional record, and public opinion answer different questions. |
   154→| **fate / luck / mandate / lifespan** | Probability, social legitimacy, a heavenly command/allotment, and remaining biological life are not synonyms. |
   155→| **spirit / soul / consciousness / memory / identity** | They may separate under death, possession, copying, sleep, trauma, or reincarnation. The identity ontology must decide relations explicitly. |
   156→| **qi capacity / qi throughput / qi quality / qi control / qi signature** | A large reservoir does not guarantee safe output, precision, compatibility, concealment, or recovery. |
   157→| **realm / cultivation state / institutional rank / public reputation** | What a person has become, what a school certifies, and what others believe can diverge. |
   158→| **righteous / orthodox / lawful / benevolent** | Institutional orthodoxy, legal authorization, cosmological compatibility, and ethical conduct are independent. |
   159→| **demonic / heterodox / contaminated / predatory** | These can be accusations, lineages, metaphysical conditions, practice mechanisms, or moral judgments. The game must say which. |
   160→| **inner demon / obsession / trauma / spirit intrusion / qi deviation** | Psychological conflict, social language, literal entities, and energetic failure require different evidence and treatment. |
   161→
   162→### 3.4 Style rules for final writing
   163→
   164→1. Use **qi**, not *chi*.
   165→2. Use **Dao** consistently; do not alternate Tao/Dao except in historical publication titles.
   166→3. Use simplified and traditional Hanzi together in research registers when they differ: 氣/气.
   167→4. Use tonal pinyin in the encyclopedia's etymology field, not in every dialogue line.
   168→5. Italicize romanized specialist terms in English research prose; do not italicize established English loan vocabulary after adoption if the style guide later decides otherwise.
   169→6. Give one crisp English sentence before any philological note.
   170→7. Never use romanization to make a shallow system look culturally specific.
   171→8. Preserve meaningful disagreement among teachers. A disputed in-world term should store speaker, school, period, and claim—not receive one omniscient glossary definition unless canon truly settles it.
   172→9. Descriptions must match mechanics. “Shattered his meridians,” “lost his cultivation,” and “died” are three different state transitions.
   173→10. No real-world breath retention, ingestion, fasting, sexual, needling, dosage, or alchemical procedure should be taught through game text.

   177→## 4. Original cultivation ontology
   178→
   179→Everything in this section is **PROJECT ORIGINAL**. Historical scholarship motivates design questions, but the following architecture is not presented as a historical doctrine.
   180→
   181→### 4.1 Definition
   182→
   183→> **Cultivation is the sustained, taught, tested, and socially situated transformation of a living being's capacity to participate in the world's patterns without losing the ability to remain a coherent person.**
   184→
   185→This definition makes four promises:
   186→
   187→- cultivation changes ways of living, perceiving, acting, enduring, relating, and understanding;
   188→- power is produced through practices, bodies, environments, materials, teachers, institutions, and choices;
   189→- progress creates new dependencies, risks, duties, enemies, time horizons, and forms of ignorance; and
   190→- a breakthrough is genuine only when world behavior changes, not when a label changes.
   191→
   192→### 4.2 The cultivation state is multidimensional
   193→
   194→No actor has one “cultivation power” number. Every cultivated person has at least the following coupled domains.
   195→
   196→| Domain | What must be represented | Questions it must answer |
   197→|---|---|---|
   198→| **Body** | Structure, integrity, adaptation, pain, fatigue, nutrition, healing, senses, tolerance, scars, age, and cultivated organs/pathways. | What can this body safely contain, perform, survive, recover from, and sense? |
   199→| **Qi** | Sources, reservoir, pressure, quality, phase/affinity, signature, purity, circulation, throughput, efficiency, leakage, control, replenishment, and contamination. | What qi can enter, be changed, be moved, be emitted, be hidden, and be recovered—and at what loss? |
   200→| **Heart-mind and spirit** | Attention, emotional pattern, intention, memory, perception, self-model, dream, volition, integration, clarity, fragmentation, and spirit boundaries. | What can the person notice, hold, interpret, resist, remember, and remain themselves through? |
   201→| **Dao orientation** | Lived commitments, observations, models, contradictions, enacted understanding, attunements, blind spots, and claimed insights. | Which world relationships can this person reliably understand or participate in, and where is the claim false? |
   202→| **Vital continuity** | Chronological age, bodily wear, remaining recovery, lifespan effects, transformed embodiments, continuity across sleep/death/possession/rebirth where applicable. | What persists, what ages, what can die, and what counts as the same person? |
   203→| **Practice repertoire** | Learned methods, proficiency, compatible sequences, habits, improvisations, omissions, prohibitions, and maintenance. | What can the actor actually do repeatedly—not merely recite? |
   204→| **Transmission provenance** | Teacher, lineage claim, manual version, translator, demonstrations, corrections, oaths, missing passages, and counterfeit risks. | Why should this knowledge work, and what could have been mistransmitted? |
   205→| **Environmental relation** | Local qi ecology, season, altitude/depth, terrain, weather, spirits, formations, settlement use, contamination, and law climate. | Where and when does this path thrive, fail, deplete, or transform its surroundings? |
   206→| **Social/institutional state** | Household obligations, sect membership, rank, license, orthodoxy claims, debts, enemies, patronage, law, market access, and recognition. | Who permits, teaches, supplies, exploits, certifies, or hunts this cultivator? |
   207→| **Failure and debt** | Injuries, deviations, impurities, vows, resource debts, unresolved transitions, ecological damage, trauma, pursuit, and suppressed symptoms. | What past cost remains active and what future choices does it constrain? |
   208→
   209→The realm capability envelope belongs above this state as a derived feasibility boundary. A realm name may summarize a transformation family, but it may never erase the underlying domain state.
   210→
   211→### 4.3 Body ontology
   212→
   213→The body is not a disposable container that becomes irrelevant after the tutorial. It is:
   214→
   215→- the current limit on safe qi pressure, throughput, sensory load, and technique recoil;
   216→- a history of labor, diet, illness, injury, care, climate, sex, age, disability, and training;
   217→- a living topology that may include flesh, bones, organs, meridians, collaterals, dantian, apertures, scars, seals, foreign matter, and transformed structures only where canon defines them;
   218→- a source of signals that can be correctly read, misread, hidden, numbed, or forged; and
   219→- a social body subject to family expectation, examination, beauty norms, stigma, law, medicine, exploitation, and ritual.
   220→
   221→Required body-state distinctions:
   222→
   223→- **capacity** versus **current load**;
   224→- **injury** versus **adaptation**;
   225→- **temporary reinforcement** versus **permanent transformation**;
   226→- **functional loss** versus **pain** versus **visible damage**;
   227→- **natural variation** versus **cultivated structure** versus **implanted/borrowed structure**;
   228→- **healed** versus **scarred** versus **compensated**; and
   229→- **personal capability** versus protection supplied by clothing, artifact, formation, medicine, ally, or environment.
   230→
   231→### 4.4 Qi ontology
   232→
   233→Project qi should be treated as a family of canonically real, transformable media and capacities, not as undifferentiated blue fuel. Every qi instance or category ultimately needs:
   234→
   235→- **provenance:** body, food, breath, landscape, celestial cycle, material, spirit, artifact, formation, another being, or unknown;
   236→- **carrier/state:** diffuse, circulating, stored, bound, crystallized, inscribed, embodied, emitted, or otherwise defined;
   237→- **qualities:** phase, polarity, temperature-like behavior, movement tendency, texture, purity, age, will/imprint, and compatibility where canon supports them;
   238→- **conservation and loss:** what is consumed, transformed, dispersed, borrowed, returned, or leaves waste;
   239→- **access conditions:** body, spiritual roots, method, authority, season, location, consent, ownership, or preparation;
   240→- **transport:** meridian path, tissue, breath, artifact circuit, formation, environmental flow, spirit relation, or direct law interface;
   241→- **limits:** reservoir, instantaneous throughput, sustained throughput, pressure, control bandwidth, heat/strain, contamination tolerance, recovery, and coupled body/spirit limits;
   242→- **observability:** what senses, instruments, symptoms, traces, or experts can infer and with what confidence; and
   243→- **failure behavior:** leakage, stagnation, turbulence, inversion, contamination, overpressure, depletion, incompatible conversion, runaway coupling, and dissipation.
   244→
   245→Two non-negotiable rules:
   246→
   247→1. **More qi cannot solve a control, authority, perception, bodily, temporal, or compatibility deficit by itself.**
   248→2. **Every spectacular feat has a contribution ledger.** Personal qi, prepared resources, artifacts, allies, environmental potential, and external authority remain separately attributable.
   249→
   250→### 4.5 Heart-mind and spirit ontology
   251→
   252→The heart-mind/spirit domain should avoid both “sanity bar” reduction and limitless metaphysical handwaving. It includes:
   253→
   254→- attention width, depth, switching, and endurance;
   255→- emotional tendencies and the ability to experience, express, regulate, repress, or misinterpret them;
   256→- intention formation and conflict;
   257→- memory encoding, recall, alteration, loss, and provenance;
   258→- sensory integration and susceptibility to overload or illusion;
   259→- dream, trance, visualization, contemplation, and spirit perception where canon supports them;
   260→- self-recognition and continuity under body change, possession, copying, reincarnation, or long life;
   261→- integration among belief, action, emotion, memory, and Dao claim; and
   262→- boundaries by which foreign will, spirit, oath, artifact, domain, or technique may influence the person.
   263→
   264→Clarity is not emotionlessness. Strong emotion is not automatic deviation. Trauma is not an evil power-up. A teacher may value tranquility, fierce sincerity, compassion, disciplined ritual attention, skepticism, or artistic absorption; the game should simulate what that practice actually trains rather than award one universal “calm mind” score.
   265→
   266→### 4.6 Dao orientation ontology
   267→
   268→A Dao orientation is a living relationship among:
   269→
   270→1. **observed regularities** in the world;
   271→2. **interpretation** of those regularities;
   272→3. **practice** that attempts to participate in them;
   273→4. **values and commitments** about how to live or act;
   274→5. **evidence** from successful and failed action;
   275→6. **contradictions** the cultivator has not resolved; and
   276→7. **social language** supplied by a lineage, text, teacher, craft, place, or personal history.
   277→
   278→Dao comprehension is not lore-quiz currency. A claimed insight must expose itself to tests:
   279→
   280→- Can the cultivator predict or recognize a relevant change?
   281→- Can they act with greater precision, efficiency, flexibility, or restraint?
   282→- Can they explain failure without merely renaming it?
   283→- Does their practice remain coherent in an unfamiliar environment?
   284→- Can they teach, demonstrate, or embody the insight?
   285→- What counterexample, contradiction, or cost remains?
   286→
   287→Different paths may produce incompatible but locally effective maps. The omniscient encyclopedia may record canonical relations, but characters should possess situated models with evidence and uncertainty.
   288→
   289→### 4.7 Spiritual roots as an original aptitude topology
   290→
   291→Because **spiritual roots** are expected genre vocabulary but not a fixed historical organ, the game should define them afresh. Recommended candidate definition:
   292→
   293→> A spiritual-root profile is a developing topology of access and discrimination: it describes which forms of ambient or mediated qi a body-spirit system can notice, admit, distinguish, transform, and stabilize, under which conditions and costs.
   294→
   295→This avoids a single birth lottery while preserving aptitude as consequential. A full profile may include:
   296→
   297→- sensitivity thresholds;
   298→- admission rate;
   299→- discrimination among similar qi;
   300→- conversion loss;
   301→- compatibility breadth and interference;
   302→- stability under mixtures;
   303→- learning plasticity;
   304→- environmental dependence;
   305→- hereditary, prenatal, developmental, injured, trained, artificial, or transformed components;
   306→- ease of testing and likelihood of false classification; and
   307→- institutional interpretation of the result.
   308→
   309→A “poor” root by one sect's test may be valuable to another method or ecology. Tests measure through an instrument, theory, and threshold; they do not directly print the world's omniscient truth.
   310→
   311→### 4.8 Dantian and meridian topology
   312→
   313→The project should not assume every person and path shares one identical circuit. The future canon must decide which of the following are universal, developmental, lineage-specific, or disputed:
   314→
   315→- one primary dantian versus multiple fields;
   316→- fields as pre-existing loci, trained functions, constructed organs, relational zones, or competing interpretations;
   317→- fixed meridians versus developing pathways;
   318→- main channels, collaterals, extraordinary routes, apertures, nodes, reservoirs, gates, and seals;
   319→- bidirectional versus directional circulation;
   320→- whether a path enlarges, repairs, reroutes, duplicates, externalizes, or deliberately abandons parts of the network; and
   321→- what bodily variation means for manuals written around a different norm.
   322→
   323→Every anatomical claim needs an observer model. A healer, cultivator, corpse examiner, artifact scan, spirit sense, and sect test may reveal different aspects and can be wrong for different reasons.

   327→## 5. Practice ontology
   328→
   329→### 5.1 Every practice is a complete causal record
   330→
   331→A cultivation practice is not a flavor paragraph plus a progress rate. Every generated or authored practice must specify:
   332→
   333→| Field | Requirement |
   334→|---|---|
   335→| **Aim** | The transformation it claims to produce and the school's reason for valuing it. |
   336→| **World model** | What the practice assumes about body, qi, spirit, environment, and Dao. |
   337→| **Eligible practitioners** | Required body state, roots, senses, knowledge, vows, tools, authority, age, recovery, and exclusions. |
   338→| **Inputs** | Time, attention, food, qi, place, season, material, teacher labor, ritual support, money, risk, and opportunity cost. |
   339→| **Operation** | A safe abstract description of what in-world variables change; no actionable real-world medical or alchemical procedure. |
   340→| **Cadence** | Session, cycle, maintenance interval, rest, consolidation, seasonal window, and interruption behavior. |
   341→| **Environment** | Helpful, required, harmful, depleted, and contaminated conditions. |
   342→| **Signals** | Subjective sensations, visible signs, instrument readings, teacher observations, and false positives. |
   343→| **Outputs** | Domain changes, new affordances, changed needs, residues, byproducts, and environmental effects. |
   344→| **Costs** | Immediate, cumulative, social, ecological, economic, bodily, spiritual, and opportunity costs. |
   345→| **Failure modes** | Incorrect performance, wrong interpretation, incompatibility, overdose/overload, interruption, fraud, and hostile interference. |
   346→| **Correction** | What a qualified teacher, healer, text comparison, rest period, or counter-practice can and cannot repair. |
   347→| **Transmission** | Teacher, lineage, manual revision, translation, demonstration, oath, missing piece, and provenance confidence. |
   348→| **Verification** | Tests that distinguish real transformation from temporary effect, artifact assistance, placebo-like expectation, fraud, or misclassification in-world. |
   349→| **Maintenance and decay** | What must continue and what happens when it stops. |
   350→
   351→### 5.2 Practice families
   352→
   353→These are original design families, not claims that historical traditions used this exact taxonomy.
   354→
   355→| Family | Primary game pressures | Typical coupled risks |
   356→|---|---|---|
   357→| **Breath, posture, and stillness** | Attention, circulation, perception, integration, low-resource access, time. | Misread sensations, overcontrol, stagnation, obsession, unsuitable physiology, teacher absence. |
   358→| **Movement and embodied skill** | Footwork, weapon handling, labor, balance, terrain, timing, body-qi coordination. | Repetition injury, compensatory pattern, technique-body mismatch, over-specialization. |
   359→| **Circulation and refinement** | Routing, conversion, reservoir, throughput, purity, concealment. | Leakage, overpressure, incompatible qi, contamination, damaged pathways. |
   360→| **Contemplation and observation** | Dao orientation, prediction, restraint, perception, emotional integration. | False insight, confirmation bias, detachment from evidence, social manipulation, overload. |
   361→| **Material and alchemical practice** | Resource provenance, transformation, craft, economy, tools, environment. | Poisoning, impurity, runaway reaction, counterfeit ingredient, ecological depletion, unsafe transfer. |
   362→| **Ritual and relational practice** | Spirits, ancestors, offices, vows, communal time, place, legitimacy, consent. | Wrong jurisdiction, breached obligation, impersonated authority, social exclusion, unpaid debt. |
   363→| **Ecological and locative practice** | Sacred geography, seasons, cultivation sites, restoration, weather, local species. | Depletion, displacement, imbalance, dependence on one place, conflict over access. |
   364→| **Craft and inscription** | Talismans, formations, artifacts, medicine, architecture, records, standards. | Hidden defects, version drift, stolen provenance, maintenance burden, catastrophic scaling. |
   365→| **Dream and spirit practice** | Memory, identity, remote perception, spirit relations, death questions. | Confusion of source, intrusion, fragmentation, false memory, body neglect. |
   366→| **Ethical, social, and service practice** | Habit, responsibility, community trust, self-knowledge, role mastery, institutional recognition. | Hypocrisy, coerced conformity, reputation mistaken for transformation, moral injury, exploitation. |
   367→
   368→No family is automatically higher or lower. A path is a tested composition of practices, not a decorative name plus damage type.

   372→## 6. Teaching, transmission, and knowledge
   373→
   374→Historical Daoist traditions make lineage, transmission, ordination, scripture, ritual, and institution central in many contexts; they also include lay, local, non-monastic, and contested forms ([Religious Daoism](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2024/entries/daoism-religion/)). The game should translate that complexity into living knowledge systems.
   375→
   376→### 6.1 Teacher record
   377→
   378→Every teacher needs:
   379→
   380→- what they can personally demonstrate;
   381→- what they only know textually or by report;
   382→- who taught or authorized them and how credible that claim is;
   383→- which bodies, roots, ages, environments, and failures they have actually encountered;
   384→- diagnostic skill versus cultivation skill versus teaching skill;
   385→- motives, obligations, prejudices, secrets, fears, and institutional pressures;
   386→- willingness to correct, adapt, or admit uncertainty;
   387→- time, attention, accessibility, payment, service, oath, and social requirements; and
   388→- failure behavior when a student is harmed or surpasses them.
   389→
   390→A high-realm cultivator is not automatically a good novice teacher.
   391→
   392→### 6.2 Manual and oral transmission record
   393→
   394→Every manual, tablet, memory inheritance, demonstration, or oral formula needs:
   395→
   396→- author/compiler and date or historical layer;
   397→- language, script, dialect, notation, and intended audience;
   398→- complete, abridged, censored, damaged, forged, reconstructed, translated, or commentary status;
   399→- assumed prior instruction and missing embodied correction;
   400→- version lineage and material provenance;
   401→- practices described versus practices merely praised;
   402→- recorded failures and whose experiences were omitted;
   403→- copying errors, intentional traps, political edits, and later interpolation; and
   404→- confidence per claim, not one trust score for the whole artifact.
   405→
   406→### 6.3 Access is gameplay
   407→
   408→Knowledge access can depend on:
   409→
   410→- household wealth and freedom from labor;
   411→- literacy, language, disability, travel, and time;
   412→- sex/gender rules, class, ethnicity, origin, age, and lineage prejudice;
   413→- examination, patronage, adoption, marriage, debt, service, hostage status, or oath;
   414→- temple, sect, academy, guild, state, clan, market, hermitage, and informal network access;
   415→- war, migration, disaster, archive loss, censorship, and translation; and
   416→- the player's earned trust, theft, negotiation, reconstruction, observation, or invention.
   417→
   418→The mortal beginning becomes meaningful when cultivation knowledge has real custodians, costs, and errors.

   422→## 7. Breakthrough ontology without a realm ladder
   423→
   424→### 7.1 Definition
   425→
   426→> A breakthrough is a qualitative, persistent state transition in which a prior limiting relationship is reorganized and the cultivator survives or otherwise continues in a newly coherent form.
   427→
   428→It is not synonymous with level-up, full healing, lightning strike, or victory.
   429→
   430→### 7.2 Breakthrough contract
   431→
   432→Every breakthrough type must define:
   433→
   434→1. **prior state:** exact body, qi, heart-mind/spirit, Dao, practice, environment, and debt requirements;
   435→2. **limiting relation:** what bottleneck actually prevents transition;
   436→3. **preparation:** time, teaching, materials, social support, location, formation, ritual, and recovery;
   437→4. **trigger:** deliberate act, accumulated transformation, insight, crisis, season, external authority, or other causal event;
   438→5. **transition dynamics:** what becomes unstable, what must be controlled, what can interrupt, and what observers can perceive;
   439→6. **proof vector:** bodily, qi, control, perceptual, spiritual, Dao, environmental, and behavioral changes that together establish success;
   440→7. **new capabilities:** only those entailed by the new state and realm envelope;
   441→8. **unchanged limits:** what the transition explicitly does not grant;
   442→9. **new dependencies:** maintenance, resource, place, practice, social, or ecological needs;
   443→10. **failure branches:** clean failure, partial transition, unstable success, injury, deviation, regression, transformation into another path, loss of identity, or death;
   444→11. **consolidation:** the vulnerable period before the change is stable;
   445→12. **social recognition:** which institutions will accept what evidence; and
   446→13. **persistence:** how the state behaves across sleep, unconsciousness, suppression, body change, death, save/load, and simulation distance.
   447→
   448→### 7.3 Bottleneck types
   449→
   450→- **capacity:** insufficient stable body, reservoir, attention, or recovery;
   451→- **throughput:** enough total resource but unsafe rate;
   452→- **control:** insufficient precision, concurrency, or error correction;
   453→- **compatibility:** method, body, roots, qi, environment, oath, or artifact conflict;
   454→- **integration:** body, qi, emotion, memory, values, and technique pull in incompatible directions;
   455→- **knowledge:** missing model, observation, correction, language, or prerequisite;
   456→- **evidence/insight:** theory is untested, false, or too narrow;
   457→- **environment:** absent season, ecology, pressure, spirit, material, or lawful condition;
   458→- **social/institutional:** access, authorization, safety team, ritual office, resource, or political permission;
   459→- **debt:** unresolved injury, contamination, oath, consequence, or borrowed power;
   460→- **identity:** the proposed transition cannot preserve the continuity the cultivator values; and
   461→- **false bottleneck:** an institution, teacher, test, or cultivator has diagnosed the wrong limit.
   462→
   463→### 7.4 Tribulation is optional and causal
   464→
   465→Tribulation should be one possible transition or consequence family, not the universal breakthrough animation. If selected, every tribulation needs:
   466→
   467→- cause: environmental instability, law response, accumulated incompatibility, external opposition, office, oath, fate module, karma module, or another defined mechanism;
   468→- trigger owner and target selection;
   469→- jurisdiction and range;
   470→- readable phases and counter windows;
   471→- whether it tests, punishes, transforms, balances, exposes, or simply follows causally;
   472→- collateral and environmental effects;
   473→- survivable, redirectable, shareable, suppressible, delayable, or unavoidable dimensions;
   474→- repeated-use adaptation and anti-farming rules; and
   475→- persistent aftermath.
   476→
   477→“Heaven was angry” may be a character's interpretation. Canonical mechanism must be separately stated.
   478→

   481→## 8. Deviation, injury, regression, and failure
   482→
   483→The project should retain **qi deviation** as fluent genre language while generating more precise diagnoses.
   484→
   485→### 8.1 Failure families
   486→
   487→| Failure family | Causal examples | Required consequences |
   488→|---|---|---|
   489→| **Structural injury** | Channel tear, organ strain, bone remodeling failure, seal damage, dantian instability. | Function loss, pain/signals, compensation, healing route, scar risk, future compatibility changes. |
   490→| **Energetic dysregulation** | Overpressure, leakage, stagnation, turbulent circulation, inversion, depletion, uncontrolled emission. | Source and route, affected domain, dissipation/recovery, environmental trace, exploit prevention. |
   491→| **Contamination and incompatibility** | Foreign qi, impure material, mixed paths, hostile ecology, spirit imprint, counterfeit medicine. | Dose/exposure history, detection uncertainty, transformation, treatment limits, spread or persistence. |
   492→| **Heart-mind/spirit destabilization** | Attention collapse, sensory overload, false memory, obsession, identity conflict, intrusion, fragmentation. | Person-respecting portrayal, distinct diagnosis, consent/agency implications, recovery and support, no automatic villainy. |
   493→| **Dao/model failure** | False insight, overgeneralized pattern, doctrinal trap, confirmation bias, misread coincidence. | Predictive failure, changed choices, possible correction, social consequences, retained partial truth. |
   494→| **Transmission failure** | Missing step, wrong body assumption, bad translation, forged lineage, negligent teacher. | Provenance trail, responsibility dispute, repair path, institutional response. |
   495→| **Environmental mismatch** | Wrong season, depleted site, hostile law climate, incompatible altitude/depth, disturbed spirit ecology. | Local cause, visible environmental evidence, adaptation or relocation choices. |
   496→| **Institutional harm** | Forced practice, unsafe exam, resource rationing, secrecy, exploitation, status pressure. | Responsible actors, records/cover-up, legal/social recourse, reform or retaliation possibilities. |
   497→| **Breakthrough fracture** | Partial state change, unstable coupling, failed consolidation, borrowed support withdrawn. | Mixed old/new capabilities, vulnerability window, regression or alternate-path possibility. |
   498→| **Predatory extraction** | Stolen cultivation, coerced transfer, devouring, sacrificial formation, lineage exploitation. | Conservation ledger, victim state, identity/consent, contamination, witnesses, law and reputation response. |
   499→
   500→### 8.2 Failure must be playable, not merely punitive
   501→
   502→Failure can generate:
   503→
   504→- new diagnostic quests;
   505→- changed technique options and compensatory styles;
   506→- teacher or institution conflict;
   507→- debt, shame, secrecy, stigma, or solidarity;
   508→- an alternate cultivation path;
   509→- visible scars and changed embodiment;
   510→- environmental cleanup or restitution;
   511→- recovery as long-form play; and
   512→- new understanding purchased at a cost.
   513→
   514→Failure does not guarantee an interesting reward. Some losses are permanent. The design goal is causal dignity and continued agency, not universal consolation.
   515→
   516→### 8.3 Safety boundary
   517→
   518→Real-world “qigong deviation” has been used in medical and cultural contexts, including reports of distress associated with practice ([PubMed 8088187](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8088187/)). The game's fictional symptoms and remedies must not be framed as diagnoses or treatment instructions. Mental distress should never be reduced to possession, moral weakness, or a combat buff; literal spirits may exist in canon, but characters and systems must still distinguish spirit causation from trauma, illness, fraud, coercion, and energetic injury.
   519→

   522→## 9. Lifespan, aging, death, and continuity
   523→
   524→Historical Daoist materials contain multiple concepts of longevity, immortality, transcendence, ascent, celestial office, and transformed postmortem personhood rather than one invariant outcome ([Religious Daoism, “Immortality”](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2024/entries/daoism-religion/)). The project should preserve that plurality by splitting lifespan into different state questions.
   525→
   526→### 9.1 Required distinctions
   527→
   528→- **chronological age:** elapsed world time;
   529→- **bodily age/wear:** tissue and structure condition, not necessarily equal to appearance;
   530→- **appearance:** socially perceived age and presentation;
   531→- **vital reserve:** ability to heal, adapt, reproduce where relevant, and withstand strain;
   532→- **expected remaining life:** a probabilistic projection under current state and environment;
   533→- **fixed or contingent allotment:** only if a future fate/mandate system explicitly creates one;
   534→- **spirit continuity:** what persists if body function ends;
   535→- **identity continuity:** whether memory, intention, relationships, body, name, soul/spirit, causal history, and self-recognition persist;
   536→- **legal/social identity:** who institutions recognize after transformation or return; and
   537→- **dependency:** medicine, environment, worship, office, artifact, practice, body replacement, or other support required to continue.
   538→
   539→### 9.2 Longevity design rule
   540→
   541→Every longevity effect must say what it slows, repairs, replaces, transforms, externalizes, or makes irrelevant. “+500 years” is insufficient.
   542→
   543→Long life must change gameplay:
   544→
   545→- relationships age at different rates;
   546→- institutions remember, rewrite, or forget;
   547→- wealth, property, debt, office, and inheritance accumulate conflict;
   548→- language, custom, ecology, and political order change;
   549→- memory becomes selective, externalized, edited, or burdensome;
   550→- old techniques encounter new environments and counters; and
   551→- the cultivator must decide what continuity means when every supporting context changes.
   552→

   555→## 10. Environment and cultivation ecology
   556→
   557→Ambient qi cannot be a decorative density noise field. Every cultivation environment should arise from causal history.
   558→
   559→### 10.1 Environmental state
   560→
   561→- qi sources, sinks, flows, cycles, transformations, and residence times;
   562→- geological, hydrological, climatic, biological, spiritual, celestial, and constructed contributors;
   563→- seasonal and event-driven changes;
   564→- species and materials that consume, store, refine, poison, indicate, or redistribute qi;
   565→- settlements, mines, farms, temples, sects, formations, wars, graves, disasters, and industry;
   566→- local law climate or jurisdiction where future canon supports it;
   567→- contamination, depletion, recovery, succession, and restoration;
   568→- who measures the environment, by which model and instrument, with what error; and
   569→- access, ownership, stewardship, taboo, pilgrimage, extraction, and conflict.
   570→
   571→### 10.2 Site types are consequences, not random adjectives
   572→
   573→A “blessed cultivation cave” must have:
   574→
   575→1. a geological/spiritual/history cause;
   576→2. inputs and replenishment;
   577→3. an interior microclimate and qi behavior;
   578→4. species, materials, inhabitants, or absences explained by that behavior;
   579→5. discovery and use history;
   580→6. ownership and competing claims;
   581→7. maintenance, depletion, defense, and failure;
   582→8. sensory evidence at mortal and cultivated perception levels; and
   583→9. long-term consequences of exploitation or restoration.
   584→
   585→The generator may vary these causes and consequences. It may not merely combine “ancient + azure + ninefold + heaven cave.”

   589→## 11. Institutional ontology
   590→
   591→Cultivation organizations should exist because transformation is expensive, dangerous, knowledge-intensive, politically consequential, and materially situated.
   592→
   593→| Institution | What it can uniquely coordinate | Typical conflicts |
   594→|---|---|---|
   595→| **Household/clan** | Childhood, care, inheritance, labor, body knowledge, marriage, memory, local property. | Talent extraction, unequal investment, succession, duty, secrecy, generational harm. |
   596→| **Local teacher/apprenticeship** | Close correction, embodied craft, adaptive teaching, local ecology. | Dependence, limited corpus, personality conflict, fraud, succession. |
   597→| **Sect** | Curriculum, resource pooling, defense, archives, exams, territory, collective techniques. | Orthodoxy, rank, access, bureaucracy, faction, unsafe incentives, mission drift. |
   598→| **Lineage** | Authorization, method identity, transmission claims, ritual or technical legitimacy. | Broken chains, forged descent, rival heirs, adaptation versus purity. |
   599→| **Temple/ritual community** | Communal rites, spirits, ancestors, calendar, local mediation, charity, legitimacy. | Jurisdiction, patronage, doctrinal plurality, state pressure, local needs. |
   600→| **Monastery** | Long-duration communal discipline, education, archives, ritual, controlled life. | Withdrawal versus service, rule enforcement, property, gender/class access, reform. |
   601→| **Academy** | Comparative study, examination, public curriculum, standardization, research. | Credentialism, theory/practice split, state capture, unsafe experimentation. |
   602→| **State office/court** | Licensing, law, taxation, public works, registries, military coordination, disaster response. | Surveillance, monopoly, corruption, legitimacy, sect autonomy, imperial overreach. |
   603→| **Guild/market association** | Craft standards, supply, prices, insurance, provenance, apprenticeships. | Cartel behavior, counterfeits, scarcity, labor exploitation, dangerous substitution. |
   604→| **Hermitage/network** | Independence, experimentation, refuge, informal exchange. | Isolation, lack of correction, weak continuity, romanticized poverty, predation. |
   605→
   606→Institutional rank is not cultivation truth. A state license can be valid without proving wisdom; a sect elder can retain office after decline; an unknown hermit can be powerful but wrong; a fraudulent school can produce one effective technique; and an orthodox school can commit harm.

   610→## 12. Intelligent procedural generation contract
   611→
   612→The ontology should eventually drive generation through constraint, provenance, and consequence rather than random naming.
   613→
   614→### 12.1 Generated cultivation path record
   615→
   616→Every generated path must inherit or author:
   617→
   618→- a worldview and Dao orientation;
   619→- target transformation across the state domains;
   620→- practice composition and sequence;
   621→- compatible and incompatible bodies, roots, qi, and environments;
   622→- required teachers, texts, tools, materials, institutions, and time;
   623→- breakthrough contracts without assuming a universal ladder;
   624→- specific deviation and failure ecology;
   625→- maintenance and lifespan consequences;
   626→- combat/noncombat affordances, counters, signals, and traces;
   627→- social role, economic base, political history, and reputation;
   628→- adaptation history and internal disputes;
   629→- what its adherents believe incorrectly; and
   630→- what it cannot do.
   631→
   632→### 12.2 Anti-noise constraints
   633→
   634→A generated method is rejected if:
   635→
   636→- its name cannot be explained by history, imagery, practice, founder, location, or doctrine;
   637→- its effects do not follow from its operations and inputs;
   638→- its required environment has no causal world source;
   639→- its institution has no material reason to preserve it;
   640→- its breakthrough gives unrelated abilities;
   641→- its failures are generic “explosion/insanity/death” without mechanism;
   642→- it has no counter, cost, trace, maintenance, or incompatibility;
   643→- it merely reskins a famous copyrighted source's method, ladder, or signature motif;
   644→- it uses Chinese words as interchangeable exotic adjectives; or
   645→- it violates realm envelopes or launders external power into personal capability.
   646→
   647→### 12.3 Variation should occur along meaningful axes
   648→
   649→- transformation aim;
   650→- body topology;
   651→- qi source and conversion;
   652→- attention/emotional discipline;
   653→- Dao claim and proof;
   654→- environment and calendar;
   655→- teaching mode;
   656→- social contract;
   657→- resource economy;
   658→- failure behavior;
   659→- lifespan model;
   660→- combat and noncombat verbs;
   661→- institutional history; and
   662→- unresolved contradiction.
   663→
   664→The same generated seed should reproduce canonical truth. Characters may have different beliefs about that truth.

   672→- Can a seasoned xianxia reader read ordinary dialogue without glossary fatigue?
   673→- Does every retained Chinese term earn its place through established fluency or untranslatable precision?
   674→- Does every Hanzi/pinyin field identify an actual supported expression rather than a back-translation invented from English?
   675→- Are Qi Condensation, spiritual roots, and tribulation marked as genre conventions where their familiar mechanics are modern?
   676→- Are Dao, qi, heart-mind, spirit, lifespan, mandate, fate, luck, karma, law, and reputation kept distinct?
   677→- Can cultural reviewers see which tradition, period, or genre layer supports each claim?
   678→- Can localization replace an English genre convention without breaking canonical IDs or mechanics?
   679→
   680→### 13.2 Practice gauntlet
   681→
   682→- Remove the flavor text: do inputs, operations, costs, outputs, signals, and failures still form a coherent practice?
   683→- Give the same manual to different bodies, roots, teachers, and environments: do outcomes differ for reasons the world can explain?
   684→- Interrupt, mistranslate, counterfeit, overtrain, under-recover, and institutionalize the practice.
   685→- Test whether a charismatic fraud can fake early signs but not durable proof.
   686→- Test whether a competent teacher can identify uncertainty instead of possessing omniscient diagnostics.
   687→
   688→### 13.3 Breakthrough gauntlet
   689→
   690→- Attempt a transition with every prerequisite individually missing.
   691→- Supply enormous qi but insufficient body, control, understanding, or authority.
   692→- Substitute an artifact or formation and verify attribution remains external/prepared.
   693→- Interrupt every phase; test atomic costs and persistent partial states.
   694→- Simulate false success, unstable success, delayed failure, regression, alternate-path survival, and death.
   695→- Require social institutions to disagree plausibly about the same evidence.
   696→- Verify that no new ability appears unless the transition contract and realm envelope entail it.
   697→
   698→### 13.4 Deviation and recovery gauntlet
   699→
   700→- Distinguish structural injury, energetic dysregulation, contamination, trauma, literal spirit intrusion, fraud, and social accusation.
   701→- Ensure “inner demon” is not an automatic diagnosis or excuse for villainy.
   702→- Test rest, healer, teacher, counter-practice, environment change, resource, and time separately.
   703→- Preserve permanent consequences when appropriate; avoid total reset after one potion.
   704→- Test exploit loops that intentionally farm injury, contamination, tribulation, or recovery rewards.
   705→
   706→### 13.5 Lifespan and continuity gauntlet
   707→
   708→- Advance time by years, decades, centuries, and longer scales while households, languages, institutions, property, ecology, and memory change.
   709→- Separate youthful appearance from bodily integrity and actual remaining life.
   710→- Kill, copy, possess, reincarnate, transform, seal, and awaken the actor; require an explicit continuity ruling each time.
   711→- Test whether longevity collapses inheritance, labor, population, law, and politics; if so, make institutions respond.
   712→
   713→### 13.6 Environment and institution gauntlet
   714→
   715→- Extract from a cultivation site until depletion, then stop, restore, contaminate, monopolize, tax, and weaponize it.
   716→- Remove a sect's economic base and verify curriculum, recruitment, politics, and safety change.
   717→- Destroy or alter an archive and track downstream manual drift across generations.
   718→- Let rival institutions measure the same cultivator with different instruments and incentives.
   719→- Verify offscreen NPC cultivation uses the same laws, risks, resources, and elapsed time as player cultivation.
   720→
   721→### 13.7 Protagonist and scale gauntlet
   722→
   723→- Clone the protagonist's full canonical state into an NPC and require behavioral parity.
   724→- Remove the future protagonist advantage and verify only its declared contribution disappears.
   725→- Run the existing Qi Condensation mountain proof: personal output fails; prepared, environmental, allied, artifact, or external-authority success requires a complete ledger.
   726→- Verify that early exceptionalism creates meaningful possibility, not hidden late-realm force.

   782→## 16. Source and scope limitations
   783→
   784→1. **No exact critical edition cards yet.** The *Daodejing*, *Neiye*, *Zhuangzi*, medical classics, alchemical works, and later manuals require source-version records, exact passages, translators, and claim-level review before direct quotation or canonization.
   785→2. **Chinese Text Project access and translation caution.** Direct automated access was blocked during this pass, and the project atlas already notes that some site translations may be AI-generated. CText is being used as a discovery locator, not sole translation authority.
   786→3. **English-language scholarship bias.** This pass is weighted toward English-accessible reference works and open scholarship. Chinese-, Japanese-, and other-language specialist research is underrepresented.
   787→4. **Tradition imbalance.** Daoist and Daoism-adjacent material is better represented here than Buddhist, Confucian, local-religious, spirit-medium, medical, minority, frontier, and regional traditions. Those must receive separate expert tracks rather than being absorbed under “Daoism.”
   788→5. **Gender and body imbalance.** Most summarized traditions and modern cultivation conventions are male-default. Scholarship such as Yi-Li Wu's body history helps expose this problem but does not solve it. Women's practices, reproductive embodiment, queer perspectives, disability, aging, and non-normative bodies need dedicated research and cultural review.
   789→6. **Modern genre corpus is insufficient.** Academic studies establish broad conventions, not frequency tables or reader consensus for every term. Spiritual roots, Qi Condensation translations, dantian usage, tribulation mechanics, sect ranks, body/qi/spirit path separation, and deviation tropes need a licensed, multilingual, claim-coded corpus study.
   790→7. **Copyright boundary.** Modern novels and games were treated only at abstract comparative-pattern level. No project ladder, method, character, artifact, sect, scene, prose, or signature mechanic should be copied from them.
   791→8. **Medicine and safety.** Historical medical and cultivation categories are not modern clinical facts. In-world procedures must remain fictional and non-actionable; real distress needs appropriate safety framing.
   792→9. **Interpretive disagreement.** Stanford entries and modern monographs are authored arguments, not neutral consensus machines. Claims about *dao*, holism, qi, naturalism, and religious/philosophical boundaries need comparison among scholars.
   793→10. **Tribulation provenance remains unresolved.** Broader Chinese religious literature contains calamity, salvation, retribution, and eschatological concepts, but the familiar individualized lightning breakthrough is safest treated as a modern genre convention until a dedicated genealogy is completed.
   794→11. **No realm ladder conclusion.** Familiar sequences reported by genre scholarship are evidence of convention, not a recommendation. This draft intentionally does not name, order, or cap the project's realms.
   795→12. **No cultural review panel yet.** Before canon promotion, this work needs review by qualified readers of Chinese, historians of Chinese religions/medicine, contemporary Chinese xianxia readers, translators, and sensitivity reviewers. Disagreement should be recorded, not averaged away.
