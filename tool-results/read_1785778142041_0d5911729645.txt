   100→- Selection of who crosses tribulation. The courts do not choose. The cultivator's own boundary-pushing triggers tribulation; the courts observe, record, and (sometimes) intervene.
   101→- Appointment of Mahayana cultivators. There is no appointment. A Mahayana cultivator is a cultivator who has successfully authored a law; the courts recognize this after the fact or do not, at their political discretion. Their recognition has no bearing on whether the law holds.
   102→
   103→### 2.3 Hierarchy
   104→
   105→The courts are staffed exclusively by cultivators who crossed tribulation and chose to take office. There are no mortals in the courts; there are no Acquired cultivators in the courts. The lowest celestial official is a Tribulation Crossing cultivator; the highest are Mahayana cultivators who took office early in the courts' history and have not yet been removed.
   106→
   107→- **Minor officials (吏).** The lowest rank. Records-clerks, oath-registrars, road-keepers. Most newly-crossed cultivators who take office begin here. A minor official is, in power, a heavenly immortal; in authority, a clerk. This gap is a source of constant friction.
   108→- **Magistrates (判官).** Mid-rank. Adjudicators of disputes, administrators of regions within the courts' jurisdiction. A magistrate typically commands a staff of minor officials and has the authority to issue binding judgments within their assigned region.
   109→- **Ministers (卿).** Senior rank. Each minister heads a department — Records, Oaths, Adjudication, Roads, Defense, Foreign Affairs (with the rogues and the spirit wilds). Ministers sit on the Heavenly Court Council.
   110→- **The Heavenly Court Council (天庭議會).** The senior body. Composed of the ministers and a small number of senior advisors (ancestral spirits invited as observers, retired ministers, the occasional Mahayana cultivator who holds a non-office seat). The Council sets policy, ratifies appointments, and adjudicates the disputes the magistrates cannot.
   111→
   112→### 2.4 Politics
   113→
   114→The courts are a political institution, and the politics are the politics of extremely long-lived, extremely powerful, extremely invested beings. The factional structure is not left-right or good-evil; it is interest-based and coalition-based.
   115→
   116→Common factions include:
   117→
   118→- **The Lineage Bloc.** Ministers and magistrates who hold their office as a sinecure from their lineage's ancestral court. They vote in the lineage's interest. Their loyalty is to the ancestor first, the court second.
   119→- **The Reform Bloc.** Officials who believe the courts are corrupt and should be reformed from within. They are correct about the corruption; they are perpetually disappointed about the reform.
   120→- **The Conservative Bloc.** Officials who believe the courts are working as designed and any change is dangerous. They are not always wrong.
   121→- **The Rogue Outreach Bloc.** Officials who believe the courts should incorporate rogues rather than marginalize them. They are a small faction with disproportionate influence because every rogue incorporated is one less rogue on the wild paths.
   122→- **The Wild Suppression Bloc.** Officials who believe the spirit wilds should be brought under court jurisdiction by force. This faction has been advocating for the same campaign for several millennia and has been defeated, each time, by the cost.
   123→
   124→The slow corruption of power over millennia is the courts' central institutional failure mode. A minister who has held office for three thousand years is not the same person who took the office. They have been shaped by the office, by the qi-authority they have accumulated, by the dependencies and enmities of their position. They are still, in principle, a cultivator who could lose a tribulation and die; in practice, they have insulated themselves so thoroughly that the only threat to them is another minister. This is the courts' tragedy: the institution that was founded to administer lawful order has become an order of its own, and the order's first priority is its own perpetuation.
   125→
   126→### 2.5 What it feels like
   127→
   128→To enter the Courts of Heaven is to enter a bureaucracy that is also a court of law that is also a military encampment. The qi is dense and orderly; the maintained roads hum with the courts' enforcement authority; the minor officials move through the corridors with the slightly harried look of clerks everywhere. The grandeur is real and the corruption is real and they coexist without contradiction. A cultivator who grew up reading about Heaven as a paradise will, on their first visit, be disoriented by how familiar it feels. It is a government. It works like a government. It fails like a government.

   132→## 3. The Ancestral Courts (宗祠)
   133→
   134→### 3.1 What they are
   135→
   136→Every great lineage — every lineage that has produced at least one cultivator of Void Amalgamation or higher, in the lineage's recorded history — has an ancestral court in the Precelestial. The ancestral court is a persistent zone, anchored to the lineage's first great cultivator, in which the anchors of the lineage's dead great cultivators persist.
   137→
   138→This is not the same as the Courts of Heaven. The two institutions are sometimes allied, sometimes in conflict, and never identical. The courts of Heaven are a public, inter-lineage institution. The ancestral court is private to its lineage. The courts of Heaven have jurisdiction over the dead in general; the ancestral court has custody of its own.
   139→
   140→### 3.2 What persists
   141→
   142→When a great cultivator of the lineage dies — bodily death, with the anchor surviving — the anchor may, under specific conditions, migrate to the ancestral court and persist there. The conditions are: the cultivator was of sufficient standing (Void Amalgamation at minimum, more typically Spirit Severance or higher); the lineage's court still exists and has not been destroyed; the cultivator's anchor was not dispersed in the death; and the lineage's living cultivators of sufficient rank performed the proper retrieval rite within the bardo window (per 00_FOUNDATIONAL_DECISIONS §2).
   143→
   144→A persistent ancestor is not a ghost. They are the cultivator, in their anchor, in a substrate they can sustain. They retain their memories, their cultivation (in a transformed state suited to persistence without a body), and their interest in the lineage they founded or served. They are slower than they were in life — the Precelestial's time runs slow even against them, and an ancestral court's internal time can run slower still. Ancestor-time is measured in centuries the way mortal-time is measured in years.
   145→
   146→### 3.3 Consultation
   147→
   148→A Nascent Soul cultivator of the lineage may, through the lineage's proper rite, consult the ancestral court. The consultation is not free: it costs the cultivator qi, time, and (often) a specific offering the ancestor demands. The consultation is not guaranteed: the ancestor may refuse to speak, may speak in riddles, may speak to a different question than the one asked. The consultation is binding in a moral but not a lawful sense: the ancestor can give counsel, but the living cultivator remains sovereign over their own choices.
   149→
   150→Consultation is the lineage's most reliable access to deep strategic wisdom. It is also the lineage's most reliable source of generational conflict — the ancestor's counsel, given from a position of centuries of perspective, often conflicts with the living cultivator's understanding of their immediate situation.
   151→
   152→### 3.4 Intervention
   153→
   154→An ancestral spirit can intervene in the Acquired world. This is rare, costly, and consequential. The cost is paid in the ancestor's persistence-budget — the qi-authority that sustains them in the court. An intervention that exhausts the budget results in the ancestor's anchor dispersing, which is final death (寂滅). Most ancestors will not intervene except in the lineage's existential crisis, and many not even then.
   155→
   156→The forms of intervention include:
   157→
   158→- **Direct projection (顯靈).** The ancestor projects a partial manifestation into the Acquired world. This manifestation is far weaker than the ancestor was in life, but it is still a Spirit Severance or higher being, which is usually decisive.
   159→- **Blessing (賜福).** The ancestor invests a portion of their authority into a living descendant, temporarily elevating the descendant's capability. The elevation is real but the cost is paid by the ancestor.
   160→- **Curse (詛咒).** The ancestor invests authority against an enemy of the lineage. This is rarer than blessing and more expensive; it is also the form most likely to attract the courts' attention, as it can be construed as a heaven-sealed oath violation depending on the framing.
   161→
   162→### 3.5 Corruption
   163→
   164→A sufficiently old ancestor may become something other than what it was. This is the ancestral courts' central horror, and it is the reason consultation is not a guaranteed safe source of wisdom.
   165→
   166→The mechanism is this: an ancestral spirit is sustained by their persistence-budget, which is in turn sustained by the lineage's veneration, by the court's own qi-reservoir, and by the ancestor's own internal coherence. Over centuries, the internal coherence erodes. The ancestor's memories become less a record and more a story they tell themselves. The story drifts. The ancestor who was a great cultivator of the Cangli Riverlands becomes, over three thousand years, an idealized figure who only ever held the values the current story requires. Over five thousand years, the figure may become something the original ancestor would not have recognized — a being whose will is the lineage's, but whose will has drifted so far from any living human's that consultation becomes a kind of madness.
   167→
   168→The lineage's living cultivators often do not recognize when this has happened. The ancestor still speaks with the ancestor's voice; still gives counsel that sounds like counsel; still demands the offerings they have always demanded. The corruption is slow, and the corrupted ancestor is often more confident, more commanding, more present than the uncorrupted one. This is the trap.
   169→
   170→A corrupted ancestor cannot be removed by the lineage's living cultivators alone. Removing one requires either the courts of Heaven's intervention (which the courts are reluctant to provide, because it sets a precedent for court interference in lineage affairs) or the lineage's own Mahayana cultivator, if one exists, who can contest the ancestor's law directly.
   171→
   172→### 3.6 What it feels like
   173→
   174→To enter an ancestral court is to enter a room that is also a memory. The qi is the lineage's qi, condensed over centuries; the walls are the lineage's history, inscribed in law; the ancestors are present, in their anchors, speaking with voices that span millennia. The first time a cultivator of the lineage enters, they recognize the place — not from having been there, but from having grown up inside the lineage's stories. The recognition is comforting. The comfort is the trap.

   178→## 4. The Spirit Wilds (靈荒)
   179→
   180→### 4.1 What they are
   181→
   182→The Spirit Wilds are the Precelestial's unregulated zones. They are the regions where the law is not fixed — where the courts of Heaven have no jurisdiction, where no ancestral court's will holds, where the substrate-law of the Precelestial has not been inscribed with any specific pattern. They are the gaps between jurisdictions, and they are vast.
   183→
   184→The wilds are not empty. They are full, in fact, but full of things that should not exist by the rules of the Acquired world and do not exist by the rules of the Precelestial's institutions. They are the Precelestial's own interior, the part that has not been domesticated.
   185→
   186→### 4.2 Why cultivators go there
   187→
   188→Cultivators enter the Spirit Wilds for three reasons, in increasing order of danger:
   189→
   190→1. **Lost inheritances (遺澤).** The wilds contain the ruins of cultivators and lineages that did not survive. An inheritance — a practice, a treasure, a fragment of a law — may persist in the wilds for millennia after its author's death, sustained by the wild's own undifferentiated qi. Finding one is a matter of skill, luck, and tolerance for the wild's dangers.
   191→2. **Ancient beasts (古獸).** Beasts native to the wilds are sought for their cores, their body parts (which have properties no Acquired beast's parts have), and their companionship (rare; only possible for cultivators with the right disposition). Hunting a wild beast is one of the few ways to acquire materials for tribulation preparation that cannot be acquired through the courts.
   192→3. **Death.** Some cultivators enter the wilds to die. The wild's undifferentiated qi, in sufficient density, can disperse even a Tribulation Crossing cultivator's anchor. This is sometimes chosen — a cultivator who wishes to end their cultivation without leaving a persistent anchor — and sometimes involuntary, when a cultivator enters the wilds for inheritance or beast and is overmatched.
   193→
   194→### 4.3 The alien ecology
   195→
   196→The wilds' ecology does not follow the five-phase model. The five-phase model is an Acquired Stratum artifact; it describes how qi differentiates in the lower world. In the wilds, qi is undifferentiated, and beings native to the wilds have metabolisms that operate on undifferentiated qi directly.
   197→
   198→This means a wild beast does not have a phase-affinity in the Acquired sense. It cannot be said to be a "fire-phase beast" or a "water-phase beast." It is, instead, a being whose internal architecture is organized around a pattern that does not map onto the five phases at all. A cultivator who fights a wild beast using five-phase tactics is fighting at a disadvantage: their phase-targeted attacks do not interact with the beast's qi in the way they expect, and the beast's attacks do not have the phase-signatures the cultivator's defenses are tuned to resist.
   199→
   200→The wild's plants are similarly alien. Herbs that have no phase-affinity can be gathered, but their effects on an Acquired cultivator's body are unpredictable — sometimes beneficial in ways no Acquired herb can be, sometimes injurious in ways no Acquired herb would produce.
   201→
   202→The wild's weather is its own. Storms of undifferentiated qi move through the wilds in patterns that do not correspond to Acquired meteorology. A storm in the wilds is not wind and rain; it is a moving density of pre-differentiated potency that, on a cultivator caught in it, has effects ranging from accelerated cultivation to forced transformation to dispersion.
   203→
   204→### 4.4 What it feels like
   205→
   206→To enter the Spirit Wilds is to step off the road and into a place that does not care whether you are there. The qi does not feel hostile; it feels *indifferent*. The beasts do not feel malicious; they feel like themselves, doing what they do. The wilds are the only place in the cosmos where a cultivator can stand and know that no law is watching them — not the courts', not the ancestors', not the stratum's. This is, for some cultivators, the most liberating experience of their lives. It is also, for the same cultivators, the most dangerous.
   207→

   209→
   210→## 5. The Law Reaches (法域)
   211→
   212→### 5.1 What they are
   213→
   214→The Law Reaches are regions of the Precelestial where the law has decayed or not formed. They are deeper than the Spirit Wilds — the wilds are unregulated, but the substrate-law of the Precelestial still operates in them. In the reaches, even the substrate-law has thinned. They are the Precelestial's interior wounds.
   215→
   216→The reaches are the project's specified instance of the charter's `can:proposal:cosmology:unstable-law-reaches` concept: regions where stable law has decayed or not formed, possibly allowing world creation.
   217→
   218→### 5.2 Why they exist
   219→
   220→The Precelestial is finite, and its law was authored in historical time — partly by the Nameless Origin's generative function, partly by accumulated Mahayana authorship. The reaches are the regions where the authorship was thin, or where it has decayed through age, or where it was deliberately undone by some past conflict whose scars persist.
   221→
   222→A reach is not chaos. It is a region where the law is *pending* — where it could be authored, where it has not been. The substrate is present; the inscription is absent. A cultivator standing in a reach can feel the absence as a specific, perceptible condition.
   223→
   224→### 5.3 What they allow
   225→
   226→The reaches are the only regions in the cosmos where a Mahayana cultivator can author a law that *creates a new local substrate* — a new mini-cosmos, with its own internal law, distinct from the Acquired Stratum's law and the Precelestial's substrate-law. This is the closest the project's cosmology permits to "world creation." It is not the creation of a new stratum (forbidden by 00_FOUNDATIONAL_DECISIONS §1); it is the inscription of a new law in a region where no law yet holds, with the consequence that the region becomes a place with the cultivator's law as its own.
   227→
   228→The Mahayana cultivator's endgame, in the deepest sense, is to author laws in the reaches. This is the project's positive specification of what the Mahayana station is *for* — not infinite power, not apotheosis, but the slow, careful, consequential work of inscribing law in places where law has not yet been written.
   229→
   230→### 5.4 Why this is dangerous
   231→
   232→Authoring a law in a reach is dangerous in three ways.
   233→
   234→1. **Law-paradox.** A law that contradicts a deeper law fails, often catastrophically. The reaches are not exempt from the Nameless Origin's generative function; a cultivator who authors a law that conflicts with that function will have their law collapse, and the collapse will often kill them.
   235→2. **Reach-collapse.** A reach is not infinitely stable. A poorly authored law can destabilize the reach itself, causing it to collapse inward. A collapsing reach takes the cultivator with it.
   236→3. **Contest.** A reach with a newly authored law is a region of new authority. Other Mahayana cultivators may contest the law, may overturn it, may seize the reach for their own authorship. The cultivator who authored the law bears the consequences — including the consequence of being killed in the contest.
   237→
   238→### 5.5 What it feels like
   239→
   240→To enter a Law Reach is to enter a place where the silence is not the absence of sound but the absence of *law*. The qi is present, undifferentiated; the substrate is present; the inscription is not. A Mahayana cultivator standing in a reach and preparing to author a law feels, for the first time since their own tribulation, the vertiginous recognition that the world is not finished — that the law they have lived inside their entire life was authored, and that they are now about to author a law of their own. The vertigo is the verb. The law is the consequence.

   244→## 6. Tribulation (天劫)
   245→
   246→### 6.1 What tribulation is
   247→
   248→Tribulation is the Precelestial Stratum's lawful boundary-enforcement mechanism. It is the stratum's response when a cultivator violates or completes a boundary condition. It is not a moral judgment. It is not Heaven weighing the cultivator's virtue. It is not a punishment. It is a *test* — specifically, a test of whether the cultivator can sustain the new state they have entered or are about to enter.
   249→
   250→This is the project's hard commitment against the moralized tribulation trope. A wicked cultivator who stays within their realm's laws will not be tribulated. A righteous cultivator who pushes a boundary will. The tribulation does not care which one is the better person; it cares whether the boundary has been crossed.
   251→
   252→### 6.2 What triggers tribulation
   253→
   254→Tribulation is triggered by specific, lawful boundary conditions. The major triggers:
   255→
   256→1. **Crossing a major realm boundary.** Specifically: Core Formation (station 5), Nascent Soul (station 6), Spirit Severance (station 7), and Tribulation Crossing (station 9). These four transitions are the cultivator's major re-architectures of their own internal law; each is a boundary the Precelestial's enforcement system responds to. Foundation Establishment, Qi Condensation, and Qi Induction are not tribulation-triggering transitions; they are internal refinements that do not cross a stratum-relevant boundary.
   257→
   258→2. **Authoring a new law (Mahayana, station 10).** The act of inscribing a law in the Acquired Stratum (or in a Law Reach, see §5) is itself a boundary condition. The tribulation tests whether the cultivator's authored law is compatible with existing law (see §6.4 below).
   259→
   260→3. **Violating a cosmic boundary.** Two specific violations trigger tribulation independent of realm transition:
   261→   - **Killing a being above your realm.** A Qi Condensation cultivator who kills a Core Formation cultivator has crossed a boundary — not the cultivation boundary, but the lawful authority boundary that says a cultivator of station N cannot, by rights, end a cultivator of station N+1 or higher. The tribulation tests whether the killer can sustain the consequence of having done so. (This is not a moral judgment. A righteous Qi Condensation cultivator who kills a Core Formation cultivator in self-defense is tribulated the same as a wicked one who kills in aggression. The boundary that was crossed is the same.)
   262→   - **Breaking a heaven-sealed oath (天誓).** A heaven-sealed oath is registered with the courts (see §2.2). Breaking it triggers tribulation. The tribulation tests whether the cultivator can sustain the burden of having broken a law they themselves ratified.
   263→
   264→### 6.3 The forms
   265→
   266→Tribulation takes different forms depending on what it is testing. The four forms:
   267→
   268→1. **Lightning tribulation (雷劫).** The most common form. A storm of dense, phase-resonant lightning descends on the cultivator. The tribulation tests two things: the cultivator's qi reservoir (can they sustain the tribulation's energy load without exhausting their reserves) and their meridian integrity (can their internal architecture absorb and route the tribulation's energy without rupturing). The lightning is not random; it is structured to probe specific weaknesses in the cultivator's architecture. A cultivator with a flawed meridian in their right arm will find the tribulation's lightning disproportionately striking that arm. This is not malice; it is the test doing its job.
   269→
   270→2. **Heart demon tribulation (心魔劫).** Tests psychospiritual stability. The cultivator is confronted by their 心魔 — the persistent condition of unresolved attachment, obsession, or trauma that has accumulated through their cultivation (per 00_FOUNDATIONAL_DECISIONS §8). The confrontation is internal, but its effects are external — a cultivator in heart demon tribulation may appear to onlookers as if standing still, or wandering, or speaking to no one, while internally they are fighting for their coherence. The heart demon tribulation is the most feared of the four forms, because it cannot be prepared for by accumulating power. A cultivator with profound power and profound unresolved attachment is *more* likely to fail heart demon tribulation than a weaker cultivator with less attachment, because the demon has more material to work with.
   271→
   272→3. **Law tribulation (法劫).** Tests whether an authored law is compatible with existing law. Specific to Mahayana cultivators authoring a new law. The tribulation takes the form of the existing law resisting the new inscription — sometimes as a counter-storm of energy, sometimes as a paradox-induced collapse of the inscription itself, sometimes as the new law producing immediate and unintended consequences that the cultivator must survive. Law tribulation is the rarest form and the most studied; Mahayana cultivators preparing to author a law spend years in preparation, modeling the law's compatibility against every known existing law in its scope.
   273→
   274→4. **Karmic tribulation (業劫).** Tests the cultivator's karmic ledger. Conditional: this form only fires if the karma module is ratified (per 00_FOUNDATIONAL_DECISIONS §7, karma is an optional module that must be ratified or rejected before the protagonist reaches Core Formation). If ratified, a cultivator with significant karmic debt — accumulated through specific acts defined by the ratified karma system — faces karmic tribulation as a component of their realm-boundary tribulation. The tribulation tests whether the cultivator can sustain the weight of their karmic ledger in the new state; failure disperses the anchor (final death, 寂滅), which is also the lawful resolution of an unsustainable karmic ledger.
   275→
   276→A single tribulation event may include multiple forms. A Tribulation Crossing tribulation typically includes lightning, heart demon, and (if karma is ratified) karmic components. A Mahayana law-authorship tribulation includes law tribulation as its primary form, sometimes with lightning as a secondary component.
   277→
   278→### 6.4 Survival criteria
   279→
   280→The tribulation is not a damage event the cultivator must outlast. It is a proof the cultivator must demonstrate. The proof is: *can the new state sustain itself* — can the new golden core hold, can the new nascent soul act, can the new domain open, can the new law hold its scope.
   281→
   282→A cultivator who survives the tribulation's damage but does not demonstrate the proof has not survived the tribulation. They have survived a wave of it. The tribulation will return — typically within a year, sometimes sooner — and the return will be harder. This escalation continues until the cultivator either demonstrates the proof (the new state stabilizes) or fails to survive (the anchor disperses).
   283→
   284→This is the mechanism that makes tribulation non-farmable (see §6.6) and non-trivial. A cultivator cannot "tank" a tribulation and call it done. They must emerge from it with the new state actually integrated.
   285→
   286→### 6.5 The aftermath
   287→
   288→A survived tribulation leaves traces. The traces are consequential and persistent.
   289→
   290→- **Qi-residue (氣殘).** The site of a tribulation is saturated with the qi-signature of the tribulation itself, and with the qi-signature of the cultivator who survived it. This residue is readable by other cultivators (Core Formation and higher, per the protagonist's advantage specification in 00_FOUNDATIONAL_DECISIONS §5). A skilled reader can reconstruct the tribulation's form, the cultivator's architecture, and the new state's structure from the residue. This is a security vulnerability: a cultivator who has just survived a tribulation is, for a window of weeks to months, more legible to adversaries than at any other point in their cultivation.
   291→- **Local law changes (法變).** A survived tribulation, particularly a Spirit Severance domain-opening tribulation or a Mahayana law-authorship tribulation, can permanently alter the local law. The site where a domain was opened may thereafter carry a residue of that domain's law, even after the cultivator has left. The site where a law was authored is, by definition, the site of that law's inscription.
   292→- **Environmental alteration (地變).** A lightning tribulation of sufficient intensity may permanently alter the local qi-topography — turning a mundane hill into a spirit-veined peak, or vice versa. These alterations are the source of much of the Acquired Stratum's spirit-geography. Many of the great sacred mountains of the lower world began as the sites of past tribulations.
   293→
   294→### 6.6 Tribulation farming
   295→
   296→Tribulation farming is the exploit: a cultivator who deliberately triggers tribulations in order to harvest the tribulation's energy for their own cultivation. The tribulation's lightning, in particular, is a dense and phase-resonant energy source that, with the right preparation, can be partially absorbed rather than merely endured.
   297→
   298→Tribulation farming is criminal under most jurisdictions. The Courts of Heaven prosecute it (within their jurisdiction) as a form of unlawful boundary manipulation; the ancestral courts treat it as a lineage shame; the rogues treat it as a personal risk assessment. The legal basis for the courts' prosecution is that the tribulation system is a lawful mechanism of the Precelestial, and deliberate manipulation of it for personal energy gain is a form of obstruction — analogous to tampering with a court's enforcement process.
   299→
   300→The practical basis for the prohibition is that tribulation farming is dangerous in a way that deters most cultivators who try it. The tribulation escalates (per §6.4). A cultivator who triggers a tribulation to harvest its energy and survives without stabilizing the new state will face another, harder tribulation. The escalation does not plateau. The cultivator who farms tribulations is, in effect, taking out a loan against their own survival, with interest compounding until they either stop farming (and face the accumulated tribulation) or die. Most tribulation farmers die. The few who survive are, almost without exception, beings no one wants to be in the same room with.

   304→## 7. The Precelestial–Mortal Relationship
   305→
   306→### 7.1 The time-debt
   307→
   308→The 1:365 time ratio is the central fact of the Precelestial–Acquired relationship. One Precelestial day equals one Acquired year. The ratio is symmetric and lawful — it is not a perception effect, not an illusion, not negotiable. It is the substrate of the strata's separation.
   309→
   310→The ratio applies to any being in the Precelestial, regardless of their origin. A heavenly immortal who spends a month in the Precelestial returns to the Acquired world to find thirty years have passed. A mortal dragged into the Precelestial by some catastrophe (rare; usually fatal) and returned a week later finds seven years have passed. The ratio does not care about the being's nature; it cares about the stratum.
   311→
   312→### 7.2 The tragic engine
   313→
   314→This single fact is the genre's central tragic engine, made mechanical. Every visit to the Precelestial costs mortal-world time. A cultivator who retreats to the Precelestial to train for a month returns to find a mortal village aged thirty years — parents dead, siblings aged, children grown, the world they knew remade without them.
   315→
   316→This is not a bug; it is the design's specified consequence. The doctrine (00_FOUNDATIONAL_DECISIONS §1) names this as the genre's central dramatic engine, and this document inherits it as a hard constraint on Precelestial content: every Precelestial scene in the game must, by design, cost the player Acquired-world time. The cost must be visible, must be felt, and must be weighed against the benefit. A Precelestial training arc that the player enters without understanding the cost will, on their return, find the world they were trying to protect aged past recognition.
   317→
   318→### 7.3 The cultivator's calculation
   319→
   320→A cultivator planning a Precelestial visit must therefore make a calculation that has no good answer. The benefits of Precelestial training, consultation, or inheritance-seeking are real and often decisive. The costs — in mortal-world time, in relationships aged past relevance, in the world remade without them — are also real and often decisive. The cultivator who chooses to ascend to the Precelestial for a year of training returns to find three hundred and sixty-five years have passed. There is no version of this calculation that does not cost something irreplaceable.
   321→
   322→This is the engine of the genre's melancholy, and it is the design's specified tragic mechanism. It is also the design's specified constraint on power acceleration (per the doctrine's central tension in AGENTS.md Part 3): the Precelestial cannot be farmed for power without paying its cost, and the cost is the world the cultivator was trying to gain power to protect.
   323→
   324→### 7.4 The crossing of correspondence
   325→
   326→The ratio runs one direction. The Precelestial is slower than the Acquired. This means the Acquired cultivator who visits the Precelestial returns to a world that has moved on without them. It does not mean the Precelestial cultivator can intervene in the Acquired world on Acquired time — a Precelestial being acting in the Acquired world acts on Acquired time, and is bound by it for the duration of their presence. This is why ancestor interventions (per §3.4) are so costly: the ancestor, acting in the Acquired world, is operating on Acquired time and burning their persistence-budget on Acquired-time, not Precelestial-time.

   330→## 8. Ascension (昇仙)
   331→
   332→### 8.1 What ascension is
   333→
   334→Ascension — the act of crossing from the Acquired Stratum to the Precelestial — is the Tribulation Crossing station (station 9). It is the single most dangerous verb in cultivation. It is also the verb that defines the cultivator as a being of the Precelestial rather than the Acquired.
   335→
   336→The verb is, in essence, a proof. The cultivator has reached the peak of what the Acquired Stratum can sustain. They have built an internal architecture that, in principle, can sustain Precelestial qi. The tribulation is the proof that the architecture holds. If it holds, the cultivator ascends — their internal architecture is transformed, their qi-metabolism is re-tuned to undifferentiated qi, and they become a heavenly immortal (天仙). If it does not hold, the cultivator dies — their anchor disperses (final death, 寂滅), and there is no second attempt.
   337→
   338→There is no middle outcome. The cultivator either crosses or dies.
   339→
   340→### 8.2 Who attempts it
   341→
   342→The cultivators who reach station 9 are, without exception, beings who have spent centuries preparing. A Void Amalgamation cultivator (station 8) preparing for ascension has typically spent a thousand years or more in their bonded place, gradually re-tuning their internal architecture toward the Precelestial's substrate. They have consulted their lineage's ancestral court (per §3.3). They have studied the records of past ascensions (those that survived and those that did not). They have prepared their domain, their place-bond, their meridians, their heart-mind.
   343→
   344→Most of them still die.
   345→
   346→The ones who cross are the ones who were not just powerful but *matched* — the specific form of the tribulation happened to probe specific strengths of their specific architecture. The cultivator whose tribulation was light on heart demon components and heavy on lightning components, and whose architecture was lightning-strong and heart-demon-stable, crosses. The cultivator whose tribulation probed their specific weakness does not.
   347→
   348→This is the second tragedy of ascension (the first being the time-debt of any subsequent Precelestial residence): the crossing is partly a matter of fortune. A more powerful cultivator is more likely to cross than a less powerful one, but the gap is not as large as power alone would suggest. A cultivator can do everything right and still fail. This is the genre's "the heavens are indifferent to your merit" made mechanical.
   349→
   350→### 8.3 What changes
   351→
   352→A cultivator who crosses becomes a heavenly immortal (天仙). The transformation is comprehensive:
   353→
   354→- **Qi-metabolism.** The cultivator can now admit undifferentiated Precelestial qi and either let it pass through unchanged or, with effort, condense it into a specific phase for emission. They are no longer dependent on Acquired qi for sustenance.
   355→- **Stratum-access.** The cultivator can travel between strata (昇降). They can enter the Precelestial courts as a peer (朝天), not a supplicant. They can stand in the Spirit Wilds and the Law Reaches without being immediately crushed by the substrate.
   356→- **Political status.** The cultivator is now a being of the Precelestial, subject to its politics. They may take office in the courts (per §2.3), they may join their lineage's ancestral court as a persistent ancestor upon bodily death (per §3.2), they may go rogue (per §1.6). They are also, henceforth, legible to the courts' records and to the ancestral courts' attention in ways they were not before.
   357→- **Lawful accountability.** The cultivator's actions now affect both strata. A tribulation triggered by their actions in the Acquired world will be felt in the Precelestial; a law they author in a Law Reach will, eventually, propagate to the Acquired world's law-substrate. They are accountable to both strata's lawful processes.
   358→- **Time-debt.** The cultivator is now subject to the Precelestial's slow time. Every day they spend in the Precelestial is a year in the Acquired world. They cannot escape this by ascending; ascending makes it their permanent condition.
   359→
   360→### 8.4 What it feels like
   361→
   362→The tribulation gathers and the cultivator understands, for the first time, that the world is *judging* them — not morally, but lawfully, the way a river judges a dam. Either the dam is strong enough to hold the water, or the dam breaks. There is no negotiation. The crossing itself is not a fight; it is a proof. The cultivator does not defeat the tribulation; they *demonstrate* to it that their integrated system can sustain the Precelestial's density. If they can, the tribulation ends, and they are in the Precelestial, and they are a heavenly immortal. If they cannot, the tribulation ends, and they are dead.
   363→
   364→The cultivators who survive describe the moment of crossing as a transformation of the sense of time. The Acquired world's time, which they have lived inside their entire life, suddenly feels *fast* — a rushing river they have stepped out of. The Precelestial's time feels *thick* — a medium they can move through with weight and intention. They are, for the first time, in the world the laws come from. They are also, for the first time, accountable to that world's slow, fallible, beautiful, corrupt institutions. They are no longer a cultivator. They are an immortal. The difference is the difference between a being in the world and a being of it.

   368→## 9. What this document enables
   369→
   370→This document specifies the Precelestial Stratum and the tribulation system in enough detail that downstream political, social, endgame, and tragic-content design can proceed against it. Specifically:
   371→
   372→- **A Heaven that is an institution,** not a god. Plot and content can engage the courts as a fallible bureaucracy with factions, jurisdictional limits, and corruption. The courts can be wrong; the cultivator can be right; the conflict between them is the engine.
   373→- **Ancestral courts as lineage stakes.** Every great lineage has a Precelestial presence. Lineage politics extends into the upper stratum; lineage corruption extends into the upper stratum; lineage tragedy extends into the upper stratum.
   374→- **Spirit Wilds as alien content.** A region where the rules do not apply, where lost inheritances persist, where the ecology is genuinely strange, where the cultivator can go to find things that cannot be found anywhere else — and to die.
   375→- **Law Reaches as endgame.** The Mahayana cultivator's positive specification: not infinite power, but the slow, careful, consequential work of inscribing law where law has not been written.
   376→- **Tribulation as lawful proof.** A system that is fair (lawful, non-moral, predictable in its triggers) without being kind (escalating, often fatal, no second attempts at the crossing). The cultivator who understands the system can prepare for it; the cultivator who does not will be unprepared.
   377→- **The time-debt as tragic engine.** Every Precelestial scene costs Acquired-world time. The cost is the design's specified constraint on power acceleration and the genre's specified engine of melancholy.
   378→- **Ascension as proof, not apotheosis.** The crossing is a proof the cultivator demonstrates, not a reward they earn. Most who attempt it die. The ones who cross are transformed, accountable, and subject to the Precelestial's institutions for the remainder of their existence.
   379→
   380→The doctrine's test (AGENTS.md Part 3) is honored: every gate specified here is paired with what it unlocks. The courts unlock political content. The ancestral courts unlock lineage content. The wilds unlock exploration content. The reaches unlock endgame content. Tribulation unlocks the lawful boundary-enforcement that gives every realm transition its stakes. The time-debt unlocks the tragic engine. Ascension unlocks the upper stratum as a playable space.

   384→## 10. What this document does not decide
   385→
   386→- **The specific roster of factions within the courts.** The five blocs named in §2.4 are illustrative, not exhaustive. The specific factions, their leaders, their current disputes, and their membership will be specified in a later political-content document, generated against the seed.
   387→- **The specific grotto-heavens accessible from the Precelestial.** Grotto-heavens (per 00_FOUNDATIONAL_DECISIONS §1) exist within the Acquired Stratum; some are accessible from the Precelestial via the maintained roads. Their specific contents are procedural content, not a decision.
   388→- **The protagonist's specific Precelestial encounters.** The protagonist may reach Tribulation Crossing only at the late-game horizon, and may not reach Mahayana at all (per 00_FOUNDATIONAL_DECISIONS §3). The specific Precelestial scenes the protagonist will encounter are a content-design decision, not a cosmology decision.
   389→- **Karma.** Per 00_FOUNDATIONAL_DECISIONS §7, karma is an optional module that must be ratified or rejected before the protagonist reaches Core Formation. The karmic tribulation form (§6.3.4) is specified conditionally on ratification.
   390→- **The courts' founding history.** The courts were founded in historical time by cultivators who crossed and organized. The specific founding story, the founding cultivators, and the founding compromises are lore to be developed in a later document.
   391→
   392→These are deferred details, not deferred forks. The decisions this document makes — the cosmology of the Precelestial, the lawful character of tribulation, the institutional nature of the courts, the persistence and corruption of ancestral spirits, the alien ecology of the wilds, the world-creation potential of the reaches, the time-debt as tragic engine, ascension as proof — are committed. If they prove wrong in prototype, the prototype's evidence overrides this document, and this document is revised — not appended with a candidate alternative.
