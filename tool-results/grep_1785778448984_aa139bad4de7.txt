What they left unspecified was the congregation — the beings and forces that fill a mortal's actual religious life. The earth god in the small shrine at the village entrance. The river god who floods when offended. The ghosts that walk on the fifteenth of the seventh month. The fox-spirit in the mountain who seduces travelers. The demonic cultivator who harvests the dying. The underworld the dead are said to descend to. The dreams the ancestors send.
20:The folk religion of the Cangli Riverlands is not fantasy. The earth god is real. The river god is real. The ghosts of the unburied dead are real. The fox-spirit in the Cangwu foothills is real. The underworld is real. The dreams the ancestors send are real.
22:What is partial, what is myth, what is mortal projection, is the *interpretation*. The earth god is not a small bearded old man in a green robe who lives in the shrine — that is how mortal iconography depicts a being whose actual ontological category is "the consciousness of a spirit vein's surface trace." The underworld is not the ten courts of Yama with their mirror-pools and bridges of gold and silver — that is how Mahayana Buddhist popular eschatology depicts a bureaucratic processing center. The ancestors do not literally descend on the fifteenth of the seventh month — that is the calendar on which the boundary between the Mortal stratum and the bardo is thinnest, and on which a certain kind of perception is easiest.
32:A place-spirit is the **consciousness of a spirit vein's surface trace** when that trace is dense enough and venerated long enough. It is not a god, not a Precelestial official, not an ancestor, not a cultivator, not a beast, not a ghost. It is a sui generis ontological category: a self-sustaining qi-pattern that has acquired, through density and duration, the qualities of attention, memory, and will.
36:A place-spirit is, in the body/qi/anchor triad (24 §2.3), a being of **qi with a quasi-anchor**. It has no body in the mortal sense; its body is the place itself — the soil, the water, the stone, the boundary of its jurisdiction. Its qi is the vein's surface trace, concentrated and patterned. Its anchor is a *quasi*-anchor: a self-inscribed law-pattern that resembles a true anchor in persistence and uniqueness but lacks a true anchor's noncopyability and karmic-trace capacity. A place-spirit cannot be reincarnated; it cannot be possessed; it does not bear karma in the human sense. It can be destroyed, and when destroyed, it ends. The quasi-anchor is fragile in a way a true anchor is not.
56:- **Boundary enforcement.** A place-spirit can, within its jurisdiction, enforce a boundary against low-tier threats. A low-tier spirit beast (14 §2.2) that would range into the village is turned aside — not by force, but by the spirit's qi-pattern making the village's ambient qi feel *wrong-colored* to the beast, the way a smell makes a room unwelcoming. A wandering ghost (§3 below) is slowed, sometimes stopped, at the boundary. A Qi Condensation cultivator of malign intent feels, at the boundary, a faint pressure to turn back. None of these are hard barriers; a determined intruder of any tier above mortal passes through. The boundary is a modulation, not a wall.
62:The relationship to the Courts of Heaven (15 §2) is **independence without sovereignty**. A place-spirit is not a court official. The courts did not appoint it; the courts cannot promote, demote, transfer, or remove it. The courts' records (生死簿) do not register place-spirits; they are not dead mortals, they are not cultivators, they are not beasts. They are a category the courts recognize but do not administer.
108:The city god is, in personality, **bureaucratic and deputized**. Of the three named place-spirits here, it is the only one with a formal deputyship from the local court magistrate (a Tribulation Crossing cultivator in the courts' Records department, who administers the Jiangnan region from a Precelestial office). The deputyship requires the city god to register, in its own quasi-anchor's memory, every death within its jurisdiction — name, date, cause — and to relay the register to the courts' Records department via a periodic rite performed by the county's Daoist priest. This is the place-spirit's most important function for the courts: it is the **local recorder of the dead**, the first link in the chain by which the dead are processed (see §6 below). The city god does not adjudicate; it records. Its register is duplicated in the courts'生死簿; discrepancies between the two are investigated, and a city god caught falsifying its register is destroyed (its anchor-object shattered, its pattern unwound). This has happened twice in the Cangli County Seat's history, in both cases for bribery by lineages seeking to conceal a murder.
116:A ghost is an **anchor in extended bardo, anchored to a Mortal place by unresolved attachment**. It is the same ontological category as a living being's anchor (24 §2.2) — a self-inscribed law-pattern, noncopyable, karmic-trace-bearing — but in a state of disembodied persistence. A living being's anchor is integrated with a body and qi (the yin-yang triad of 24 §2.3); a ghost's anchor is severed from body and partially severed from qi, persisting in the bardo state (00 §2) past the typical window.
118:The bardo state is bounded. Per 00 §2, the bardo is "an intermediate state between death and rebirth that lasts a bounded, lawful time." The typical window is forty-nine days (the genre's traditional period, ratified here as the lawful default). Within that window, the anchor is processed — retrieved by a lineage rite (if the deceased was a great cultivator and the lineage is competent), retrieved by a sect rite (if the deceased was a sect member and the sect is motivated), or processed by the underworld (§6 below) for rebirth. A ghost is what happens when the anchor is **not** processed within the window. The anchor, at the window's end, has three lawful paths: rebirth (the default), final death (寂滅 — the anchor disperses, the being ends), or — if the anchor's karmic trace contains an attachment of sufficient strength — persistence in bardo, anchored to a Mortal place, becoming a ghost.
122:A ghost forms when three conditions converge at the bardo window's end.
124:1. **Unresolved attachment.** The deceased's anchor carries, in its karmic trace, an attachment of sufficient strength that the natural momentum toward rebirth is overcome. The attachment may be grief (for a child not yet grown), hatred (for a killer not yet punished), an oath (unfulfilled), or love (for a surviving spouse the dead cannot leave). Not every strong attachment produces a ghost; the lawful threshold is high. Most strong attachments produce only a slow rebirth (the anchor drawn to a rebirth that reflects the attachment — per 24 §2.1). Only the strongest attachments, in the most extreme cases, overcome the rebirth momentum entirely.
126:2. **An anchor-point in the Mortal stratum.** The attachment must be anchored to a specific place — the place where the attachment was formed, or the place where the attachment's object resides, or the place where the attachment was betrayed. A ghost cannot roam; it is bound to its anchor-point within a small radius (typically the place itself and a few hundred paces around it). The anchor-point is, in effect, the ghost's substitute for a body — a mortal-place qi-pattern that the disembodied anchor latches onto.
128:3. **Failure of normal processing.** The lineage's rites did not retrieve the anchor (no lineage, no rites, or rites improperly performed). The sect's rites did not retrieve it (no sect, or sect unaware). The underworld's processing did not retrieve it — usually because the death was not registered (a murder concealed, a death in a remote place, a death the city god's register missed). The bardo window closes with the anchor unprocessed; the attachment and the anchor-point hold it; the ghost forms.
132:The ghost's persistence is sustained by its attachment and its anchor-point. The attachment is the *reason* the ghost persists; the anchor-point is the *means*. The two together constitute the ghost's substitute for a body's metabolism.
134:- **Grief** anchors the ghost to the place where the griever lives. The ghost of a mother who died in childbirth may persist, anchored to the household where her child grows, until the child comes of age or the grief is resolved. Grief-ghosts are the most common and the least harmful; they watch, they sometimes nudge, they rarely manifest.
135:- **Hatred** anchors the ghost to the place where the hatred was incurred. The ghost of a murdered traveler persists at the crossroads where he was killed, anchored by the hatred for his killer. Hatred-ghosts are the most dangerous; they project their hatred onto any living being who enters the anchor-point, and they can, over years, accumulate enough qi-density to manifest visibly and to harm.
136:- **An oath** anchors the ghost to the place where the oath was sworn or the place where it was broken. The ghost of a sworn sibling who died before fulfilling the oath persists at the sworn place, anchored by the unfulfilled obligation. Oath-ghosts are the most active; they seek, sometimes across centuries, a living being who can fulfill the oath and release them.
137:- **Love** anchors the ghost to the place where the beloved resides. The ghost of a spouse who died before the other persists, anchored to the household, watching the surviving spouse age. Love-ghosts are the saddest; they rarely manifest, rarely nudge, almost never harm. They simply persist, watching, until the beloved dies and the attachment resolves through reunion in bardo (or, more commonly, the beloved's own rebirth, which severs the love-attachment and releases the ghost).
141:A ghost, anchored to its anchor-point and sustained by its attachment, can do a limited set of things, all of them qi-mediated modulations of the Mortal stratum.
143:- **Manifest as a qi-presence.** A ghost with sufficient qi-density (sustained by years of persistence and amplified by mortal fear, which is itself a qi-input) can manifest as a perceptible qi-presence — a coldness, a heaviness, a sound (footsteps, a voice, a name called), a shape (a figure, a face, a hand). The manifestation is not the ghost's body; the ghost has no body. The manifestation is the ghost's anchor's qi-projection, shaped by the ghost's will and colored by its attachment. A mortal perceives the manifestation as a haunting; a Qi Induction cultivator perceives it as a coherent qi-presence with a recognizable signature; a Qi Condensation cultivator can read the attachment directly in the signature's color.
145:- **Influence the heart-mind of the living.** A ghost can, by projecting its qi-presence into the qi-field of a sleeping or distracted mortal, influence the mortal's heart-mind (心). A grief-ghost projects sorrow; a hatred-ghost projects anger; an oath-ghost projects urgency; a love-ghost projects longing. The influence is subtle — a mood that seems to come from nowhere, a thought that seems the mortal's own. A mortal with strong heart-mind coherence (a Qi Condensation cultivator, a disciplined meditator, a person of profound stability) resists easily; a mortal with weak coherence (the exhausted, the grieving, the sick, the drunk) is susceptible. This is the mechanism behind the mortal world's "ghost-sickness" (14 §6.3) — a chronic low-grade malaise caused by a ghost's persistent influence on a susceptible mortal's heart-mind.
147:- **Possess the weak.** A ghost with sufficient qi-density and a susceptible mortal host can, in extreme cases, displace the mortal's own qi-coherence and take partial control of the mortal's body. This is **not** true possession (奪舍, per 00 §2) — true possession displaces the anchor; a ghost cannot displace an anchor, only suppress its expression through the body's qi. The mortal's anchor remains; the mortal's will is suppressed. A possessed mortal speaks with the ghost's voice, moves with the ghost's will, and acts out the ghost's attachment. The possession is exhausting for the ghost (it burns qi-density rapidly) and damaging for the mortal (the suppressed anchor, on release, often bears a karmic-trace scar). Possession is rare; it is the ghost's last resort, when no other modality of influence has produced the resolution the attachment seeks.
151:The distinction is critical and often confused in mortal usage. A **ghost** is an anchor in extended bardo in the Mortal stratum, anchored to a Mortal place by unresolved attachment. It is disembodied, place-bound, attachment-driven, and (usually) temporary — the attachment will eventually resolve (the griever dies, the killer is punished, the oath is fulfilled) and the ghost will move on to rebirth.
153:An **ancestor** is an anchor that has been retrieved from bardo and lifted into the Precelestial stratum (15 §3), sustained by the lineage's veneration and the ancestral court's qi-authority. It is disembodied in the same sense (no body), but it is not place-bound (it resides in the ancestral court, a Precelestial zone), it is not attachment-driven (it persists by the lineage's rite, not by an unresolved attachment), and it is intended to be permanent (it persists as long as the court and the veneration sustain it). The mortal world conflates the two — the village's "ancestors" include both the lineage-hall's venerated dead (some of whom may be true ancestors in a cultivation lineage, most of whom are merely remembered) and the graveyard's ghosts (some of whom may persist as actual ghosts, most of whom have long since reincarnated). The cosmology distinguishes sharply. A ghost is what happens when the normal system fails; an ancestor is what happens when the normal system succeeds at a high enough level.
157:A ghost is laid to rest by one of two methods.
159:1. **Resolve the attachment.** The grief-ghost is released when the griever dies, or when the griever performs a rite of farewell that the ghost perceives as release. The hatred-ghost is released when the killer is brought to justice (or killed, or forgiven — the ghost's attachment resolves at any of these, regardless of the moral valence). The oath-ghost is released when the oath is fulfilled (by the ghost's surviving kin, by a stranger who takes up the oath, by the ghost's own act through possession). The love-ghost is released when the beloved dies, or when the beloved formally releases the ghost (a rite performed at the grave, with a Daoist priest's assistance). When the attachment resolves, the ghost's anchor is freed from the anchor-point, the bardo closes around it, and it enters the rebirth path it should have entered at the original window's end.
161:2. **Forcibly disperse the anchor-fragment.** A Core Formation cultivator (the threshold of anchor-perception, per 00 §2) can perceive a ghost's anchor directly and can, with the right technique, disperse the anchor-fragment that constitutes the ghost's persistence in bardo. This is **not** final death (寂滅) in the full sense — the ghost's anchor was already severed from the body at the original death; what is dispersed is the fragment that persisted as the ghost. The dispersal ends the ghost; the being's karmic trace, already recorded in the courts' register, is not erased. The being does not reincarnate from this fragment; the fragment ends. Whether the being's broader anchor (the original law-pattern) persists in some other form is a question the cosmology does not answer; the cosmology specifies only that the ghost ends.
163:The first method is preferred by every tradition — Buddhist, Daoist, lineage, sect. The second is a last resort, used when the ghost is harmful and the attachment cannot be resolved (a hatred-ghost whose killer is dead and whose hatred persists; an oath-ghost whose oath is impossible to fulfill). The second is criminal under the courts' jurisdiction if performed without authorization — dispersing an anchor-fragment without first attempting resolution is treated as a form of unlawful killing, and a cultivator who does it carelessly faces tribulation (15 §6.2, "killing a being above your realm" can apply if the ghost's original being was of higher station than the cultivator). The Azure Sword Sect's punishment hall has a standing commission for ghost-dispersal in the Cangwu foothills, and the commission's rules require documented attempts at resolution before dispersal is authorized.
167:The Ghost Festival is the fifteenth day of the seventh lunar month. Mortal tradition says the gates of the underworld open on this day and the dead walk among the living. The metaphysical reality is more precise and more interesting.
169:The bardo — the intermediate state between death and rebirth — is not a place; it is a **condition** of an anchor, and the condition has a lawful relationship to the Mortal stratum's ambient qi. The bardo's "boundary" with the Mortal stratum is the threshold at which a disembodied anchor's qi-projection can manifest in Mortal-stratum qi. This threshold varies with the Mortal stratum's qi-climate (14 §5): highest at the summer solstice (yang-dominant, bardo suppressed), lowest at the winter solstice (yin-dominant, bardo amplified). It also varies with the lunar month and the diurnal cycle.
171:The fifteenth of the seventh month is, by long observation, the day on which the bardo-boundary is **annually thinnest** across the widest geographic range. It is not that the gates open; it is that the boundary is, on this day, thin enough that even weak ghosts (ghosts that cannot manifest on ordinary days) can manifest, and even strong ghosts can manifest more legibly than usual. The thinness lasts roughly three days, and during this window the mortal world's ambient qi is, in places where ghosts persist, threaded with bardo-residue that mortals perceive as the characteristic "ghost-festival feeling" (cold, attentive, watched).
173:The mortal rites of the festival — the offerings at the shrine, the lineage rite for unburied or unremembered dead, the burning of paper money and paper goods — are **functional**. The offerings are qi-inputs (the food's qi, the incense's qi, the paper-goods' burning-qi) directed at the bardo-boundary, where they are absorbed by whatever anchors are present. The lineage rite for unremembered dead is a controlled bardo-feeding: it provides enough qi-density to fading anchors (the unremembered, the unvenerated, the orphans) to allow them to either resolve their attachment (with the lineage's farewell) or to be processed by the underworld (which uses the festival's thinness as an efficient processing window — see §6 below). The burning of paper money is, metaphysically, the burning of qi-charged paper for the qi-release; the paper-money's "value" in the underworld is a mortal projection onto a real qi-input. The festival is **inauspicious for travel** because the bardo-boundary is thin everywhere, and a traveler moving through unfamiliar places encounters unfamiliar ghosts at near-full manifestation. The mortal advice to stay home is sound advice.
177:To a mortal, a ghost is felt as a chill, a heaviness, a sense of being watched, a mood that seems to come from outside. The mortal does not perceive the ghost as a being; they perceive the ghost's qi-influence on their own heart-mind. A mortal who has lived near a ghost for years develops, without knowing it, a sensitivity to that specific ghost's signature — they "know" when the ghost is active, the way a spouse knows when their partner is in a mood.
179:To a Qi Induction cultivator, a ghost is perceived as a **cold spot** in the ambient qi — a region where the qi is denser, slower, and yin-dominant in a way that does not match the local qi-climate. The cold spot has a shape (often the shape of the anchor-point — a room, a crossroads, a stretch of riverbank) and a movement (slow, often circular, often returning to a center). The cultivator can feel the ghost's presence but cannot read its attachment.
181:To a Qi Condensation cultivator, the ghost is legible as a coherent being — an anchor-fragment with a signature, an attachment, a will. The cultivator can read the attachment's type (grief, hatred, oath, love) in the signature's color, and can address the ghost directly. This is the conversation a Daoist priest believes he is having when performing an exorcism; the Qi Condensation cultivator is the first being who is actually having it. Most ghosts, addressed directly by a Qi Condensation cultivator, will speak (in qi-modulations the cultivator can read) about their attachment and what would resolve it. A cultivator who listens and acts on what they hear can lay a ghost to rest without combat. A cultivator who does not listen — who treats the ghost as a thing to be dispersed — must resort to the second method (§3.6), which is harder, slower, and riskier.
183:To a Core Formation cultivator, the ghost is fully legible — the anchor-fragment perceived directly, its coherence read, its attachment read, its history (the being's life, death, and persistence) readable in the karmic trace. The Core Formation cultivator can also perceive the ghost's relationship to the courts' register — whether the death was recorded, whether the ghost is known to the underworld, whether dispersal would be lawful. A Core Formation cultivator who disperses a ghost without consulting the register risks the courts' attention.
193:This distinguishes the yao sharply from a spirit beast. Per 14 §2, a spirit beast is an animal that has absorbed ambient qi and developed a crude internal circulation. The four tiers (low, mid, high, peak) describe the depth of that qi-cultivation. But a spirit beast, even at peak tier, has only body and qi — it has no anchor. Its persistence is the persistence of an animal; its death is the death of an animal; it does not bear karmic trace; it does not reincarnate. A yao is what happens when a peak-tier spirit beast's inner core (內丹, per 14 §2.2) — the proto-anchor that the peak-tier beast begins to form — **completes its inscription** and becomes a true anchor. This is rare. Most peak-tier beasts never complete the transition; their inner core remains a proto-anchor, a dense qi-structure that resembles an anchor but lacks an anchor's noncopyability, karmic-trace capacity, and persistence across death. The beast dies; the proto-anchor disperses; the being ends.
195:The peak-tier beast that completes the transition becomes a yao. The yao has, in addition to its animal body and qi, a true anchor — and with the anchor comes everything the anchor implies: karmic trace, persistence across death, the bardo state, the possibility of rebirth, the possibility of final death (寂滅). The yao is, in this sense, the only nonhuman being in the Mortal stratum that is **metaphysically equivalent to a human**. It is not human; it does not have a human body or human cognition or human culture. But it has an anchor, and the anchor is the seat of personhood in this cosmology. This is why the genre treats yao as persons — fox-spirits who seduce, snake-demons who scheme, tree-spirits who protect groves — and not as beasts. The cosmology agrees. A yao is a person, in the metaphysical sense, with an animal body and an animal's ecology. The moral and lawful implications are significant: a yao can be wronged (its anchor can be dispersed unlawfully); a yao can bear karma (its acts inscribe trace on its anchor); a yao can be saved (its anchor can be retrieved from bardo by a lineage or sect rite, if the yao had such ties); a yao can be reborn.
236:The mortal world's tradition of fox-spirits as seducers and tricksters is a partial truth derived from cases like Lady Bai's: yao-foxes do take human form, do walk among humans, do occasionally form attachments. The tradition's elaboration — that fox-spirits steal men's semen to fuel their cultivation, that they are uniformly malicious, that they can be exorcised by Daoist priests — is mortal projection. Lady Bai's cultivation is sustained by the wood-phase vein and her own practice, not by any human substance. She is not malicious. A Daoist priest's exorcism has no effect on her (she has an anchor; an exorcism designed to disperse a ghost's anchor-fragment does not work on a true anchor integrated with a living body). The Azure Sword Sect knows of her: the previous sect master (who pursued Zhou Wugui, §5.4 below) consulted her once, seventy years ago, on the question of whether a particular Cangwu foothills spirit beast was a yao or not (it was not; he let it live). The current sect master, Leng Wushuang, has not contacted her. The Cangwu Sect (the small local sect, 24 §4.3) treats her as a known presence in their territory and leaves her alone, because she is more powerful than their sect master and because she has never caused them trouble. The mortal world mostly does not know she exists; the few who have met her in human form remember a young woman from the market town whom they cannot quite place.
240:The snake-demon of the Cangwu foothills is named, in the local tradition, the Black Creek Serpent — a name the yao itself does not use (it does not speak, having never developed the capacity, and does not take human form). It is a king cobra (Ophiophagus hannah) that achieved peak-tier spirit beast status roughly six hundred years ago in the western Cangwu, over a water-phase vein. It attempted the anchor-transition three hundred years ago and survived — barely — a heart-demon tribulation whose central attachment was the memory of a predator (a Cangwu Sect Core Formation cultivator, two centuries dead, who had killed its first mate). The yao's anchor is true; its cognition is not. The transition completed the anchor but did not complete the cognitive development that usually accompanies it.
276:The evil is metaphysical, not moral. The cosmology's anchor (24 §2.2) is noncopyable; it is the being's persistent law-pattern; it is what makes the being a being rather than a process. Final death (寂滅, per 00 §2) is the dispersion of the anchor — the one true end, the unrecoverable. The cosmos permits final death in specific lawful circumstances: high-realm conflict, tribulation failure, the natural expiry of the bardo window. These are lawful because they are either the being's own act (the cultivator who pushes a tribulation past their capacity), a contest between peers, or the cosmos's own boundary-enforcement.
278:The demonic cultivator's act is **the deliberate dispersion of another being's anchor for the cultivator's own power**. This is the cosmology's one true evil because it is the one act that produces **irreversible metaphysical destruction** for the cultivator's benefit. Killing a mortal inscribes a karmic trace (24 §2.1), but the mortal's anchor persists in bardo and enters rebirth — the being continues. Killing a cultivator inscribes a heavier trace, but the cultivator's anchor can be retrieved, can persist in an ancestral court, can be reborn. Even unlawful killing leaves the anchor intact; the being's persistence is not destroyed, only the body is. The demonic cultivator destroys the persistence: the harvested anchor's law-pattern is unwound, its karmic trace is erased, the being's continuity is ended. There is no rebirth from a dispersed anchor. There is no ancestral court. There is no future life, no future meeting, no future redemption. The being is gone, finally and irreversibly, and the cultivator has done it for power.
284:The demonic cultivator's technique is the harvesting and refining of anchors. The specifics vary by lineage and innovation, but the general form is constant.
286:1. **Harvest.** The demonic cultivator seeks a being whose anchor is accessible — a dying cultivator (the anchor is in bardo, easy to perceive and grasp), a freshly dead mortal (the anchor is in the bardo window, still coherent), a ghost (the anchor-fragment is already disembodied and persistent), a yao (the anchor is in a body, but the body can be killed). The harvest is a qi-technique that grasps the anchor in bardo and draws it into the cultivator's own qi-reservoir. The act is, for the victim, final death — the anchor is removed from the bardo and prevented from entering rebirth.
288:2. **Refine.** The harvested anchor is, in the cultivator's qi-reservoir, a foreign law-pattern — noncopyable, coherent, hostile to the cultivator's own anchor. The cultivator cannot integrate it; the cosmos's noncopyability forbids that. The cultivator can, however, **unwind** it — break the foreign anchor's law-pattern into raw law-substrate, which the cultivator's own qi can then absorb. The unwinding is the act of dispersion; the foreign anchor's coherence is destroyed, its karmic trace is erased, the being's persistence ends. The raw law-substrate is metabolized by the cultivator's qi as a dense, undifferentiated qi-input, accelerating the cultivator's cultivation.
290:3. **Burn.** The cultivator's cultivation, fed by the refined anchor, advances. The acceleration is significant — a single harvested anchor can advance a Qi Condensation cultivator to Foundation Establishment, or a Foundation Establishment cultivator toward Core Formation. The acceleration is also corrupting — the refined anchor's residue (the karmic trace, which the unwinding does not fully erase but rather disperses into the cultivator's own qi) accumulates in the cultivator's heart-mind, producing 心魔 (24 §1.2, the delusional conviction) at minimum, and often the full psychospiritual break. The demonic cultivator's career is short; the cultivator either advances past their capacity to integrate the accumulated residue (and deviates, often lethally) or is killed by other cultivators (see §5.3).
296:- **Under the Courts of Heaven.** The courts' Records department (15 §2.2) maintains the register of the dead. A demonic cultivator's harvest removes anchors from the bardo without the courts' processing, falsifying the register (the dead are recorded as dead, but their anchors' disposition is unknown). This is record-tampering, the courts' most-prosecuted internal crime; when committed by an Acquired cultivator against the dead, it is treated as a more serious offense — a form of contempt of court that triggers the courts' enforcement attention. The courts' Defense department (15 §2.3) maintains a standing commission for demonic-cultivator suppression, and a demonic cultivator who reaches the courts' attention can expect a Tribulation Crossing cultivator to be dispatched.
302:- **Under lineage law.** A lineage that discovers a demonic cultivator in its membership expels the cultivator and erases their name from the genealogy. The expelled cultivator's anchor, on their eventual death, is not retrieved by the lineage's rite; the cultivator is, in the lineage's eyes, already metaphysically dead. A lineage that harbors a demonic cultivator knowingly is subject to alliance sanction and, in extreme cases, to extermination by a coalition of neighboring lineages.
304:- **Under karma.** The harvested anchor's karmic trace, dispersed into the cultivator's own qi, accumulates as the heaviest possible karmic trace on the cultivator's anchor. The cultivator's karmic ledger (24 §2.1) becomes, within a few harvests, unsustainable. At the cultivator's next realm-boundary tribulation (15 §6.3 form 4), the karmic tribulation is overwhelming; failure disperses the cultivator's own anchor (final death, 寂滅), which is the cosmos's lawful resolution of an unsustainable ledger. A demonic cultivator who reaches Core Formation is rare; one who reaches Nascent Soul is rarer still; one who reaches Spirit Severance is legend, and the legend always ends in karmic tribulation and dispersal. The cosmos's karma system is, in this sense, the ultimate enforcement against demonic cultivation: the demonic cultivator's power is borrowed against a karmic debt the cosmos will eventually call.

The Azure Sword sect master visited the three villages, perceived (at Core Formation) the qi-residue of anchor-harvesting at the death-sites, and pursued.
316:The pursuit lasted three months. Zhou Wugui, by the time of the pursuit, had advanced from Qi Condensation to late Foundation Establishment — an extraordinary acceleration, sustained by the harvested anchors of forty-one mortals. He had also, by the pursuit's end, accumulated enough karmic residue to be visibly unstable: his qi-signature was mottled, his heart-mind was fragmented, he had begun speaking to the dead (whose residue he carried) as if they were present. He was, at the moment of the Azure Sword sect master's confrontation, in a Foundation Establishment state that should have been coherent but was not.
318:The confrontation took place in the western Cangwu, in a ravine that the locals still call the Demon's Cut. The Azure Sword sect master, by his own account (recorded in the sect's punishment hall archives), offered Zhou Wugui the chance to surrender for trial. Zhou Wugui refused. The fight lasted, by the sect master's account, "less time than I expected." Zhou Wugui's anchor was dispersed in the fight — not by the sect master's technique (which would have killed the body but left the anchor in bardo), but by the cultivator's own karmic tribulation, which fired during the fight (the cultivator's accumulated karmic debt, amplified by the combat's stress, triggered the tribulation, which he could not survive). The sect master perceived the anchor disperse; the cultivator ended; the incursion was over.
320:The Azure Sword sect master returned to the three villages, performed the proper rites for the dead (the lineage rites the villages had not been able to afford; the sect paid for them from its own stores), and ordered the Cangwu Sect to maintain a standing watch in the foothills — a watch maintained, with varying diligence, for ninety-five years. The event is still spoken of in the market town, in the form the mortal tradition prefers — a story of a demon who came down from the mountains and was slain by a wandering hero. The metaphysical reality — that a middling Qi Condensation cultivator destroyed forty-one mortals' persistence for eighteen months of acceleration, and that the cosmos's own karmic enforcement ended him — is not the story the mortal world tells. The story the cultivation world tells is more precise, and more cautionary.
326:This makes the demonic cultivator a useful antagonist for three reasons. First, the demonic cultivator's guilt is not in question; the cosmology has decided it. A protagonist who pursues one is enforcing a metaphysical judgment, not making a moral one. Second, the demonic cultivator's power is real but unstable; the protagonist who faces one faces an antagonist already on a countdown to karmic tribulation. The protagonist does not need to outpower the demonic cultivator; they need to survive long enough for the cosmos's enforcement to fire. Third, the demonic cultivator's harvest is irreversible; the protagonist who arrives too late cannot undo the harm. The protagonist's failure is measured in dispersed anchors, not in personal defeat.
332:To a mortal, a demonic cultivator is indistinguishable from any other cultivator — perhaps a trifle cold, perhaps with a faint wrongness to the air around them, but not perceptibly different. A mortal who has lived near a demonic cultivator's harvest-site may perceive the site as "haunted" (a cold, heavy, attended-to quality that is in fact the qi-residue of dispersed anchors), but they do not perceive the cultivator.
338:To a Core Formation cultivator, the demonic cultivator's anchor is fully legible, and the karmic trace on it is overwhelming — the harvested anchors' dispersed karmic residue is visible as a heavy, mottled, anguished weight on the cultivator's own anchor. A Core Formation cultivator perceiving a demonic cultivator for the first time typically reports a visceral reaction — grief, anger, nausea — that is the perception of the dispersed beings' residue, not the perceiver's own emotion. This reaction is itself a perceptual datum; the cultivator who has it is perceiving, at the heart-mind level, the metaphysical wrongness of the demonic cultivator's act. The cultivator who does not have it — who perceives the demonic cultivator without grief or anger — is themselves suspect, and the sects watch such cultivators carefully.
342:## 6. The Underworld (地府)
346:The Underworld (地府) is **a sub-jurisdiction of the Courts of Heaven**. It is not a separate stratum; it is not a separate institution; it is not a separate cosmology. It is the Records department's enforcement arm — the part of the courts' apparatus that processes anchors in bardo for rebirth.
348:The mortal world's tradition of the Underworld — the ten courts of Yama (十殿閻羅), the mirror-pools of reflection, the bridges of gold and silver, the eighteen hells of punishment, the wheel of rebirth — is **partial myth**. The beings the tradition names (Yama, the ten kings, the demonic punishers) are mortal projections onto a bureaucratic process. The places the tradition describes (the halls of mirror-reflection, the punishment-hells, the rebirth-bridge) are mortal interpretations of processing-stages whose actual nature is more mundane.
350:The Underworld is, in actuality, **a vast, dim, orderly archive** where anchors in bardo wait to be processed. It is staffed by courts-appointed officials — Tribulation Crossing cultivators who took office in the Records department (15 §2.3) and were assigned to the Underworld's processing arm. There is no Yama. There is no Buddhist-influenced ten-kings hierarchy. There are clerks, magistrates, and ministers, the same hierarchy as the rest of the courts, doing the specific work of bardo-processing.
354:The Underworld is located in the Precelestial Stratum, in a region administered by the courts but physically separate from the courts' main territory. It is accessed (by cultivators who have lawful business there) through a specific maintained road that descends from the courts' main territory into a lower, quieter, dimmer region of the Precelestial.
356:The first impression is **dimness**. The Precelestial's general quality of "thick" time (15 §1.7) is here amplified; the air feels heavy, the light is diffuse and unsaturated, the qi is dense but slow. The Underworld's qi-climate is yin-dominant — cold, still, condensing — which is the appropriate climate for bardo-processing (bardo is a yin-state; the anchors in bardo are sustained by yin-qi).
358:The second impression is **order**. The Underworld is laid out as a vast archive, with corridors of shelves, each shelf holding tablets of jade (the medium the courts use for permanent records). The tablets record every anchor currently in processing — the anchor's karmic trace, the bardo window's remaining time, the rebirth-path being calculated. The shelves extend in every direction; the archive's scale is, to a mortal cultivator's perception, effectively infinite (it is not; the cosmos is finite, per 00 §1; but the archive is vast enough that its finite extent is not perceivable from any single vantage).
360:The third impression is **quiet**. The anchors in bardo are present, but they do not speak; they wait. The officials move through the corridors with the unhurried purpose of clerks everywhere. The processing itself — the reading of karmic trace, the calculation of rebirth-path, the assignment — is performed in silence or in low, formal speech. The Underworld is not the fiery, screaming hell of popular imagination; it is an archive, and archives are quiet. The mortal tradition's eighteen hells are mortal projection onto the karmic-trace-reading process — the anchor, in processing, is confronted with its own karmic ledger, which the mortal imagination (informed by mortal guilt) projects as a punishment. The processing is not a punishment; it is a reading. The anchor's karmic trace is read, the rebirth-path is calculated (an anchor with heavy killing-trace is drawn to rebirths that reflect the entanglement, per 24 §2.1 — this is not punishment, it is causal momentum), and the anchor is assigned. The mortal tradition's "punishment" is the anchor's own karmic momentum, perceived (by the anchor, in the bardo's heightened self-perception) as a confrontation with the being's own acts. The Underworld does not punish; it processes. The processing can be painful (confronting one's own karmic ledger is not pleasant), but the pain is the anchor's own, not the Underworld's.
364:The Underworld's processing of an anchor in bardo follows a fixed sequence.
366:1. **Registration.** The anchor, on the death of the body, enters bardo. The local place-spirit's register (the city god's register, §2.9 above) records the death and relays it (via the periodic rite) to the courts' Records department. The Records department assigns the anchor a processing-clerk, who locates the anchor in bardo (a nontrivial task; bardo is not a place but a condition, and locating a specific anchor requires a Tribulation Crossing cultivator's perception) and brings it into the Underworld's archive.
368:2. **Karmic reading.** The processing-clerk reads the anchor's karmic trace — every act that affected another being's anchor, every oath, every debt, every healing, every harm. The reading is performed by the clerk's perception of the anchor's law-pattern, which carries the trace as inscriptions upon itself (24 §2.2). Nothing is omitted. The anchor, in this stage, is present and perceiving — the bardo-state permits a heightened self-perception that the mortal tradition remembers as the "mirror-pool of reflection." The anchor confronts its own karmic ledger in full, which is (for an anchor with heavy trace) the closest the cosmology comes to the mortal tradition's "punishment."
372:4. **Assignment.** The anchor is assigned to its rebirth-path. The assignment is recorded in the courts' register; the anchor is escorted (by a minor official) to the boundary between bardo and the Mortal stratum, where it enters the rebirth-path and is drawn to its next embodiment — typically a mortal birth (most anchors return to the Mortal stratum); rare assignments are to animal rebirths (for anchors whose karmic momentum is best expressed in animal form) or, rarer still, to Acquired-stratum rebirths (for anchors whose cultivation-state and karmic momentum qualify them for a cultivator's rebirth).
374:5. **Special cases.** Some anchors are not assigned to rebirth. An anchor retrieved by a lineage rite (for a great cultivator's persistence in an ancestral court, per 15 §3.2) is released to the lineage's retrieval before processing. An anchor retrieved by a sect rite is similarly released. An anchor whose karmic ledger is unsustainable (the heaviest possible trace, the residue of acts the cosmos cannot process into a rebirth-path) is dispersed — final death, 寂滅 — as the lawful resolution. An anchor that has become a ghost (§3 above) is, in principle, retrieved by the Underworld's enforcement arm and processed; in practice, many ghosts persist long enough to be processed only by resolution of their attachment (§3.6) rather than by Underworld retrieval.
378:The mortal tradition's Underworld is **partially true**. The dead do go somewhere — the anchors in bardo are processed by the Underworld. The processing does involve a confrontation with one's karmic ledger — the mirror-pool of reflection is a mortal image of the karmic-reading stage. The processing does result in rebirth — the wheel of rebirth is a mortal image of the rebirth-path assignment. The mortal tradition's general shape — death, judgment, rebirth — is correct.
380:The mortal tradition's specifics are projection. There is no Yama; the ten kings are mortal imaginations of the Underworld's bureaucratic hierarchy. There are no eighteen punishment-hells; the "punishment" is the anchor's own karmic confrontation, not an external torment. There are no bridges of gold and silver; the rebirth-path is a calculated assignment, not a chosen crossing. The mortal tradition's elaboration is mortal culture's interpretation of a process the mortal tradition has not directly perceived.
382:The mortal tradition's purpose — to give the dead a comprehensible fate, to give the living a moral framework, to give the community a ritual structure for mourning — is real and valuable. The Underworld does not require the mortal tradition to be metaphysically accurate; it requires only that the mortal tradition's practices (the funerals, the offerings, the lineage rites) continue to feed the place-spirits and the lineages and the courts' record-keeping. The machinery does not care whether the mortals understand it correctly; it cares whether the mortals perform the rites.
384:A Core Formation cultivator who visits the Underworld (a rare event, requiring the courts' authorization) typically reports a sense of recognition — the archive resembles, in its structure and its quiet, every library and lineage hall and magistrate's office the cultivator has ever known. The Underworld is the cosmos's acknowledgment that even the dead require a bureaucracy: it is not magical; it is lawful, and law requires administration, and administration requires clerks, and clerks require quiet archives.
388:The Underworld is not directly perceptible to mortals or to most cultivators. A mortal who has attended a funeral at which a Core Formation cultivator is performing the soul-rite may, in the moment of the rite's climax, perceive a faint coldness and a faint attentiveness — the trace of the Underworld's processing, mediated by the cultivator's qi. This perception is fleeting and usually mistaken for grief.
390:A Qi Condensation cultivator performing a funeral rite perceives, in the rite's climax, a thinning of the boundary between the Mortal stratum and the bardo — the rite's purpose is to thin this boundary, allowing the deceased's anchor to be processed more cleanly. The cultivator perceives the thinning as a cold, attentive quality to the air, and may (if sensitive) perceive a faint presence — the processing-clerk, dispatched from the Underworld to receive the anchor, perceived as a vague, formal, attentive qi-presence.
392:A Core Formation cultivator performing a soul-rite perceives the processing-clerk directly — a Tribulation Crossing cultivator in the Records department's processing arm, manifesting at the rite's site as a qi-presence with the cold, formal, attentive quality of the Underworld's archive. The Core Formation cultivator can address the clerk (in qi-modulations the clerk can read), confirm the deceased's identity and karmic state, and release the anchor to the clerk's custody. This is the conversation the mortal tradition's "soul-guiding" describes; the Core Formation cultivator is the first being who can actually have it. A Nascent Soul cultivator with the courts' authorization may visit the Underworld directly, walking the maintained road down into the archive. The visit is, by all accounts, profoundly affecting — the scale of the archive, the quiet of the processing, the weight of every anchor's karmic ledger being read in the dim light. The Azure Sword Sect's Leng Wushuang is reported (in the sect's archives, by the previous sect master's account) to have visited the Underworld once, two centuries ago, and to have described the visit as "the most useful day I have spent in three hundred years of cultivation."
408:A dream's content is, in this cosmology, a perception of real metaphysical phenomena. The content's apparent absurdity — the strange imagery, the impossible geography, the heightened emotion — is the anchor's perception of phenomena the body cannot perceive, experienced through the body's cognitive apparatus (which is the apparatus the being's consciousness uses to interpret perception). The absurdity is the translation gap: the anchor perceives qi-residue, ambient qi, bardo-presence; the body's cognitive apparatus, designed for sensory perception, interprets these as sensory imagery. The interpretation is imperfect; the perception is real.
412:- **Qi-residue.** The qi-residue of recent events in the dreamer's vicinity (the residue 14 §6 describes) is perceived by the dreaming anchor as imagery related to the residue's source. A dreamer who sleeps near a murder-site dreams of violence; a dreamer who sleeps near a deviation-site dreams of confusion and fear; a dreamer who sleeps near a lineage hall dreams of ancestors (the hall's accumulated grief-rite-residue, perceived by the dreaming anchor as the presence of the dead).
416:- **Bardo-presence.** If a ghost (§3 above) is present near the dreamer, the dreaming anchor perceives the ghost's anchor-fragment directly. This is the mechanism behind the mortal tradition's "ghost-dreams" — the dream in which a dead relative appears and speaks. The dead relative's ghost (if it persists) is perceived by the dreaming anchor as a presence in the dream, and the dream's cognitive apparatus interprets the presence as the relative's form. The ghost's attachment (grief, hatred, oath, love) is perceived as the dream's emotional tone.
426:The practice continues with **direction** — the cultivator learns to direct the dream's content, choosing what to perceive. This is not the same as controlling the dream's imagery (which is the body's cognitive apparatus's interpretation); it is the same as directing the anchor's perception. The cultivator chooses to perceive a specific qi-residue, a specific ambient qi, a specific bardo-presence. The dream's imagery follows the perception; the cultivator does not control the imagery, but they control what the perception is directed at.
432:- **Communicating with the dead.** The cultivator, sleeping near a ghost, directs their anchor's perception to perceive the ghost's anchor-fragment directly. The cultivator can address the ghost (in qi-modulations the ghost can read) and the ghost can respond. This is the conversation a Qi Condensation cultivator can have while waking (§3.8); in dream, the conversation is easier and more legible, because the anchor's perception is unfiltered. A dream-cultivator can lay a ghost to rest (§3.6 method 1) without ever waking.
444:The mortal tradition's "ancestor-dreams" — the dream in which a dead parent appears and gives advice — are, in this cosmology, real for cultivation-lineage members (whose lineage's ancestral court can send impressions) and largely imaginary for mortal-lineage members (whose lineage's dead have been processed by the Underworld and reborn, with no persistent anchor to send impressions). The mortal-lineage member who dreams of a dead parent is, in most cases, dreaming the lineage hall's accumulated grief-rite-residue (perceived as the parent's presence), not the parent's actual anchor. The distinction is invisible to the dreamer and is, in practice, irrelevant — the dream's emotional truth (the ancestor's continued presence in the lineage's life) is the same in both cases, and the lineage's rites are designed to sustain both.
448:To a mortal, a dream is the mortal's ordinary dream-experience — vivid, absurd, emotional, fading on waking. The mortal does not perceive the dream as perception; they perceive it as imagination. The mortal who learns (from a Qi Induction cultivator, or from a Daoist priest, or from their own practice) to attend to their dreams as perception can, over years, develop a faint awareness of the dream's perceptual content — the mortal begins to recognize that dreams of a certain quality correspond to certain waking events (a dream of cold water preceding a flood; a dream of a dead relative preceding a death in the family). This recognition is the beginning of dream cultivation, and it is within the mortal's reach if they have the discipline and the instruction.
450:To a Qi Induction cultivator, dreams are perceived as perception — the cultivator recognizes, within the dream, that they are perceiving something real (qi-residue, ambient qi, bardo-presence). The recognition is partial; the cultivator cannot fully direct the perception, but they can attend to it. This is the threshold at which dream-cultivation becomes possible.
464:Neither register is wrong. Both are partial. The mortal register is partial because it perceives the phenomena only through their effects (the earth god's nudges felt as luck, the ghost's presence felt as a chill, the yao's curiosity felt as a strange encounter). The cultivation register is partial because it perceives the phenomena only at the threshold of perception (a Qi Condensation cultivator perceives a place-spirit but cannot perceive its full coherence; a Core Formation cultivator perceives the place-spirit's anchor but cannot perceive the courts' register's full contents). The full reality of the phenomena is, in this cosmology, perceptible only to a Mahayana cultivator with the right perception-training, and even then only partially.
470:- **The earth god, the river god, and the city god are real.** All three are place-spirits (§2). The mortal beliefs that they protect, modulate weather, and record the dead are accurate within the limits of the spirit's actual capability (§2.3). The river god's "flooding when offended" is partially accurate — the floods are hydrological, but the spirit's withholding of its nudges in response to a broken oath is real (§2.8).
471:- **Ghosts and the Ghost Festival are real.** The mortal belief that the unburied dead walk is accurate, with the qualification that the walking is qi-projection within a small radius (§3). The Ghost Festival's "walking dead" is the bardo-boundary's annual thinness on the fifteenth of the seventh month (§3.7).
473:- **Ancestor veneration is real.** The mortal belief that the dead benefit from veneration is accurate, with the qualification that veneration feeds either a place-spirit's quasi-anchor (for the unremembered dead), a cultivation-lineage's ancestral court (for the lineage's great cultivators), or the local grief-rite residue (for the ordinary dead). The veneration feeds the metaphysical infrastructure the dead depend on; it does not, in most cases, "reach" the specific dead.
478:- **The Buddhist afterlife.** The dead do go somewhere (the Underworld, §6), and the processing does involve a karmic confrontation and a rebirth-assignment. The specifics — Yama, the ten kings, the eighteen hells, the bridges of gold and silver — are mortal projection onto a bureaucratic process.
479:- **The Daoist celestial hierarchy.** The Courts of Heaven exist (15 §2), and they are staffed by cultivators who crossed tribulation and took office. The specifics — the Jade Emperor, the specific ministries, the specific ranks — are mortal projection onto a fallible political institution.
480:- **The mortal tradition's demonic-cultivator tropes.** Demonic cultivators are real (§5), and their practice is metaphysically criminal. The specifics — that they eat infants, that they worship dark gods, that they can be exorcised by Daoist priests — are mortal projection. The actual demonic cultivator is a cultivator who harvests anchors; the actual countermeasure is a stronger cultivator or the cosmos's karmic enforcement.
481:- **The mortal tradition's dream-interpretation.** Dreams are real perception (§7), and some dream-content corresponds to real metaphysical phenomena. The specifics — that dreams predict the future, that dreams of the dead are always the dead's actual presence, that dream-imagery can be read literally — are mortal projection. Dreams perceive present qi-phenomena, not the future; dreams of the dead may be grief-residue or actual ancestral impressions; dream-imagery requires interpretation, not literal reading.
486:- **Yama as a personal king of hell.** There is no Yama; the Underworld is a bureaucratic processing center (§6.1).
488:- **The mortal tradition's "ghost-marriage" (冥婚).** The belief that a dead son can be married to a dead daughter to provide them companionship in the afterlife is myth. The dead are processed by the Underworld and reborn; they do not persist as a couple. The rite of ghost-marriage is, metaphysically, a veneration-qi-input to the place-spirits of the two dead's anchor-points, which may (if the ghosts persist) provide them a brief respite from their attachments. It does not marry them.
489:- **The mortal tradition's "paper money" for the dead.** The burning of paper money is, metaphysically, a qi-input to whatever anchors are present at the burning-site. It does not "reach" the specific dead in the Underworld (the Underworld does not use currency); it feeds the local bardo-boundary's qi-density, which benefits whatever anchors are present. The rite is functional, but not in the way the mortal tradition believes.
493:The mortal projection varies across traditions (Buddhist, Daoist, Confucian, folk, sectarian) because the mortal projection is **mortal culture's interpretation of real phenomena, filtered through mortal culture's cognitive and institutional apparatus**. A Buddhist-temple community interprets the Underworld through Buddhist eschatology because that is the interpretive framework the community has. A Daoist-temple community interprets the courts of Heaven through Daoist celestial hierarchy because that is its framework. A lineage community interprets the dead through ancestor veneration because that is its framework.
495:The phenomena do not prefer one interpretation. The earth god does not care whether the village calls it a 土地公 or a 地主 or a 福德正神; it responds to the veneration, not the name. The Underworld's processing is the same whether the dead are sent by a Buddhist monk's rite, a Daoist priest's rite, or a lineage elder's rite. The courts' crossing is the same whether the cultivator is Buddhist, Daoist, Confucian, or folk-religion. The mortal tradition's variation is the variation of mortal culture, not the variation of the phenomena. The phenomena are one; the interpretations are many.
499:The cultivator who reaches Core Formation — the threshold of direct anchor-perception — inherits a burden that the mortal does not. The mortal believes; the cultivator perceives. The mortal's belief can be wrong without consequence (the mortal who believes the earth god is a small bearded old man is wrong, but the wrongness does not affect the mortal's relationship with the place-spirit). The cultivator's perception cannot be wrong without consequence (the cultivator who perceives a yao and treats it as a spirit beast, or who perceives a ghost and treats it as a place-spirit, makes a metaphysical error with lawful consequences).
501:This is the cultivator's burden: **the responsibility to act on what is actually there rather than on what the iconography says**. The mortal can burn incense to the earth god and trust the iconography; the cultivator must perceive the earth god, read its coherence and its jurisdiction and its disposition, and act on the perception. The mortal can perform the funeral rite as the tradition prescribes; the cultivator must perceive the deceased's anchor, its bardo-state, its karmic trace, and perform the rite as the perception requires. The mortal can hunt the fox-spirit as the tradition demonizes it; the cultivator must perceive Lady Bai's anchor, her karmic trace, her five centuries of good-faith residence, and decide — on the perception, not the tradition — whether to hunt, to negotiate, or to leave alone.
503:This is the burden that makes the cultivation world morally complex, and that makes the protagonist's progression meaningful. A protagonist who reaches Core Formation and returns to Wang Family Bend returns as a being who perceives the earth god, the river god, the ghosts of the bend's dead, the yao in the Cangwu foothills, the qi-residue of the Zhou Wugui incursion still faintly present in the western Cangwu's ravines. The protagonist's choices, from Core Formation onward, are made on perception, not belief. The choices are harder for it; they are also more honest. This is the cosmology's commitment to the cultivator as a being who knows more and is therefore responsible for more. The cathedral has a congregation; the congregation, at Core Formation and above, can see the cathedral's actual structure. What they do with the sight is the game.

525:- **The protagonist's specific folk-religion encounters.** The protagonist's first encounter with a place-spirit, a ghost, a yao, a demonic cultivator, an Underworld trace, or a meaningful dream is a narrative-design decision, not a cosmology decision. This document specifies what the protagonist will perceive when the encounter occurs; it does not specify when or how the encounter happens.
526:- **The courts' specific Underworld administration.** The Underworld's specific hierarchy, its specific officials, its specific processing-queues, and its specific political disputes are lore to be developed in a later Precelestial-content document. This document specifies the Underworld's category, its function, and its relationship to mortal belief; it does not specify its personnel.
529:These are deferred details, not deferred forks. The decisions this document makes — the place-spirit as a sui generis ontological category, the ghost as an extended-bardo anchor, the yao as an animal with a true anchor, the demonic cultivator as the cosmology's one true evil, the Underworld as a courts sub-jurisdiction, the dream as a perception mode, and the mortal-religion/metaphysics relationship as two registers of the same phenomena — are committed. If they prove wrong in prototype, the prototype's evidence overrides this document, and this document is revised — not appended with a candidate alternative.

This document specifies the folk-religion metaphysical layer in enough detail that the cathedral the cosmology documents built has, finally, a congregation — the earth gods, the river gods, the ghosts, the yao, the demonic cultivators, the underworld clerks, the dreamers, and the mortals who venerate and fear them. The cathedral was always beautiful; it now has someone inside it.
