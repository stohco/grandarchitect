   200→The **North Cloud Vein** (北雲脈) is the sect's secondary vein, breaking the surface two li north of the compound, on the far side of Biluo Peak. Its current is shallower (one hundred and fifty meters) and its output is roughly half the Biluo Vein's. The vein's surface trace is a cold, mineral-heavy spring in a small hanging valley that the sect has developed into the **North Cloud Garden** (北雲苑), a terraced herb garden growing metal-phase and water-phase spirit herbs for the Pill Hall. The garden is managed by a Foundation Establishment elder (currently Elder Qi Shoujing, an Isolationist-leaning quiet ally of Jing You) and staffed by six inner disciples on rotating assignment. The North Cloud Vein's output is not directly tapped for cultivation; it feeds the herb garden, which feeds the Pill Hall, which produces the sect's pills. The chain is the sect's pharmaceutical backbone.
   201→
   202→The **Iron Root Vein** (鐵根脈) is the sect's tertiary vein, breaking the surface four li southwest of the compound, in a wild region of the peak's lower slopes. Its current is the shallowest of the three (sixty meters) and its output is the smallest (sufficient for thirty Qi Condensation cultivators). The vein's surface trace is a stand of iron-root pine (the same species that grows in the grotto-heaven, doc 2.3 — the surface trace and the grotto-heaven population share a common ancestor population isolated when the grotto-heaven's boundary formed). The Iron Root Vein is the sect's sword-craft resource: the iron-root pine's wood, saturated by the vein's metal-phase qi, is dense enough to be forged into practice swords and, with sufficient treatment, into low-grade artifact-grade weapons. The Sword-Craft Hall manages the stand and the forge.
   203→
   204→The three veins together produce enough qi to sustain the sect's four hundred disciples at Qi Condensation practice and approximately forty at Foundation Establishment practice — but not enough to fund a Nascent Soul breakthrough. Leng Wushuang's two failed attempts depleted the sect's spirit-stone reserves (the sect held, before the first attempt, a reserve of three thousand stones; the reserve is now four hundred). The third attempt requires, by Leng Wushuang's estimate, two thousand stones of concentrated qi and six months of uninterrupted grotto-heaven access. The Cangli Riverlands' deep vein extension (§2.4) is, for Leng Wushuang, the resource that closes this gap — either by direct tapping (if the formal claim succeeds) or by trade (if the Jade Void Holy Land can be induced to supply stones in exchange for territorial concessions).
   205→
   206→### 2.8 The grotto-heaven rotation system
   207→
   208→The Jade Edge Grotto's six-month rotations are the Azure Sword Sect's most contested internal resource. The system: every inner disciple is eligible for a six-month rotation once per three-year cycle; the rotation places them inside the grotto-heaven for intensive cultivation, with access to the grotto's metal-phase-accelerated qi, the herb population, and (under supervision) the beast population for combat practice. Three hundred and seventy of the four hundred disciples cycle through; the remaining thirty (the sect master, the three peak masters, the eight hall heads, and senior inner disciples on sect duty) practice in the compound's main cultivation hall, which is fed by a direct tap from the Biluo Vein.
   209→
   210→Rotation slots are allocated by the Merit Hall (Expansionist-controlled, §2.5) on the basis of merit-points accumulated through sect missions, grotto-heaven herb-gathering, and (in practice) factional alignment. A disciple in good standing with Elder Rong Zhaoyuan's faction can expect a slot every three years; a disciple out of favor may wait five. The protagonist, on first contact with the Azure Sword Sect (session ~200, doc 26 §4.2), is offered a courtesy slot by Leng Wushuang — a political gesture (the protagonist is not a sect member and would not normally be offered grotto-heaven access) that the Expansionists receive as provocation and the Isolationists receive as a sign that Leng Wushuang is, despite her expansionist position, willing to extend the sect's courtesies to a powerful outsider. The protagonist's acceptance or refusal of the slot is the first Azure Sword Sect political choice and shapes the factional response to the protagonist for the rest of Act 2.
   211→
   212→The grotto-heaven's rotation system has, over the sect's 180-year history, produced one structural problem: the grotto's ecology is not infinite. The sword-grass stands, the blue-flame lotus beds, and the iron-root pine groves are renewable but only at a rate set by the grotto's qi-climate; over-harvesting depletes them. The herb population has thinned measurably in the last sixty years (Jing You's Scripture Hall has documented this; Rong Zhaoyuan's Merit Hall has not acknowledged it). The silver-furred fox population is also down (the Isolationists attribute this to over-hunting by rotation disciples; the Expansionists attribute it to a natural cycle). The jade-scaled serpent, by all observations, is unaffected — but no one in the sect is willing to test whether that would change. The grotto's slow ecological decline is, for the Isolationists, an argument against expansion (the sect cannot manage what it has); for the Expansionists, an argument for it (the sect needs a second grotto-heaven to relieve the first).
   213→
   214→### 2.9 The regional position: the Jade Void, the Alliance, and the courts
   215→
   216→The Azure Sword Sect is the regional hegemon of the central and northern Cangwu range, but it is not the regional hegemon of the broader Cangli watershed. That position belongs, in cultivation-world accounting, to the **Jade Void Holy Land** (玉虛聖地, doc 24 §4.3) — a Nascent Soul+ holy land on another continent, with three thousand disciples and a peak grotto-heaven, whose founder (Daoist Jade Void, two thousand one hundred years old) was, per the holy land's own records, born in the Cangli Riverlands. The Jade Void's interest in the Cangli region is, formally, ancestral veneration — the holy land maintains a small shrine (staffed by two Foundation Establishment elders on rotating assignment) at the site of the founder's birth village, which is, by the holy land's archives' best estimate, somewhere in the central Cangwu foothills within thirty li of Wang Family Bend. The shrine's exact location has been lost (the founder's birth village was abandoned in a flood fourteen hundred years ago and the site has shifted); the Jade Void's rotating elders search for it, quietly, on their assignments, and have not found it. The Azure Sword Sect knows of this search and tolerates it, because the Jade Void's interest is, on balance, a strategic asset — a holy land's claim to the region deters other holy lands from contesting it.
   217→
   218→The Azure Sword Sect's relationship to the Jade Void is therefore careful. The sect pays nominal homage to the holy land (a small tribute of one mid-grade spirit stone per decade, delivered by a Foundation Establishment elder to the Jade Void's rotating shrine staff); the Jade Void, in return, extends the sect its implicit protection against other holy lands. The arrangement is unwritten and is, in the cultivation world's accounting, fragile. If the Azure Sword Sect's expansion into the Cangli Riverlands is perceived by the Jade Void as a violation of the region's ancestral-veneration status, the holy land's protection could withdraw — and a holy land's withdrawn protection is, in the cultivation world's brutal accounting, an invitation to other powers to contest the now-unprotected region. This is the Isolationist faction's strongest argument (§2.5): the expansion risks the Jade Void's goodwill, and the Jade Void's goodwill is the sect's long-term strategic anchor.
   219→
   220→The Azure Sword Sect's relationship to the **Immortal Alliance** (仙盟, doc 12 §6, doc 20 §1.3) is formal. The sect holds a mid-tier alliance seat (one of thirty in the eastern region), pays annual alliance dues (twelve mid-grade spirit stones), and is entitled to alliance adjudication of disputes with other member sects. The Cangli spirit vein dispute (§2.4), when it is filed at session ~300, will be filed with the alliance. The alliance's adjudication is slow (the average dispute resolution takes eight years) and the alliance's enforcement is weak (the alliance has no standing army; it relies on collective action by member sects). The Azure Sword Sect knows this; the Cangwu Sect knows this; both know that the formal adjudication is, in practice, a stalling tactic while the two sects maneuver on the ground. The protagonist's intervention (mediation, alliance lobbying, or — if the player chooses — bringing in the Jade Void as a third party) is the Act 2 political verb.
   221→
   222→The Azure Sword Sect's relationship to the **Courts of Heaven** (天庭, doc 15, doc 20 §1.3) is distant. The courts have no direct jurisdiction over Acquired-stratum sect disputes; the courts' concern is records of the dead and heaven-sealed oaths. The Azure Sword Sect, however, has one courts-related obligation: the sect's Foundation Establishment+ cultivators, when they die, are subject to the courts' soul-rites (per doc 25 §5). The sect maintains a small temple (the **Sword-Ancestor Side-Shrine**, a single-room building near the Founder's Garden) staffed by a Qi Condensation lay cultivator who performs the basic soul-rites for the sect's dead. The shrine is not a temple in the institutional sense (doc 12 §4); it is a side-function the sect performs for its own. The courts know of the shrine and tolerate it; the courts do not, in practice, intervene in the sect's funerary practice as long as the basic rites are performed. This distant relationship is the sect's only direct contact with the Precelestial, and it is, for the sect's cultivators, an abstract concern — the courts are a thing one deals with at death, not in life. The protagonist, at Foundation Establishment, will deal with the courts directly only if and when a sect member's anchor requires formal processing (the death of Old Chen, doc 26 §12, is the first such event the protagonist navigates).

   226→## Part 3 — The Eight Hexagrams
   227→
   228→### 3.1 What the hexagrams are and how Wang Lun casts them
   229→
   230→Wang Lun (王倫, 41, the lineage school teacher, doc 06 Scene 1) casts the hexagram by the yarrow-stalk method — the slow, careful procedure (fifty stalks, divided and counted in eighteen passes, producing six lines from bottom to top, each line solid or broken, each line possibly changing) that yields one of the sixty-four hexagrams of the Zhou Book of Changes (周易). The cast is deterministic from `seed + playerState` (doc 06 Scene 1, Procedural implications): the same seed and the same player state produce the same hexagram. The player state, at Scene 1 (autumn of the player's sixteenth year), is determined by the player's choices over the first five sessions — primarily the player's responses to the first two qi perceptions, the player's dialogue with Wang Lun during lessons, and the player's behavior in the lineage hall (whether they have hidden the perception, spoken of it, grieved in it, etc.). The state is one of eight profiles. Each profile produces one hexagram. The hexagram, in Wang Lun's reading, names what the player is becoming; the player's journal records what they heard.
   231→
   232→The eight specified below are the eight most likely. (The other fifty-six hexagrams are reachable in principle but require player states that the first five sessions make unlikely — states of profound depression, ecstatic mania, or already-formed cultivation intent that the sixteen-year-old mortal protagonist is not yet capable of.) Each entry gives: the hexagram's name (Hanzi), the judgment text (the cryptic line Wang Lun reads aloud from the Book of Changes, in the traditional form), how the hexagram points toward Old Chen (the direction Wang Lun gives), and the journal entry the protagonist writes that night.
   233→
   234→### 3.2 Hexagram 1: Zhun (屯) — Initial Difficulty
   235→
   236→**Player state:** open and curious; the protagonist has perceived qi two or three times, has not hidden it, has spoken of it to no one, and approaches the perception with attention rather than fear.

   240→**How it points to Old Chen:** Wang Lun reads the line "it furthers to install helpers" and pauses. He says, slowly, that the hexagram names a beginning — a difficult one — and that beginnings of this kind are not undertaken alone. He says he knows of one helper. He does not name Old Chen yet; he asks the protagonist whether they would accept a helper if one could be found. (The protagonist's answer here does not change the direction; Wang Lun gives the direction regardless. But the protagonist's answer changes the wording of the introduction.)
   241→
   242→**Journal entry (written that night, in the protagonist's childish hand):** "Teacher Wang cast a hexagram for me. Zhun. Initial difficulty. He said beginnings like this are hard and you don't do them alone. He said he knew a helper. He asked if I would accept one. I said yes. He wrote a letter and sealed it and gave it to me. He said to take it to the hermit on the hill above Li Family Creek, four li east. He said the hermit's name is Old Chen. He said Old Chen might not teach me. He said to go anyway."

   250→**How it points to Old Chen:** Wang Lun reads the line "I do not seek the young fool; the young fool seeks me" and almost smiles. He says the hexagram is about the relationship between a teacher and a student, and the hexagram is clear: the teacher does not come to the student. He says there is a teacher, on the hill above Li Family Creek, four li east, who will not come to the protagonist. He says the protagonist must go to him.    258→**Judgment text (坎: 習坎, 有孚, 維心亨, 行有尚):** "The Abysmal repeated. If you are sincere, you have success in your heart, and whatever you do succeeds."

He says the hexagram names a state of being in danger and getting through it, not by escaping the danger but by going through it. He says the protagonist is in danger, though not the kind with a sword. He says there is a man on the hill above Li Family Creek, four li east, who went through this danger once and survived. He says the man's name is Old Chen. He says Old Chen lost someone, once, and did not mourn them in time, and the not-mourning spoiled something in him, and he spent thirty years repairing it.    314→### 3.10 On the other fifty-six hexagrams
   315→
   316→The eight specified above are the eight most likely at Scene 1, given the player states the first five sessions can produce. The other fifty-six hexagrams of the Zhou Book of Changes are reachable in principle — the deterministic cast from `seed + playerState` can, for unusual seeds or unusual player states, produce any of the sixty-four — but each requires a player state that the sixteen-year-old mortal protagonist is structurally unlikely to occupy at session ~5. A brief accounting of the categories the simulation must handle (in case a seed produces them) and how Wang Lun handles each:
   317→
   318→**The thirty-six hexagrams of stable, formed states** (Qian 乾, Kun 坤, Tai 泰, Pi 否, et al.) — these name conditions of cosmic equilibrium or its absence. The sixteen-year-old mortal protagonist has not lived long enough to occupy a stable equilibrium; Wang Lun, receiving one of these, would read it as a hexagram about the world's larger motion rather than the protagonist's, and would redirect the reading toward the smaller, more personal hexagram nested inside it (the nuclear hexagram, the hu gua 互卦). The redirection is a real divinatory technique; Wang Lun knows it; the simulation implements it.
   319→
   320→**The twelve hexagrams of advanced cultivation states** (Guai 夬, Gou 姤, Bo 剝, Fu 復 — the twelve消息 hexagrams that describe the annual cycle of qi rising and falling) — these name realm-level transitions the protagonist has not yet approached. Wang Lun, receiving one, would read it as a forecast of the protagonist's long cultivation arc rather than a present-state description, and would (correctly) treat it as beyond his ability to interpret fully. He would say, in this case, "I cannot read this hexagram for you. It is too large. Go to Old Chen; he will know what to do with it." This is the only player-state category in which Wang Lun explicitly defers to Old Chen by name in the casting itself, rather than in the direction-giving after.
   321→
   322→**The sixteen hexagrams of extreme emotional states** (Dui 兌 in its extreme forms, Zhen 震 in its extreme forms, et al.) — these name conditions of overwhelming joy, rage, terror, or grief that the protagonist could, in principle, occupy at session ~5 (a protagonist who has just learned of Wang Lun's impending pneumonia, a protagonist consumed by rage at Wang Zongde, a protagonist in the grip of unmourned Wang Xiaodi grief so acute it has produced a qi-deviation onset). These are reachable in principle but require specific player choices that the first five sessions make available only at the extremes. Wang Lun, receiving one, would read it carefully and would name what he sees — the protagonist's extremity — and would point to Old Chen with greater urgency than the eight common hexagrams warrant. The protagonist's journal entry, in these cases, is shorter (the protagonist is in no state to write at length) and more fragmentary.
   323→
   324→The simulation must, for completeness, produce Wang Lun's reading for all sixty-four hexagrams (the eight specified in detail in §3.2-§3.9, plus the fifty-six accounted for here in summary). The fifty-six are not specified in this document at the same depth as the eight because they are, by the procedural contract, unlikely at session ~5 — but the simulation must handle them gracefully, with Wang Lun's reading and the protagonist's journal entry produced by a template (the eight specified entries provide the template's calibration set). The template is: name the hexagram, read the judgment text, name the player state the hexagram describes, point to Old Chen (with urgency proportional to the player state's extremity), write the letter. The eight specified entries are the template's eight calibration points; the fifty-six are the template's interpolated outputs.

   326→### 3.11 Procedural and rendering implications
   327→
   328→The hexagram cast is, per doc 06 Scene 1, deterministic from `seed + playerState`. The implementation contract: the engine's dialogue plugin (per doc 22) consumes the player-state vector produced by the first five sessions (a vector of approximately twelve dimensions, including perception-count, perception-attitude, dialogue-tone with Wang Lun, lineage-hall behavior, grief-state, ambition-state, fear-state, loneliness-state, contentment-state, doubt-state, reverence-state, and a residual state for outliers), hashes it together with the world seed, and selects one of the sixty-four hexagrams by a deterministic mapping. The mapping is biassed toward the eight specified in §3.2-§3.9 — the eight player-state vectors that the first five sessions can produce map directly to the eight hexagrams; the fifty-six summary hexagrams require either unusual seeds or player-state vectors at the extremes of the twelve dimensions.
   329→
   330→The rendering of the cast (per doc 06 Scene 1, Rendering implications): the yarrow-stalk procedure is rendered accurately. Wang Lun's animation begins with the stalks spread on the low table; the player watches (or, if impatient, can skip — but the skip is regrettable, because the procedure is the scene's pacing and skipping it makes Wang Lun's reading feel rushed, which is the wrong feeling). Each of the eighteen passes is rendered: Wang Lun divides the stalks with his right hand, counts the left bundle in fours, sets the remainder aside, repeats. The six lines build from bottom to top on a slip of paper, each line drawn with a brushstroke (solid for yang, broken for yin), each line marked with a small dot if it is a changing line. The completed hexagram is held up; Wang Lun reads the judgment text aloud; the text appears in the journal as the protagonist writes the entry that night.
   331→
   332→The journal entry (per doc 06 Scene 1, Aftermath) is a persistent object in the player's inventory — a small notebook with the hexagram's name, the judgment text, Wang Lun's reading, and the protagonist's own words. The protagonist can consult the journal at any point in the game. The journal's text does not change; what changes is the protagonist's understanding of it. A player who reads the journal at session ~5 (the night of the cast) reads one meaning; the same player reading the same journal at session ~450 (the night of Old Chen's death) reads a different meaning, because the protagonist has, by then, lived into the hexagram. This is the design intent: the hexagram is a fixed text the protagonist grows into, not a fixed prediction the protagonist fulfills. Wang Lun's divination (per doc 24 §2.5) perceives the qi-topography's current momentum, not the future; the hexagram names the momentum, and the protagonist's life is what the momentum becomes under the protagonist's free action. The journal, persistent and unchanged, is the textual record of this: a fixed cast, a living reading.

   334→The hexagram also affects Old Chen's reception of the protagonist (per doc 06 Scene 2). Old Chen, on receiving the protagonist, asks (if the protagonist offers the letter) what hexagram Wang Lun cast. The protagonist tells him. Old Chen's response varies by hexagram: for Zhun (Initial Difficulty), Old Chen nods slowly and says "Good. A difficult beginning is the only kind worth having"; for Meng (Youthful Folly), Old Chen says "Good. The teacher does not come to the student. You came. That is the first lesson"; for Kan (The Abysmal), Old Chen is quiet for a long moment and then says "I know that hexagram. I cast it for myself, sixty years ago. Come in"; for Gu (What Has Been Spoiled), Old Chen says nothing for a long time and then says "I am sorry. Wang Lun should not have — but he was right to. Come in"; for Jin (Progress), Old Chen frowns and says "Be careful of that hexagram. It is the one I cast before my own failed breakthrough. Come in, and we will be slow"; for Ming Yi (Darkening of the Light), Old Chen says softly "Yes. I know. Come in. There is tea"; for Bo (Peeling Away), Old Chen says "Then you have come at the right time. Come in"; for Bi (Holding Together), Old Chen says, with a small and unexpected smile, "Good. I have been alone with it for thirty years. Come in." The eight responses are Old Chen's first teaching, delivered before the fraudulent-manual test (doc 06 Scene 2). They are the protagonist's first perception that Old Chen is not a hermit by temperament but a hermit by circumstance — a man who has been waiting, in a way he would not have admitted to anyone, for company.

   350→## 5. Reviewer voices (per the doctrine: "Exhibit reviewer voices; do not self-certify")
   351→
   352→**Reviewer A (the genre traditionalist):** "The Cangwu Sect is too sympathetic and the Azure Sword Sect is too nuanced. The genre wants clearly evil great sects and clearly virtuous small sects. You have given Wu Changqing a notebook and Leng Wushuang a dead husband and a third attempt, and now neither side is a villain. The player will not know whom to root for."
   353→
   354→**Response:** The genre's clearly-evil-great-sect convention is the power-fantasy version of xianxia, in which the protagonist's rise is validated by the obvious villainy of those above them. This game's thesis (doc 26 §0, doc 05 §4.3) is that cultivation is becoming less limited, not becoming more powerful than the villains. The political engine of Act 2 requires that the Azure Sword Sect's expansion be lawful (if ruthless) and the Cangwu Sect's resistance be understandable (if doomed), because the protagonist's choice — defend the small sect, mediate, or step aside — is only meaningful if both sides have a real case. Leng Wushuang is not a villain; she is a woman who has failed twice and is gambling on a third attempt, and the resources for the attempt require an expansion the small sect cannot survive. Wu Changqing is not a hero; he is a man who broke himself at twenty-one and has spent thirty years presiding over the slow decline of the sect he inherited, and his grief has curdled into a bitterness he writes about in a notebook no one reads. The player roots for the protagonist, who must navigate between them. This is the doctrine's "confront the central tension directly" (AGENTS.md Part 3): the tension is real, the sides are real, and the protagonist's choice is the engine.
   355→
   356→**Reviewer B (the systems designer):** "Nine named disciples at the Cangwu Sect is too many for the simulation to track with the depth you specify. Each disciple has an arc, a faction, a relationship to the protagonist, and a death-or-transformation trajectory. Across Act 2 (sessions 120-500), the simulation must track all nine plus the protagonist plus the Azure Sword Sect's named characters plus the companions. This is a cast of thirty. The simulation will choke."
   357→
   358→**Response:** The simulation does not track all nine at full depth at all times. Each disciple has a baseline state (realm, faction, relationship vector) updated per solar term, and a foreground-depth that activates only when the protagonist is in the disciple's presence or when the disciple is the protagonist's current mission-relevant NPC. The nine named disciples are the sect's load-bearing NPCs; the other twenty-one (the unnamed outer and inner disciples) are simulated as a cohort with aggregate state. The cast of thirty is the cast of named, foreground-depth characters across the entire Act 2-3 arc, not the cast of simultaneously-foreground characters in any single session. The precedent is Crusader Kings' character-density model (hundreds of named characters, of which a dozen are foreground at any time) and Baldur's Gate 3's companion model (six companions, of which three are in the party at any time). This is shippable; the depth is calibrated to the foreground/background distinction, not to uniform simulation.
   359→
   360→**Reviewer C (the production lead):** "Eight hexagrams with full judgment text, direction-giving, journal entry, and Old Chen response, plus a template for the other fifty-six. This is a writing load you have not budgeted."
   361→
   362→**Response:** The eight specified entries are written (this document). The fifty-six template outputs are not fully written; they are produced by the template (§3.10) from the eight calibration points, with the writing team filling in the template's gaps for the fifty-six in a single sprint after the eight are validated. The total writing load is eight full entries (done) plus fifty-six template interpolations (a sprint). The Old Chen responses (§3.11) are eight additional lines of dialogue, written (this document). The journal entries' persistence across the 1500-session arc (the design intent that the same journal text reads differently at session 5 and session 450) is a rendering and UX concern, not a writing concern — the text does not change; the player's reading context does. This is shippable in the Act 1 vertical slice (the eight hexagrams are Scene-1 content) with the fifty-six template interpolations deferred to the Act 2 expansion.
