**PERCEPTUAL EVIDENCE REPORT: STAGE A**

**Actual Content**
*   **Setting:** An outdoor environment consisting of a flat green plane and tan-colored flat zones.
*   **Objects:** A large, flat wooden canopy supported by thin vertical pillars; several conical green shapes representing trees; boxy, brown rectangular structures in the mid-ground; white pointed shapes in the far distance; small white cylinders and one brown conical object resting on the tan ground.
*   **Scale:** Inconsistent. The support pillars are disproportionately thin relative to the mass of the roof. The "trees" are primitive geometric cones.
*   **Density:** Extremely low. Large expanses of empty, featureless terrain.

**Player Perception**
A player would perceive this as a placeholder environment or a "greybox" stage. It reads as a rudimentary village or camp layout where the primary goal is to define spatial boundaries rather than provide a visual experience.

**Art Style and Production Quality**
*   **Style:** Low-poly primitive.
*   **Quality:** Very low. Textures are flat, tiled, or stretched (notably on the roof). There is a total absence of complex lighting, ambient occlusion, or material definition. Edges are perfectly sharp with no beveling or organic variation.

**Level of Completion**
**BLOCKOUT**

**Defects and Distractions**
*   **Texture Stretching:** The wood grain on the overhead canopy is stretched across the geometry.
*   **Primitive Geometry:** Trees are simple cones; mountains are simple triangles.
*   **Lack of Ground Integration:** Objects appear to "sit" on top of the plane rather than being integrated into the environment.
*   **Empty Zones:** The foreground and mid-ground are devoid of any detail, debris, or environmental storytelling.
*   **Lighting:** Flat, global illumination with no directional shadows to provide depth or grounding.

**Inspection Limit**
The image stops rewarding closer inspection immediately. There are no high-resolution textures, hidden details, or complex meshes to discover. Once the primitive nature of the cone-trees and flat planes is identified, there is no further visual information to extract.
