## 0. Why this document exists

The cosmology documents (00, 14, 15, 24) specified the cathedral: the three strata, the anchor, the realm ladder, the spirit veins, the courts of Heaven, the ancestral courts, the tribulation system. What they left unspecified was the congregation — the beings and forces that fill a mortal's actual religious life. The earth god in the small shrine at the village entrance. The river god who floods when offended. The ghosts that walk on the fifteenth of the seventh month. The fox-spirit in the mountain who seduces travelers. The demonic cultivator who harvests the dying. The underworld the dead are said to descend to. The dreams the ancestors send.

The five-critic audit identified this gap as "a beautiful cathedral with no congregation." A xianxia world in which the cosmology is specified down to the Precelestial law reaches but in which the village's earth god is undefined is a world whose upper register is articulate and whose lower register is mute. This document speaks the lower register, under one constraint: every being specified here must be coherent with the ratified cosmology. The karma ledger (24 §2.1), the anchor ontology (24 §2.2), the yin-yang of body/qi/anchor (24 §2.3), and the divination system (24 §2.5) are the spine. Folk religion is the muscle and skin that moves around it.

**Conventions.** Hanzi appear in parentheses after the English term on first use. No pinyin romanization with tone marks is used; the project's working vocabulary is English xianxia terminology and Hanzi, per the writing brief. The 1:365 Precelestial-to-Acquired time ratio (15 §1.4) is referenced as the **time-debt**. The body/qi/anchor triad (24 §2.3) — body yin-dominant, qi yang-within-yin, anchor yang-dominant — is the ontological frame for every being specified here.

## 1. The premise: a real metaphysics behind mortal religion

The folk religion of the Cangli Riverlands is not fantasy. The earth god is real. The river god is real. The ghosts of the unburied dead are real. The fox-spirit in the Cangwu foothills is real. The underworld is real. The dreams the ancestors send are real.

What is partial, what is myth, what is mortal projection, is the *interpretation*. The earth god is not a small bearded old man in a green robe who lives in the shrine — that is how mortal iconography depicts a being whose actual ontological category is "the consciousness of a spirit vein's surface trace." The underworld is not the ten courts of Yama with their mirror-pools and bridges of gold and silver — that is how Mahayana Buddhist popular eschatology depicts a bureaucratic processing center. The ancestors do not literally descend on the fifteenth of the seventh month — that is the calendar on which the boundary between the Mortal stratum and the bardo is thinnest, and on which a certain kind of perception is easiest.

This document's commitment is twofold. First: the phenomena are real. Second: the mortal accounts of them are interpretations, varying in accuracy, varying in elaboration, varying in moral coloring, but always interpretations of something that is actually there. The cultivator who reaches Core Formation and learns to perceive these beings directly inherits a responsibility that the mortal who merely believes in them does not: the responsibility to act on what is actually there rather than on what the iconography says.

## 2. Place-spirits (土地公 / 河神 / 城隍)

### 2.1 Ontological category

A place-spirit is the **consciousness of a spirit vein's surface trace** when that trace is dense enough and venerated long enough. It is not a god, not a Precelestial official, not an ancestor, not a cultivator, not a beast, not a ghost. It is a sui generis ontological category: a self-sustaining qi-pattern that has acquired, through density and duration, the qualities of attention, memory, and will.

The vernacular calls them 公 (a respectful term for a low-ranking supernatural being): the earth god (土地公), the river god (河神), the city god (城隍). The cosmological term is place-spirit. Both are correct in their registers; the cosmological term is what a Core Formation cultivator uses when perceiving one directly, the vernacular term is what a mortal uses when burning incense at the shrine.

A place-spirit is, in the body/qi/anchor triad (24 §2.3), a being of **qi with a quasi-anchor**. It has no body in the mortal sense; its body is the place itself — the soil, the water, the stone, the boundary of its jurisdiction. Its qi is the vein's surface trace, concentrated and patterned. Its anchor is a *quasi*-anchor: a self-inscribed law-pattern that resembles a true anchor in persistence and uniqueness but lacks a true anchor's noncopyability and karmic-trace capacity. A place-spirit cannot be reincarnated; it cannot be possessed; it does not bear karma in the human sense. It can be destroyed, and when destroyed, it ends. The quasi-anchor is fragile in a way a true anchor is not.

### 2.2 Formation

A place-spirit forms when three conditions are met simultaneously, over a duration measured in generations.

1. **A spirit vein's surface trace.** Per 14 §1.1, a spirit vein is a subterranean river of dense differentiated qi. Its surface trace is the plume of phase-aligned qi that diffuses upward through rock and soil to the surface. Most surface traces are too thin and too diffuse to support a place-spirit; they manifest only as the indicator ecologies 14 §1.3 describes (a grove of healthier trees, a spring that never fails). A place-spirit requires a surface trace dense enough to sustain a coherent qi-pattern — typically the trace above a shallow vein, or the trace at a vein's crown where it approaches the surface.

2. **Veneration.** A surface trace, however dense, does not become a place-spirit without veneration. The mechanism is this: mortal veneration — incense, offering, prayer, the focused attention of generations of supplicants — is itself a qi-input. The supplicant's intention, their hope, their fear, their gratitude, their request — each is a small discharge of human qi, and each discharge, when directed at the same site over years and generations, accumulates. The accumulated veneration-qi enters the surface trace, condenses with the vein's qi, and begins to pattern. The pattern, sustained, develops the qualities of attention. The pattern, attended to by mortals who now direct their veneration at it specifically, develops the qualities of memory and will. A place-spirit is, in this sense, **co-authored** by the vein and the community. Neither alone suffices.

3. **Time.** The minimum duration is roughly three generations of continuous veneration — about ninety years of mortal time. Below that threshold, the pattern is present but not coherent; it is what a Qi Induction cultivator perceives as the "good feeling" of a shrine. Above that threshold, the pattern coheres into a being. Three generations is the floor; most place-spirits are far older. The earth god of Wang Family Bend has been venerated at the same shrine for nine generations (per 04 §5.3); the river god of the Cangli at the south-bank temple for at least seven.

### 2.3 Capabilities

A place-spirit can do, within its jurisdiction, what the vein's qi and the community's veneration empower it to do. Specifically:

- **Local weather.** Within its jurisdiction, a place-spirit can nudge weather — delaying a frost by a day, concentrating a rain-cloud over its community's paddies, dispersing a hail-storm. The nudging is small (a day, a few li, a degree of severity) and is constrained by the larger weather-patterns the spirit cannot override. A place-spirit cannot end a drought; it can shift the drought's edge by a few li. A place-spirit cannot call a storm; it can sharpen the edge of one already coming. Mortals attribute to the spirit what is in fact the spirit's minor modulation of much larger forces.

- **Minor fortune and misfortune.** A venerated place-spirit can, at the cost of its own accumulated qi, slightly bias events within its jurisdiction. A lost child is found a half-hour earlier; a fever breaks an hour before it would have; a thief's foot catches a root. These are tiny qi-nudges — the spirit routing a fragment of the vein's qi into the local qi-momentum (24 §2.5) in a direction that favors its community. The spirit cannot perform miracles; it can perform nudges. The mortal gratitude that follows is itself veneration, which feeds the spirit back. This is the virtuous cycle of a healthy place-spirit.

- **Boundary enforcement.** A place-spirit can, within its jurisdiction, enforce a boundary against low-tier threats. A low-tier spirit beast (14 §2.2) that would range into the village is turned aside — not by force, but by the spirit's qi-pattern making the village's ambient qi feel *wrong-colored* to the beast, the way a smell makes a room unwelcoming. A wandering ghost (§3 below) is slowed, sometimes stopped, at the boundary. A Qi Condensation cultivator of malign intent feels, at the boundary, a faint pressure to turn back. None of these are hard barriers; a determined intruder of any tier above mortal passes through. The boundary is a modulation, not a wall.

### 2.4 Jurisdiction and the courts

A place-spirit's jurisdiction is **bounded to its place**. The earth god of Wang Family Bend has no authority in Li Family Creek, four li east. The river god of the Cangli has no authority over the Cangwu Mountains' streams. The city god of Cangli County Seat has no authority outside the city walls. This is not a politeness; it is ontological. The place-spirit's quasi-anchor *is* the place; outside the place, the spirit does not exist as a coherent being. A place-spirit that tries to extend its jurisdiction beyond its place simply dissipates — the qi-pattern that constitutes it unwinds when it leaves the surface trace that sustains it.

The relationship to the Courts of Heaven (15 §2) is **independence without sovereignty**. A place-spirit is not a court official. The courts did not appoint it; the courts cannot promote, demote, transfer, or remove it. The courts' records (生死簿) do not register place-spirits; they are not dead mortals, they are not cultivators, they are not beasts. They are a category the courts recognize but do not administer.

However: in regions where the courts' jurisdiction is firm (the maintained roads, the court-recognized territories), a place-spirit may be **deputized** by a local magistrate (15 §2.3) for a specific task — usually weather-modulation in a famine year, or boundary-enforcement against a specific threat. The deputyship is a contract: the spirit performs the task, the court pays in qi-authority (which the spirit can metabolize as concentrated veneration-equivalent), and the spirit's jurisdiction is formally recognized (which protects it from being absorbed by a neighboring spirit or destroyed by a careless cultivator). Most place-spirits accept deputyships readily. In regions where the courts' jurisdiction is thin (the frontiers, the wild margins, the places where the maintained roads do not reach), place-spirits survive on their own. They are the *de facto* supernatural authority in most of the Mortal stratum; a traveler in the deep country deals with place-spirits, not with the courts.

### 2.5 Death

A place-spirit dies in one of three ways.

1. **Vein collapse.** If the spirit vein that sustains the place-spirit fails — through over-tapping (14 §1.4), through enemy action, through geological accident — the surface trace disperses, the qi-pattern that constitutes the spirit unwinds, and the spirit ends. This is the most common cause of place-spirit death in regions subject to sect cultivation. A sect that over-taps a vein kills, often unknowingly, the place-spirit above it. The mortal community that depended on the spirit experiences the death as the slow fading of the shrine's "feel" — incense seems less effective, prayers seem unheard, the village's luck goes sour. The mortals blame themselves, or the spirit, or the weather. They are wrong about all three.

2. **Veneration cessation.** If the community that venerated the place-spirit disperses — through famine, war, plague, migration — the veneration-qi input stops. The qi-pattern that constitutes the spirit, no longer fed, unwinds over years. This is a slow death; a place-spirit whose community abandons it persists for a generation or two as a fading echo, then ends. The ruins of a village with an unattended shrine often harbor such fading echoes. A Qi Induction cultivator passing through perceives them as a thin, melancholy presence — the spirit's last pattern, repeating without coherence.

3. **Destruction of the anchor-object.** A place-spirit's quasi-anchor, unlike a true anchor, has a physical seat — the shrine's main image (the small carved figure, the painted tablet, the inscribed stone) that the community's veneration has, over generations, invested with the spirit's pattern. This is the spirit's *anchor-object*. Destroying the anchor-object does not, by itself, kill the spirit — the spirit's pattern is in the place, not in the object — but it severs the spirit's primary channel of veneration-input and shatters the coherence of the pattern. A spirit whose anchor-object is destroyed enters a confused, diminished state from which it recovers only if the community rebuilds the object quickly (within a season) and resumes veneration. If the community does not — if the destruction was deliberate, by a hostile cultivator or a rival sect — the spirit usually unwinds within a year.

This third vulnerability is what makes place-spirits politically fragile. A cultivator who wishes to displace a place-spirit (to clear a site for sect construction, to free a vein for direct tapping, to remove a community's supernatural patron) does not need to destroy the vein or the community; destroying the anchor-object and preventing its reconstruction suffices. This is why place-spirits are protective of their shrines and why communities are protective of their shrines' images. The image is not the spirit; the image is the spirit's handle.

### 2.6 What it feels like to perceive one

To a mortal, a place-spirit is felt only as the *quality of a place*. The shrine feels heavier than its size; the air around it feels attended to; one has the sense of being watched by something that is not hostile and not friendly, just present. A mortal who has venerated at a shrine for years develops, without knowing it, a faint Qi-Induction-level sensitivity to the shrine's qi-pattern. They "know" when the spirit is pleased and "know" when it is not, without being able to say how.

To a Qi Induction cultivator, the place-spirit is perceived as a thickening of the ambient qi — the surface trace becomes legible as a coherent texture rather than a background diffusion. The cultivator can feel the spirit's attention as a pressure, like the pressure of being watched but with a quality (color, weight, temperature) that the cultivator can begin to describe. They cannot yet perceive the spirit as a being with a shape and a voice.

To a Qi Condensation cultivator, the place-spirit is legible as a presence — a qi-pattern with coherence, memory, and will. The cultivator can address it; the spirit can respond (not in words, but in modulations of the ambient qi that the cultivator can read). This is the conversation a village priest believes he is having; the Qi Condensation cultivator is the first being who is actually having it.

To a Core Formation cultivator, the place-spirit is fully legible. The cultivator perceives the spirit's quasi-anchor directly (per 00 §2 — Core Formation is the threshold of anchor-perception), reads its coherence, its age, its veneration-input, its jurisdiction's boundaries. The cultivator can also perceive the vein beneath and the community around, and can see how the three sustain each other. A Core Formation cultivator visiting a village for the first time typically perceives the place-spirit within the first hour and may, if courteous, present themselves at the shrine with a small offering — a courtesy the spirit registers and the community does not understand.

### 2.7 Named: the earth god of Wang Family Bend

The earth god of Wang Family Bend is named, in the village's tradition, Wang Tugeng — a name the village gave the spirit three generations ago, when an elder dreamed of an old man in a green robe who said he had been the lineage's first tenant farmer in the founder's time. The dream was a real perception (per §7 below); the spirit did not, in fact, claim to be Wang Tianzu's tenant farmer. The spirit's quasi-anchor is older than the Wang lineage's settlement; it cohered above a minor wood-phase vein's surface trace sometime in the centuries before the Wangs arrived, sustained by the veneration of an earlier community whose name is forgotten. The Wangs adopted the spirit when they settled the bend; the spirit adopted the Wangs in return.

The spirit is, in personality, cautious and conservative. Nine generations of veneration have made it coherent and reasonably powerful for a village-scale place-spirit, but the long memory of the bend's repeated floods (most recently forty years ago, per 24 §4.4) has made it wary. It nudges the village's luck in small ways — a child's fever breaking an hour early, a fox that turns away from the henhouse — but it does not intervene in the larger patterns of the bend's life. The village perceives this as the spirit being "old and tired"; the spirit perceives this as the spirit being prudent. The Azure Sword Sect's Core Formation sect master, Leng Wushuang, has visited the shrine once, sixty years ago, and left a small offering; the spirit remembers the visit and speaks of it (to the rare Qi Condensation cultivator who asks) as the day "the tall one with the cold eyes came and was polite."

The earth god's anchor-object is a small carved soapstone figure, ~30cm tall, depicting an old man in a green robe holding a walking stick. The figure is in the shrine at the west entrance of the village (04 §3). It is the spirit's primary veneration-channel; its destruction would severely damage the spirit.

### 2.8 Named: the river god of the Cangli

The river god of the Cangli is named, in the riverlands' tradition, Lord Cangli — a name the spirit accepts but did not choose. The spirit cohered above the Cangli's main water-phase vein's surface trace roughly four hundred years ago, sustained by the veneration of the riverlands' fishing and ferrying communities along a sixty-li stretch of the middle river. Its jurisdiction is the river itself (water, banks, fords, fish, boats) within that stretch; it has no jurisdiction over the villages on the banks except where they touch the water.

The spirit is, in personality, **temperamental and oath-bound**. The river's floods are real (the river is a real river with real hydrology); the spirit modulates them but does not cause them. The spirit does, however, take oaths sworn at its shrine seriously — an oath sworn at the south-bank temple (04 §5.3) is registered in the spirit's quasi-anchor, and the spirit will, within its jurisdiction, withhold its minor nudges from an oath-broken household. The village's belief that the great flood forty years ago was caused by the river god's anger at a Wang elder's broken oath (24 §4.4) is, in this sense, partially correct: the flood was hydrological, but the spirit's withholding of its nudges (the minor luck-modulations that would have helped the village recover faster) was a real response to the broken oath. The village's theology reads the withholding as the cause of the flood; the cosmology reads the withholding as a minor modulation of a much larger event. Both are true in their register.

The river god's anchor-object is a black wooden plaque inscribed with the spirit's name, kept in the south-bank temple's main hall. The plaque is replaced every thirty years (the river's humidity rots wood); the replacement rite, performed by the Zhengyi priest from the market town, transfers the spirit's pattern from the old plaque to the new. The rite works because the spirit's pattern is in the place, not the plaque; the plaque is the handle, and the handle can be replaced if the spirit consents.

### 2.9 Named: the city god of Cangli County Seat

The city god (城隍) of Cangli County Seat (24 §4.3) is named, in the county's tradition, Magistrate Cang — a name the spirit does not accept and does not refuse. The spirit cohered above an earth-phase vein's surface trace beneath the county seat's old wall, roughly two hundred years ago, sustained by the veneration of the county's bureaucracy and citizenry. Its jurisdiction is the city within the old wall — a denser, more urban jurisdiction than a village earth god's.

The city god is, in personality, **bureaucratic and deputized**. Of the three named place-spirits here, it is the only one with a formal deputyship from the local court magistrate (a Tribulation Crossing cultivator in the courts' Records department, who administers the Jiangnan region from a Precelestial office). The deputyship requires the city god to register, in its own quasi-anchor's memory, every death within its jurisdiction — name, date, cause — and to relay the register to the courts' Records department via a periodic rite performed by the county's Daoist priest. This is the place-spirit's most important function for the courts: it is the **local recorder of the dead**, the first link in the chain by which the dead are processed (see §6 below). The city god does not adjudicate; it records. Its register is duplicated in the courts'生死簿; discrepancies between the two are investigated, and a city god caught falsifying its register is destroyed (its anchor-object shattered, its pattern unwound). This has happened twice in the Cangli County Seat's history, in both cases for bribery by lineages seeking to conceal a murder.

## 3. Ghosts (鬼)

### 3.1 Ontological category

A ghost is an **anchor in extended bardo, anchored to a Mortal place by unresolved attachment**. It is the same ontological category as a living being's anchor (24 §2.2) — a self-inscribed law-pattern, noncopyable, karmic-trace-bearing — but in a state of disembodied persistence. A living being's anchor is integrated with a body and qi (the yin-yang triad of 24 §2.3); a ghost's anchor is severed from body and partially severed from qi, persisting in the bardo state (00 §2) past the typical window.

The bardo state is bounded. Per 00 §2, the bardo is "an intermediate state between death and rebirth that lasts a bounded, lawful time." The typical window is forty-nine days (the genre's traditional period, ratified here as the lawful default). Within that window, the anchor is processed — retrieved by a lineage rite (if the deceased was a great cultivator and the lineage is competent), retrieved by a sect rite (if the deceased was a sect member and the sect is motivated), or processed by the underworld (§6 below) for rebirth. A ghost is what happens when the anchor is **not** processed within the window. The anchor, at the window's end, has three lawful paths: rebirth (the default), final death (寂滅 — the anchor disperses, the being ends), or — if the anchor's karmic trace contains an attachment of sufficient strength — persistence in bardo, anchored to a Mortal place, becoming a ghost.

### 3.2 Formation

A ghost forms when three conditions converge at the bardo window's end.

1. **Unresolved attachment.** The deceased's anchor carries, in its karmic trace, an attachment of sufficient strength that the natural momentum toward rebirth is overcome. The attachment may be grief (for a child not yet grown), hatred (for a killer not yet punished), an oath (unfulfilled), or love (for a surviving spouse the dead cannot leave). Not every strong attachment produces a ghost; the lawful threshold is high. Most strong attachments produce only a slow rebirth (the anchor drawn to a rebirth that reflects the attachment — per 24 §2.1). Only the strongest attachments, in the most extreme cases, overcome the rebirth momentum entirely.

2. **An anchor-point in the Mortal stratum.** The attachment must be anchored to a specific place — the place where the attachment was formed, or the place where the attachment's object resides, or the place where the attachment was betrayed. A ghost cannot roam; it is bound to its anchor-point within a small radius (typically the place itself and a few hundred paces around it). The anchor-point is, in effect, the ghost's substitute for a body — a mortal-place qi-pattern that the disembodied anchor latches onto.

3. **Failure of normal processing.** The lineage's rites did not retrieve the anchor (no lineage, no rites, or rites improperly performed). The sect's rites did not retrieve it (no sect, or sect unaware). The underworld's processing did not retrieve it — usually because the death was not registered (a murder concealed, a death in a remote place, a death the city god's register missed). The bardo window closes with the anchor unprocessed; the attachment and the anchor-point hold it; the ghost forms.

### 3.3 What anchors them

The ghost's persistence is sustained by its attachment and its anchor-point. The attachment is the *reason* the ghost persists; the anchor-point is the *means*. The two together constitute the ghost's substitute for a body's metabolism.

- **Grief** anchors the ghost to the place where the griever lives. The ghost of a mother who died in childbirth may persist, anchored to the household where her child grows, until the child comes of age or the grief is resolved. Grief-ghosts are the most common and the least harmful; they watch, they sometimes nudge, they rarely manifest.
- **Hatred** anchors the ghost to the place where the hatred was incurred. The ghost of a murdered traveler persists at the crossroads where he was killed, anchored by the hatred for his killer. Hatred-ghosts are the most dangerous; they project their hatred onto any living being who enters the anchor-point, and they can, over years, accumulate enough qi-density to manifest visibly and to harm.
- **An oath** anchors the ghost to the place where the oath was sworn or the place where it was broken. The ghost of a sworn sibling who died before fulfilling the oath persists at the sworn place, anchored by the unfulfilled obligation. Oath-ghosts are the most active; they seek, sometimes across centuries, a living being who can fulfill the oath and release them.
- **Love** anchors the ghost to the place where the beloved resides. The ghost of a spouse who died before the other persists, anchored to the household, watching the surviving spouse age. Love-ghosts are the saddest; they rarely manifest, rarely nudge, almost never harm. They simply persist, watching, until the beloved dies and the attachment resolves through reunion in bardo (or, more commonly, the beloved's own rebirth, which severs the love-attachment and releases the ghost).

### 3.4 Capabilities

A ghost, anchored to its anchor-point and sustained by its attachment, can do a limited set of things, all of them qi-mediated modulations of the Mortal stratum.

- **Manifest as a qi-presence.** A ghost with sufficient qi-density (sustained by years of persistence and amplified by mortal fear, which is itself a qi-input) can manifest as a perceptible qi-presence — a coldness, a heaviness, a sound (footsteps, a voice, a name called), a shape (a figure, a face, a hand). The manifestation is not the ghost's body; the ghost has no body. The manifestation is the ghost's anchor's qi-projection, shaped by the ghost's will and colored by its attachment. A mortal perceives the manifestation as a haunting; a Qi Induction cultivator perceives it as a coherent qi-presence with a recognizable signature; a Qi Condensation cultivator can read the attachment directly in the signature's color.

- **Influence the heart-mind of the living.** A ghost can, by projecting its qi-presence into the qi-field of a sleeping or distracted mortal, influence the mortal's heart-mind (心). A grief-ghost projects sorrow; a hatred-ghost projects anger; an oath-ghost projects urgency; a love-ghost projects longing. The influence is subtle — a mood that seems to come from nowhere, a thought that seems the mortal's own. A mortal with strong heart-mind coherence (a Qi Condensation cultivator, a disciplined meditator, a person of profound stability) resists easily; a mortal with weak coherence (the exhausted, the grieving, the sick, the drunk) is susceptible. This is the mechanism behind the mortal world's "ghost-sickness" (14 §6.3) — a chronic low-grade malaise caused by a ghost's persistent influence on a susceptible mortal's heart-mind.

- **Possess the weak.** A ghost with sufficient qi-density and a susceptible mortal host can, in extreme cases, displace the mortal's own qi-coherence and take partial control of the mortal's body. This is **not** true possession (奪舍, per 00 §2) — true possession displaces the anchor; a ghost cannot displace an anchor, only suppress its expression through the body's qi. The mortal's anchor remains; the mortal's will is suppressed. A possessed mortal speaks with the ghost's voice, moves with the ghost's will, and acts out the ghost's attachment. The possession is exhausting for the ghost (it burns qi-density rapidly) and damaging for the mortal (the suppressed anchor, on release, often bears a karmic-trace scar). Possession is rare; it is the ghost's last resort, when no other modality of influence has produced the resolution the attachment seeks.

### 3.5 Ghosts vs ancestors

The distinction is critical and often confused in mortal usage. A **ghost** is an anchor in extended bardo in the Mortal stratum, anchored to a Mortal place by unresolved attachment. It is disembodied, place-bound, attachment-driven, and (usually) temporary — the attachment will eventually resolve (the griever dies, the killer is punished, the oath is fulfilled) and the ghost will move on to rebirth.

An **ancestor** is an anchor that has been retrieved from bardo and lifted into the Precelestial stratum (15 §3), sustained by the lineage's veneration and the ancestral court's qi-authority. It is disembodied in the same sense (no body), but it is not place-bound (it resides in the ancestral court, a Precelestial zone), it is not attachment-driven (it persists by the lineage's rite, not by an unresolved attachment), and it is intended to be permanent (it persists as long as the court and the veneration sustain it). The mortal world conflates the two — the village's "ancestors" include both the lineage-hall's venerated dead (some of whom may be true ancestors in a cultivation lineage, most of whom are merely remembered) and the graveyard's ghosts (some of whom may persist as actual ghosts, most of whom have long since reincarnated). The cosmology distinguishes sharply. A ghost is what happens when the normal system fails; an ancestor is what happens when the normal system succeeds at a high enough level.

### 3.6 Laying to rest

A ghost is laid to rest by one of two methods.

1. **Resolve the attachment.** The grief-ghost is released when the griever dies, or when the griever performs a rite of farewell that the ghost perceives as release. The hatred-ghost is released when the killer is brought to justice (or killed, or forgiven — the ghost's attachment resolves at any of these, regardless of the moral valence). The oath-ghost is released when the oath is fulfilled (by the ghost's surviving kin, by a stranger who takes up the oath, by the ghost's own act through possession). The love-ghost is released when the beloved dies, or when the beloved formally releases the ghost (a rite performed at the grave, with a Daoist priest's assistance). When the attachment resolves, the ghost's anchor is freed from the anchor-point, the bardo closes around it, and it enters the rebirth path it should have entered at the original window's end.

2. **Forcibly disperse the anchor-fragment.** A Core Formation cultivator (the threshold of anchor-perception, per 00 §2) can perceive a ghost's anchor directly and can, with the right technique, disperse the anchor-fragment that constitutes the ghost's persistence in bardo. This is **not** final death (寂滅) in the full sense — the ghost's anchor was already severed from the body at the original death; what is dispersed is the fragment that persisted as the ghost. The dispersal ends the ghost; the being's karmic trace, already recorded in the courts' register, is not erased. The being does not reincarnate from this fragment; the fragment ends. Whether the being's broader anchor (the original law-pattern) persists in some other form is a question the cosmology does not answer; the cosmology specifies only that the ghost ends.

The first method is preferred by every tradition — Buddhist, Daoist, lineage, sect. The second is a last resort, used when the ghost is harmful and the attachment cannot be resolved (a hatred-ghost whose killer is dead and whose hatred persists; an oath-ghost whose oath is impossible to fulfill). The second is criminal under the courts' jurisdiction if performed without authorization — dispersing an anchor-fragment without first attempting resolution is treated as a form of unlawful killing, and a cultivator who does it carelessly faces tribulation (15 §6.2, "killing a being above your realm" can apply if the ghost's original being was of higher station than the cultivator). The Azure Sword Sect's punishment hall has a standing commission for ghost-dispersal in the Cangwu foothills, and the commission's rules require documented attempts at resolution before dispersal is authorized.

### 3.7 The Ghost Festival (中元)

The Ghost Festival is the fifteenth day of the seventh lunar month. Mortal tradition says the gates of the underworld open on this day and the dead walk among the living. The metaphysical reality is more precise and more interesting.

The bardo — the intermediate state between death and rebirth — is not a place; it is a **condition** of an anchor, and the condition has a lawful relationship to the Mortal stratum's ambient qi. The bardo's "boundary" with the Mortal stratum is the threshold at which a disembodied anchor's qi-projection can manifest in Mortal-stratum qi. This threshold varies with the Mortal stratum's qi-climate (14 §5): highest at the summer solstice (yang-dominant, bardo suppressed), lowest at the winter solstice (yin-dominant, bardo amplified). It also varies with the lunar month and the diurnal cycle.

The fifteenth of the seventh month is, by long observation, the day on which the bardo-boundary is **annually thinnest** across the widest geographic range. It is not that the gates open; it is that the boundary is, on this day, thin enough that even weak ghosts (ghosts that cannot manifest on ordinary days) can manifest, and even strong ghosts can manifest more legibly than usual. The thinness lasts roughly three days, and during this window the mortal world's ambient qi is, in places where ghosts persist, threaded with bardo-residue that mortals perceive as the characteristic "ghost-festival feeling" (cold, attentive, watched).

The mortal rites of the festival — the offerings at the shrine, the lineage rite for unburied or unremembered dead, the burning of paper money and paper goods — are **functional**. The offerings are qi-inputs (the food's qi, the incense's qi, the paper-goods' burning-qi) directed at the bardo-boundary, where they are absorbed by whatever anchors are present. The lineage rite for unremembered dead is a controlled bardo-feeding: it provides enough qi-density to fading anchors (the unremembered, the unvenerated, the orphans) to allow them to either resolve their attachment (with the lineage's farewell) or to be processed by the underworld (which uses the festival's thinness as an efficient processing window — see §6 below). The burning of paper money is, metaphysically, the burning of qi-charged paper for the qi-release; the paper-money's "value" in the underworld is a mortal projection onto a real qi-input. The festival is **inauspicious for travel** because the bardo-boundary is thin everywhere, and a traveler moving through unfamiliar places encounters unfamiliar ghosts at near-full manifestation. The mortal advice to stay home is sound advice.

### 3.8 What it feels like to perceive one

To a mortal, a ghost is felt as a chill, a heaviness, a sense of being watched, a mood that seems to come from outside. The mortal does not perceive the ghost as a being; they perceive the ghost's qi-influence on their own heart-mind. A mortal who has lived near a ghost for years develops, without knowing it, a sensitivity to that specific ghost's signature — they "know" when the ghost is active, the way a spouse knows when their partner is in a mood.

To a Qi Induction cultivator, a ghost is perceived as a **cold spot** in the ambient qi — a region where the qi is denser, slower, and yin-dominant in a way that does not match the local qi-climate. The cold spot has a shape (often the shape of the anchor-point — a room, a crossroads, a stretch of riverbank) and a movement (slow, often circular, often returning to a center). The cultivator can feel the ghost's presence but cannot read its attachment.

To a Qi Condensation cultivator, the ghost is legible as a coherent being — an anchor-fragment with a signature, an attachment, a will. The cultivator can read the attachment's type (grief, hatred, oath, love) in the signature's color, and can address the ghost directly. This is the conversation a Daoist priest believes he is having when performing an exorcism; the Qi Condensation cultivator is the first being who is actually having it. Most ghosts, addressed directly by a Qi Condensation cultivator, will speak (in qi-modulations the cultivator can read) about their attachment and what would resolve it. A cultivator who listens and acts on what they hear can lay a ghost to rest without combat. A cultivator who does not listen — who treats the ghost as a thing to be dispersed — must resort to the second method (§3.6), which is harder, slower, and riskier.

To a Core Formation cultivator, the ghost is fully legible — the anchor-fragment perceived directly, its coherence read, its attachment read, its history (the being's life, death, and persistence) readable in the karmic trace. The Core Formation cultivator can also perceive the ghost's relationship to the courts' register — whether the death was recorded, whether the ghost is known to the underworld, whether dispersal would be lawful. A Core Formation cultivator who disperses a ghost without consulting the register risks the courts' attention.

## 4. Yao (妖)

### 4.1 Ontological category — animal with an anchor

A yao is **a beast that has developed an anchor**. It is, in the body/qi/anchor triad (24 §2.3), the only nonhuman being in the Mortal stratum that possesses the full triad — body (the animal's body), qi (the qi the animal has absorbed and circulated, per 14 §2), and anchor (a true self-inscribed law-pattern, noncopyable, karmic-trace-bearing).

This distinguishes the yao sharply from a spirit beast. Per 14 §2, a spirit beast is an animal that has absorbed ambient qi and developed a crude internal circulation. The four tiers (low, mid, high, peak) describe the depth of that qi-cultivation. But a spirit beast, even at peak tier, has only body and qi — it has no anchor. Its persistence is the persistence of an animal; its death is the death of an animal; it does not bear karmic trace; it does not reincarnate. A yao is what happens when a peak-tier spirit beast's inner core (內丹, per 14 §2.2) — the proto-anchor that the peak-tier beast begins to form — **completes its inscription** and becomes a true anchor. This is rare. Most peak-tier beasts never complete the transition; their inner core remains a proto-anchor, a dense qi-structure that resembles an anchor but lacks an anchor's noncopyability, karmic-trace capacity, and persistence across death. The beast dies; the proto-anchor disperses; the being ends.

The peak-tier beast that completes the transition becomes a yao. The yao has, in addition to its animal body and qi, a true anchor — and with the anchor comes everything the anchor implies: karmic trace, persistence across death, the bardo state, the possibility of rebirth, the possibility of final death (寂滅). The yao is, in this sense, the only nonhuman being in the Mortal stratum that is **metaphysically equivalent to a human**. It is not human; it does not have a human body or human cognition or human culture. But it has an anchor, and the anchor is the seat of personhood in this cosmology. This is why the genre treats yao as persons — fox-spirits who seduce, snake-demons who scheme, tree-spirits who protect groves — and not as beasts. The cosmology agrees. A yao is a person, in the metaphysical sense, with an animal body and an animal's ecology. The moral and lawful implications are significant: a yao can be wronged (its anchor can be dispersed unlawfully); a yao can bear karma (its acts inscribe trace on its anchor); a yao can be saved (its anchor can be retrieved from bardo by a lineage or sect rite, if the yao had such ties); a yao can be reborn.

### 4.2 Formation

The transition from peak-tier spirit beast to yao is the rarest event in the Mortal stratum's ecology. It requires:

1. **A peak-tier beast** — already rare, per 14 §2.2; a beast that has formed an inner core and can shapeshift imperfectly.
2. **A tribulation.** The proto-anchor's completion into a true anchor is a boundary-crossing — the being is crossing from "animal with qi" to "being with an anchor." The Precelestial's boundary-enforcement (15 §6) responds. The tribulation is typically a heart-demon tribulation (心魔劫, 15 §6.3 form 2) — the beast, in the moment of anchor-completion, is confronted by the unresolved attachments of its animal life (a predator it has killed, a mate it has lost, a territory it has been driven from). The beast that integrates these attachments into its new anchor becomes a yao. The beast that does not is dispersed — the proto-anchor collapses, the beast dies, the being ends. The tribulation is not a moral judgment; it is a proof that the new anchor can sustain itself.

Most peak-tier beasts, sensing the tribulation approaching, decline to complete the transition. They remain peak-tier beasts for the rest of their natural lifespan (~500 years, per 14 §2.2), then die. The proto-anchor disperses; they are reborn as animals (typically of their own species, with a slight qi-affinity). This is the safe path. The peak-tier beasts that attempt the transition are the ambitious, the desperate, the grief-stricken, or the unlucky. Most of them die. The few that succeed become yao.

### 4.3 The cultivation path

A yao's cultivation path is **harder than a human's** in two ways and different in a third.

1. **The yao begins with an animal body and an animal's cognition.** A human cultivator begins with a body evolved for tool-use, social cognition, and language; the human's cultivation builds on these. A yao begins with a body evolved for its species' niche — a fox's body for stealth and cunning, a snake's for ambush and constriction, a tree's for rooted patience. The yao's cultivation must, first, develop the cognitive and somatic capacities that a human begins with. This is the yao's first additional burden.

2. **The yao must take human form.** The human form is, in this cosmology, the architecture best suited to anchor-expression — the body's meridian system, the brain's cognitive complexity, the vocal apparatus's capacity for speech and oath. A yao that wishes to advance past the most basic anchor-integration must take stable human form. The peak-tier beast's imperfect shapeshifting (the tail that won't vanish, the eyes that stay beast-shaped) becomes, for the yao, a cultivation practice — the yao refines its shapeshifting until the human form is stable, fluent, and indistinguishable (to mortal perception) from a human's. This is the genre's "beast-takes-human-form" trope, and it is, in this cosmology, a metaphysical necessity: the human form is the architecture the anchor can most fully express through.

3. **The yao faces additional tribulations.** Each major realm boundary the yao crosses requires a tribulation that includes, in addition to the standard forms (15 §6.3), a **form-tribulation** — a test of the yao's human form's stability. A yao that crosses Core Formation, Nascent Soul, Spirit Severance, and Tribulation Crossing faces four form-tribulations in addition to the standard tribulations. The form-tribulation tests whether the human form can sustain the new state; failure collapses the human form (the yao reverts to animal shape, often with the proto-anchor damaged, often lethally). This is the yao's third burden: every realm boundary is harder than the equivalent human boundary, by the addition of the form-test.

The yao that survives all four form-tribulations and crosses to the Precelestial becomes a **wayward immortal (妖仙)**, per 14 §2.2 — a being native to the Mortal stratum that has crossed to the Precelestial, with an animal's body, a human's form, and an anchor that has survived every test the cosmos offers. Such beings are rare beyond legend. None are documented in the Cangli Riverlands' history.

### 4.4 Relationship to spirit beasts

The relationship is straightforward and often confused in mortal usage.

A **spirit beast** (14 §2) is an animal with qi. It has body and qi; it has no anchor. It lives an animal's life, dies an animal's death, and (if it has lived long enough over a spirit vein) may have advanced through the four tiers. Its inner core (at peak tier) is a proto-anchor, not a true anchor.

A **yao** is an animal that has developed an anchor. It has body, qi, and anchor. It is metaphysically a person, with everything that implies. Its body is still an animal's body; its qi is still its species' qi; but its anchor is a true anchor, and the anchor changes everything.

The confusion arises because the mortal world calls both "demons" (妖, the same character used for the yao) and treats both as threats. The cosmology distinguishes sharply. A mid-tier spirit tiger that preys on livestock is a beast problem, solvable by hunting. A tiger-yao that preys on livestock is a person committing a crime, solvable by negotiation, treaty, or (if the yao refuses to cease) lawful killing — and lawful killing of a yao is, under most jurisdictions, treated as the killing of a person, with the karmic and lawful consequences that entails (24 §2.1, 15 §6.2). A Qi Condensation cultivator hunting a mid-tier spirit wolf is hunting an animal; the same cultivator hunting a wolf-yao is killing a person. The two acts feel similar (the cultivator tracks, fights, kills a wolf-shaped being) but are metaphysically and lawfully distinct. The cultivator who cannot tell the difference — who kills a yao thinking it a spirit beast — bears the karmic trace of the killing nonetheless, and faces the tribulation that killing a being of equivalent station triggers (15 §6.2). This is why sects train their hunting disciples to distinguish spirit beasts from yao, and why a hunting disciple who reports a yao-kill is subject to a sect investigation.

### 4.5 The fox-spirit (狐仙)

The fox-spirit of the Cangwu foothills is named, in the market town's tradition, Lady Bai — a name she did not give and does not use. She is a red fox (Vulpes vulpes) who achieved peak-tier spirit beast status roughly four hundred years ago, in the eastern Cangwu range, over a minor wood-phase vein. She completed the anchor-transition roughly two hundred and fifty years ago, surviving a heart-demon tribulation whose central attachment was the memory of her first mate, killed by a hunter in her second century. She took stable human form — a young woman of about twenty, with dark hair, dark eyes, and (in moments of inattention) a faint reddish cast to her shadow — roughly two hundred years ago.

Lady Bai's cultivation is, by human reckoning, late Foundation Establishment — she has integrated body, qi, and anchor into a coherent system, and her human form is fluent. She has not crossed Core Formation; the form-tribulation at Core Formation is the next test, and she has been preparing for it for thirty years. Her ecology is the ecology of a fox — she hunts small game in the eastern Cangwu, she avoids the larger predators (the Cangwu Sect's mid-tier beasts), she mates (occasionally, with mortal foxes, producing offspring that are mortal with slight qi-affinity), she dens in a copse of old-growth oak above the wood-phase vein that sustained her peak-tier cultivation.

Her relationship with mortals is the genre's classic fox-spirit relationship. She is curious about humans (a yao's cognition includes the curiosity its anchor permits); she occasionally takes human form and walks in the market town (Qinghe Market Town, 15 li east of Wang Family Bend) to listen to gossip and buy fruit (she pays with copper she has acquired honestly, by healing a farmer's child in exchange for the coins — a transaction neither party fully understands); she has, twice in the last century, taken a mortal lover (in both cases a young scholar; in both cases the relationship ended when the scholar aged past her ability to maintain the pretense, and she left, leaving behind a grief that is now part of her karmic trace). She has never harmed a mortal unprovoked. She is, by any reasonable measure, a good-faith resident of the Cangwu foothills.

The mortal world's tradition of fox-spirits as seducers and tricksters is a partial truth derived from cases like Lady Bai's: yao-foxes do take human form, do walk among humans, do occasionally form attachments. The tradition's elaboration — that fox-spirits steal men's semen to fuel their cultivation, that they are uniformly malicious, that they can be exorcised by Daoist priests — is mortal projection. Lady Bai's cultivation is sustained by the wood-phase vein and her own practice, not by any human substance. She is not malicious. A Daoist priest's exorcism has no effect on her (she has an anchor; an exorcism designed to disperse a ghost's anchor-fragment does not work on a true anchor integrated with a living body). The Azure Sword Sect knows of her: the previous sect master (who pursued Zhou Wugui, §5.4 below) consulted her once, seventy years ago, on the question of whether a particular Cangwu foothills spirit beast was a yao or not (it was not; he let it live). The current sect master, Leng Wushuang, has not contacted her. The Cangwu Sect (the small local sect, 24 §4.3) treats her as a known presence in their territory and leaves her alone, because she is more powerful than their sect master and because she has never caused them trouble. The mortal world mostly does not know she exists; the few who have met her in human form remember a young woman from the market town whom they cannot quite place.

### 4.6 The snake-demon (蛇妖)

The snake-demon of the Cangwu foothills is named, in the local tradition, the Black Creek Serpent — a name the yao itself does not use (it does not speak, having never developed the capacity, and does not take human form). It is a king cobra (Ophiophagus hannah) that achieved peak-tier spirit beast status roughly six hundred years ago in the western Cangwu, over a water-phase vein. It attempted the anchor-transition three hundred years ago and survived — barely — a heart-demon tribulation whose central attachment was the memory of a predator (a Cangwu Sect Core Formation cultivator, two centuries dead, who had killed its first mate). The yao's anchor is true; its cognition is not. The transition completed the anchor but did not complete the cognitive development that usually accompanies it.

The Black Creek Serpent is, in consequence, a yao with the cognition of a peak-tier spirit beast and the anchor of a Foundation Establishment-equivalent being. It does not take human form (it has not developed the cognitive capacity for the practice); it does not speak; it does not negotiate. It hunts (other snakes, small mammals, occasionally a mid-tier spirit beast it can overpower), it defends its territory (a five-li stretch of the western Cangwu's black-creek valley), and it kills humans who enter its territory without the sense to retreat. It has killed eleven humans in the last century — mostly charcoal burners and herb-gatherers who wandered into its valley.

The Cangwu Sect treats it as a problem. The Azure Sword Sect treats it as a Cangwu Sect problem. The immortal alliance's regional office (12 §6) has, twice, asked the Azure Sword Sect to disperse it; the Azure Sword Sect has, twice, declined, on the grounds that the yao has an anchor (and is therefore a person under alliance law) and that dispersal would be unlawful without a documented attempt at resolution. The documented attempts at resolution have failed (the yao does not negotiate; the territory cannot be ceded because mortal charcoal burners depend on it). The situation is, at present, a slow-burning problem that no one has the will or the resources to solve. A mortal who enters the western Cangwu's black-creek valley and does not return is mourned by their family and not avenged by the sects. This is the cultivation world's moral failure at small scale, and it is common.

### 4.7 The thousand-year tree-spirit (樹精)

The thousand-year tree-spirit of the eastern Cangwu is named, in the lineage traditions of three Cangwu-village lineages, Old Cypress — a name the yao accepts (it perceives the name, in the qi-modulations of the lineages' veneration, as a qi-input that feeds its anchor). It is a cypress tree (Platycladus orientalis) that achieved peak-tier spirit beast status roughly eight hundred years ago, over a wood-phase vein that surfaces beneath its roots. It completed the anchor-transition roughly five hundred years ago, surviving a heart-demon tribulation whose central attachment was — and remains — the memory of the grove it had grown in, which was logged by a mortal lineage four hundred years before its transition. The grove's loss is the yao's defining attachment; it has spent the last five centuries protecting the remaining cypress grove of which it is the oldest tree.

Old Cypress cannot move. Its body is a tree; its body's roots are in the earth; the yao is, in its animal-ecology sense, a sessile being. It does not take human form (it has no need to; its anchor's expression does not require a human body when the body is a tree that lives for centuries). Its cognition is slow — tree-time, not animal-time; a thought takes days, a decision takes weeks. It perceives (through the wood-phase qi of its roots and canopy) everything within a half-li radius: every being that enters its grove, every change in the ambient qi, every act of veneration or harm directed at the trees.

Its cultivation is, by human reckoning, late Foundation Establishment, sustained by the wood-phase vein and by the veneration of the three Cangwu-village lineages that have, for centuries, treated the grove as sacred. The veneration-qi feeds its anchor the way veneration-qi feeds a place-spirit (§2.2 above); Old Cypress is, in this sense, both a yao and the place-spirit of its grove. The cosmology permits this overlap; the two categories are not mutually exclusive when a yao's body is the place.

Old Cypress protects its grove. A mortal who cuts a cypress in the grove dies — the yao, perceiving the cut through its root-network, routes a surge of wood-phase qi through the soil that stops the mortal's heart (a mortal's heart is a delicate thing; a wood-phase qi-surge through the earth is enough). A mortal who venerates at the grove's central tree is blessed — the yao routes a minor wood-phase qi-nudge that strengthens the mortal's constitution for a season. A Qi Condensation cultivator who enters the grove with hostile intent is challenged — the yao's roots shift, the canopy thickens, the ambient qi becomes heavy and difficult. A Foundation Establishment cultivator who enters the grove with hostile intent is fought — the yao can project its anchor's qi as a quasi-domain (not a true Spirit Severance domain, but a localized qi-authority within its grove), and within the grove it is the equal of a mid-Foundation Establishment human cultivator.

Old Cypress has, in its five centuries as a yao, killed thirty-two mortals (all cypress-cutters) and zero cultivators (the sects know the grove is protected and do not contest it). The Cangwu Sect treats it with wary respect; the Azure Sword Sect treats it as a regional asset (a Foundation Establishment yao protecting a wood-phase vein is a stabilizing presence); the three Cangwu-village lineages venerate it. Old Cypress remembers every name venerated at its central tree; its anchor's karmic trace is a record of five centuries of grove-life, and the record is the yao's most valued possession.

### 4.8 What it feels like to perceive one

To a mortal, a yao in animal form is perceived as a spirit beast of its tier — unusually wary, unusually intelligent, with a quality of presence that the mortal cannot quite name. The mortal cannot tell a yao from a peak-tier spirit beast; both feel "more than animal." The mortal who knows the local ecology well may notice that this particular fox/serpent/tree has been here for as long as anyone can remember, which is itself an indicator (peak-tier beasts live ~500 years; yao live longer).

To a Qi Induction cultivator, a yao in animal form is perceived as a peak-tier spirit beast with an anomalous qi-pattern — the proto-anchor's qi-density is present, but there is an additional quality, a depth, that the proto-anchor does not have. The cultivator cannot read the additional quality; they perceive it as "this beast is different in a way I cannot describe." This perception is the first hint a cultivator gets that they are dealing with a yao and not a spirit beast.

To a Qi Condensation cultivator, the yao's anchor is legible as a presence — not as a fully perceived anchor (that requires Core Formation), but as a coherent qi-presence with a quality the cultivator recognizes as anchor-like. A Qi Condensation cultivator who has met a yao before can identify one reliably; a Qi Condensation cultivator encountering a yao for the first time often mistakes it for an unusually powerful peak-tier beast, with sometimes disastrous consequences.

To a Core Formation cultivator, the yao's anchor is fully legible. The cultivator perceives the anchor's coherence, its karmic trace, its attachments, its cultivation-state. The cultivator can address the yao directly (in qi-modulations the yao can read), and the yao can respond. This is the conversation the mortal tradition of "negotiating with the demon" describes; the Core Formation cultivator is the first human who can actually have it. A Core Formation cultivator meeting Lady Bai for the first time typically perceives, within the first minute, a young woman whose anchor is a fox's, whose karmic trace includes two centuries of curiosity and two griefs, and whose cultivation is at the threshold of Core Formation. The cultivator's appropriate response is courtesy; most Core Formation cultivators, meeting Lady Bai, extend it.

## 5. Demonic cultivators (魔修)

### 5.1 Ontological category and the cosmology's one true evil

A demonic cultivator is **a cultivator who uses other beings' anchors as fuel**. The ontological category is "human cultivator" — body, qi, anchor, the standard triad. What makes the demonic cultivator demonic is not a different ontology; it is a specific practice that the cosmology, the courts, the alliance, and every sect and lineage treat as the one true evil.

The evil is metaphysical, not moral. The cosmology's anchor (24 §2.2) is noncopyable; it is the being's persistent law-pattern; it is what makes the being a being rather than a process. Final death (寂滅, per 00 §2) is the dispersion of the anchor — the one true end, the unrecoverable. The cosmos permits final death in specific lawful circumstances: high-realm conflict, tribulation failure, the natural expiry of the bardo window. These are lawful because they are either the being's own act (the cultivator who pushes a tribulation past their capacity), a contest between peers, or the cosmos's own boundary-enforcement.

The demonic cultivator's act is **the deliberate dispersion of another being's anchor for the cultivator's own power**. This is the cosmology's one true evil because it is the one act that produces **irreversible metaphysical destruction** for the cultivator's benefit. Killing a mortal inscribes a karmic trace (24 §2.1), but the mortal's anchor persists in bardo and enters rebirth — the being continues. Killing a cultivator inscribes a heavier trace, but the cultivator's anchor can be retrieved, can persist in an ancestral court, can be reborn. Even unlawful killing leaves the anchor intact; the being's persistence is not destroyed, only the body is. The demonic cultivator destroys the persistence: the harvested anchor's law-pattern is unwound, its karmic trace is erased, the being's continuity is ended. There is no rebirth from a dispersed anchor. There is no ancestral court. There is no future life, no future meeting, no future redemption. The being is gone, finally and irreversibly, and the cultivator has done it for power.

This is the one act that the cosmos cannot repair. Tribulation can be retaken. Karma can be processed. Deviation can be treated. Even final death by tribulation is, in a sense, the cosmos's lawful boundary-enforcement — the cultivator took a risk and the risk did not pay. The demonic cultivator's act is none of these. It is the unilateral, irreversible, unauthorized destruction of a being's persistence for the destroyer's gain. The courts have no record that restores it. The ancestral courts have no anchor to retrieve. The being is gone, and the cultivator who did it has the being's power.

### 5.2 The technique

The demonic cultivator's technique is the harvesting and refining of anchors. The specifics vary by lineage and innovation, but the general form is constant.

1. **Harvest.** The demonic cultivator seeks a being whose anchor is accessible — a dying cultivator (the anchor is in bardo, easy to perceive and grasp), a freshly dead mortal (the anchor is in the bardo window, still coherent), a ghost (the anchor-fragment is already disembodied and persistent), a yao (the anchor is in a body, but the body can be killed). The harvest is a qi-technique that grasps the anchor in bardo and draws it into the cultivator's own qi-reservoir. The act is, for the victim, final death — the anchor is removed from the bardo and prevented from entering rebirth.

2. **Refine.** The harvested anchor is, in the cultivator's qi-reservoir, a foreign law-pattern — noncopyable, coherent, hostile to the cultivator's own anchor. The cultivator cannot integrate it; the cosmos's noncopyability forbids that. The cultivator can, however, **unwind** it — break the foreign anchor's law-pattern into raw law-substrate, which the cultivator's own qi can then absorb. The unwinding is the act of dispersion; the foreign anchor's coherence is destroyed, its karmic trace is erased, the being's persistence ends. The raw law-substrate is metabolized by the cultivator's qi as a dense, undifferentiated qi-input, accelerating the cultivator's cultivation.

3. **Burn.** The cultivator's cultivation, fed by the refined anchor, advances. The acceleration is significant — a single harvested anchor can advance a Qi Condensation cultivator to Foundation Establishment, or a Foundation Establishment cultivator toward Core Formation. The acceleration is also corrupting — the refined anchor's residue (the karmic trace, which the unwinding does not fully erase but rather disperses into the cultivator's own qi) accumulates in the cultivator's heart-mind, producing 心魔 (24 §1.2, the delusional conviction) at minimum, and often the full psychospiritual break. The demonic cultivator's career is short; the cultivator either advances past their capacity to integrate the accumulated residue (and deviates, often lethally) or is killed by other cultivators (see §5.3).

### 5.3 Criminality

Demonic cultivation is criminal under every jurisdiction in the cosmos.

- **Under the Courts of Heaven.** The courts' Records department (15 §2.2) maintains the register of the dead. A demonic cultivator's harvest removes anchors from the bardo without the courts' processing, falsifying the register (the dead are recorded as dead, but their anchors' disposition is unknown). This is record-tampering, the courts' most-prosecuted internal crime; when committed by an Acquired cultivator against the dead, it is treated as a more serious offense — a form of contempt of court that triggers the courts' enforcement attention. The courts' Defense department (15 §2.3) maintains a standing commission for demonic-cultivator suppression, and a demonic cultivator who reaches the courts' attention can expect a Tribulation Crossing cultivator to be dispatched.

- **Under the immortal alliance (12 §6).** The alliance's founding treaty (12 §6.3) names demonic cultivation as one of the three threats the alliance exists to suppress. Any member sect that harbors a demonic cultivator is in breach of its founding oath and subject to alliance sanctions up to and including the member's removal and the sect's dissolution. The alliance's regional offices maintain demonic-suppression commissions; the Azure Sword Sect's commission (24 §4.4 references the sect's previous master's pursuit of Zhou Wugui) is one such.

- **Under sect law.** Every sect's punishment hall (12 §1.1) treats demonic cultivation as the highest crime. A disciple discovered to be a demonic cultivator is killed (not merely expelled); an elder discovered is killed with greater ceremony but equal finality. The sect's founder's token (12 §1.1) is, in some sects, used to authorize the killing — a sect master who kills a demonic cultivator with the founder's token is, in some traditions, held to have performed a sacred duty.

- **Under lineage law.** A lineage that discovers a demonic cultivator in its membership expels the cultivator and erases their name from the genealogy. The expelled cultivator's anchor, on their eventual death, is not retrieved by the lineage's rite; the cultivator is, in the lineage's eyes, already metaphysically dead. A lineage that harbors a demonic cultivator knowingly is subject to alliance sanction and, in extreme cases, to extermination by a coalition of neighboring lineages.

- **Under karma.** The harvested anchor's karmic trace, dispersed into the cultivator's own qi, accumulates as the heaviest possible karmic trace on the cultivator's anchor. The cultivator's karmic ledger (24 §2.1) becomes, within a few harvests, unsustainable. At the cultivator's next realm-boundary tribulation (15 §6.3 form 4), the karmic tribulation is overwhelming; failure disperses the cultivator's own anchor (final death, 寂滅), which is the cosmos's lawful resolution of an unsustainable ledger. A demonic cultivator who reaches Core Formation is rare; one who reaches Nascent Soul is rarer still; one who reaches Spirit Severance is legend, and the legend always ends in karmic tribulation and dispersal. The cosmos's karma system is, in this sense, the ultimate enforcement against demonic cultivation: the demonic cultivator's power is borrowed against a karmic debt the cosmos will eventually call.

### 5.4 The Zhou Wugui incursion

The Zhou Wugui incursion (24 §4.4) is the Cangli Riverlands' most recent demonic-cultivator event, ninety-five years ago. The facts, as established by the Azure Sword Sect's investigation and recorded in the sect's punishment hall archives, are these.

Zhou Wugui was a Qi Condensation cultivator of the Cangwu Sect (24 §4.3) — a middling disciple, not particularly talented, who had been at Qi Condensation for fifteen years without significant progress. He was, by the sect's records, a quiet and unremarkable member, the third son of a minor Cangwu-village lineage, who had entered the sect at twelve and shown no special aptitude. He disappeared from the sect at thirty-seven, ostensibly on a long herb-gathering trip; the sect marked him as a washout and did not search.

In the eighteen months following his disappearance, three villages in the Cangwu foothills — White Stone Village, Mill Ford, and the original settlement at Black Creek (later re-founded, after the incursion, a half-li downstream) — suffered a sequence of deaths that the villagers attributed first to plague, then to a hunting spirit beast, then to the wrath of a place-spirit whose shrine had been neglected. The deaths were, in total, forty-one mortals across the three villages, over eighteen months. The victims were mostly the dying (the elderly, the chronically sick) and the recently dead (whose funerals had been delayed by poverty or weather). The deaths were not violent; the victims simply died, or failed to recover from illnesses they had been surviving.

The Azure Sword Sect's previous sect master (a Core Formation cultivator whose name is recorded in the sect's archives as Leng Wushuang's master, but whose personal name the sect does not publish) was alerted by the Cangwu Sect after the Cangwu Sect's own investigation produced no beast and no place-spirit grievance. The Azure Sword sect master visited the three villages, perceived (at Core Formation) the qi-residue of anchor-harvesting at the death-sites, and pursued.

The pursuit lasted three months. Zhou Wugui, by the time of the pursuit, had advanced from Qi Condensation to late Foundation Establishment — an extraordinary acceleration, sustained by the harvested anchors of forty-one mortals. He had also, by the pursuit's end, accumulated enough karmic residue to be visibly unstable: his qi-signature was mottled, his heart-mind was fragmented, he had begun speaking to the dead (whose residue he carried) as if they were present. He was, at the moment of the Azure Sword sect master's confrontation, in a Foundation Establishment state that should have been coherent but was not.

The confrontation took place in the western Cangwu, in a ravine that the locals still call the Demon's Cut. The Azure Sword sect master, by his own account (recorded in the sect's punishment hall archives), offered Zhou Wugui the chance to surrender for trial. Zhou Wugui refused. The fight lasted, by the sect master's account, "less time than I expected." Zhou Wugui's anchor was dispersed in the fight — not by the sect master's technique (which would have killed the body but left the anchor in bardo), but by the cultivator's own karmic tribulation, which fired during the fight (the cultivator's accumulated karmic debt, amplified by the combat's stress, triggered the tribulation, which he could not survive). The sect master perceived the anchor disperse; the cultivator ended; the incursion was over.

The Azure Sword sect master returned to the three villages, performed the proper rites for the dead (the lineage rites the villages had not been able to afford; the sect paid for them from its own stores), and ordered the Cangwu Sect to maintain a standing watch in the foothills — a watch maintained, with varying diligence, for ninety-five years. The event is still spoken of in the market town, in the form the mortal tradition prefers — a story of a demon who came down from the mountains and was slain by a wandering hero. The metaphysical reality — that a middling Qi Condensation cultivator destroyed forty-one mortals' persistence for eighteen months of acceleration, and that the cosmos's own karmic enforcement ended him — is not the story the mortal world tells. The story the cultivation world tells is more precise, and more cautionary.

### 5.5 The genre antagonist

The demonic cultivator is, in this cosmology, the genre's antagonist category. Not because every antagonist is a demonic cultivator (the genre's antagonists include the corrupt court official, the arrogant sect elder, the rogue cultivator, the rival lineage, the corrupted ancestor) but because the demonic cultivator is the one antagonist whose existence is **metaphysically criminal** — whose act is the one act the cosmos itself, through karma, will not permit to persist.

This makes the demonic cultivator a useful antagonist for three reasons. First, the demonic cultivator's guilt is not in question; the cosmology has decided it. A protagonist who pursues one is enforcing a metaphysical judgment, not making a moral one. Second, the demonic cultivator's power is real but unstable; the protagonist who faces one faces an antagonist already on a countdown to karmic tribulation. The protagonist does not need to outpower the demonic cultivator; they need to survive long enough for the cosmos's enforcement to fire. Third, the demonic cultivator's harvest is irreversible; the protagonist who arrives too late cannot undo the harm. The protagonist's failure is measured in dispersed anchors, not in personal defeat.

The genre's tradition of demonic cultivators as irredeemable is ratified. A demonic cultivator cannot be redeemed in the sense of "their crime is forgiven"; the dispersed anchors cannot be recovered. They can, however, be **lawfully killed**, and the killing is a sacred duty rather than a sin. The protagonist who kills a demonic cultivator inscribes a killing-trace on their own anchor (per 24 §2.1), but the trace is light (the cultivator's own karmic ledger was unsustainable; the killing is the cosmos's enforcement, mediated by the protagonist's hand). The cosmos, in effect, recognizes the killing as lawful.

### 5.6 What it feels like to perceive one

To a mortal, a demonic cultivator is indistinguishable from any other cultivator — perhaps a trifle cold, perhaps with a faint wrongness to the air around them, but not perceptibly different. A mortal who has lived near a demonic cultivator's harvest-site may perceive the site as "haunted" (a cold, heavy, attended-to quality that is in fact the qi-residue of dispersed anchors), but they do not perceive the cultivator.

To a Qi Induction cultivator, a demonic cultivator is perceived as a cultivator with an anomalous qi-signature — denser than expected for their realm, with a mottled quality (the dispersed anchors' residue, not fully metabolized) that the cultivator cannot quite read. The Qi Induction cultivator perceives that something is wrong; they cannot say what.

To a Qi Condensation cultivator, a demonic cultivator is legible as wrong — the mottled qi-signature is recognizable as the residue of foreign anchors, and the cultivator can, if they have been trained, identify it as demonic-cultivator residue. This is the threshold at which a cultivator can reliably identify a demonic cultivator; the sects train their Qi Condensation disciples in the identification, and a Qi Condensation cultivator who perceives a demonic cultivator is expected to report to their sect immediately rather than engage.

To a Core Formation cultivator, the demonic cultivator's anchor is fully legible, and the karmic trace on it is overwhelming — the harvested anchors' dispersed karmic residue is visible as a heavy, mottled, anguished weight on the cultivator's own anchor. A Core Formation cultivator perceiving a demonic cultivator for the first time typically reports a visceral reaction — grief, anger, nausea — that is the perception of the dispersed beings' residue, not the perceiver's own emotion. This reaction is itself a perceptual datum; the cultivator who has it is perceiving, at the heart-mind level, the metaphysical wrongness of the demonic cultivator's act. The cultivator who does not have it — who perceives the demonic cultivator without grief or anger — is themselves suspect, and the sects watch such cultivators carefully.

## 6. The Underworld (地府)

### 6.1 What it is and is not

The Underworld (地府) is **a sub-jurisdiction of the Courts of Heaven**. It is not a separate stratum; it is not a separate institution; it is not a separate cosmology. It is the Records department's enforcement arm — the part of the courts' apparatus that processes anchors in bardo for rebirth.

The mortal world's tradition of the Underworld — the ten courts of Yama (十殿閻羅), the mirror-pools of reflection, the bridges of gold and silver, the eighteen hells of punishment, the wheel of rebirth — is **partial myth**. The beings the tradition names (Yama, the ten kings, the demonic punishers) are mortal projections onto a bureaucratic process. The places the tradition describes (the halls of mirror-reflection, the punishment-hells, the rebirth-bridge) are mortal interpretations of processing-stages whose actual nature is more mundane.

The Underworld is, in actuality, **a vast, dim, orderly archive** where anchors in bardo wait to be processed. It is staffed by courts-appointed officials — Tribulation Crossing cultivators who took office in the Records department (15 §2.3) and were assigned to the Underworld's processing arm. There is no Yama. There is no Buddhist-influenced ten-kings hierarchy. There are clerks, magistrates, and ministers, the same hierarchy as the rest of the courts, doing the specific work of bardo-processing.

### 6.2 What it looks like

The Underworld is located in the Precelestial Stratum, in a region administered by the courts but physically separate from the courts' main territory. It is accessed (by cultivators who have lawful business there) through a specific maintained road that descends from the courts' main territory into a lower, quieter, dimmer region of the Precelestial.

The first impression is **dimness**. The Precelestial's general quality of "thick" time (15 §1.7) is here amplified; the air feels heavy, the light is diffuse and unsaturated, the qi is dense but slow. The Underworld's qi-climate is yin-dominant — cold, still, condensing — which is the appropriate climate for bardo-processing (bardo is a yin-state; the anchors in bardo are sustained by yin-qi).

The second impression is **order**. The Underworld is laid out as a vast archive, with corridors of shelves, each shelf holding tablets of jade (the medium the courts use for permanent records). The tablets record every anchor currently in processing — the anchor's karmic trace, the bardo window's remaining time, the rebirth-path being calculated. The shelves extend in every direction; the archive's scale is, to a mortal cultivator's perception, effectively infinite (it is not; the cosmos is finite, per 00 §1; but the archive is vast enough that its finite extent is not perceivable from any single vantage).

The third impression is **quiet**. The anchors in bardo are present, but they do not speak; they wait. The officials move through the corridors with the unhurried purpose of clerks everywhere. The processing itself — the reading of karmic trace, the calculation of rebirth-path, the assignment — is performed in silence or in low, formal speech. The Underworld is not the fiery, screaming hell of popular imagination; it is an archive, and archives are quiet. The mortal tradition's eighteen hells are mortal projection onto the karmic-trace-reading process — the anchor, in processing, is confronted with its own karmic ledger, which the mortal imagination (informed by mortal guilt) projects as a punishment. The processing is not a punishment; it is a reading. The anchor's karmic trace is read, the rebirth-path is calculated (an anchor with heavy killing-trace is drawn to rebirths that reflect the entanglement, per 24 §2.1 — this is not punishment, it is causal momentum), and the anchor is assigned. The mortal tradition's "punishment" is the anchor's own karmic momentum, perceived (by the anchor, in the bardo's heightened self-perception) as a confrontation with the being's own acts. The Underworld does not punish; it processes. The processing can be painful (confronting one's own karmic ledger is not pleasant), but the pain is the anchor's own, not the Underworld's.

### 6.3 What happens there

The Underworld's processing of an anchor in bardo follows a fixed sequence.

1. **Registration.** The anchor, on the death of the body, enters bardo. The local place-spirit's register (the city god's register, §2.9 above) records the death and relays it (via the periodic rite) to the courts' Records department. The Records department assigns the anchor a processing-clerk, who locates the anchor in bardo (a nontrivial task; bardo is not a place but a condition, and locating a specific anchor requires a Tribulation Crossing cultivator's perception) and brings it into the Underworld's archive.

2. **Karmic reading.** The processing-clerk reads the anchor's karmic trace — every act that affected another being's anchor, every oath, every debt, every healing, every harm. The reading is performed by the clerk's perception of the anchor's law-pattern, which carries the trace as inscriptions upon itself (24 §2.2). Nothing is omitted. The anchor, in this stage, is present and perceiving — the bardo-state permits a heightened self-perception that the mortal tradition remembers as the "mirror-pool of reflection." The anchor confronts its own karmic ledger in full, which is (for an anchor with heavy trace) the closest the cosmology comes to the mortal tradition's "punishment."

3. **Rebirth-path calculation.** Based on the karmic reading, the clerk calculates the rebirth-path — the next embodiment that reflects the anchor's karmic momentum. An anchor with heavy killing-trace is drawn to a violent rebirth; an anchor with healing-trace is drawn to a rebirth in a healing profession; an anchor with unprocessed grief is drawn to a rebirth that will re-present the grief for processing. The calculation is not moral; it is causal. The clerk does not judge; the clerk computes.

4. **Assignment.** The anchor is assigned to its rebirth-path. The assignment is recorded in the courts' register; the anchor is escorted (by a minor official) to the boundary between bardo and the Mortal stratum, where it enters the rebirth-path and is drawn to its next embodiment — typically a mortal birth (most anchors return to the Mortal stratum); rare assignments are to animal rebirths (for anchors whose karmic momentum is best expressed in animal form) or, rarer still, to Acquired-stratum rebirths (for anchors whose cultivation-state and karmic momentum qualify them for a cultivator's rebirth).

5. **Special cases.** Some anchors are not assigned to rebirth. An anchor retrieved by a lineage rite (for a great cultivator's persistence in an ancestral court, per 15 §3.2) is released to the lineage's retrieval before processing. An anchor retrieved by a sect rite is similarly released. An anchor whose karmic ledger is unsustainable (the heaviest possible trace, the residue of acts the cosmos cannot process into a rebirth-path) is dispersed — final death, 寂滅 — as the lawful resolution. An anchor that has become a ghost (§3 above) is, in principle, retrieved by the Underworld's enforcement arm and processed; in practice, many ghosts persist long enough to be processed only by resolution of their attachment (§3.6) rather than by Underworld retrieval.

### 6.4 The myth vs the reality

The mortal tradition's Underworld is **partially true**. The dead do go somewhere — the anchors in bardo are processed by the Underworld. The processing does involve a confrontation with one's karmic ledger — the mirror-pool of reflection is a mortal image of the karmic-reading stage. The processing does result in rebirth — the wheel of rebirth is a mortal image of the rebirth-path assignment. The mortal tradition's general shape — death, judgment, rebirth — is correct.

The mortal tradition's specifics are projection. There is no Yama; the ten kings are mortal imaginations of the Underworld's bureaucratic hierarchy. There are no eighteen punishment-hells; the "punishment" is the anchor's own karmic confrontation, not an external torment. There are no bridges of gold and silver; the rebirth-path is a calculated assignment, not a chosen crossing. The mortal tradition's elaboration is mortal culture's interpretation of a process the mortal tradition has not directly perceived.

The mortal tradition's purpose — to give the dead a comprehensible fate, to give the living a moral framework, to give the community a ritual structure for mourning — is real and valuable. The Underworld does not require the mortal tradition to be metaphysically accurate; it requires only that the mortal tradition's practices (the funerals, the offerings, the lineage rites) continue to feed the place-spirits and the lineages and the courts' record-keeping. The machinery does not care whether the mortals understand it correctly; it cares whether the mortals perform the rites.

A Core Formation cultivator who visits the Underworld (a rare event, requiring the courts' authorization) typically reports a sense of recognition — the archive resembles, in its structure and its quiet, every library and lineage hall and magistrate's office the cultivator has ever known. The Underworld is the cosmos's acknowledgment that even the dead require a bureaucracy: it is not magical; it is lawful, and law requires administration, and administration requires clerks, and clerks require quiet archives.

### 6.5 What it feels like to perceive (a trace of) it

The Underworld is not directly perceptible to mortals or to most cultivators. A mortal who has attended a funeral at which a Core Formation cultivator is performing the soul-rite may, in the moment of the rite's climax, perceive a faint coldness and a faint attentiveness — the trace of the Underworld's processing, mediated by the cultivator's qi. This perception is fleeting and usually mistaken for grief.

A Qi Condensation cultivator performing a funeral rite perceives, in the rite's climax, a thinning of the boundary between the Mortal stratum and the bardo — the rite's purpose is to thin this boundary, allowing the deceased's anchor to be processed more cleanly. The cultivator perceives the thinning as a cold, attentive quality to the air, and may (if sensitive) perceive a faint presence — the processing-clerk, dispatched from the Underworld to receive the anchor, perceived as a vague, formal, attentive qi-presence.

A Core Formation cultivator performing a soul-rite perceives the processing-clerk directly — a Tribulation Crossing cultivator in the Records department's processing arm, manifesting at the rite's site as a qi-presence with the cold, formal, attentive quality of the Underworld's archive. The Core Formation cultivator can address the clerk (in qi-modulations the clerk can read), confirm the deceased's identity and karmic state, and release the anchor to the clerk's custody. This is the conversation the mortal tradition's "soul-guiding" describes; the Core Formation cultivator is the first being who can actually have it. A Nascent Soul cultivator with the courts' authorization may visit the Underworld directly, walking the maintained road down into the archive. The visit is, by all accounts, profoundly affecting — the scale of the archive, the quiet of the processing, the weight of every anchor's karmic ledger being read in the dim light. The Azure Sword Sect's Leng Wushuang is reported (in the sect's archives, by the previous sect master's account) to have visited the Underworld once, two centuries ago, and to have described the visit as "the most useful day I have spent in three hundred years of cultivation."

## 7. Dreams (夢)

### 7.1 Ontological category — perception mode

A dream is **a perception mode, not a stratum**. Dreams are not a place beings go; dreams are a way beings perceive — specifically, the perception of the anchor when the body's sensory constraints are suspended.

The body, in the body/qi/anchor triad (24 §2.3), is yin-dominant — dense, still, the being's condensed aspect. The body's sensory apparatus (eyes, ears, skin, nose, tongue) is constrained by the body's material limits. These constraints are not the limit of what can be perceived; they are the limit of what the body can perceive. The anchor, yang-dominant, is the being's authoritative aspect — the law-pattern that constitutes the being's persistence. The anchor has its own perception, independent of the body's sensory apparatus: it perceives qi (the body's qi, the ambient qi, the qi of other beings), and it perceives other anchors (at Core Formation and above, per 00 §2). In waking life, the anchor's perception is largely suppressed by the body's sensory input — the body's signals are louder, more immediate, more demanding of attention.

In sleep, the body's sensory apparatus is dampened. The body's signals recede; the anchor's perception, no longer suppressed, expands. The expansion is what the mortal tradition calls dreaming. A dream is the anchor's unfiltered perception, experienced by the being (whose consciousness is, in sleep, identified more with the anchor than with the body) as a sequence of perceptions not constrained by the body's sensory limits.

### 7.2 What dreams actually are

A dream's content is, in this cosmology, a perception of real metaphysical phenomena. The content's apparent absurdity — the strange imagery, the impossible geography, the heightened emotion — is the anchor's perception of phenomena the body cannot perceive, experienced through the body's cognitive apparatus (which is the apparatus the being's consciousness uses to interpret perception). The absurdity is the translation gap: the anchor perceives qi-residue, ambient qi, bardo-presence; the body's cognitive apparatus, designed for sensory perception, interprets these as sensory imagery. The interpretation is imperfect; the perception is real.

Specifically, dreams perceive:

- **Qi-residue.** The qi-residue of recent events in the dreamer's vicinity (the residue 14 §6 describes) is perceived by the dreaming anchor as imagery related to the residue's source. A dreamer who sleeps near a murder-site dreams of violence; a dreamer who sleeps near a deviation-site dreams of confusion and fear; a dreamer who sleeps near a lineage hall dreams of ancestors (the hall's accumulated grief-rite-residue, perceived by the dreaming anchor as the presence of the dead).

- **Ambient qi.** The ambient qi of the dreamer's sleeping-place is perceived by the dreaming anchor as a quality of the dream — a wood-phase ambient qi produces dreams of growth and forest; a water-phase ambient qi produces dreams of flow and water; a fire-phase ambient qi produces dreams of heat and passion. This is why sleeping in different places produces different dream-quality, and why a cultivator practicing dream-cultivation (§7.3 below) chooses their sleeping-place carefully.

- **Bardo-presence.** If a ghost (§3 above) is present near the dreamer, the dreaming anchor perceives the ghost's anchor-fragment directly. This is the mechanism behind the mortal tradition's "ghost-dreams" — the dream in which a dead relative appears and speaks. The dead relative's ghost (if it persists) is perceived by the dreaming anchor as a presence in the dream, and the dream's cognitive apparatus interprets the presence as the relative's form. The ghost's attachment (grief, hatred, oath, love) is perceived as the dream's emotional tone.

- **Ancestral qi-impressions.** If the dreamer is a member of a cultivation lineage with an active ancestral court (15 §3), the ancestral court can, at cost to its persistence-budget, send a qi-impression to a sleeping descendant. The impression is perceived by the dreaming anchor as a dream of the ancestor. The ancestor's actual communication (if any) is encoded in the qi-impression's structure, which the dreaming anchor perceives and the body's cognitive apparatus interprets as dream-imagery. This is the mechanism behind the mortal tradition's "ancestor-dreams"; the cultivation-lineage version is real and is a recognized consultation modality (15 §3.3).

### 7.3 Dream cultivation

A cultivator can, with practice, learn to direct their dreams. This is **dream cultivation** — a recognized practice-track, taught by some sects (particularly the contemplative Buddhist-temple traditions, per 12 §4.1) and by some Daoist hermit lineages.

The practice begins with **lucidity** — the cultivator learns to recognize, within the dream, that they are dreaming. This recognition is itself a perception: the cultivator's anchor, perceiving its own perception as perception (rather than as reality), achieves a moment of meta-awareness that the body's cognitive apparatus interprets as "I am dreaming." The recognition is the first verb of dream cultivation.

The practice continues with **direction** — the cultivator learns to direct the dream's content, choosing what to perceive. This is not the same as controlling the dream's imagery (which is the body's cognitive apparatus's interpretation); it is the same as directing the anchor's perception. The cultivator chooses to perceive a specific qi-residue, a specific ambient qi, a specific bardo-presence. The dream's imagery follows the perception; the cultivator does not control the imagery, but they control what the perception is directed at.

The advanced practice includes:

- **Perceiving at distance.** The cultivator, sleeping in one place, directs their anchor's perception to perceive the qi-residue of a distant place. The perception is limited by the anchor's perceptual range (which expands with cultivation: a Qi Condensation cultivator can perceive at a distance of a few li; a Core Formation cultivator at hundreds of li; a Nascent Soul cultivator at thousands). The dream-imagery is the distant place's qi-residue, interpreted by the body's cognitive apparatus as imagery. This is the genre's "dream-scrying" trope, and it is, in this cosmology, a real perception modality.

- **Communicating with the dead.** The cultivator, sleeping near a ghost, directs their anchor's perception to perceive the ghost's anchor-fragment directly. The cultivator can address the ghost (in qi-modulations the ghost can read) and the ghost can respond. This is the conversation a Qi Condensation cultivator can have while waking (§3.8); in dream, the conversation is easier and more legible, because the anchor's perception is unfiltered. A dream-cultivator can lay a ghost to rest (§3.6 method 1) without ever waking.

- **Investigating qi-residue.** The cultivator, sleeping at a site of interest (a murder-site, a deviation-site, an old battlefield), directs their anchor's perception to perceive the site's qi-residue in detail. The perception is more detailed than waking perception (the body's sensory apparatus, in waking life, partly obscures the qi-perception); a dream-cultivator can perceive the residue's age, source, and character with a precision that waking perception does not permit. This is the practice-track of choice for cultivators who investigate crimes, hauntings, and historical events; the Azure Sword Sect's punishment hall has a standing dream-cultivator on staff for investigations.

### 7.4 Ancestral dream-messages

The ancestral court's dream-messages (15 §3.3, §3.4) are a specific application of the dream-perception modality. The ancestral spirit, in the Precelestial, projects a qi-impression toward a sleeping descendant's anchor. The descendant's dreaming anchor perceives the impression; the body's cognitive apparatus interprets it as a dream of the ancestor.

The communication is **real but imperfect**. The ancestor's intention (what they wish to communicate) is encoded in the qi-impression's structure. The descendant's dreaming anchor perceives the structure. The body's cognitive apparatus interprets the structure as dream-imagery — and the interpretation is lossy. The ancestor's "your lineage is in danger from the eastern sect" may be perceived by the descendant as a dream of a threatening figure in green armor (the eastern direction's qi-quality, perceived as green; the threatening figure, the ancestor's communication of danger). The descendant, on waking, has the dream's imagery and the dream's emotional tone (urgency, warning), but not the ancestor's exact words. The interpretation is the descendant's work.

This is why ancestral consultations, in the cultivation-lineage tradition, are performed with a Core Formation cultivator present (15 §3.3) — the Core Formation cultivator can perceive the qi-impression directly, without the lossy interpretation, and can confirm or correct the descendant's dream-interpretation. A Nascent Soul cultivator of the lineage can commune with the ancestor directly, without the dream-modality, but most lineages do not have a Nascent Soul cultivator available for routine consultations.

The mortal tradition's "ancestor-dreams" — the dream in which a dead parent appears and gives advice — are, in this cosmology, real for cultivation-lineage members (whose lineage's ancestral court can send impressions) and largely imaginary for mortal-lineage members (whose lineage's dead have been processed by the Underworld and reborn, with no persistent anchor to send impressions). The mortal-lineage member who dreams of a dead parent is, in most cases, dreaming the lineage hall's accumulated grief-rite-residue (perceived as the parent's presence), not the parent's actual anchor. The distinction is invisible to the dreamer and is, in practice, irrelevant — the dream's emotional truth (the ancestor's continued presence in the lineage's life) is the same in both cases, and the lineage's rites are designed to sustain both.

### 7.5 What it feels like

To a mortal, a dream is the mortal's ordinary dream-experience — vivid, absurd, emotional, fading on waking. The mortal does not perceive the dream as perception; they perceive it as imagination. The mortal who learns (from a Qi Induction cultivator, or from a Daoist priest, or from their own practice) to attend to their dreams as perception can, over years, develop a faint awareness of the dream's perceptual content — the mortal begins to recognize that dreams of a certain quality correspond to certain waking events (a dream of cold water preceding a flood; a dream of a dead relative preceding a death in the family). This recognition is the beginning of dream cultivation, and it is within the mortal's reach if they have the discipline and the instruction.

To a Qi Induction cultivator, dreams are perceived as perception — the cultivator recognizes, within the dream, that they are perceiving something real (qi-residue, ambient qi, bardo-presence). The recognition is partial; the cultivator cannot fully direct the perception, but they can attend to it. This is the threshold at which dream-cultivation becomes possible.

To a Qi Condensation cultivator with dream-cultivation training, dreams are a directed perception-modality. The cultivator can choose, before sleeping, what to perceive; can achieve lucidity within the dream; can direct the perception at a specific target; and can recall the perception's content on waking with the fidelity of a waking perception. The dream is no longer "a dream" in the mortal sense; it is a perception-session conducted in the sleep-state.

To a Core Formation cultivator, dreams are continuous with waking perception — the cultivator's anchor, no longer fully dependent on the body's sleep-state for unfiltered perception, can perceive at waking what a Qi Condensation cultivator can perceive only in dream. The Core Formation cultivator's dreams are less necessary than the Qi Condensation cultivator's, but more powerful — the anchor's perception, in the body's sleep-state, is even less filtered than at Core Formation waking, and a Core Formation cultivator's dream-perception can achieve a precision that waking perception cannot. This is why Core Formation dream-cultivators are the most skilled investigators in the cultivation world; their dream-perception is the most precise perception the Mortal stratum permits.

## 8. The relationship between mortal religion and actual metaphysics

### 8.1 The principle

Mortal religion and actual metaphysics are **two registers of the same phenomena**. The phenomena are real; the interpretations vary. The mortal tradition interprets the phenomena through the mortal world's cognitive and cultural apparatus — iconography, mythology, ritual, moral framework. The cultivation world interprets the same phenomena through the cultivation world's apparatus — anchor-perception, qi-perception, lawful analysis, karmic computation.

Neither register is wrong. Both are partial. The mortal register is partial because it perceives the phenomena only through their effects (the earth god's nudges felt as luck, the ghost's presence felt as a chill, the yao's curiosity felt as a strange encounter). The cultivation register is partial because it perceives the phenomena only at the threshold of perception (a Qi Condensation cultivator perceives a place-spirit but cannot perceive its full coherence; a Core Formation cultivator perceives the place-spirit's anchor but cannot perceive the courts' register's full contents). The full reality of the phenomena is, in this cosmology, perceptible only to a Mahayana cultivator with the right perception-training, and even then only partially.

### 8.2 The truth table

The following mortal beliefs are **true** (the phenomena they describe are real and the belief's general shape is accurate):

- **The earth god, the river god, and the city god are real.** All three are place-spirits (§2). The mortal beliefs that they protect, modulate weather, and record the dead are accurate within the limits of the spirit's actual capability (§2.3). The river god's "flooding when offended" is partially accurate — the floods are hydrological, but the spirit's withholding of its nudges in response to a broken oath is real (§2.8).
- **Ghosts and the Ghost Festival are real.** The mortal belief that the unburied dead walk is accurate, with the qualification that the walking is qi-projection within a small radius (§3). The Ghost Festival's "walking dead" is the bardo-boundary's annual thinness on the fifteenth of the seventh month (§3.7).
- **Divination is real.** The mortal belief that hexagrams can reveal the future is partially accurate — divination perceives qi-momentum (24 §2.5), which is the present's lawful tendency, not the future. A skilled diviner is more right than an unskilled one; a Core Formation diviner is more right than a Qi Condensation diviner.
- **Ancestor veneration is real.** The mortal belief that the dead benefit from veneration is accurate, with the qualification that veneration feeds either a place-spirit's quasi-anchor (for the unremembered dead), a cultivation-lineage's ancestral court (for the lineage's great cultivators), or the local grief-rite residue (for the ordinary dead). The veneration feeds the metaphysical infrastructure the dead depend on; it does not, in most cases, "reach" the specific dead.
- **Fox-spirits, snake-demons, tree-spirits are real.** Yao (§4) are real, and the mortal tradition's named types correspond to the actual yao-ecology of the Cangwu foothills.

The following mortal beliefs are **partially true** (the phenomena they describe are real, but the belief's specifics are projection):

- **The Buddhist afterlife.** The dead do go somewhere (the Underworld, §6), and the processing does involve a karmic confrontation and a rebirth-assignment. The specifics — Yama, the ten kings, the eighteen hells, the bridges of gold and silver — are mortal projection onto a bureaucratic process.
- **The Daoist celestial hierarchy.** The Courts of Heaven exist (15 §2), and they are staffed by cultivators who crossed tribulation and took office. The specifics — the Jade Emperor, the specific ministries, the specific ranks — are mortal projection onto a fallible political institution.
- **The mortal tradition's demonic-cultivator tropes.** Demonic cultivators are real (§5), and their practice is metaphysically criminal. The specifics — that they eat infants, that they worship dark gods, that they can be exorcised by Daoist priests — are mortal projection. The actual demonic cultivator is a cultivator who harvests anchors; the actual countermeasure is a stronger cultivator or the cosmos's karmic enforcement.
- **The mortal tradition's dream-interpretation.** Dreams are real perception (§7), and some dream-content corresponds to real metaphysical phenomena. The specifics — that dreams predict the future, that dreams of the dead are always the dead's actual presence, that dream-imagery can be read literally — are mortal projection. Dreams perceive present qi-phenomena, not the future; dreams of the dead may be grief-residue or actual ancestral impressions; dream-imagery requires interpretation, not literal reading.

The following mortal beliefs are **myth** (the phenomena they describe are not real, or are real but misidentified):

- **The Jade Emperor as a personal god.** There is no Jade Emperor; the courts are an institution, not a theocracy (15 §2.1).
- **Yama as a personal king of hell.** There is no Yama; the Underworld is a bureaucratic processing center (§6.1).
- **The wheel of rebirth as a moral judgment.** Rebirth is causal momentum (24 §2.1), not moral judgment; the cosmos is amoral (24 §2.1).
- **The mortal tradition's "ghost-marriage" (冥婚).** The belief that a dead son can be married to a dead daughter to provide them companionship in the afterlife is myth. The dead are processed by the Underworld and reborn; they do not persist as a couple. The rite of ghost-marriage is, metaphysically, a veneration-qi-input to the place-spirits of the two dead's anchor-points, which may (if the ghosts persist) provide them a brief respite from their attachments. It does not marry them.
- **The mortal tradition's "paper money" for the dead.** The burning of paper money is, metaphysically, a qi-input to whatever anchors are present at the burning-site. It does not "reach" the specific dead in the Underworld (the Underworld does not use currency); it feeds the local bardo-boundary's qi-density, which benefits whatever anchors are present. The rite is functional, but not in the way the mortal tradition believes.

### 8.3 Why the projection varies

The mortal projection varies across traditions (Buddhist, Daoist, Confucian, folk, sectarian) because the mortal projection is **mortal culture's interpretation of real phenomena, filtered through mortal culture's cognitive and institutional apparatus**. A Buddhist-temple community interprets the Underworld through Buddhist eschatology because that is the interpretive framework the community has. A Daoist-temple community interprets the courts of Heaven through Daoist celestial hierarchy because that is its framework. A lineage community interprets the dead through ancestor veneration because that is its framework.

The phenomena do not prefer one interpretation. The earth god does not care whether the village calls it a 土地公 or a 地主 or a 福德正神; it responds to the veneration, not the name. The Underworld's processing is the same whether the dead are sent by a Buddhist monk's rite, a Daoist priest's rite, or a lineage elder's rite. The courts' crossing is the same whether the cultivator is Buddhist, Daoist, Confucian, or folk-religion. The mortal tradition's variation is the variation of mortal culture, not the variation of the phenomena. The phenomena are one; the interpretations are many.

### 8.4 The cultivator's burden

The cultivator who reaches Core Formation — the threshold of direct anchor-perception — inherits a burden that the mortal does not. The mortal believes; the cultivator perceives. The mortal's belief can be wrong without consequence (the mortal who believes the earth god is a small bearded old man is wrong, but the wrongness does not affect the mortal's relationship with the place-spirit). The cultivator's perception cannot be wrong without consequence (the cultivator who perceives a yao and treats it as a spirit beast, or who perceives a ghost and treats it as a place-spirit, makes a metaphysical error with lawful consequences).

This is the cultivator's burden: **the responsibility to act on what is actually there rather than on what the iconography says**. The mortal can burn incense to the earth god and trust the iconography; the cultivator must perceive the earth god, read its coherence and its jurisdiction and its disposition, and act on the perception. The mortal can perform the funeral rite as the tradition prescribes; the cultivator must perceive the deceased's anchor, its bardo-state, its karmic trace, and perform the rite as the perception requires. The mortal can hunt the fox-spirit as the tradition demonizes it; the cultivator must perceive Lady Bai's anchor, her karmic trace, her five centuries of good-faith residence, and decide — on the perception, not the tradition — whether to hunt, to negotiate, or to leave alone.

This is the burden that makes the cultivation world morally complex, and that makes the protagonist's progression meaningful. A protagonist who reaches Core Formation and returns to Wang Family Bend returns as a being who perceives the earth god, the river god, the ghosts of the bend's dead, the yao in the Cangwu foothills, the qi-residue of the Zhou Wugui incursion still faintly present in the western Cangwu's ravines. The protagonist's choices, from Core Formation onward, are made on perception, not belief. The choices are harder for it; they are also more honest. This is the cosmology's commitment to the cultivator as a being who knows more and is therefore responsible for more. The cathedral has a congregation; the congregation, at Core Formation and above, can see the cathedral's actual structure. What they do with the sight is the game.
