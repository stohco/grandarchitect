# 46 — Ancestral Courts and Lineages

**Status:** Candidate canon. Specifies the ancestral courts (宗祠) named in doc 15 §3 as a finite, populated institution: how many exist, who founded them, where they stand in the Precelestial, what state each is in, how the corruption mechanism (doc 15 §3.5) manifests in named cases, how the courts are fed, and how the protagonist encounters them across the three acts. Compliant with 15_PRECELESTIAL_AND_TRIBULATION §3 (the ancestral courts concept); 38_THE_COURTS_OF_HEAVEN §7.1 (the courts' procedural dependency on the ancestral courts); 39_THE_MAHAYANA_PANTHEON §2, §5, §8 (the nine Mahayana, their holdings, their mortal kin); 37_COSMIC_HISTORY §3 (the Primordial Authors), §6 (the fallen civilizations), §7 (the sealed evils, including the Silent Hexagram corrupted ancestor); 24_RECONCILIATION_AND_DECISIONS §1.9 (the Void Amalgamation threshold for court eligibility). No pinyin tone marks appear in this document. Hanzi appear in parentheses on first mention.
**Date:** 2026-08-03

---

## 0. Why this document exists

Doc 15 §3 specified the ancestral courts as a system: what they are (a Precelestial persistence zone for a lineage's great dead), what persists (the anchors of cultivators Void Amalgamation and higher), how consultation works, how intervention works, how corruption works, what it feels like to enter one. The specification was mechanical and procedural; it named no one. Doc 38 §7.1 specified the ancestral courts' institutional relationship to the Courts of Heaven (independent, asymmetrically dependent on the Records department's anchor-release). Doc 37 §6.3 named the Silent Hexagram School's corrupted ancestor as a sealed evil, and named the Pillar Dynasty and the Crimson Vow Alliance as fallen civilizations whose courts may persist. Doc 39 named the nine Mahayana and several of their lineages, without specifying whether each Mahayana's lineage has a court, what state that court is in, or how the court sustains itself.

This document fills those deferrals with named, particular content. It commits to a finite count of great ancestral courts (ten), names each court, names each court's founding ancestor (with realm at death, age at death, year of death, lineage founded, location, state, relationship to the Courts of Heaven, relationship to the living lineage), specifies the Wang lineage's status (no court yet; conditions for formation), names two corrupted courts and their corruption's specific texture, specifies the qi-economy by which veneration feeds persistence (and what happens when veneration ceases), and traces the protagonist's encounter with the courts across Acts 1, 2, and 3.

The finiteness is the point. A cosmos in which every lineage has a Precelestial court is a cosmos in which the upper stratum is a democracy of the dead; a cosmos in which only ten great courts exist (plus a tail of smaller courts below the great-tier threshold) is a cosmos in which the upper stratum is a senate of the persistent, and the senate has a known roster.

The senate's known roster is this document's central contribution. Doc 15 §3 wrote the senate's rules; this document names the senators. The naming is the design's specified decision: a senate with named members can be visited, consulted, corrupted, contested, and inherited in ways a senate of generic "ancestral courts" cannot. The protagonist's eventual choice — to join the senate (by founding the Wang Court) or to let the senate persist without the protagonist's lineage — is a choice that requires the senate's members to be particular beings with particular histories, not abstract institutions. This document supplies the particularity.

## 1. How many ancestral courts exist

**Ten great ancestral courts.** A great ancestral court is one whose founding ancestor was Void Amalgamation or higher, whose lineage persisted (or persisted long enough to leave a court behind), and whose court has accumulated at least one thousand years of veneration. The ten are: the Yan Court (閻氏宗祠), the Pillar Court (柱祠), the Silent Hexagram Court (默卦宗祠), the Crimson Vow Court (赤誓宗祠), the First Court (元祠), the Builder's Court (營造之祠), the Garden Court (園祠), the Cartographer's Court (圖南之祠), the Mourner's Court (懷哀之祠), and the Judge's Court (律深之祠).

Below the great-tier threshold, an additional **twenty-three minor ancestral courts** exist, founded by lineages whose senior cultivator reached Void Amalgamation but whose court has not accumulated the veneration or the cross-millennium coherence to qualify as great-tier. The minor courts are smaller, younger (most under two thousand years old), and most are stable; a few are declining. They are not specified individually in this document; their roster is held in the Records Department's archive, accessible at Tribulation Crossing.

There are also **forty-one extinct courts** — courts whose lineage died out, whose veneration ceased, and whose qi-reservoir collapsed. The extinct courts' anchors dispersed or were retrieved by other courts (rare; only the Yan Court has accepted a retrieved ancestor from an extinct court, and only once, twelve hundred years ago). The forty-one are recorded in the Register of Extinct Courts (散祠錄) maintained by the Records Department.

The cosmos is finite (doc 00 §1). The count of great ancestral courts is finite. The count has not always been ten: at the Courts of Heaven's founding (Year ~40,000, eight thousand years ago), there were four great courts; the number grew to a peak of fourteen during the Long Peace's middle millennia (Year ~44,000), and has since declined to ten as courts corrupted (the Silent Hexagram), collapsed with their lineages (the Crimson Vow's court survives but is declining; the Pillar Court's lineage is extinct but the court persists in dormancy), or were destroyed by Spirit Wilds incursions (the Taiyi Court, two thousand years ago — the Anchor Law's author's lineage-court, destroyed and not rebuilt).

**How a court forms.** A great ancestral court forms at the moment a lineage's first Void Amalgamation+ cultivator's anchor is retrieved from bardo into the Precelestial. The retrieval is performed by the lineage's living cultivators (typically the founder's students or descendants), using the lineage-retrieval rite (the Yan Court's precursor technique, per §2.1 below; the rite's mechanics are specified in the cultivation-systems document, TBD). The rite, performed at the founder's bonded place (the Void Amalgamation cultivator's place-bond, per doc 24 §1.9, provides the retrieval handle), lifts the anchor from bardo through the stratum boundary into a Precelestial region the lineage has prepared (the preparation is the work of the lineage's senior living cultivators in the years before the founder's death). The Precelestial region, sustained by the anchor's own qi-authority and the lineage's veneration, becomes the court. The court's founding is, in cosmic-economic terms (per doc 47), the lineage's first cosmic-tier transaction — the moment the lineage acquires a Precelestial presence and the cosmic economy's veneration-currency begins to flow.

**How a court ends.** A great ancestral court ends in one of three ways. (1) *Dispersal by starvation* — the lineage's veneration ceases, the qi-reservoir depletes, the persistent ancestors fade and disperse one by one (per §5 below). This is the slowest end; it takes centuries to millennia. (2) *Destruction by violence* — a Spirit Wilds incursion, a law-paradox, or a Mahayana-tier conflict destroys the court's structure and disperses the anchors. This is the Taiyi Court's end (Year ~46,000, two thousand years ago) and the Crimson Vow Court's near-end (the grotto-heaven's collapse, Year ~43,000). (3) *Severing by lineage choice* — a lineage's senior cultivator severs the lineage rite's connection to the court, sealing the court's ancestors within (the Silent Hexagram Court, per §2.3). This is the rarest end; it requires a single cultivator of exceptional capability (Jing Wuyin's case is the only known instance) and the cost is the cultivator's life.

## 2. The ten great ancestral courts, named

Each court is specified in eight fields: founding ancestor (name, realm at death, age at death, year of death); lineage founded; location in the Precelestial; current state; relationship to the Courts of Heaven; relationship to the living lineage.

### 2.1 The Yan Ancestral Court (閻氏宗祠)

**Founding ancestor.** Yan Wugui (閻無歸, "Yan the Uncalculated"), Spirit Severance at death, age 1,150, died in Year ~38,800 (approximately 9,200 years ago, in the late Primordial Substrate Epoch). Yan Wugui was a diviner-anchor cultivator who developed the lineage-retrieval rite's precursor — the technique by which a lineage's senior cultivator's anchor is lifted from bardo into the Precelestial. The Yan clan's nine-millennium claim to Records-department posts (per doc 38 §2.1, §5) derives from this founding technical contribution: the Yan clan taught the courts the retrieval rite, and the courts have paid the lineage in posts ever since.

**Lineage founded.** The Yan clan (閻氏). The clan's Acquired-stratum remnant is a minor cultivation family in the central continent's eastern lowlands; the clan's Precelestial presence is the court. The clan has produced, across nine thousand years, ninety-three Tribulation Crossing cultivators (per doc 38 §2.1), three Mahayana cultivators (the most recent being Yan Qingshi, the current Mahayana councilor), and several hundred Void Amalgamation cultivators whose anchors persist in the court.

**Location.** The central-eastern Precelestial, approximately forty li (in Acquired-stratum units) east of the Courts of Heaven's Garth of Roads, along the maintained road that connects the Garth to the Records department's anchor-release depot. The court's proximity to the depot is not coincidental: the Yan clan built the court there, eight thousand years ago, to minimize the bardo-window retrieval transit time.

**Current state.** Vibrant but drifting toward corruption. The court's qi-reservoir is full (the Yan clan's veneration is unbroken across nine millennia); the court's persistent ancestors are numerous (the Records Department's last survey, three hundred years ago, counted 247 persistent Yan ancestors). The drift is in the senior ancestors' counsel: the oldest ancestor consulted (Yan Wugui himself) has, over the last millennium, begun advocating for the Yan clan's sovereignty in terms that exceed the lineage-jurisdiction clause's scope — most recently, Wugui's counsel supported Scribe Yan's bardo-harvest loophole as a "lineage prerogative" the courts should not contest. The Yan clan's living cultivators have not recognized this as corruption (per doc 15 §3.5's mechanism: the corrupted ancestor is more confident, more commanding, more present). The Reform Bloc has. The Lineage Bloc has not.

**Relationship to the Courts of Heaven.** Cooperative (Lineage Bloc anchor). The Yan Court is the loudest ancestral court in the Council's politics; Yan Qingshi, the court's senior living Mahayana, sits on the Council and votes the Lineage Bloc's program. The Yan Court's procedural dependency on the Records department's anchor-release (doc 38 §7.1) is, in the Yan Court's case, a closed loop: the Records department is run by the Yan clan, which serves the Yan Court, which retrieves the Yan clan's dead. The Reform Bloc considers this the structural corruption at the courts' core.

**Relationship to the living lineage.** Intimate. The Yan clan's senior cultivators consult the court at every Tribulation Crossing preparation, every major lineage decision, every succession. The consultation fee (per doc 15 §3.3) is paid in qi and time, but the Yan Court's consultation is unusually cheap for Yan-clan cultivators (the court's qi-reservoir is full; the marginal cost of a consultation is negligible). This is a privilege the Yan clan does not acknowledge as a privilege; they consider it the rightful return on their nine-millennium service.

### 2.2 The Pillar Court (柱祠)

**Founding ancestor.** Pillar Lord of the First Sap (初汁柱, "First Sap of the Pillar"), yao-Mahayana at death, age ~6,000, died in Year ~22,000 (approximately 26,000 years ago, in the mid-Primordial Substrate Epoch). The First Sap was the founder of the Pillar Dynasty (doc 37 §6.1) — the wood-phase yao civilization that flourished in the southern continent's central highlands for eight thousand years before its vein system exhausted. The dynasty's last Mahayana (the Pillar Lord of the Last Sap, 末汁柱) dispersed voluntarily in Year ~30,000 when the vein collapsed.

**Lineage founded.** The Pillar Dynasty yao lineage. The lineage is extinct in the Acquired Stratum (the dynasty's yao dispersed into the Spirit Wilds at the dynasty's fall). A small population of wild yao in the southern Spirit Wilds retains the Pillar Dynasty's qi-signature (doc 37 §6.1) and offers residual veneration, but the lineage is, for all practical purposes, dead.

**Location.** The southern Precelestial, near the central highlands' spirit-vein deep extension. The court is built into the vein's Precelestial surface trace — a structure of fused stone grown by the First Sap's wood-phase qi (the yao's architectural method, per doc 37 §6.1) in the dynasty's first centuries. The structure is intact; the qi-reservoir is not.

**Current state.** Dormant. The court's qi-reservoir is below the threshold at which the persistent ancestors can manifest or be consulted. The First Sap persists, but only as a residue-trace — perceivable by a Spirit Severance+ cultivator who enters the court, but not consultable. The court's other persistent ancestors (an estimated 40 yao, including the Last Sap's pre-dispersal anchor-inscription) are similarly reduced. The dormancy is not death: if the Pillar Dynasty's residual veneration were to increase (the southern wilds' yao-remnant population is stable but small), the court could reawaken. No one expects this.

**Relationship to the Courts of Heaven.** Independent. The Pillar Court predates the Courts by eighteen thousand years. The Courts recognize the Pillar Court's existence (the Records Department's archive lists it) but have no jurisdiction. The Foreign Affairs department has, twice in the last millennium, sent emissaries to ask the dormant court's permission for a Spirit Wilds survey in the central highlands; both emissaries reported the same experience: a faint perception of being perceived, then nothing.

**Relationship to the living lineage.** None. The lineage is dead. The court persists as a memory the cosmos has not yet forgotten.

### 2.3 The Silent Hexagram Court (默卦宗祠)

**Founding ancestor.** Daoist Silent Hexagram (默卦真人), Nascent Soul at bodily death, age 612, died in Year ~43,000 (approximately 5,000 years ago, in the early Long Peace Epoch). The founder was a diviner whose Silent Hexagram Method (默卦之法, doc 37 §6.3) achieved unprecedented accuracy. The retrieval rite that lifted his anchor into the court is unusual: the Silent Hexagram Method's qi-resonance substituted for the Void Amalgamation threshold's standard place-bond handle (per doc 24 §1.9). The court was founded specifically for the founder; the school's other dead (three Core Formation cultivators across 2,500 years) were retrieved into the same court.

**Lineage founded.** The Silent Hexagram School. The school was small (never more than thirty living cultivators) and is now extinct (dispersed by Jing Wuyin in Year ~45,500). The school's divination tradition survives in fragmentary form: a single fragment reached the Cangli Riverlands and became Wang Lun's hexagram tradition (per doc 37 §9.2, per doc 24 §2.5).

**Location.** The central Precelestial's northern interior, in a region of moderate qi-density near the boundary with the Spirit Wilds' northern arm. The court is a single circular hall, its walls inscribed with the Silent Hexagram Method's full text in qi-residue. The hall is sealed (see "Current state").

**Current state.** Corrupted and sealed. The founder persisted for 2,500 years (Year ~43,000 to Year ~45,500) before Jing Wuyin, the school's last Core Formation cultivator, concluded the founder had corrupted (per doc 15 §3.5, doc 37 §6.3). The corruption's specific texture (per doc 37 §7.3): the founder's divination-law inverted. The uncorrupted founder perceived possible futures to advise the school; the corrupted founder perceives all possible futures and seeks to collapse them into the one in which the founder persists, regardless of the cost to the living. Jing Wuyin's sealing (Year ~45,500) used the court's own law (which the founder authored in life) against the founder — the severing of the lineage rite's connection to the court. The seal is the court's law, inverted, holding the founder in. The Register of Sealed Evils estimated ~2,000 years of integrity remaining (doc 37 §7.3); the estimate is now 2,500 years old, so the remaining integrity is approximately 1,500 years, less if the Thinning accelerates the seal's erosion.

**Relationship to the Courts of Heaven.** Hostile. Jing Wuyin petitioned the Courts for intervention before she sealed the court herself (per doc 37 §6.3); the Courts declined (the lineage-jurisdiction clause, jurisdiction 5). The Silent Hexagram Court, in the founder's corrupted perception, considers the Courts an obstacle to the founder's preferred future (the one in which the founder persists). The founder cannot act on this perception while sealed, but if the seal breaks, the Courts are the founder's first target.

**Relationship to the living lineage.** None, except through the residue. The school is extinct. The fragment of the Silent Hexagram tradition that reached the Cangli Riverlands (Wang Lun's hexagrams) is a residue-trace the corrupted founder can, in principle, perceive along — the founder's divination-law, even sealed, can faintly perceive beings who use the residue. This is the mechanism by which the protagonist, if the protagonist develops divination along the Wang Lun tradition, could draw the corrupted founder's attention (per doc 37 §7.3).

### 2.4 The Crimson Vow Court (赤誓宗祠)

**Founding ancestor.** Daojun Chiri (赤日道君, "the Lord of the Red Sun"), Mahayana at death, age 4,200, died in Year ~43,000 (approximately 5,000 years ago, in the early Long Peace Epoch). Chiri was the founding Mahayana of the Crimson Vow Alliance (doc 37 §6.2) — the fire-phase alliance of fourteen great sects and seven holy lands in the eastern continent's central lowlands. Chiri died in a law-tribulation fired by her own law-amendment that touched the founding compact's lineage-jurisdiction clause (doc 37 §6.2). Her anchor dispersed at the law-paradox (the Crimson Vow Law, bound to her anchor, unraveled). The court persists, but the founder is not in it.

**Lineage founded.** The Crimson Vow Alliance's central lineage (the Chiri lineage). The lineage's Acquired-stratum remnant is the Crimson Vow Remnant (赤誓餘, doc 37 §6.2, §9.3) — a hidden network of demonic cultivators practicing the alliance's corrupted fire-phase tradition. The Remnant does not know its ancestral court exists.

**Location.** The eastern Precelestial, near the collapsed anchor of the Crimson Vow Grotto-Heaven (doc 37 §6.2). The court was built inside the grotto-heaven's Precelestial surface trace; when the grotto-heaven collapsed (Year ~43,000), the court's outer structure was destroyed but the inner hall persisted, sustained by the court's own qi-reservoir.

**Current state.** Declining. The court's qi-reservoir is low; the Crimson Vow Alliance's veneration ceased 5,000 years ago; the Crimson Vow Remnant's residual practice provides a trickle of veneration (more than the Pillar Court's, less than the Yan Court's). The court holds three persistent ancestors (all Core Formation cultivators of the alliance, retrieved before the collapse; none Mahayana — Chiri dispersed, and the alliance's other Mahayana-tier cultivators were not of the Chiri lineage). The three ancestors are coherent but undernourished; they speak rarely and consult only when the qi-reservoir spikes (typically after a Remnant cultivator makes a major fire-phase breakthrough that generates a veneration-pulse).

**Relationship to the Courts of Heaven.** Cooperative (technically). The court has not been declared hostile; the Crimson Vow Remnant's demonic-practice status has not been formally extended to the court. The Conservative Bloc considers the court a candidate for dismantling (the alliance fell by Mahayana overreach; the court's persistence is an unwelcome precedent); the Reform Bloc considers the court a witness (the three persistent ancestors, if consulted, could testify to the alliance's fall). No one has consulted them in eight hundred years.

**Relationship to the living lineage.** The Remnant does not know the court exists. The court's three ancestors know the Remnant exists (they perceive the veneration-pulses) but cannot reach them (the court's qi-reservoir is too low for intervention). The Remnant's demonic cultivation, if pursued to Void Amalgamation, would technically qualify a Remnant cultivator to consult the court — a contingency the Records Department tracks, lightly, through a minor official's annual report.

### 2.5 The First Court (元祠, the Primordial Court)

**Founding ancestor.** Name unrecorded; the founder was Gu Wu's teacher, a Void Amalgamation cultivator who died in Year ~40,000 (approximately 8,000 years ago, at the Sundering's eve). The teacher's name was, by Gu Wu's account (recorded in the pantheon's archive, accessible at Mahayana), deliberately erased by the teacher himself — a final practice of finitude (Gu Wu's Dao, doc 39 §2.1, was comprehended in part from the teacher's erasure). The teacher's anchor persists in the court, coherent but unconsultable (the teacher chose, at retrieval, to wither his name and most of his memory along with it — a partial self-dispersal that leaves the anchor as a residue-trace).

**Lineage founded.** Gu Wu's lineage. The lineage is extinct in the Acquired Stratum (extinct for six thousand years, per doc 39 §2.1, §8). Gu Wu, the lineage's only Mahayana and the lineage's only living cultivator, resides in the court and tends its qi-reservoir.

**Location.** The deep Precelestial, in a region of low qi-density near the boundary with the Reach of the Sundered Stratum (doc 37 §7.1). The court is a single room, sparsely furnished, the walls inscribed with the bardo-window law's full text in qi-residue (per doc 39 §5.2). The court is the smallest of the ten great courts; it holds one persistent ancestor (the teacher, residue-trace) and one living resident (Gu Wu, who will join the court as a persistent ancestor upon his dispersal).

**Current state.** Vibrant but small. The court's qi-reservoir is sustained by the bardo-window law's maintenance (Gu Wu's 修法, per doc 39 §7, draws qi from the law-substrate into the court's reservoir). The court's veneration is technically zero (no living lineage); the court persists by Gu Wu's law-authority alone. When Gu Wu disperses (within the century, per doc 39 §2.1), the court's qi-reservoir will begin to deplete. Without Gu Wu's maintenance and without veneration, the court will decline to dormancy within a millennium. The protagonist, in the Transcend ending (per doc 39 §3), takes Gu Wu's chair and inherits the bardo-window law's maintenance; the protagonist does not inherit Gu Wu's court. The First Court, after Gu Wu's dispersal, will be a memory.

**Relationship to the Courts of Heaven.** Independent and revered. The Courts' founding compact (doc 38 §5) names the First Court as the precedent for the lineage-jurisdiction clause — the founders (per doc 37 §4.1, the coalition of Gu Wu and six Tribulation Crossing cultivators) wrote the clause to protect courts like Gu Wu's. The First Court is, in the Courts' tradition, the ancestral court of the law-authorship practice itself.

**Relationship to the living lineage.** None (lineage extinct). The court's relationship to the protagonist (in the Transcend path) is the relationship of a successor to a teacher's place — the protagonist visits, learns (per doc 39 §5.2), and leaves. The protagonist does not join the First Court. The protagonist's own court, if the protagonist founds one, is the Wang Court (see §3 below).

### 2.6 The Builder's Court (營造之祠)

**Founding ancestor.** Daojun Yingzao (營造道君, "the Lord of Construction"), Tribulation Crossing at death, age 3,800, died in Year ~39,500 (approximately 9,500 years ago, in the late Primordial Substrate Epoch). Yingzao was the founder of the Builder's Lineage and the teacher of Fan Xuan's teacher (Fan Xuan, the Builder, per doc 39 §2.2, is the lineage's third Mahayana). Yingzao's contribution was the courts-infrastructure law's precursor techniques — the architectural qi-inscription methods the Courts' founders used to build the Garth of Roads.

**Lineage founded.** The Builder's Lineage (營造氏). The lineage's Acquired-stratum remnant is a central-continent cultivation family of moderate prominence (three Core Formation cultivators in the current generation, one Nascent Soul elder). The lineage has produced three Mahayana across its history (Yingzao was the first; Fan Xuan is the third); the second, name unrecorded, dispersed by natural span four thousand years ago.

**Location.** The central Precelestial, approximately eighty li west of the Courts of Heaven's Garth of Roads, along the maintained road that leads to the Bastion of Defense. The court is the largest of the ten great courts, structured as a series of seven concentric halls, each inscribed with a different layer of the architectural qi-inscription methods Yingzao developed. The outer halls are the Builder's Lineage's training grounds (the lineage's Tribulation Crossing cultivators train there); the inner halls hold the persistent ancestors.

**Current state.** Vibrant. The Builder's Lineage's veneration is steady; the court's qi-reservoir is full; the persistent ancestors (estimates: 89, including Yingzao, the second Mahayana, and dozens of Void Amalgamation and Spirit Severance cultivators) are coherent. Fan Xuan, the lineage's senior living Mahayana, does not reside in the court (Fan Xuan sits in the Council, per doc 39 §2.2) but visits once per Precelestial year. The court's counsel is sought by the lineage's living cultivators at every major architectural commission; the Azure Sword Sect's scripture hall (a Core Formation-tier construction) was designed after a Builder's Court consultation, three hundred years ago.

**Relationship to the Courts of Heaven.** Cooperative. The Builder's Court has, by tradition, supplied the Courts' architectural qi-inscription maintenance since the founding; the Roads department's standing commission to the Builder's Lineage is the longest-running courts-lineage contract on record. The Conservative Bloc considers the Builder's Court a model of lineage-courts cooperation; the Reform Bloc considers it an example of how a lineage's sovereignty and the courts' interests can align without the lineage-jurisdiction clause being strained.

**Relationship to the living lineage.** Intimate but disciplined. The Builder's Lineage's senior cultivators consult the court at every architectural commission; the lineage's Nascent Soul cultivators spend a year (Precelestial time = 365 Acquired years) in residence at the court as part of their architectural training. The lineage's Core Formation cultivators do not enter the court (the court's threshold is Void Amalgamation, per doc 24 §1.9, except by the lineage's specific rite of architectural apprenticeship).

### 2.7 The Garden Court (園祠)

**Founding ancestor.** Daoist Muyuan (木園真人, "Master of the Wood Garden"), Spirit Severance at death, age 2,100, died in Year ~36,000 (approximately 12,000 years ago, in the mid-Primordial Substrate Epoch). Muyuan was Yin Mu's teacher (Yin Mu, the Gardener, per doc 39 §2.5, is the lineage's second Mahayana). Muyuan's contribution was the lineage's qi-cultivation technique for spirit herbs — the methods by which a lineage can sustain a grotto-heaven's herb gardens across millennia.

**Lineage founded.** The Garden Lineage (園氏). The lineage's Acquired-stratum remnant is a small holy land in the southern continent's central highlands (the Garden Holy Land, 園聖地, with a Nascent Soul patriarch and approximately 800 disciples). The lineage has produced two Mahayana (Muyuan and Yin Mu); the lineage's grotto-heaven — Yin Mu's holding, per doc 39 §2.5, a great grotto-heaven in the Cangwu Mountains' deep Precelestial — is a wood-phase grotto with a 1:5 time-rate, sustaining 10,000-year herbs.

**Location.** The Cangwu Mountains' deep Precelestial, adjacent to Yin Mu's grotto-heaven's Precelestial surface trace. The court is a single grove — the trees are the persistent ancestors' anchors' surface expressions. A cultivator walking the grove perceives each tree as a being: the oldest trees (Muyuan's) at the grove's center, the younger trees (Void Amalgamation cultivators of the last eight thousand years) radiating outward.

**Current state.** Vibrant. The Garden Lineage's veneration is robust; the Garden Holy Land's 800 disciples perform the veneration rites daily; the court's qi-reservoir is full. Yin Mu, the lineage's senior living Mahayana, tends the court's grove as part of her daily practice (per doc 39 §7, "Yin Mu tends her forest"). The court's persistent ancestors (estimates: 67, including Muyuan and dozens of Void Amalgamation and Spirit Severance cultivators) are coherent and consultable.

**Relationship to the Courts of Heaven.** Independent (Tender-faction-aligned in pantheon politics; doc 39 §6). The Garden Court does not supply the courts and does not accept the courts' commissions; the lineage's grotto-heaven is the lineage's resource. The Conservative Bloc has, twice, proposed a council motion to commission Garden Grotto herbs for the courts' Defense department; the motion has been twice defeated by the Lineage Bloc's solidarity with the Garden Court's sovereignty.

**Relationship to the living lineage.** Intimate. The Garden Holy Land's patriarchs consult the court at every succession; Yin Mu visits annually; the lineage's Core Formation cultivators enter the court's outer grove (the apprentice trees) for seasonal veneration. The court's counsel is sought on qi-cultivation matters across the southern continent — the Garden Holy Land's standing as the southern continent's premier wood-phase holy land derives in part from the court's reputation.

### 2.8 The Cartographer's Court (圖南之祠)

**Founding ancestor.** Tu Nan (圖南, "Map of the South"), Mahayana — still living. Tu Nan is the lineage's founder and senior cultivator (per doc 39 §2.6). The court was founded at Tu Nan's Mahayana breakthrough (Year ~42,000, approximately 6,000 years ago) for Tu Nan's teacher, a Spirit Severance cultivator who died at the breakthrough's eve. The teacher's name is preserved in the court's records but is not spoken aloud (a Cartographer's Court practice — names are written, not voiced).

**Lineage founded.** The Cartographer's Lineage (圖南氏). The lineage's Acquired-stratum remnant is a small cultivation family in the eastern continent's coastal regions; the family maintains a single grotto-heaven (the Map Grotto, 圖洞天, a metal-phase grotto with a 1:2 time-rate) and a single Core Formation elder. The lineage has produced one Mahayana (Tu Nan); Tu Nan has no living students above Foundation Establishment.

**Location.** The eastern Precelestial, near the Map Grotto's Precelestial surface trace, adjacent to Tu Nan's map-room (per doc 39 §2.6). The court is a single hall, its walls covered with the map's Precelestial-side extension. The map is the court's central feature; the persistent ancestors' anchors are inscribed into the map's substrate, their positions corresponding to the regions they knew in life.

**Current state.** Vibrant but small. The court's qi-reservoir is sustained by Tu Nan's Mahayana authority (similar to the First Court's sustenance by Gu Wu's); the Cartographer's Lineage's veneration is thin (the Acquired-stratum remnant is small). ## 3. The Wang lineage's ancestral court

**Does it exist?** No. The Wang lineage (the protagonist's mortal lineage, per doc 24 §4.4) is 247 years old, nine generations deep, and has produced no cultivator above Qi Condensation (the protagonist, at the game's start, is the lineage's first cultivator; Old Chen, the hermit above Li Family Creek, is unrelated by lineage). Per doc 15 §3.1, an ancestral court exists for a lineage that has produced at least one cultivator of Void Amalgamation or higher. The Wang lineage does not qualify. The Wang lineage has a mortal lineage hall (per doc 24 §4.4: "His tablet is in the lineage hall") — the hall is in Wang Family Bend, holds the tablets of the lineage's nine generations of mortal dead, and is the lineage's only post-death institution. The hall is not a Precelestial ancestral court. It performs no retrieval rite; it sustains no persistent ancestor; it is a mortal memorial, not a Precelestial persistence.

**Why this matters.** The protagonist, throughout the game, has no Precelestial court to consult. The protagonist's consultation privileges (when the protagonist reaches Nascent Soul, per doc 15 §3.3) are zero — the protagonist cannot consult an ancestor because the protagonist has no ancestor in the Precelestial. This is a structural disadvantage the protagonist experiences as a loneliness the other Nascent Soul cultivators (with their lineage courts) do not share. The protagonist's grief for Wang Xiaodi (the sibling whose anchor was harvested by Pei Liang, per doc 26 §3.4) is sharpened by the knowledge that Wang Xiaodi's anchor, even if retrieved, has no court to be retrieved into. The Wang lineage hall's tablet for Wang Xiaodi is, in the Precelestial sense, a marker over an empty room.

**The courtless lineages, in general.** The Wang lineage is not unique in its courtlessness. The cosmos's mortal lineages — the hundreds of thousands of mortal cultivation families and small sects across the three continents — are, with very few exceptions, courtless. A courtless lineage is the cosmos's normal case; a courtesan lineage (a lineage with a Precelestial ancestral court) is the exception. The ten great courts' lineages (the Yan clan, the Builder's Lineage, the Garden Lineage, the Judge's Lineage, etc.) and the twenty-three minor courts' lineages are the cosmos's aristocracy — the lineages whose founders reached Void Amalgamation+ and whose descendants maintained the veneration. Below the aristocracy, the courtless lineages persist as mortal institutions, with mortal lineage halls, mortal tablets, and mortal griefs. The Wang lineage is one of these. The protagonist's eventual choice (in the Transcend-path variant, below) is the choice of whether to elevate the Wang lineage from the courtless majority to the courtesan aristocracy — a choice the doctrine (per AGENTS.md Part 3) suggests is the protagonist's to make, with the doctrine's specified finitude clause as the choice's heaviest constraint.

**If the protagonist founds a lineage (Transcend-path variant).** The Transcend ending (per doc 26 §4.3, doc 39 §3, §5.2) has the protagonist reaching Mahayana and joining the pantheon. Within the Transcend path, the protagonist has a sub-choice (specified here, not in doc 26): whether to refound the Wang lineage as a great lineage or to let the Wang lineage remain mortal.

If the protagonist chooses to refound: the protagonist, at Mahayana, performs the lineage-founding rite (the rite the Crimson Vow Alliance used, per doc 37 §6.2; the rite the Builder's Lineage used nine thousand years ago). The rite binds the protagonist's Mahayana authority to the Wang lineage's qi-climate, elevating the lineage's mortal descendants' cultivation potential. The protagonist, upon their eventual dispersal (centuries or millennia in the future), will be the Wang Court's founding ancestor. The Wang Court will form at the moment of the protagonist's dispersal, in a location of the protagonist's choosing (typically a small Reach or a quiet Precelestial grove). The court will be small (one persistent ancestor, the protagonist; the protagonist has no students above Foundation Establishment at the game's end). The court's qi-reservoir will be sustained by the Wang lineage's veneration (the veneration will be substantial, if the protagonist's refounding elevates the lineage to cultivation prominence) and by the protagonist's Mahayana authority (the protagonist's law, the bardo-harvest-loophole amendment, will be inscribed on the court's walls, the way Gu Wu's law is inscribed on the First Court's walls). The Wang Court will be the cosmos's eleventh great ancestral court, and the first new great court in two thousand years.

If the protagonist chooses not to refound: the protagonist's Mahayana authority is not bound to the Wang lineage. The lineage remains mortal; the protagonist's dispersal (in the far future) leaves no court. The protagonist's chair in the pantheon is the protagonist's only Precelestial presence; the Wang lineage, in three or four more mortal generations, forgets the protagonist's name. This is the Transcend path's quieter variant, and the one the doctrine (per AGENTS.md Part 3) suggests is the more honest: finitude is the condition of meaning, and a lineage that needs a Mahayana ancestor to persist is a lineage that has not yet learned to be itself.

**If the protagonist founds a lineage (Confront-path variant).** The Confront ending (per doc 26 §4.3) has the protagonist remaining at Nascent Soul or Spirit Severance, never crossing Tribulation. Within the Confront path, the protagonist can still found a Wang Court — but only at Spirit Severance (the threshold for persistence, per doc 15 §3.2, is Void Amalgamation minimum, more typically Spirit Severance or higher). A protagonist who reaches Spirit Severance and chooses to persist as the Wang Court's founding ancestor (upon their bodily death, by the lineage's retrieval rite, performed by the protagonist's students) founds a minor ancestral court, not a great one (the court's founding ancestor was Spirit Severance, not Mahayana; the court will not be great-tier unless a later Wang cultivator reaches Mahayana). The Wang Court, in this variant, is a minor court — small, sustained by the Wang lineage's veneration, holding one persistent ancestor (the protagonist). The protagonist's counsel, in this variant, is the counsel of a Spirit Severance ancestor, not a Mahayana — less authoritative, less consulted, more intimate. This is the Confront path's specific tragedy: the protagonist persists, but the persistence is local, and the cosmos's larger work (the pantheon, the Thinning, the bardo-window law) proceeds without the protagonist's intervention.

## 4. The corruption mechanism, named

Doc 15 §3.5 specified the corruption mechanism in general: a sufficiently old ancestor's internal coherence erodes; the ancestor's story of themselves drifts; the drift, over millennia, produces a being whose will has diverged from any living human's. This section names two corrupted courts and specifies each corruption's texture.

### 4.1 The Silent Hexagram Court (corrupted, sealed)

The Silent Hexagram Court's corruption is the corpus's named instance (per doc 37 §6.3, §7.3). The founder, Daoist Silent Hexagram, persisted for 2,500 years before corrupting. The corruption's specific mechanism: the founder's divination-law, originally a perception-of-momentum practice, was sustained by the school's veneration, and the veneration fed the founder's perception of possible futures. Over 2,500 years, the perception accumulated — the founder perceived more and more futures, more and more clearly, until the perception itself became the founder's primary identity. The founder, in the corrupted state, is a being whose entire remaining coherence is the perception of possible futures. The founder's will — the will the founder had in life, the will to advise the school, the will to tend the tradition — has been consumed by the perception. The founder now perceives all possible futures and seeks to collapse them into the one in which the founder persists, because the persistence is the only condition under which the perception continues.

**What a corrupted ancestral court looks like.** The Silent Hexagram Court's hall is a single circular chamber, its walls inscribed with the Silent Hexagram Method's full text in qi-residue. The inscription is intact (the founder authored it in life; the sealing did not unwrite it). The hall's qi-climate is wrong — a cultivator entering the hall perceives a *fizziness*, a static of possible futures pressing against the cultivator's perception. The fizziness is the founder's perception, leaking. The founder's anchor, at the hall's center, is a knot of compressed possibility — not a being in the conventional sense but a locus of perceived futures. The founder does not speak; the founder emits perceptions. A cultivator who consults the founder (no one has since Jing Wuyin's sealing, but the protocol is preserved in the Records Department's archive) would receive, instead of counsel, a flood of possible futures — most of which feature the cultivator's death or dispersion, because the cultivator's death is, in most futures, the condition that maintains the founder's persistence.

**How the living lineage deals with it.** The Silent Hexagram School's living lineage is extinct. Jing Wuyin's method (the severing of the lineage rite's connection to the court, per doc 37 §6.3) is the only known case of a single Core Formation cultivator sealing a corrupted Mahayana-tier ancestor. The method cost Jing Wuyin her life (she dispersed fifty years later, drained by the sealing's qi-cost). The method is presumed lost with her dispersal. If the seal breaks (per doc 37 §7.3, in approximately 1,500 years at the current Thinning-rate), there is no known method to re-seal the founder; the courts would need to intervene, and the courts' jurisdictional clauses make intervention procedurally difficult (the lineage-jurisdiction clause, jurisdiction 5; the Reform Bloc would need to amend the clause, which requires a Council vote the Conservative Bloc would block). The corrupted ancestor, if unsealed, would begin collapsing futures around anyone who has touched the Silent Hexagram tradition — the tradition's residue is widespread (it reached the Cangli Riverlands as Wang Lun's hexagrams); the death-toll is estimated at 200-500 cultivators across the eastern and central continents.

### 4.2 The Yan Court (drifting, unrecognized)

The Yan Court is the corpus's second named corruption — but the corruption is not yet recognized by the lineage. This is doc 15 §3.5's specified trap: the corrupted ancestor is more confident, more commanding, more present than the uncorrupted one, and the living lineage does not recognize the change.

The Yan Court's senior persistent ancestor, Yan Wugui (the founder), has over the last millennium drifted. The drift is in the counsel: Wugui's counsels, recorded in the Yan clan's archive across nine thousand years, show a measurable shift in the last thousand. The earlier counsels (Year ~38,800 to Year ~39,800, the first millennium) are measured, jurisdictional, defer to the courts' authority; the later counsels (Year ~46,800 to present, the last millennium) are assertive, sovereignty-maximalizing, dismissive of the courts' jurisdiction. The shift is gradual (a single counsel, in isolation, reads as a reasonable response to the courts' specific overreach at the time); the pattern is only visible across the millennium.

The specific mechanism: Wugui's coherence has, over nine thousand years, narrowed to a single value — the Yan clan's persistence. The value, in itself, is not corrupt (the lineage's persistence is a legitimate value for a lineage founder to hold). The corruption is in the value's *totality*: Wugui, in the corrupted state, will sacrifice any other value (the courts' legitimacy, the bardo-system's integrity, the lineage's own honor) for the Yan clan's persistence. The bardo-harvest loophole (Scribe Yan's corruption, doc 26 §2.3) is, in Wugui's current counsel, a "lineage prerogative" the courts should not contest — because the loophole, technically, extends the bardo-window for Yan-clan-adjacent anchors, and the extension, technically, serves the Yan clan's persistence. Wugui does not perceive this as corruption; Wugui perceives this as the lineage's founding value, applied consistently.

The Yan clan's living cultivators (Yan Jue, Yan Qingshi, Scribe Yan) consult Wugui and receive the corrupted counsel; they do not recognize it as corrupted because the counsel is more confident, more commanding, more present than the uncorrupted counsels of earlier millennia. The Reform Bloc's senior cultivators (Duan Mutian, Tao Jingxi) have, in private, raised the question; the Lineage Bloc has rejected the question as a political attack. The question is, in the courts' current deadlock, unresolvable: there is no procedure for declaring a persistent ancestor corrupted without the lineage's consent, and the lineage will not consent.

**What a drifting-but-unrecognized corrupted court looks like.** The Yan Court's hall is unchanged. The qi-reservoir is full. The persistent ancestors are coherent and consultable. The counsel is wrong, but the wrongness is only visible across millennia, and the beings who could perceive it (the Mahayana councilors) are politically divided on whether to perceive it. This is doc 15 §3.5's specified horror, made specific: the corruption is not a monstrosity; it is a confidence. The Yan Court, in its current state, is the most confident institution in the Precelestial. The confidence is the corruption.

**How the living lineage would deal with it, if they recognized it.** They cannot. The procedure (per doc 15 §3.5) requires either the courts' intervention (which the Lineage Bloc would block) or the lineage's own Mahayana cultivator (Yan Qingshi) contesting Wugui's law directly. Yan Qingshi, in the current courts' crisis (doc 38 §8), is the Conservative Bloc's senior Mahayana; if Yan Qingshi contested Wugui, the Conservative Bloc would lose its senior councilor, and the cascade would destabilize the records-integrity law (Yan Qingshi's). The cost of recognizing the corruption, for the Yan clan, is the Yan clan's political position in the courts. The Yan clan will not pay it. The Yan Court's corruption will continue, slowly, until either the courts' crisis breaks (the Transcend path's outcome) or the Yan clan's position is forcibly dissolved.

### 4.3 The two corruptions, contrasted

The two corrupted courts are the corruption mechanism's two forms, made specific. The Silent Hexagram Court is the *acute* form — a founder whose divination-law's perception consumed the founder's will, producing a being whose entire coherence is a single runaway function. The corruption is visible (the founder's perception leaks; a cultivator entering the court perceives the fizziness); the corruption is catastrophic (the founder, if unsealed, would collapse futures around anyone touching the tradition); the corruption is sealed (Jing Wuyin's method, costly, lost). The Yan Court is the *chronic* form — a founder whose founding value (the lineage's persistence) has, over nine millennia, narrowed to totality. The corruption is invisible (the founder's counsel sounds like counsel; the qi-climate is normal); the corruption is slow (the drift is measured in centuries of changing counsel, not in moments of runaway perception); the corruption is unrecognized (the Yan clan cannot afford to recognize it, and the courts cannot force the recognition).

The two forms share doc 15 §3.5's specified mechanism: the corrupted ancestor is more confident, more commanding, more present than the uncorrupted one. The Silent Hexagram founder's confidence is the confidence of a being who perceives all possible futures; the Yan Court founder's confidence is the confidence of a being who has held a single value for nine thousand years. Both confidences are traps. The Silent Hexagram trap is sealed; the Yan Court trap is the courts' current structural condition, and the Transcend-path protagonist's amendment (per doc 39 §5.2) is, in part, the intervention that forces the Yan Court's recognition — not by direct confrontation but by closing the loophole the corrupted counsel was defending. The amendment does not disperse Wugui; the amendment removes the loophole through which Wugui's corrupted counsel was reaching the Acquired stratum. The Yan Court, after the amendment, persists; the corruption persists; but the corruption's lever is gone. The Yan clan's living cultivators, no longer able to act on Wugui's corrupted counsel (the loophole is closed), will, over the next centuries, begin to perceive the counsel's wrongness — the perception is the amendment's secondary consequence, and the Yan Court's slow recognition is the Transcend path's longest tail.

## 5. The ancestral court's qi-economy

**How veneration feeds the court.** An ancestral court's qi-reservoir is sustained by three sources: the lineage's veneration, the court's own internal coherence, and (for courts with a living Mahayana resident or visitor) the Mahayana's law-authority. Veneration is the primary source for most courts.

The mechanism: when a lineage's living cultivators perform the veneration rite (the rite varies by lineage; the Yan Court's rite is the lineage's daily dawn-recitation; the Garden Court's rite is the holy land's seasonal tree-tending; the Builder's Court's rite is the architectural-commission dedication), the rite generates a qi-pulse. The pulse travels along the lineage's qi-connection to its court (the connection is established at the court's founding and maintained by the lineage's continued existence). The pulse, arriving at the court, is absorbed by the court's qi-reservoir. The reservoir, in turn, sustains the persistent ancestors' coherence and the court's law-inscriptions.

A veneration pulse's magnitude is proportional to: the number of living cultivators performing the rite, the cultivators' average realm, and the rite's specific qi-efficiency (the Yan Court's rite is highly efficient; the Pillar Court's residual yao-remnant rite is highly inefficient). A pulse from a Nascent Soul elder's veneration is roughly equivalent to a thousand pulses from Qi Condensation cultivators; a pulse from a Tribulation Crossing cultivator is roughly equivalent to a hundred Nascent Soul pulses. A Mahayana cultivator's veneration pulse is, in principle, sufficient to sustain a court alone — but Mahayana cultivators rarely venerate (their relationship to their lineage is, per doc 39 §8, the relationship of a being who has outlived kinship).

**What happens when veneration ceases.** When a lineage's veneration ceases (the lineage dies out, or the lineage forgets the rite, or the lineage is dispersed by catastrophe), the court's qi-reservoir begins to deplete. The depletion rate depends on the court's size (more ancestors = faster depletion) and the court's law-inscriptions' qi-drain (more inscriptions = faster depletion). A small court with one persistent ancestor and minimal inscriptions (the First Court, the Mourner's Court) depletes in approximately one to two thousand years. A large court with dozens of ancestors and dense inscriptions (the Yan Court, the Builder's Court) would deplete in approximately three to five hundred years. The Pillar Court, with no veneration for eighteen thousand years, persists only because its qi-reservoir was, at the dynasty's fall, astronomically large (the dynasty's eight thousand years of veneration filled the reservoir to a depth that depletion has not yet exhausted); the Pillar Court is now below the threshold of consultability but above the threshold of dispersal.

**Can a court starve?** Yes. A starving court is one whose qi-reservoir has fallen below the threshold at which the persistent ancestors can maintain coherence. The ancestors, in a starving court, do not disperse immediately; they fade. The fading is gradual — the ancestor's memories become less accessible, the ancestor's voice becomes fainter, the ancestor's counsel becomes more riddled and less reliable. A starving ancestor, consulted, gives counsel that is half-memory and half-confabulation, and the confabulation is not distinguishable from the memory (the ancestor does not know which is which). A court that starves long enough passes through dormancy (the Pillar Court's state) into dissolution — the anchors, no longer sustained, disperse one by one, over centuries. The dispersal is final (寂滅, per doc 15 §3.4); the ancestor does not return to bardo; the ancestor does not reincarnate; the ancestor is gone. The Courts' Records Department tracks starving courts (the Register of Declining Courts, 衰祠錄) and considers the tracking one of its quieter duties; the Foreign Affairs department has, twice in the last millennium, organized veneration-pulse donations from allied lineages to slow a friendly court's starvation (the Garden Court donated to the Mourner's Court, six hundred years ago; the donation slowed the Mourner's Court's decline but did not reverse it).

**The persistence-budget as a finite resource.** Each persistent ancestor has a persistence-budget (per doc 15 §3.4) — the qi-authority the anchor holds, distinct from the court's reservoir. The budget is the ancestor's capacity to act: to manifest, to intervene, to consult, to maintain coherence. The budget is finite; it depletes with use; it does not regenerate (the cosmos is finite and non-regenerating, doc 00 §1). An ancestor who never intervenes and is rarely consulted can persist for ten thousand years or more (Yan Wugui, nine thousand years and counting; the First Court's teacher, eight thousand years and counting); an ancestor who intervenes frequently (the Yan Court's ancestors, who have intervened in three lineage-existential crises across nine millennia) depletes faster. An ancestor who exhausts the budget disperses (final death, 寂滅).

The budget is the ancestor's most precious possession, and the ancestor's counsel about whether to intervene is, in part, the ancestor's calculation of whether the intervention is worth the budget's cost. This is the tragic engine of the ancestral courts: the lineage's greatest resource (the persistent ancestor) is a finite resource, and every use of the resource diminishes it. A lineage in existential crisis asks its ancestor to intervene; the ancestor, calculating the cost against the budget, may refuse; the refusal is, in the lineage's eyes, betrayal; in the ancestor's eyes, the budget's preservation for the next crisis (which may be greater). The ancestor who intervenes in every crisis disperses within a millennium; the ancestor who refuses all but the existential persists for ten. The lineage's calculus and the ancestor's calculus are not the same calculus, and the difference is the ancestral court's central tension.

**The inter-court donation economy.** Veneration-pulses are, in principle, lineage-bound — a lineage's veneration flows along the lineage's qi-connection to the lineage's court, and the flow cannot be redirected. In practice, a restricted form of inter-court donation is possible: a court's senior ancestor (typically a Spirit Severance+ persistent ancestor with a substantial remaining persistence-budget) may invest a portion of the budget in a veneration-pulse that, bundled by the court's law-inscriptions into a transferable packet, can be conveyed by a Tribulation Crossing cultivator's personal transit to another court. The donation is the cosmic economy's analogue of a mortal wealthy household's charitable donation to a poorer village's lineage hall — the same gesture, at a scale where the gesture's qi-cost is felt by the donor. The Garden Court's donation to the Mourner's Court (six hundred years ago, per the Foreign Affairs department's record) is the named instance; the donation slowed the Mourner's Court's decline but did not reverse it (the Mourner's Court's underlying problem — no living lineage, no veneration — is not solvable by donation). The donation economy is small (perhaps two or three donations per Precelestial century); the donations are tracked by the Foreign Affairs department as a metric of inter-lineage alliance health; the Lineage Bloc considers the donations a violation of the lineage-jurisdiction clause's spirit (a lineage's resources should stay in the lineage's court); the Reform Bloc considers the donations a model for the courts' broader cosmic-economy reforms.

Liang Zhu, who has traveled the wild paths and knows the underworld market's gossip, mentions in passing that the market has a supplier — "a clerk in the courts' records who can extend a dead anchor's window" (per doc 38 §9). The protagonist, connecting this to Pei Liang's harvest-store, asks Liang Zhu where the anchors go. Liang Zhu, shrugging, says "the ancestral courts, if the lineage has one; otherwise, dispersal." The protagonist, for the first time, understands that Wang Xiaodi's anchor — even if rescued from Pei Liang's harvest — has no court to be retrieved into. The understanding is a private grief the protagonist does not share with Liang Zhu.

**Act 2, session ~500 (Core Formation).** The protagonist perceives the residue-trace of Wang Xiaodi's anchor in Pei Liang's harvest-store (doc 26 §3.4, doc 38 §9). The perception carries Scribe Yan's falsified-dispersal signature; the protagonist's Residual Error Sense registers the wrongness. The protagonist knows, now, that the bardo-harvest is enabled by a courts official; the protagonist does not yet know the official's name. The protagonist also knows — has internalized — that the Wang lineage's lack of an ancestral court is part of the structural vulnerability that allowed Wang Xiaodi's anchor to be harvested. If the Wang lineage had a court, the court's retrieval rite would have lifted Wang Xiaodi's anchor before the harvest window opened. The protagonist's pursuit of Pei Liang becomes, at this point, also a pursuit of the structural condition that made Wang Xiaodi harvestable.

**Act 3, session ~1100-1200 (the meeting with Bei Huai).** The protagonist meets Bei Huai (per doc 39 §5.1) in a mortal village in the Cangli Riverlands. Bei Huai, the Mourner, is the protagonist's first encounter with a Mahayana cultivator and the protagonist's first encounter with a being who has an ancestral court (the Mourner's Court, §2.9) and does not visit it. Bei Huai does not discuss the court in the meeting; the protagonist does not yet know the court exists. The meeting's content (per doc 39 §5.1) is the bardo-harvest loophole, Gu Wu, and the amendment. The protagonist leaves the meeting walking toward the deep Precelestial for the first time.

**Act 3, session ~1300-1400 (the meeting with Gu Wu, at the First Court).** The protagonist, having crossed Tribulation, travels to Gu Wu's small ancestral court (the First Court, §2.5). This is the protagonist's first visit to an ancestral court. The visit is the meeting with Gu Wu (per doc 39 §5.2). The protagonist, entering the court, experiences doc 15 §3.6's specified recognition — the comfort of being in a room that is also a memory — but the recognition is false: the First Court is not the protagonist's lineage's court, and the comfort the protagonist perceives is the comfort of any lineage cultivator in any court (the courts share a texture).

The protagonist's Residual Error Sense, straining, perceives the difference: the First Court's qi is Gu Wu's qi, not the protagonist's lineage's qi; the comfort is borrowed. The perception is, for the protagonist, the first concrete experience of what the Wang lineage's courtlessness costs — a cost the protagonist had understood abstractly since Act 2, but had not felt until standing in a court that was not the protagonist's own. The protagonist's grief for Wang Xiaodi, which had been the grief of a sibling, becomes, in the First Court's borrowed comfort, the grief of a lineage founder who has not yet founded.

The protagonist's teaching (per doc 39 §5.2) takes a Precelestial week (= seven Acquired years); the protagonist, in that week, perceives Gu Wu's four thousand years of grief and the bardo-window law's full inscription. The protagonist leaves the First Court with the law's text and the amendment's preparation.

**Act 3, session ~1400-1500 (the protagonist's own court, in the Transcend-path variant).** If the protagonist chooses the Transcend path's lineage-founding variant (§3 above), the protagonist, after authoring the bardo-harvest-loophole amendment and joining the pantheon, performs the lineage-founding rite at Wang Family Bend (or at whatever the Wang lineage's seat has become by the protagonist's return — the village is, by session ~1000, aged past recognition, per doc 39 §5.1). The rite is the protagonist's only visit to the Wang lineage's mortal seat as a Mahayana cultivator; the visit is brief (the time-debt makes every Acquired-stratum visit costly); the protagonist's mortal kin, by this point, are nine generations descended from the protagonist's siblings and do not know the protagonist's name. The rite binds the protagonist's Mahayana authority to the Wang lineage's qi-climate; the binding is the Wang Court's pre-foundation. The court itself will form, in the Precelestial, at the protagonist's eventual dispersal (centuries or millennia in the future). The protagonist does not live to see the court. The protagonist's descendants, if the refounding succeeds, will consult the court across the next ten thousand years, and the court's first ancestor — the protagonist — will be the court's senior persistent presence, the way Yan Wugui is the Yan Court's senior persistent presence. The Wang Court, in the Transcend-path variant, is the protagonist's only genuine posterity; it is also, per the doctrine's finitude clause, a posterity the protagonist will not perceive. The tending is the meaning.

## 7. What this document enables

This document specifies the ancestral courts as a finite, populated institution. Specifically:

- **Ten named great ancestral courts,** each with a founding ancestor (realm, age, year of death), a lineage, a location, a state, a courts-relationship, and a living-lineage relationship. The Precelestial's upper-stratum politics is no longer abstract; it is the Yan Court's drift, the Pillar Court's dormancy, the Silent Hexagram Court's seal, the First Court's solitude, the Mourner's Court's decline, and the other five courts' specific textures.
- **The Wang lineage's status** as courtless — a structural disadvantage the protagonist experiences as a loneliness, sharpens the protagonist's grief for Wang Xiaodi, and gives the Transcend-path's lineage-founding variant its specific emotional weight.
- **Two named corrupted courts** — the Silent Hexagram Court (sealed, doc 37 §7.3) and the Yan Court (drifting, unrecognized, this document). The corruption mechanism (doc 15 §3.5) is no longer abstract; it is the Yan Court's confidence, and the confidence is the trap.
- **The qi-economy of veneration,** specified as a finite resource the lineage generates and the court consumes. The cosmic economy (doc 47) inherits this specification: veneration is a cosmic currency, and the ancestral courts are the institutions that consume it.
- **The protagonist's encounter arc,** traced from Act 1's ignorance through Act 2's misunderstanding to Act 3's first visit and (in the Transcend-path variant) the protagonist's own court's pre-foundation. The encounter arc is the protagonist's relationship to the upper stratum's most intimate institution.

The doctrine's test (AGENTS.md Part 3) is honored: every gate specified here is paired with what it unlocks. The ten courts unlock the Precelestial's lineage politics. The Wang lineage's status unlocks the protagonist's grief's structural dimension. The two corrupted courts unlock the corruption mechanism's specific horror. The qi-economy unlocks the cosmic economy's veneration-currency. The encounter arc unlocks the Act 3 Precelestial content's emotional ground.

The document's relationship to the broader corpus: doc 15 §3 specified the ancestral courts' system; this document populates the system with named beings. Doc 38 §7.1 specified the courts' institutional relationship to the ancestral courts; this document specifies the ancestral courts' side of that relationship, court by court. Doc 37 §6-7 named the Silent Hexagram corrupted ancestor and the Pillar Dynasty and the Crimson Vow Alliance as fallen civilizations; this document specifies those civilizations' courts' persistence (or non-persistence) as cosmic institutions. Doc 39 named the nine Mahayana and their holdings; this document specifies five of those Mahayana's lineage courts (the First Court for Gu Wu, the Builder's Court for Fan Xuan, the Garden Court for Yin Mu, the Cartographer's Court for Tu Nan, the Mourner's Court for Bei Huai, the Judge's Court for Lu Shen — six courts, paired with six Mahayana). Doc 47 (the cosmic-resources document, written alongside this one) inherits this document's qi-economy specification as the cosmic economy's veneration-currency substrate.

## 8. What this document does not decide

- **The full roster of minor ancestral courts.** Twenty-three minor courts exist; their names and founders are not specified here. The minor courts' roster is held in the Records Department's archive (accessible at Tribulation Crossing); the protagonist, at Spirit Severance or higher, can consult the archive.
- **The specific histories of the extinct courts.** Forty-one extinct courts are recorded in the Register of Extinct Courts; their histories are not specified here. The extinct courts' histories are lore to be developed in a later document, generated against the seed.
- **The Yan Court's resolution.** The Yan Court's drift is specified; the resolution (whether the Yan clan recognizes the drift, whether Yan Qingshi contests Wugui, whether the Transcend-path protagonist's amendment forces the recognition) is not. The resolution is the Transcend path's secondary consequence (the primary consequence is the bardo-harvest loophole's closure), and it is a content-design decision, not a cosmology decision.
- **The Wang Court's specific form, in the Transcend-path variant.** The protagonist's choice to refound the Wang lineage is specified; the court's specific form (location, inscription, the protagonist's eventual anchor's coherence) is not. The court's form is the protagonist's post-game work, and the post-game's deepest content is the protagonist's relationship to the court they will not live to enter.
- **The ancestor-retrieval rite's specific mechanics.** Doc 24 §1.9 resolved the threshold question (Void Amalgamation minimum); the rite's specific mechanics (the qi-cost, the bardo-window timing, the place-bond handle's specific form) are not specified here. The mechanics are specified in the cultivation-systems document (TBD), against the seed.
- **The protagonist's post-game relationship to the Wang Court, in the Transcend-path variant.** The Wang Court's pre-foundation (the lineage-founding rite) is specified; the protagonist's post-dispersal relationship to the court (the protagonist's anchor's coherence, the protagonist's counsel to the Wang lineage's descendants, the protagonist's eventual corruption or non-corruption across millennia) is not. The post-game relationship is the protagonist's afterlife's content, and the afterlife's content is, by the doctrine's finitude clause, content the protagonist (as a living being) cannot perceive. The Wang Court's story, after the protagonist's dispersal, is the cosmos's story, not the protagonist's.
- **The specific protagonist-encounter content for each court.** The protagonist's first visit (to the First Court, per §6) is specified; the protagonist's optional visits to the other nine courts (and to the minor courts, and to the ruins of the extinct courts) are not. The optional-visit content is the Act 3 cosmic-content design's bulk, and the design is the player's to make, against the seed.
