# 18 — Economy System

**Status:** Candidate canon. The cultivation world's economic substrate.
**Date:** 2026-08-03

---

## 0. What this document is

The economy is not a side system. It is the engine of scarcity that makes cultivation meaningful. Without scarcity, there is no conflict. Without conflict, there is no story. This document specifies the full economic model: currencies, markets, trade routes, production, and the flows between mortal and cultivation worlds.

---

## 1. The three currencies

### 1.1 Copper cash (文)

The daily currency of the mortal world. 1 string of 1000 cash = 1 tael of silver (nominal; the ratio fluctuates). Used for: salt, tools, cloth, daily labor, small transactions. Most mortal households rarely see silver; they live in cash.

### 1.2 Silver (銀)

The tax and large-transaction currency. Denominated in taels (兩). Used for: land purchases, bride prices, tax payments, sect tribute. The silver-grain price ratio is the peasant's economic weather: silver scarcity in a bad harvest year (when grain prices fall) is the classic crisis.

### 1.3 Spirit stones (靈石)

The cultivation world's currency. A spirit stone is a crystallized qi concentrate, produced by spirit veins (rare) or by Core Formation+ cultivators (expensive). Used for: cultivation resources, pills, talismans, formations, sect dues, mercenary contracts.

The exchange rate between silver and spirit stones is not fixed. It depends on the local spirit vein density, the local sect's demand, and the political situation. A spirit stone might be worth 100 taels in a spirit-vein-rich region and 500 taels in a barren one.

### 1.4 Grain (穀)

The reserve currency. Not a "currency" in the transactional sense, but the ultimate store of value. A household's grain reserves are its real wealth. Grain is hoarded, lent (at 50-100% annual interest in bad years), and used to pay labor. When the grain runs out, the household starves or borrows.

## 2. The markets

### 2.1 The mortal market (鎮)

Per document 04 §6.2, the market town holds a market on the 1st, 5th, 10th, 15th, 20th, and 25th of each lunar month. Villagers sell grain, cloth, pigs; buy salt, iron, paper, incense. The market is also the news network.

### 2.2 The cultivation market (坊市)

A market for cultivators, held at specific locations (usually near sects or at crossroads). Sells: spirit herbs, pills, talismans, formation materials, beast parts, techniques (manuals). Buys: the same, plus spirit stones. The cultivation market is where the two economies meet: a mortal herb gatherer can sell a spirit herb for spirit stones (if they can find a buyer who doesn't cheat them).

### 2.3 The auction (拍賣)

For high-value items (breakthrough pills, rare treasures, inheritance manuals). Held by sects or the Immortal Alliance. Bidders pay in spirit stones. The auction is a social event — who bids what reveals alliances and rivalries.

## 3. Production

### 3.1 Mortal production

Per document 04. Wet-rice agriculture (the main caloric base), dryland gardens (vegetables, hemp, mulberry), weaving (cloth as cash), pig/chicken/poultry, carpentry, smithing (limited — iron tools are bought from the market town, not made in the village). The charcoal trade (upland burners sell to lowland villages). The salt trade (licensed, state monopoly).

### 3.2 Cultivation production

- **Herb gathering** (採藥): mortals or low-realm cultivators gather spirit herbs from the wild. Dangerous (spirit beasts, contamination, terrain). The gatherer's skill is in finding and identifying herbs, not in combat.
- **Beast hunting** (獵妖): Qi Condensation+ cultivators hunt spirit beasts for their cores (內丹, the crystallized qi of a peak beast), hides, bones, and organs. The core is the most valuable part — it can be refined into pills or used directly for cultivation.
- **Alchemy** (煉丹): per document 16. Alchemists produce pills from herbs, beast parts, and minerals. The alchemist's profit margin is enormous (raw materials worth 10 spirit stones produce a pill worth 100), but the failure rate is high and the skill is rare.
- **Formation inscription** (畫陣): per document 16. Formation inscribers sell their services to sects and wealthy cultivators. A defensive formation for a sect compound might cost 1000 spirit stones.
- **Talisman inscription** (畫符): per document 16. Talisman makers sell disposable talismans to cultivators who need emergency capabilities. Lower margin than formations, but higher volume.
- **Mining** (採礦): spirit stone mines (rare, contested), iron mines (mortal-scale, state-regulated), precious metals (gold, silver — the state's reserve).

### 3.3 The production chain

```
Spirit vein → ambient qi → spirit herb (100 years) → alchemist → pill → cultivator
Spirit vein → ambient qi → spirit beast (100 years) → hunter → beast core → alchemist → pill → cultivator
Iron mine → smith → tool → mortal farmer → grain → market → cash → tax → state
```

Each link in the chain has a time cost (herbs take decades to mature, beasts take decades to cultivate, pills take days to refine). The economy's tempo is measured in years, not days. A sect that over-harvests its herb gardens today will be poor in 50 years.

## 4. Trade routes

### 4.1 The river trade

The Cangli River (per document 04) is the main trade artery. Boats carry: grain downstream (to the market town and the county seat), salt upstream (from the official depot to the villages), spirit herbs and beast parts downstream (from the uplands to the cultivation market), pills and talismans upstream (from the cultivation market to the upland sects).

### 4.2 The road trade

Overland routes connect sects to cultivation markets and to each other. Caravans (mortal-driven, cultivator-guarded) carry high-value goods. The caravan trade is dangerous (bandits, spirit beasts, weather) and profitable.

### 4.3 The cross-stratum trade

Between the Mortal and Acquired strata: the grotto-heaven trade. Grotto-heavens (per document 19, TBD) produce rare herbs and beasts that cannot survive in the Mortal stratum. The trade is controlled by the sect that holds the grotto-heaven. The margin is astronomical.

## 5. The household economy (mortal)

Per document 04 §6.3, a typical Wang tenant household runs a subsistence deficit in a normal year, surviving by the pig sale, weaving, and day labor. The economy is not a "game balance" abstraction; it is the engine of mortal suffering that makes the player want to cultivate.

### 5.1 The debt cycle

1. Bad harvest (flood, drought, pest) → grain deficit
2. Borrow grain at 50-100% annual interest
3. Next year: must grow enough to eat AND repay the loan
4. If next year is also bad: borrow more, sell assets (pig, dryland, surface rights)
5. If the spiral continues: sell children (as servants, as brides, as apprentices), lose the land, become a tenant on what was once your own farm, or flee

This is the engine of rural poverty. The player who begins as a Wang tenant household member experiences this directly. The desire to cultivate is, in part, the desire to escape the debt cycle.

### 5.2 The cultivation escape

A Qi Condensation cultivator can earn 10-50 spirit stones per month (by hunting, gathering, or mercenary work). One spirit stone = ~100-500 taels of silver. One tael of silver = ~1000 cash. One month of cultivation work = 10-50 years of mortal labor.

This is the genre's central economic fact: cultivation is an escape from poverty so dramatic that it makes the mortal economy look like a cage. But the escape is not free — it costs time (training), risk (deviation, death), and social cost (leaving the mortal world behind).

## 6. The sect economy

### 6.1 Revenue

- **Spirit vein tithes**: sects that control spirit veins charge cultivators for access. The rate is 10-30% of the cultivator's harvest.
- **Disciple labor**: outer disciples perform sect tasks (guarding, gathering, construction) as part of their dues.
- **Pill/talisman/formation sales**: the sect's crafters produce goods for sale at the cultivation market.
- **Mercenary contracts**: the sect rents out its Core Formation+ cultivators for high-danger jobs.
- **Land tax**: sects that control mortal territories (rare, usually only great sects) collect tax from mortal villages.

### 6.2 Expenses

- **Disciple support**: food, housing, basic cultivation resources for outer disciples.
- **Sect maintenance**: buildings, formations, infrastructure.
- **Treasure acquisition**: buying rare herbs, beast cores, and inheritance manuals from the market.
- **Tribute**: to the Immortal Alliance (if the sect is a member) or to a higher-ranking sect (if the sect is a vassal).

### 6.3 The sect's economic lifecycle

A new sect (founded by a Nascent Soul cultivator) is poor: one spirit vein, a few disciples, no reputation. Over centuries, if it survives, it accumulates wealth (herb gardens, beast territories, crafting facilities, disciple numbers). A great sect (Core Formation+ sect master, hundreds of disciples, multiple spirit veins) is wealthier than any mortal kingdom. But the wealth is fragile: a single failed tribulation (the sect master dies) can collapse the sect's political power and trigger a wealth-grab by rival sects.

---

## 7. What this document enables

Every economic element is a generator input and a player-facing constraint:
- The player who begins in a Wang tenant household experiences the debt cycle directly
- The player who reaches Qi Condensation can earn spirit stones and send silver home (changing the household's trajectory)
- The player who joins a sect experiences the sect economy (dues, tasks, rewards)
- The player who reaches Core Formation can participate in the auction (buying breakthrough pills, rare treasures)
- The player who reaches Nascent Soul can found a sect (managing its economy)

The economy is not a menu. It is the engine of desire — the reason a mortal would risk everything to cultivate.
