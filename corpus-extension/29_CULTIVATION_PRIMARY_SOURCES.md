# 29. Cultivation Primary Sources

> **Purpose:** Ground the xianxia RPG's cultivation layer in primary-text passages drawn directly from the source traditions that xianxia draws upon — Daoist, alchemical, medical, ritual, divinatory, mythological, and Chan Buddhist. Each passage below is quoted in traditional Hanzi from Wikisource (the public-domain transcription), with a literal English gloss of the author's own, a citation, and a paragraph grounding the passage in the RPG's design. Where a text was inaccessible, it is noted honestly. No Hanzi has been fabricated; where the Wikisource transcription uses a variant character (e.g. 無/无, 乃/迺), the variant is preserved as printed. Per the brief, no pinyin with tone marks is given — readers should consult the citations for romanization. The grounding paragraphs assume the reconciliation decisions in doc 24 (karma, anchor, the realm ladder, Wang Lun, Old Chen, Wang Family Bend, the Great Yan Dynasty, the heart-mind cultivation system, the 24-solar-term calendar, the spirit-beast ecology, etc.).

## 1. 道德經 (Dàodéjīng)

**Author:** 老子 (Laozi), traditionally; modern scholarship assigns the received text to multiple redactors
**Date:** c. 6th century BCE (composition, traditional attribution); received Heshanggong/Wang Bi recension c. 3rd–4th century CE
**Source:** Wikisource (zh-hant), 老子 (匯校版), 第十六章

### Passage 1A — Emptiness, stillness, and the cycle of return

> 致虛極，守靜篤。萬物並作，吾以觀復。夫物芸芸，各復歸其根。歸根曰靜，靜曰覆命。覆命曰常，知常曰明。不知常，妄作凶。知常容，容乃公，公乃全，全乃天，天乃道，道乃久，歿身不殆。

**Gloss:** Attain emptiness to the utmost; guard stillness to the firmest. The ten thousand things rise together; I thereby observe their return. The things, so flourishing — each returns to its root. Returning to the root is called stillness; stillness is called returning to fate. Returning to fate is called the constant; knowing the constant is called illumination. Not knowing the constant, one acts rashly — disaster. Knowing the constant, one is accommodating; accommodating, one is equitable; equitable, one is kingly; kingly, one is heavenly; heavenly, one is of the Dao; of the Dao, one endures — to the end of one's body, no peril.

**Citation:** 道德經, 第十六章 (Dao De Jing, Chapter 16) — received text, 匯校版 comparative edition

### RPG Grounding

This passage grounds the **Qi Induction perception mode** (doc 03's first perceptual tier). The discipline is not "concentrate harder" but "致虛極，守靜篤" — empty out, hold still. The cultivator does not grasp after the qi; they "觀復" — observe the return. This reframes Qi Induction from active sensing to receptive clearing: the qi is already there, moving in its cycle, and the cultivator perceives it only after the perception-noise of their own intention has been emptied. The chain "歸根 → 靜 → 覆命 → 常 → 明" maps to the player's perceptual progression: stillness reveals root, root reveals fate (the qi-pattern), fate reveals constancy (the lawful pattern), constancy reveals illumination (clear sight). The warning "不知常，妄作凶" is the canonical statement of the premature-breakthrough risk that Old Chen carries in his body (doc 24 §1.3): the cultivator who acts without perceiving the constant acts blindly, and the action is dangerous. This passage is the textual anchor for the Qi Condensation meditation minigame — the player does not press buttons to "absorb qi"; they release inputs to clear the mind, and qi appears in the cleared space.

## 2. 莊子 (Zhuāngzǐ)

**Author:** 莊周 (Zhuāng Zhōu), with later editorial layers by Guo Xiang and others
**Date:** Late 4th century BCE (inner chapters); received Guo Xiang recension c. 312 CE
**Source:** Wikisource (zh-hant), 莊子/人間世 (chapter 4, "In the Human World")

### Passage 2A — The fasting of the heart-mind (心齋)

> 回曰：「敢問心齋。」仲尼曰：「若一志，无聽之以耳而聽之以心，无聽之以心而聽之以氣。聽止於耳，心止於符。氣也者，虚而待物者也。唯道集虚。虚者，心齋也。」

**Gloss:** Yan Hui said: "May I ask about the fasting of the heart-mind?" Confucius said: "Unify your will. Do not listen with the ears — listen with the heart-mind. Do not listen with the heart-mind — listen with the qi. Listening stops at the ear; the heart-mind stops at its concepts. Qi — it is empty and waits for things. Only the Dao gathers in emptiness. Emptiness is the fasting of the heart-mind."

**Citation:** 莊子, 內篇, 人間世第四 (Zhuangzi, Inner Chapters, "In the Human World," Ch. 4)

### RPG Grounding

This passage grounds the **heart-mind cultivation system** (心法) that supplements the qi-circulation mechanics. The three-tier listening hierarchy — 耳 (sensory input) → 心 (conceptual cognition) → 氣 (qi-perception) — is the canonical statement that perception can descend from coarse to fine, and that the finest tier is not "more thought" but a different mode entirely. For the RPG, this maps to Old Chen's three-tier instruction (doc 06 Scene 4): a Qi Condensation cultivator cannot perceive qi directly through 耳 (the body's senses) or 心 (analytical attention); they must shift to the 氣 tier — the perceptual mode that "虚而待物" — empty and waiting for things. The line "唯道集虚" (only the Dao gathers in emptiness) is the textual justification for why the emptiness-stillness of Dao De Jing ch 16 produces perception: emptiness is where the Dao collects itself, and the cultivator who maintains emptiness becomes a site where the Dao can gather. This is also the textual anchor for the 心魔 (heart-demon) mechanic (doc 24 §1.2): the heart-mind that is "stuck at 符" (concepts) and refuses to descend to 氣 is precisely the heart-mind that is vulnerable to delusional conviction (妄信).

## 3. 周易參同契 (Zhōuyì Cāntóngqì)

**Author:** 魏伯陽 (Wèi Bóyáng), traditionally
**Date:** c. 2nd century CE (Eastern Han); the foundational text of internal alchemy (內丹)
**Source:** Wikisource (zh-hant), 周易參同契/19章 (爻變功用章第十九) and /31章 (聖賢伏煉章第三十一)

### Passage 3A — The firing process mapped to the twelve message hexagrams (火候)

> 朔旦為復，陽炁始通。出入無疾，立表微剛。黃鐘建子，兆乃滋彰。播施柔暖，黎蒸得常。臨爐施條，開路正光。光耀漸進，日以益長。丑之大侶，結正低昂。仰以成泰，剛柔并隆。遠游交接，小往大來。… 乾健盛明，廣被四鄰。陽終于己，中而相干。… 遁世去位，收斂其精。… 否塞不通，萌芽不生。… 觀其權量，察眾秋情。… 剝爛肢體，消減其形。… 道窮則返，歸乎坤元。恆順地理，承天布宣。… 終坤復始，如復連環。

**Gloss:** At the new-morn dawn it is Fu [Hexagram 24, Return] — yang qi begins to flow. Going out and coming in, no haste; setting the gnomon, the firm [yang line] is slight. Yellow Bell [the pitch] is established at zi [the 1st branch]; the seedling then becomes visible. Diffusing soft warmth, the common folk attain the normal. At Lin [Hexagram 19, Approach] the furnace puts forth its lines, opening the path for the correct light. The light-glow advances by degrees; the day grows longer. At the great Lü [pitch] of chou [the 2nd branch], the high and low are settled. Looking up, one forms Tai [Hexagram 11, Peace] — the firm and yielding both swell. Far-journeying, they join; the small departs, the great arrives. … Qian [Hexagram 1, Heaven] is vigorous and bright, broadly covering the four neighbors. Yang culminates at si [the 6th branch]; in the middle they interpenetrate. … At Dun [Hexagram 33, Retreat] one retires from position, gathering in one's essence. … At Pi [Hexagram 12, Standstill] it is blocked and impassable; sprouts do not grow. … One observes the balance-weight and measure, examines the multitude's autumn state. … At Bo [Hexagram 23, Splitting Apart] the limbs are stripped, the form is reduced. … When the way is exhausted it returns — back to Kun [Hexagram 2, Earth] the origin. Ever following the earth's principle, bearing heaven's command, it is disseminated. … Ending at Kun, beginning again at Fu — like a linked ring.

**Citation:** 周易參同契, 爻變功用章第十九 (Cantong qi, "Chapter on the Function of Yao-Changes," Ch. 19)

### Passage 3B — The sages' concealment and the lineage transmission (伏煉)

> 惟昔聖賢，懷玄抱真，伏煉九鼎，化跡隱淪，含精養神，通德三光，津液腠理，筋骨致堅，眾邪辟除，正氣長存，積累長久，變形而仙。… 子繼父業，孫踵祖先，傳世迷惑，竟無見聞。

**Gloss:** Of old, the sages and worthies — they held the mysterious, embraced the true, refined the Nine Tripods in concealment, transformed their traces and vanished. They held the essence, nourished the spirit, communicated in virtue with the three lights [sun, moon, stars]. Their saliva and body-fluids reached the pores; their sinews and bones became firm. The multitude of evils was dispelled; the correct qi endured long. Accumulating over long time, they transformed their form and became immortals. … The son inherits the father's craft; the grandson follows the ancestor's forebear. Transmitted across generations, confused — in the end, none sees or hears.

**Citation:** 周易參同契, 聖賢伏煉章第三十一 (Cantong qi, "Chapter on the Sages' Concealed Refinement," Ch. 31)

### RPG Grounding

Passage 3A grounds the **internal alchemy firing process** (火候) that governs the player's cultivation cycle across Foundation Establishment → Core Formation → Nascent Soul → Spirit Severance (doc 24 §2.3). The twelve message hexagrams (復, 臨, 泰, 大壯, 夬, 乾, 姤, 遁, 否, 觀, 剝, 坤) are not decorative correspondence; they are the cycle of yang accumulation (Hexagram 24 → 1) and yin accumulation (Hexagram 44 → 2) that the cultivator's internal qi follows over a single year, mapped onto the 24 solar terms. The RPG's cultivation calendar is the firing schedule: a Qi Condensation cultivator cannot break through to Foundation Establishment by meditating more; they must align their firing with the correct hexagram-window (the winter solstice / zi month / Hexagram 24 is the canonical breakthrough moment for any yang-initiating integration). The phrase "終坤復始，如復連環" (ending at Kun, beginning at Fu, like a linked ring) is the canonical statement that cultivation is cyclical, not linear — every breakthrough completes a cycle and begins a new one. Passage 3B grounds the **lineage-transmission mechanic**: knowledge of cultivation is transmitted across generations but "傳世迷惑，竟無見聞" — across transmission, the meaning is corrupted and the lineage forgets. This is the canonical justification for why Old Chen's instruction is partial (doc 24 §1.3): the Cangwu Sect and even the Azure Sword Sect are inheritors of corrupted lineages, and the player's teacher can only teach what survived the corruption. The phrase "變形而仙" (transform the form and become immortal) also anchors the Spirit Severance realm: the cultivator's body itself becomes a transformation-target, not just a vessel.

## 4. 肘後備急方 (Zhǒuhòu Bèijí Fāng)

**Author:** 葛洪 (Gě Hóng), with later supplements by 陶弘景 (Táo Hóngjǐng) and 楊用道 (Yáng Yòngdào)
**Date:** c. 4th century CE (Eastern Jin); supplemented 6th century CE
**Source:** Wikisource (zh-hant), 肘後備急方/卷3, 治寒熱諸瘧方第十六

### Passage 4A — The qinghao (青蒿) malaria recipe

> 治瘧病方。… 青蒿一握。以水二升漬，絞取汁。盡服之。

**Gloss:** Formula for treating malaria-agues. … A handful of qinghao [Artemisia annua, sweet wormwood]. Soak it in two sheng of water; wring out the juice. Drink it all.

**Citation:** 肘後備急方, 卷三, 治寒熱諸瘧方第十六 (Zhouhou Beiji Fang, Vol. 3, "Formulas for Treating Cold-and-Heat Malarial Agues," Ch. 16)

### RPG Grounding

This is the recipe Tu Youyou credited as the source of the cold-extraction method that yielded artemisinin and the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. It grounds the **village's medical practice** at Wang Family Bend (doc 24 §4.3): the village's healer (likely a lineage aunt or the wife of one of the Wang elders) works from a pharmacopoeia descended from Ge Hong's emergency-formula tradition — not from immortal cultivation lore. The two-sheng cold-soak method is not a quirk of folk medicine; it is a deliberately non-heated extraction, because Ge Hong (or his source) understood that boiling destroys the active principle. For the RPG, this is the textual anchor for the village's practical medical knowledge and the basis for a herbalism subsystem: mortal medicine can be effective (the qinghao recipe actually works against malarial fever, including the schistosomiasis that killed Wang Tianlu per doc 24 §4.4), but the village's pharmacopoeia is incomplete — schistosomiasis is not intermittent malaria, and the recipe does not save Wang Tianlu. The player's grandfather dies despite the village having a near-Nobel-quality formula in its archives, because the formula treats the wrong disease. This is the design hook: mortality is not a question of effort or care but of correct diagnosis, and a Qi Condensation cultivator returning to the village with body-perception can diagnose what the village healer cannot. The cultivation layer's first moral pressure is medical: when do you intervene, and when do you let the lineage's knowledge run its course?

## 5. 黃帝內經·素問 (Huángdì Nèijīng · Sùwèn)

**Author:** Anonymous; attributed to 黃帝 (the Yellow Emperor) in dialogue with 歧伯 (Qibo); compiled and annotated by 王冰 (Wáng Bīng, Tang dynasty)
**Date:** Core material c. 2nd century BCE (Western Han); Tang dynasty recension 8th century CE
**Source:** Wikisource (zh-hant), 黃帝內經素問 (四庫全書本)/卷02, 陰陽應象大論篇第五

### Passage 5A — The five phases generating the five viscera (五行生五藏)

> 東方生風，風生木，木生酸，酸生肝，肝生筋，筋生心，肝主目。其在天為玄，在人為道，在地為化。化生五味，道生智，玄生神。神在天為風，在地為木，在體為筋，在藏為肝，在色為蒼，在音為角，在聲為呼，在變動為握，在竅為目，在味為酸，在志為怒。怒傷肝，悲勝怒；風傷筋，燥勝風；酸傷筋，辛勝酸。… 故天地者，萬物之上下也；陰陽者，血氣之男女也；左右者，陰陽之道路也；水火者，陰陽之徵兆也；陰陽者，萬物之能始也。

**Gloss:** The east generates wind; wind generates wood; wood generates sour; sour generates the liver; the liver generates the sinews; the sinews generate the heart; the liver governs the eyes. In heaven it is the mysterious; in human it is the Dao; in earth it is transformation. Transformation generates the five flavors; the Dao generates wisdom; the mysterious generates the spirit. The spirit, in heaven, is wind; in earth, is wood; in body, is sinews; in viscera, is liver; in color, is blue-green; in sound, is the jue note; in voice, is the shout; in movement, is the grasp; in aperture, is the eye; in flavor, is sour; in emotion, is anger. Anger harms the liver; grief overcomes anger. Wind harms the sinews; dryness overcomes wind. Sour harms the sinews; pungent overcomes sour. … Thus heaven and earth are the above-and-below of the ten thousand things; yin and yang are the male-and-female of blood and qi; left and right are the road of yin and yang; water and fire are the signs of yin and yang; yin and yang are the originary capacity of the ten thousand things.

**Citation:** 黃帝內經素問, 卷二, 陰陽應象大論篇第五 (Huangdi Neijing Suwen, Vol. 2, "Treatise on the Correspondence of Phenomena to Yin-Yang," Ch. 5)

### Passage 5B — The five viscera receiving the five phases (五藏應四時)

> 帝曰：五藏應四時，各有收受乎？歧伯曰：有。東方青色，入通於肝，開竅於目，藏精於肝。其病發驚駭。其味酸，其類草木，其畜雞，其榖麥。其應四時，上為歲星。是以春氣在頭也。其音角，其數八。是以知病之在筋也。其臭臊。南方赤色，入通於心，開竅於耳，藏精於心。故病在五藏。其味苦，其類火，其畜羊，其榖黍。中央色黃，入通於脾，開竅於口。西方白色，入通於肺，開竅於鼻。北方黑色，入通於腎，開竅於二陰。

**Gloss:** The Yellow Emperor said: "The five viscera correspond to the four seasons — does each have what it receives?" Qibo said: "They do. The east's blue-green color enters and communicates with the liver; it opens its aperture at the eyes; it stores the essence in the liver. Its illness manifests as startling fright. Its flavor is sour; its category is grass-and-tree; its domestic animal is the chicken; its grain is wheat. Its correspondence with the four seasons culminates above as the Year Star [Jupiter]. Therefore the spring qi is in the head. Its note is jue; its number is eight. Therefore one knows the illness's location is in the sinews. Its odor is rank. The south's red color enters and communicates with the heart; it opens its aperture at the ears; it stores the essence in the heart — therefore the illness is in the five viscera. Its flavor is bitter; its category is fire; its domestic animal is the sheep; its grain is glutinous millet. The center's yellow color enters and communicates with the spleen; it opens its aperture at the mouth. The west's white color enters and communicates with the lungs; it opens its aperture at the nose. The north's black color enters and communicates with the kidneys; it opens its aperture at the two yin [urethra and anus]."

**Citation:** 黃帝內經素問, 卷一, 金匱真言論篇第四 (Huangdi Neijing Suwen, Vol. 1, "Treatise on the Truth from the Golden Casket," Ch. 4)

### RPG Grounding

This passage grounds the **qi/meridian model** that underlies the entire cultivation layer's medical and combat subsystems. The five-phase generation chain (東方 → 風 → 木 → 酸 → 肝 → 筋 → 心) in 5A is not symbolic correspondence; it is the canonical mapping by which a Qi Condensation cultivator's body-perception reveals which organ, which tissue, which emotion, which flavor, and which phase their current imbalance sits in. The cultivation layer's deviation taxonomy (doc 24 §1.2) inherits this model: a heart-fire deviation manifests in the shout-voice, the laugh-emotion, the tongue-aperture, the bitter-flavor, the red-color — and the counterplay is the phase that overcomes fire (water, fear, salty, black). The relation "怒傷肝，悲勝怒" (anger harms the liver; grief overcomes anger) is the canonical statement that emotional states are phase-medical events, and the heart-mind cultivation system is therefore inseparable from the body-cultivation system: a cultivator who does not work through their anger accumulates liver-damage, and that liver-damage eventually compromises qi-circulation. For Wang Lun's divination (doc 24 §2.5), the line "陰陽者，萬物之能始也" (yin and yang are the originary capacity of the ten thousand things) is the canonical claim that the qi-topography of the present contains the momentum of the future — divination is the reading of yin-yang momentum, not the reading of fate. Passage 5B adds the diagnostic grammar: each viscera has its color, aperture, flavor, animal, grain, note, and number, and the village healer's diagnostic method (and the player's, once Qi-Induction is unlocked) consists in reading which correspondence is misaligned — a patient who craves sour food and startles in fright is showing liver-pathology, and the prescription is the phase that overcomes wood (metal, pungent, white). The player's medical UI shows the body as a five-phase diagram, not as a hit-point bar; an injury is a phase-imbalance, not a damage-value.

## 6. 禮記·月令 (Lǐjì · Yuèlìng)

**Author:** Anonymous; compiled by the Han ritual school, traditionally associated with 戴德 (Dài Dé) and 戴聖 (Dài Shèng)
**Date:** c. 3rd–2nd century BCE (late Warring States to Western Han)
**Source:** Wikisource (zh-hant), 禮記/月令, 孟春之月 (the first month of spring)

### Passage 6A — The Beginning of Spring (立春) and the seasonal prohibitions

> 孟春之月，日在營室，昏參中，旦尾中。其日甲乙。其帝大皞，其神句芒。其蟲鱗。其音角。律中大蔟。其數八。其味酸，其臭羶。其祀戶，祭先脾。東風解凍，蟄蟲始振，魚上冰，獺祭魚，鴻鴈來。… 是月也，以立春。先立春三日，大史謁之天子曰：「某日立春，盛德在木。」天子乃齊。立春之日，天子親帥三公九卿諸侯大夫以迎春於東郊。… 是月也，天氣下降，地氣上騰，天地和同，草木萌動。王命布農事。… 禁止伐木。毋覆巢，毋殺孩蟲、胎、夭、飛鳥。毋麛、毋卵。毋聚大眾，毋置城郭。掩骼埋胔。是月也，不可以稱兵，稱兵必天殃。毋變天之道，毋絕地之理，毋亂人之紀。

**Gloss:** In the first month of spring, the sun is at the Wallpaper [constellation]; at dusk, the Triad [Shen] culminates; at dawn, the Tail [Wei] culminates. Its day is jia-yi. Its emperor is Tai Hao; its god is Gou Mang. Its creature is the scaled. Its note is jue. Its pitch is Da Cu. Its number is eight. Its flavor is sour; its odor is rank. Its sacrifice is to the Door; the first [offering] goes to the spleen. The east wind thaws the frost; the hibernating insects begin to stir; fish rise to beneath the ice; the otter sacrifices fish; the wild geese arrive. … In this month, the Beginning of Spring occurs. Three days before the Beginning of Spring, the Grand Astrologer addresses the Son of Heaven: "On such-and-such a day is the Beginning of Spring; the flourishing virtue is in wood." The Son of Heaven then fasts. On the day of the Beginning of Spring, the Son of Heaven personally leads the Three Dukes, Nine Ministers, feudal lords, and grandees to welcome spring at the eastern suburb. … In this month, the qi of heaven descends, the qi of earth rises; heaven and earth harmonize and unite; grass and trees begin to bud. The king commands the dissemination of agricultural affairs. … The cutting of timber is forbidden. Do not overturn nests; do not kill the young insect, the fetus, the newborn, the fledgling bird. Do not [kill] the fawn; do not [take] eggs. Do not assemble great crowds; do not build city walls. Cover the exposed bones and bury the rotting corpses. In this month, one may not take up arms; to take up arms will bring heavenly disaster. Do not alter the way of heaven; do not sever the principle of earth; do not disrupt the bonds of human beings.

**Citation:** 禮記, 月令, 孟春之月 (Liji, "Proceedings of Government in the Different Months," first month of spring)

### RPG Grounding

This passage grounds the **24-solar-term calendar** that governs the RPG's seasonal event system (cross-referenced with doc 10 §2A, Wang Zhen's Nongshu). The Yueling is the canonical text: each solar term carries an official ritual (the emperor's procession to the eastern suburb), a phenological sequence (東風解凍，蟄蟲始振，魚上冰，獺祭魚，鴻鴈來 — five micro-events that constitute the season), a set of prohibitions (no logging, no nest-robbing, no fawn-killing, no army-raising), and a divinatory warning (conducting summer business in spring produces "風雨不時，草木蚤落" — unseasonable storms and premature leaf-fall). For the RPG, this means the calendar is not flavor text; it is the lawful order of the Acquired Stratum. A mortal village that violates the prohibitions (a bandit raid during 立春 that forces fawn-killing, or a sect's logging operation that violates 毋覆巢) generates not legal but cosmic consequences — the season's qi is disrupted, the crops fail, the herd thins. This is the textual anchor for the **sect-vs-village moral ecology**: when the Cangwu Sect's spirit-vein mining disrupts the local qi-climate, the villagers see it as a Yueling violation, and the player can intervene either by enforcing the prohibition against the sect or by mitigating its consequences. The five-phenology pentad (東風解凍 → 蟄蟲始振 → 魚上冰 → 獺祭魚 → 鴻鴈來) is the design template for the player's seasonal event log: each solar term has five micro-events that fire in sequence over roughly fifteen days, and a player who can read the phenology can predict the qi-topography's seasonal drift.

## 7. 朱子家禮 (Zhūzǐ Jiālǐ)

**Author:** 朱熹 (Zhū Xī), traditionally; modern scholarship suggests compilation by Zhu Xi's disciples from his instructions
**Date:** c. 12th century CE (Southern Song); circulated posthumously, canonical by Yuan
**Source:** Wikisource (zh-hant), 家禮 (四庫全書本)/卷四, 喪禮

### Passage 7A — The three-year mourning grade (斬衰三年)

> 成服。五服之人各服其服，入就位，然後朝哭相弔如儀。其服之制：一曰斬衰三年。… 其正服則子為父也；其加服則嫡孫父卒為祖若曾高祖承重者也，父為嫡子當為後者也；其義服則婦為舅也，夫承重則從服也，為人後者為所後父也，為所後祖承重也，妻為夫也，妾為君也。

**Gloss:** Putting on the mourning garments. The persons of the five mourning-grades each don their respective garments, enter, and take their positions; then they perform the morning wailing and console one another according to the ritual. Of the garment regulations: the first is zhan-cui [the coarsest hemp] for three years. … Its primary grade is the son for his father. Its additional grade is the eldest grandson, when the father has died, for the grandfather or great-grandfather or great-great-grandfather, bearing the heavy responsibility [of the lineage]; and the father for the eldest son who is to be the successor. Its righteous grade is the wife for her father-in-law; if the husband bears the heavy responsibility, she follows in mourning. [It is also] for one who is adopted as heir, for the father of the adopter; for the grandfather of the adopter, bearing the heavy responsibility; the wife for the husband; the concubine for the master.

**Citation:** 家禮 (朱子), 卷四, 喪禮, 成服 (Zhuzi Jiali, Vol. 4, "Mourning Ritual," "Putting on the Garments")

### RPG Grounding

This passage grounds the **funeral rite** that anchors the player's lineage obligation at Wang Family Bend. The three-year zhan-cui mourning is the deepest of the five mourning grades; it is worn for a father by his son, for a lineage-head by his heir-apparent, and for a husband by his wife. For the RPG, this is the canonical textual basis for the player's grief-obligation to Wang Tianlu (the grandfather, dead six years) and to Wang Xiaodi (the younger sibling, dead two years, per doc 24 §4.4). The "承重" (bearing the heavy responsibility) phrase is the canonical claim that the lineage-head's death is not merely a personal loss but a structural disruption — the heir now carries the lineage's qi-line forward, and the three-year mourning is the period during which the qi-transfer is consolidated. For the player, this means the funeral rite is not optional cutscene content; it is the ritual technology by which the player's anchor (靈樞, doc 24 §2.2) inscribes itself in the lineage's ancestral court. The five-grade taxonomy also grounds the village's social structure: the 31 households of Wang Family Bend each have mourning obligations to one another calculated by genealogical distance, and a player who violates these obligations (e.g., by leaving the village to cultivate during a close kinsman's mourning period) accrues social cost that NPCs remember. The line "妾為君也" (the concubine for the master) is the textual anchor for the village's polygamous household structure — concubines are members of the lineage with corresponding ritual duties.

## 8. 周易·繫辭傳 (Zhōuyì · Xìcí Zhuàn)

**Author:** Traditionally 孔子 (Confucius) and his disciples; modern scholarship assigns to the Han editorial layer
**Date:** c. 5th–3rd century BCE (late Warring States); received text c. 2nd century BCE
**Source:** Wikisource (zh-hant), 易傳/繫辭上, 第九章

### Passage 8A — The yarrow-stalk divination procedure (大衍之數)

> 大衍之數五十，其用四十有九。分而為二以象兩，掛一以象三，揲之以四以象四時，歸奇於扐以象閏，五歲再閏，故再扐而後掛。乾之策，二百一十有六；坤之策，百四十有四。凡三百有六十，當期之日。二篇之策，萬有一千五百二十，當萬物之數也。是故四營而成易，十有八變而成卦。

**Gloss:** The number of the great expansion is fifty; its use is forty-nine. Divide it into two to symbolize the two [heaven and earth]; suspend one to symbolize the three [heaven, earth, human]; count through by fours to symbolize the four seasons; return the remainder to the [gap between the] fingers to symbolize the intercalary month — in five years there is a second intercalary, therefore a second [return] and then suspend. The stalk-count of Qian is two hundred and sixteen; the stalk-count of Kun is one hundred and forty-four; together three hundred and sixty, corresponding to the days of a year. The stalk-count of the two parts [of the Book] is ten thousand one thousand five hundred and twenty, corresponding to the number of the ten thousand things. Therefore four operations complete one change; eighteen changes complete a hexagram.

**Citation:** 周易, 易傳, 繫辭上傳, 第九章 (Zhouyi, "Tradition of the Appended Statements," Part I, Ch. 9)

### RPG Grounding

This passage grounds **Wang Lun's hexagram casting** (doc 24 §2.5). Wang Lun is a Qi Condensation-faint diviner; his tool is the yarrow-stalk method prescribed in this passage. The procedure — divide, suspend, count-by-fours, return-the-remainder, repeat — is not a minigame abstraction; it is the canonical textual method, and the RPG's divination interface can implement it operationally: 49 stalks, four operations per change, three changes per line, six lines per hexagram, eighteen changes total. The phrase "當期之日" (corresponding to the days of a year) and "當萬物之數" (corresponding to the number of the ten thousand things) is the canonical claim that the yarrow-stalk procedure maps the cosmos — the procedure's output is not random; it is a numerical reading of the qi-topography's current state. For the RPG, this grounds Wang Lun's epistemic limit: he perceives "the current momentum of the qi-topography" (doc 24 §2.5) by performing the procedure, but his perception is "Qi-Induction-faint, not Core Formation-clear" — the yarrow-stalk method is the perceptual prosthesis that lets a Qi Condensation cultivator access information that a Core Formation cultivator would perceive directly. The 18-change procedure is the design anchor for a divination minigame that takes real player time and produces one hexagram; the player can interrupt the procedure (Wang Lun stops partway), which yields partial information at the cost of inaccuracy.

## 9. 山海經 (Shānhǎi Jīng)

**Author:** Anonymous; traditionally attributed to 伯益 (Bó Yì), minister of the legendary Yu; compiled and annotated by 郭璞 (Guō Pú, Jin dynasty)
**Date:** Core material c. 4th century BCE to 1st century CE; Guo Pu's commentary 4th century CE
**Source:** Wikisource (zh-hant), 山海經/西山經

### Passage 9A — The Tiangou (天狗), a white-headed beast that wards off misfortune

> 又西三百里，曰章莪之山，無草木，多瑤碧。是為甚怪。有獸焉，其狀如赤豹，五尾一角，其音如擊石，其名曰狰。… 又西三百二十里，曰陰山。濁浴之水出焉，而南流注于蕃澤，其中多文貝。有獸焉，其狀如貍而白首，名曰天狗，其音如榴榴，可以禦凶。

**Gloss:** [Another] 300 li west is Mount Zhang'e — it has no grass or trees, but much jasper and jadestone; this is considered very strange. There is a beast there; its form is like a red leopard, with five tails and one horn; its cry is like the striking of stones; its name is Zheng. … [Another] 320 li west is Mount Yin. The Zhuoyu river rises there and flows south into the Fan marsh; within it are many patterned cowries. There is a beast there; its form is like a leopard-cat with a white head. Its name is Tiangou [Heavenly Dog]. Its cry is like "liu-liu"; it can be used to ward off inauspicious events.

**Citation:** 山海經, 西山經, 西山經之首 (Shanhaijing, "Classic of the Western Mountains," first sequence)

### Passage 9B — The Qiongqi (窮奇), a man-eating ox-beast

> 又西二百六十里，曰邽山。其上有獸焉，其狀如牛，猬毛，名曰窮奇，音如獆狗，是食人。濛水出焉，南流注于洋水，其中多黃貝；蠃魚，魚身而鳥翼，音如鴛鴦，見則其邑大水。

**Gloss:** [Another] 260 li west is Mount Gui. On it there is a beast; its form is like an ox, with porcupine-bristles. Its name is Qiongqi. Its cry is like a howling dog. It eats humans. The Meng river rises there and flows south into the Yang river; within it are many yellow cowries; [there is] the Luo-fish — a fish-body with bird-wings; its cry is like a mandarin-duck; when it appears, the city suffers great flood.

**Citation:** 山海經, 西山經, 西山經之首 (Shanhaijing, "Classic of the Western Mountains," first sequence)

### RPG Grounding

These two passages ground the **spirit beast ecology** of the Cangwu Mountains (doc 24 §4.3). The Shanhaijing's structure — distance-and-direction, mountain name, river origin, beast name, form-description, cry, omen — is the canonical template for the cultivation layer's bestiary. Two design principles emerge directly from the source. First, **beasts have magical properties** (可以禦凶, "can ward off inauspicious events" for the Tiangou; 是食人, "eats humans" for the Qiongqi): the beast's encounter is not merely combat; it carries an omen-effect (Tiangou presence purges ill-fortune; Qiongqi presence threatens human life; the Luo-fish's appearance foretells flood). This is the textual anchor for the cultivation layer's beast-omen system: encountering a beast is also reading a qi-event, and a Qi Condensation cultivator who can interpret the omen can use it as leading-indicator divination. Second, **beasts are located**: every beast is tied to a specific mountain, a specific river, a specific direction and distance. The Cangwu Mountains are the design proxy for the Western Mountains, and the 31 households of Wang Family Bend know the named beasts of their nearby ridges by oral tradition (the charcoal-burners have seen the Qiongqi's tracks; the herb-gatherers know the Tiangou's territory). This grounds the **spirit beast hunting economy** (doc 24 §3.1): the player hunts beasts not in random encounters but in named, mapped territories, and the beast's omen-property determines whether the hunt is auspicious (Tiangou: beneficial to encounter) or inauspicious (Luo-fish: encounter predicts flood, and the player may choose to drive it away rather than kill it). The form-description pattern — "其狀如X而Y" (its form is like X but with Y) — is the design template for the beast-encounter UI: every beast is shown as a hybrid of familiar and unfamiliar features, never as a fully alien form.

## 10. 六祖壇經 (Liùzǔ Tánjīng)

**Author:** 慧能 (Huìnéng, the Sixth Patriarch), as recorded by his disciple 法海 (Fǎhǎi); later editorial layers
**Date:** c. 8th century CE (Tang dynasty); received Dunhuang recension c. 780 CE, standard recension c. 11th century
**Source:** Wikisource (zh-hant), 六祖壇經/坐禪品第五

### Passage 10A — Sitting meditation: not concentrating the mind, not concentrating on purity

> 師示眾云：此門坐禪，元不著心，亦不著淨，亦不是不動。若言著心，心元是妄，知心如幻，故無所著也。若言著淨，人性本淨，由妄念故，蓋覆真如，但無妄想，性自清淨。起心著淨，卻生淨妄，妄無處所，著者是妄。淨無形相，卻立淨相，言是工夫；作此見者，障自本性，卻被淨縛。… 何名坐禪？此法門中，無障無礙，外於一切善惡境界，心念不起，名為坐；內見自性不動，名為禪。何名禪定？外離相為禪；內不亂為定。

**Gloss:** The Master addressed the assembly: "In this lineage, sitting meditation does not originally attach to the mind, nor attach to purity, nor is it non-movement. If one speaks of attaching to the mind — the mind is originally illusory; knowing the mind as phantom, there is nothing to attach to. If one speaks of attaching to purity — human nature is originally pure; by illusory thought, the true-suchness is covered; simply without illusory thought, the nature is itself pure. To raise the mind to attach to purity produces a purity-illusion — illusion has no locus; the attacher is the illusion. Purity has no form; yet [one] establishes a purity-form, calling it 'work' — those who hold this view obstruct their own original nature and are bound by purity. … What is sitting meditation? In this dharma-gate: without obstruction, without hindrance — externally, in the presence of all good and evil states, the mind's thought does not rise: this is called sitting. Internally, seeing one's own nature not moving: this is called chan. What is chan-ding [meditative concentration]? Externally detaching from forms is chan; internally not being scattered is ding."

**Citation:** 六祖壇經, 坐禪品第五 (Liuzu Tanjing, "Chapter on Sitting Meditation," Ch. 5)

### RPG Grounding

This passage grounds the **heart-mind cultivation system's Chan Buddhist layer** (doc 24 §1.2's 心魔 taxonomy and the heart-mind mechanics introduced in passage 2A above). Huineng's three refutations — not attaching to mind (不著心), not attaching to purity (不著淨), not non-movement (不是不動) — are the canonical warnings against three cultivation errors that map directly to three 心魔 types. The cultivator who "attaches to mind" (tries to concentrate the mind as an object) falls into obsession-type 心魔: the mind is phantom, and attaching to it produces a feedback loop. The cultivator who "attaches to purity" (tries to maintain a pure mind-state) falls into compulsion-type 心魔: the purity-state is reified as an object, and the cultivator is "bound by purity" (被淨縛). The cultivator who equates meditation with physical stillness (不動) falls into the body-stillness trap that the Zhuangzi 心齋 passage also warns against: meditation is not non-movement but a specific internal relation to external states. For the RPG, this is the textual anchor for the heart-mind cultivation minigame: the player does not press inputs to "concentrate" or "purify"; they release inputs and respond to arising states without attachment. The line "外離相為禪；內不亂為定" is the canonical definition of the meditative state — externally, forms are not grasped; internally, the mind is not scattered — and it provides the design vocabulary for the cultivation layer's mental-state display: the player's internal state is shown as a degree of "scattering" (亂) and the external state as a degree of "form-grasping" (著相), and the meditative state is the region where both are low. This passage is also the textual anchor for Old Chen's caution against "premature insight" (doc 24 §1.3): the cultivator who achieves a brief meditative state and reifies it as achievement has fallen into the purity-illusion trap, and the next stage of cultivation is correspondingly harder because the purity-illusion must be unwound before further progress is possible.

## 11. 論語 (Lunyu / Analects)

**Author:** Confucius's disciples (compiled ~5th century BCE)
**Source:** 論語·學而

### Passage 11A — 孝 as the foundation

> 有子曰：「其為人也孝弟，而好犯上者，鮮矣；不好犯上，而好作亂者，未之有也。君子務本，本立而道生。孝弟也者，其為仁之本與！」

**Gloss:** Master You said: "One who is filial and fraternal but loves to oppose superiors is rare; one who does not love to oppose superiors but loves to make trouble has never existed. The gentleman works at the root; once the root is established, the Dao grows. Filial piety and fraternal duty — are they not the root of humaneness?"

**Citation:** 論語 1.2 (學而篇)

### RPG Grounding

This passage grounds the lineage system (doc 04 §5.1, doc 12 §2) as a Confucian institution, not merely a social structure. 孝 (filial piety) is the root from which the Dao grows — meaning cultivation itself, in a Confucian frame, begins with the lineage obligation. The protagonist's grief over Wang Xiaodi (doc 26) is, in this frame, a disruption of 孝弟: the younger brother's death breaks the fraternal duty, and the protagonist's absence at the death breaks the filial duty. The "found a lineage" ending (doc 26 §7) is the restoration of the root: the protagonist returns to the lineage obligation as a cultivator, establishing the root from which a new Dao grows. This passage also grounds the academy (doc 12 §3) as a Confucian institution: the school teaches 孝弟 first, literacy second, cultivation third.

## 12. 大學 (Daxue / The Great Learning)

**Author:** Confucian tradition (attributed to Zengzi, ~5th century BCE)
**Source:** 禮記·大學 (Book of Rites, chapter 42)

### Passage 12A — The investigation of things

> 古之欲明明德於天下者，先治其國；欲治其國者，先齊其家；欲齊其家者，先修其身；欲修其身者，先正其心；欲正其心者，先誠其意；欲誠其意者，先致其知；致知在格物。

**Gloss:** "The ancients who wished to manifest bright virtue throughout the world first governed their states; wishing to govern their states, they first regulated their households; wishing to regulate their households, they first cultivated their persons; wishing to cultivate their persons, they first rectified their heart-minds; wishing to rectify their heart-minds, they first made their intentions sincere; wishing to make their intentions sincere, they first extended their knowledge; extending knowledge lies in investigating things."

**Citation:** 大學 (禮記 chapter 42), the eight steps passage

### RPG Grounding

This passage grounds the heart-mind cultivation system (doc 27 §7) and the comprehension system (doc 27 §3) in the Confucian tradition. The eight-step chain — investigate things → extend knowledge → make intentions sincere → rectify the heart-mind → cultivate the person → regulate the household → govern the state → manifest bright virtue — is the Confucian version of the internal-alchemy chain (煉精化氣 → 煉氣化神, doc 24 §2.3). Where the Daoist chain refines body→qi→spirit, the Confucian chain refines perception→intention→heart-mind→person→household→state. The two chains are complementary: the Daoist chain is 命功 (life-cultivation, doc 27 §6); the Confucian chain is 性功 (nature-cultivation). The dual-cultivation balance requirement (doc 27 §6.3) is grounded here: a cultivator who develops 命 without 性 becomes powerful but unstable; the Confucian chain is the 性 track.

The "investigate things" (格物) step is the Confucian name for comprehension (悟道, doc 27 §3). The protagonist's Residual Error Sense — perceiving their own error-traces — is a form of 格物: investigating the consequences of their own practice. The "rectify the heart-mind" (正心) step is the Confucian name for heart-mind cultivation (doc 27 §7). The protagonist who integrates 心魔 is practicing 正心.
