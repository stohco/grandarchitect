# 28 — The Village In Medias Res

**Status:** Canonical. The village set in a specific moment with current tensions, NPC inner lives, and anchored verbs.
**Date:** 2026-08-03

---

## 0. What this document is

The critics found that Wang Family Bend is "lived-in at the level of setting (materials, calendar, social structure, prices), diorama at the level of dramatic present." The village is described at equilibrium, not in medias res. No current tension, no NPC internal life, no anchored verb.

This document fixes that. It sets the village in a specific week, names three current tensions, gives five NPCs each a secret/grief/ambition, and anchors five verbs to specific moments. This is the difference between a setting and a story.

---

## 1. The moment

**The 14th year of the Jianhe reign, Great Yan Dynasty (建和十四年). Third month. Three days before Qingming (清明).**

The player is 16. The spring has been wet — the plum rain started early this year, two weeks ahead of schedule. The paddies are flooded but the transplanting has not yet begun (that comes at Mangzhong, two months away). The village is in the lull between Lichun (Beginning of Spring) and Qingming — the time of repairs, planning, and the first ancestors' rite of the year.

The air is cool and damp. The river is higher than usual for this time of year. The levee held through the winter, but Wang Shouzheng has been watching it. The plum rain's early arrival means the summer floods may come early too.

Three days from now is Qingming — the tomb-sweeping festival. The village will visit the graveyard, clean the tombs, offer food and paper money. The lineage will hold the spring ancestor rite at the hall. The player's grandfather (Wang Tianlu) has been dead six years; his tablet is in the hall. The player's younger brother (Wang Xiaodi) has been dead two years; his tablet is not in the hall (he was a child, and children's tablets are not installed — they are mourned privately). This is the first Qingming where the player will visit Xiaodi's small, unmarked grave.

## 2. The three current tensions

### 2.1 The salt merchant's license

Master Hu's salt license (鹽引) is up for renewal at the autumn equinox. Last week, a yamen runner from Cangli County Seat came to the village — ostensibly to check the baojia registers, but he spent an hour at Hu's warehouse, and the village saw him leave with a silk-wrapped gift. Hu has been quiet since. Lady Sun (his wife) was overheard arguing with him in the back room by Widow Xu, who was buying salt that morning and pretended not to hear.

The rumor (not yet confirmed, but circulating in the market town): a competing merchant from Cangli County Seat has bid for the Cangli Riverlands salt license. If Hu loses it, the village's salt supply changes hands — and the new merchant may not be as patient with credit-poor households as Hu has been. Wang Shouzheng, as li head, will have to mediate. He has no authority over the license (it's a county matter), but he has relationships — and the competing merchant's bid may be part of the eunuch faction's influence-peddling at the county level.

**Player-facing verb:** buy salt from Master Hu. Overhear the argument. Choose to tell Wang Shouzheng, keep the secret, or ask Hu directly.

### 2.2 The Widow Xu's missing son

Xu Erniu (Widow Xu's only surviving son, 19) has not been seen for three days. He left two mornings ago to cut firewood in the Cangwu foothills and has not returned. Widow Xu came to Wang Shouzheng on the morning of the second day; Shouzheng sent two young men to search the lower slopes. They found his axe and a bundle of cut wood near the Black Creek, but no sign of Erniu. The lineage elder council meets on the 15th (the day after tomorrow) to decide: search the upper slopes (dangerous — beast territory), or declare him a fugitive (which would strip Widow Xu of her last means of support and shame the household).

The truth (which the player may discover): Erniu found something in the foothills — a cave entrance that wasn't there before, sealed by a stone that crumbled when he leaned on it. Inside: the faint glow of qi, the smell of old incense, the remnants of a formation that hasn't activated in centuries. He entered. He hasn't come out. The formation is a sealed grotto-heaven anchor — too small to be a real grotto-heaven, but a cultivator's cache, left by someone centuries ago. Erniu, a mortal with no cultivation, triggered the formation's last defense and is trapped inside, unconscious but alive, his body's qi slowly being drained by the cache's maintenance system.

**Player-facing verb:** join the search party (if the player has begun Qi Induction, they may perceive the qi-residue of the cave entrance; if not, they find only the axe). This is the player's first encounter with a cultivator's legacy — and their first encounter with the fact that the world has hidden layers.

### 2.3 The betrothal that cannot be afforded

Wang Sanniang (the player's aunt, 22, Wang Shouzheng's youngest daughter) is betrothed to Li Zongwen of Li Family Creek — a good match, arranged three years ago, with a bride-price of 40 taels of silver (the Li family's expectation). The Senior Household has saved 28 taels over three years. The remaining 12 taels must come from this autumn's harvest surplus — but the plum rain's early arrival threatens the early rice, and if the harvest is poor, the marriage will be delayed another year. The Li family has hinted (through Wang Lun, who teaches at both villages) that if the marriage is delayed again, they will look elsewhere — Sanniang is 22, which is late for a first marriage, and the Li family has other suitors for their son.

Sanniang herself has not been consulted. She has met Li Zongwen three times (at the spring and autumn markets). She found him dull but kind. She does not want to marry him, but she does not want to shame her father. She has spoken of this to no one — not even her mother, Lady Chen. The player may discover Sanniang's reluctance if they are perceptive (Qi Induction's "sense residue" could perceive the grief-trace of her suppressed tears at the lineage hall altar, where she goes alone at dawn).

**Player-facing verb:** speak with Sanniang at the lineage hall at dawn. Perceive her grief-trace (if in Qi Induction mode). Choose to ask, to comfort, or to report to Shouzheng.

## 3. The five NPCs — secret, grief, ambition

### 3.1 Wang Shouzheng (王守正, 58, lineage head)

- **Secret:** He has been doctoring the genealogy. Three years ago, he altered the birth record of his second son (Wang Zongwen) to make him appear one year younger — too young for the autumn corvée levy. The alteration is small (a single stroke in the register) and unlikely to be caught, but if it is, the penalty is 60 blows of the heavy bamboo and a fine. He did it because Zongwen is the lineage school's assistant teacher, and losing him to corvée for three months would disrupt the school. He tells himself it was for the lineage. He knows it was for his son.
- **Grief:** His father, Wang Tianlu, died six years ago of schistosomiasis. Shouzheng was at the bedside. He held his father's hand and felt it go cold. He has not wept since — not at the funeral, not at the Qingming rites, not when alone at the grave. He is the lineage head; he cannot weep. The unwept grief sits in his chest like a stone.
- **Ambition:** He wants the Wang lineage to produce a cultivator. Not for power — for protection. The Azure Sword Sect's influence is creeping southward; the Cangwu Sect is weak; if a great sect decides the Cangli Riverlands' latent spirit vein is worth claiming, the Wang lineage needs a cultivator who can speak the cultivation world's language. He has been watching his grandchildren for signs of qi perception. He has seen nothing — yet.

### 3.2 Lady Chen (陳氏, 54, the player's mother)

- **Secret:** She has been weaving extra cloth at night — not for the household, but for Widow Xu. She leaves the cloth in a bundle at Xu's gate before dawn, once a month. She has never told anyone. She does it because Xu's husband died of the same disease that killed Lady Chen's father-in-law (schistosomiasis), and she remembers what it was like to watch a man die of water-disease. She does not consider this charity; she considers it a debt.
- **Grief:** Wang Xiaodi. Her youngest. Dead two years of a fever that took him in three days. She was at his bedside for all three days. She held him when the fever broke — and broke him. The player (her eldest) was at Old Chen's hermitage, a day's walk away. She sent word, but the message arrived too late. She has never blamed the player for not being there. She has never said this. The player does not know she doesn't blame them. The player assumes she does.
- **Ambition:** She wants the player to marry well. Not to a cultivator — to a good mortal spouse, in the village, where she can see them. She fears cultivation. She has seen what it does: Old Chen, alone on his hill, his wife dead, his children grown and gone. She does not want that for her child. She has not said this to the player. She will say it, eventually, in a scene the player is not expecting.

### 3.3 Wang Lun (王倫, 41, the lineage school teacher)

- **Secret:** He has been writing a book. Not a cultivation manual — a history. A history of the Cangli Riverlands, from the dynasty's founding to the present, compiled from the lineage genealogies, the market town's archives, and the stories of the oldest villagers. He writes at night, in the lineage hall, by candlelight. The book is 200 pages. It is not good — Wang Lun is a failed exam candidate, not a historian — but it is the only record of the region that is not a government register. He has shown it to no one.
- **Grief:** He is a failed man. He sat the county exam three times and failed three times. His father (deceased) spent the family's savings on tutors and exam fees. The failure is not just intellectual — it is filial. He failed his father. He teaches children their characters in a village school because it is the only use for his education, and every morning he wakes knowing he could have been more. The one time he perceived qi — briefly, in his youth, during a moment of intense concentration over a text — was the closest he came to something beyond the failure. He has never perceived it again.
- **Ambition:** He wants the player to perceive qi and not waste it the way he did. This is not selfless — it is vicarious. The player's perception is the thing Wang Lun almost had. He will push the player toward Old Chen not because it is the player's path, but because it is the path Wang Lun wishes he had taken.

### 3.4 Master Hu (胡老爺, 47, the salt merchant)

- **Secret:** He has been paying a bribe. Not to the yamen runner who visited last week — to the county magistrate's secretary, every autumn for the past six years, to ensure the salt license is renewed without competition. This year, the secretary has been replaced (the eunuch faction's doing), and the new secretary wants double. Hu cannot afford double. The competing merchant's bid is the new secretary's leverage.
- **Grief:** His wife, Lady Sun (née Wang), has not spoken to her brother (Wang Shouzheng) in 20 years. She married Hu — a non-Wang, a merchant, a man with no land — against her brother's wishes. Shouzheng did not forbid it (he had no authority — she was already betrothed to Hu when the lineage council was consulted), but he did not attend the wedding. No Wang attended. Lady Sun has carried this exile for 20 years. She lives 200 paces from her brother's house and has not crossed his threshold in two decades. Hu has tried to mediate; she has refused. The grief is not his, but he lives with it every day.
- **Ambition:** He wants to leave the Cangli Riverlands. Not now — in ten years, when his son (Hu Bao, 18) has taken over the business. He wants to retire to Cangli County Seat, where there are teahouses and opera and other merchants who understand what it is to build something from nothing. The village is his market, not his home. He has never said this to Lady Sun.

### 3.5 Old Chen (陳老, 82, the hermit)

- **Secret:** He knows about the cave. The cultivator's cache that Xu Erniu stumbled into — Old Chen sealed it, 40 years ago, when he was still a roaming cultivator. He was the one who collapsed the entrance and placed the crumbling stone. He did not take the cache's contents because the cache's formation was too degraded to safely deactivate, and he was not skilled enough. He left it sealed and hoped no one would find it. Someone has.
- **Grief:** His failed Foundation Establishment. Decades ago, he was ready — or he thought he was. The integration destabilized. He barely survived the reversion. The meridian damage left him at the Qi Condensation peak, unable to attempt again. He has lived with the knowledge that he was not good enough — not talented enough, not prepared enough, not strong enough. Every day for decades, he has woken in his hermitage on the hill and felt the damage in his meridians, the routes that will never carry qi again. He has made peace with it, mostly. But when the player succeeds where he failed (Scene 5), the peace will break, and what comes through the break will be pride and grief in equal measure.
- **Ambition:** He wants the player to succeed. Not for the player's sake (though he has grown fond of them) — for the path's sake. The path of cultivation is real, and it works, and he failed it, and he needs to know it was his failure and not the path's. If the player succeeds, the path is vindicated. If the player fails too, Old Chen will spend the rest of his life wondering whether the path itself is broken.

## 4. Five anchored verbs

The 21 verbs in doc 04 §8 are capabilities. These five are moments — specific instances of those verbs, set in this specific week, with specific NPCs present and specific consequences.

### 4.1 Carry water at dawn — the morning of the 13th day

The player wakes at dawn (卯時). Lady Chen is already at the stove. She says: "Go fetch water. Your father left early for the levee." The player takes the bucket, walks to the communal well. At the well, Widow Xu is already there — she has been coming before dawn since Erniu disappeared, because she cannot sleep and the walk to the well is something to do. She is drawing water mechanically, her eyes red. She sees the player and says: "Have you heard anything? Shouzheng says the council meets tomorrow." The player draws water. Widow Xu watches. The well's windlass creaks. The morning is cool and damp. The plum rain's early mist sits in the paddies. The player carries the water back. The bucket is heavy. The path is muddy. If the player spills, Widow Xu does not comment — she has seen worse.

This is the first verb. It is not a tutorial. It is a morning in a village where a woman's son is missing.

### 4.2 Buy salt — the afternoon of the 13th day

The player goes to Master Hu's warehouse at the dock to buy salt (the household is low). Hu is behind the counter, reading a letter. He folds it quickly when the player enters. He weighs the salt in silence. The player pays. As they turn to leave, Lady Sun's voice comes from the back room: "You cannot keep pretending it will be fine!" Hu's face tightens. He says, loudly enough for the player to hear: "The salt is weighed. The customer is leaving." The player leaves. Behind them, the argument resumes, muffled.

The player now knows something is wrong with the salt license. They do not know what. They can: ask Hu directly (he will deflect), tell Wang Shouzheng (he will investigate), or keep the secret (it will fester).

### 4.3 Visit Xiaodi's grave — Qingming, the 15th day

Qingming. The village walks to the graveyard on the hill. The lineage holds the spring ancestor rite at the hall first (the player attends; Wang Shouzheng presides; the tablets are offered rice wine and incense). Then the families walk to the graveyard to sweep the tombs.

The player's grandfather (Wang Tianlu) has a tombstone. The player's brother (Wang Xiaodi) does not — he was a child, and children are buried without markers. His grave is a small mound next to Tianlu's, unmarked, overgrown. Lady Chen kneels at the mound and pulls the weeds with her hands. She does not speak. She does not weep. She pulls weeds for a long time.

The player can: help pull weeds (Lady Chen does not acknowledge it, but her hand touches the player's hand briefly), stand and watch (Lady Chen does not look up), or walk away (Lady Chen continues pulling weeds until the others have left, then stays alone for another hour).

This is the grief that will surface in the failed breakthrough (doc 06 Scene 4). This is the moment the player first feels it — not as a cutscene, but as a verb: pull weeds at your brother's unmarked grave while your mother kneels beside you in silence.

### 4.4 Attend the elder council — the 15th day, afternoon

The lineage elder council meets in the lineage hall's side room. Wang Shouzheng presides. The five elders sit on low stools. The matter: Xu Erniu's disappearance. Wang Lun reports what the search party found (the axe, the bundle of wood, no sign of struggle). The council debates: search the upper slopes or declare fugitive?

- Wang Shouzheng argues for search — Erniu is a lineage member's son (Widow Xu married into a non-Wang, but her late husband was a Wang cousin; the lineage has obligation).
- The senior elder after Shouzheng (Wang Tianming, 73, frail but lucid) argues for waiting — the upper slopes are beast territory in spring; sending more men risks more lives.
- A third elder argues for declaring fugitive — Erniu may have run off to the county seat to find work; it would not be the first time a young man left.

The player can attend (Shouzheng allows it — the player is 16, old enough to observe lineage business). The player can: speak for search, speak for waiting, speak for declaring fugitive, or remain silent. Each choice has consequences: speaking for search impresses Shouzheng (the player shows courage) but may send men into danger; speaking for waiting impresses Tianming (the player shows caution) but leaves Erniu trapped longer; speaking for fugitive shames Widow Xu (the player betrays her hope); remaining silent is the safe choice but Shouzheng is mildly disappointed.

### 4.5 Sit with Wang Lun at the lineage hall — the evening of the 14th day

The evening before Qingming. The player's father is at the levee (the river is high). Lady Chen is weaving. The player goes to the lineage hall — Wang Lun is there, preparing the next day's rite. He is copying the ancestor tablets' names onto paper for the reading. The player sits with him. They work in silence for a time.

Wang Lun says, without looking up: "You've been staring at the incense again." (This is the opening of Golden Scene 1 — but here, the evening before, it is not yet the scene. It is the precursor: the teacher noticing, the teacher who almost perceived qi himself, the teacher who will, tomorrow, cast the hexagram that sends the player to Old Chen.)

The player can: deny it, acknowledge it, or say nothing. If they acknowledge, Wang Lun pauses his copying and looks at the player for a long time. Then he says: "Tomorrow. After the rite. Come to the school room." The player leaves. The evening is cold. The incense smoke from the hall rises into the dark.

## 5. What this document enables

The village is no longer a diorama. It is a specific week in a specific year with three specific tensions, five people with inner lives, and five verbs that are moments, not capabilities. The player who begins the game on the morning of the 13th day of the third month is stepping into a world that is already in motion — where a woman's son is missing, a merchant's license is threatened, a marriage cannot be afforded, and a teacher has noticed something in a student's eyes.

The five golden scenes (doc 06) now have a foundation: Scene 1 (Wang Lun's hexagram) is anchored to the evening of the 14th and the morning of the 15th. The grief that surfaces in Scene 4 (the failed breakthrough) is anchored to Qingming and the unmarked grave. The player's first encounter with a cultivator's legacy (the cave in the foothills) is woven into the village's present tension (Erniu's disappearance).

The village is alive. The universe can come alive.
