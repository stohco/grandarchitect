# 47 — Cosmic Resources and Economy

**Status:** Candidate canon. Specifies the cosmic-scale economy — what Spirit Severance, Void Amalgamation, Tribulation Crossing, and Mahayana cultivators fight over, trade in, and conserve. Extends doc 18 (the mortal economy) upward through the realm ladder; compliant with 15_PRECELESTIAL_AND_TRIBULATION §1-5 (Precelestial qi, courts, ancestral courts, Spirit Wilds, Law Reaches); 37_COSMIC_HISTORY §5-7 (the Sundering, fallen civilizations, sealed evils, the Thinning); 38_THE_COURTS_OF_HEAVEN §2 (the six departments, including Roads for stratum-crossing trade); 39_THE_MAHAYANA_PANTHEON §2, §6 (the nine Mahayana, their holdings, the deadlock over the Thinning); 46_ANCESTRAL_COURTS_AND_LINEAGES §5 (the ancestral court qi-economy, veneration as currency); 24_RECONCILIATION_AND_DECISIONS §3.1 (spirit-stone value: 10-50 taels); 18_ECONOMY_SYSTEM (the mortal economy's substrate). No pinyin tone marks appear in this document. Hanzi appear in parentheses on first mention.

## 0. Why this document exists

Doc 18 specified the mortal economy: copper, silver, spirit stones, grain; the household debt cycle; the sect's revenue and expenses; the genre's central economic fact (one month of Qi Condensation work ≈ 18-55 years of mortal labor, per doc 24 §3.1). The specification stopped at the Qi Condensation to Core Formation tier. The cosmic economy — what a Spirit Severance cultivator trades in, what a Mahayana cultivator conserves, what the pantheon fights over when the pantheon is finite and the cosmos is dying — was unspecified. Doc 39 §6 named the Thinning as the pantheon's deadlock (the cosmos's qi is non-regenerating; the pantheon cannot agree on whether to author new laws to slow the Thinning or to stop authoring and let the cosmos end gracefully) but did not specify the economic substrate the Thinning operates on. Doc 37 named the fallen civilizations (the Pillar Dynasty, the Crimson Vow Alliance, the Silent Hexagram School) and the sealed evils but did not specify how the ruins and the seals function as cosmic resources. Doc 15 §3-5 named the ancestral courts, the Spirit Wilds, and the Law Reaches but did not specify their economies. Doc 46 §5 specified the ancestral court's qi-economy (veneration as a finite, lineage-bound currency) but did not specify the broader cosmic economy in which veneration is one currency among several.

This document fills those deferrals. It specifies: the cosmic-scale resources that exist above the mortal tier (ancient inheritances, cosmic-tier herbs and beasts, the ancestral court persistence-budget, the time-debt itself, law-fragments, the Thinning as scarcity); the cosmic economy (Precelestial-Acquired trade, the cosmic currencies, the grotto-heaven economy, the ancestral court economy); what Mahayana cultivators fight over (Law Reaches, the Thinning, predecessors' laws); and the protagonist's economic arc from mortal poverty to cosmic-scale responsibility. Every resource is named; every price (or equivalent) is specified; every controller is identified.

The finiteness is the point. A cosmic economy without scarcity is a cosmic economy without tragedy; a cosmic economy with specified scarcity is the engine of the endgame's stakes.

## 1. Cosmic-scale resources

The mortal economy's resources (grain, cash, silver, spirit stones, mortal-tier herbs and beasts) are the substrate of the lower realm ladder. Above Core Formation, the mortal economy's resources are insufficient — a Nascent Soul cultivator can buy mortal resources in such quantity that the purchase is meaningless, and the cultivator's actual constraints (qi-authority, time, law-residue, veneration) are not priced in mortal currency. This section specifies the cosmic-scale resources that exist above the mortal tier.

### 1.1 Ancient inheritances

Ancient inheritances are the ruins and residues of past cultivators, lineages, and civilizations. They are the cosmic economy's primary treasure-class resource — the equivalent, at cosmic scale, of mortal inheritance manuals (doc 18 §2.3). The categories:

**Dispersed Mahayana anchor-residues.** When a Mahayana cultivator disperses (final death, 寂滅), the anchor's law-inscription does not fully unwrite; a residue persists in the substrate, fading over millennia. The residue, for a Tribulation Crossing cultivator with the law-legacy perception (doc 30 Station 10), is readable — the residue contains fragments of the Mahayana's authored law, the Mahayana's personal qi-signature, and (rarely) the Mahayana's lucid final perception. The residue is the only way, below Mahayana, to study a Mahayana's law-inscription directly. The residues are tracked by the Records Department (the Register of Dispersed Mahayana, 散尊錄) and are, by the founding compact's jurisdiction 6, the property of the cosmos (not of the lineage — the lineage-jurisdiction clause, jurisdiction 5, does not extend to dispersed anchors). The residues are, in practice, accessed by the pantheon's permission; the permission is rare and costly.

**Law-fragments of failed Reach-authorship.** When a Reach-authorship fails (per doc 24 §1.1, the Reach can collapse; per doc 39 §2.9, Lie Fa's Reach collapsed three thousand years ago), the law-inscription that almost held persists in the Reach's ruins. The fragment is, in principle, studyable — the law-paradox perception (doc 30 Station 10) can read the fragment's structure and perceive where the paradox destabilized it. The fragment is, in practice, dangerous — to read it is to begin to perceive the recursion that broke the author (per doc 39 §2.9, the recursion is contagious). Lie Fa's fragment (the only known surviving Reach-collapse fragment) is the cosmos's most dangerous text; access is restricted to Mahayana, and most of the pantheon has read it. The fragment's economic value is, paradoxically, low (it cannot be reused; it cannot be repaired; it can only be studied as a caution) but its strategic value is high (the Mahayana who studies it most deeply is the Mahayana most likely to perceive, in advance, the next Reach-authorship's paradox-risk).

**The ruins of fallen civilizations.** The three named fallen civilizations (the Pillar Dynasty, the Crimson Vow Alliance, the Silent Hexagram School, per doc 37 §6) each left ruins. The ruins contain: bark-strip libraries (the Pillar Cities, doc 37 §6.1), partial Code-survivals (the Crimson Vow Code, 60% held by the Jade Void Holy Land), sealed grotto-heaven libraries (the Silent Hexagram Grotto-Heaven, presumed intact). The ruins' economic value is in the texts (cultivation manuals, law-commentaries, lineage histories) and the residual qi-tools (the Pillar Dynasty's fused-stone architectural elements retain wood-phase qi that can be harvested; the Crimson Vow's fire-phase formation-fragments retain small qi-charges). Access to the ruins is restricted by the ruins' dangers (the Pillar Cities are in contested wilds; the Crimson Vow Grotto-Heaven's collapsed anchor is a qi-residue site; the Silent Hexagram Grotto-Heaven is sealed by Jing Wuyin's dispersal-method, presumed lost). The Jade Void Holy Land's library, which holds the Pillar Dynasty Annals (7 of 12 volumes) and 60% of the Crimson Vow Code, is the cosmos's most accessible ancient-inheritance archive; access is granted to Nascent Soul cultivators by petition, and the petition's approval rate is approximately 5%.

**The inheritance-economy's structural asymmetry.** The ancient-inheritance economy is structurally asymmetric: the inheritances are produced by past civilizations' collapses (the Pillar Dynasty's vein-exhaustion, the Crimson Vow Alliance's guarantor-dispersal, the Silent Hexagram School's ancestor-corruption), but the inheritances are accessed by present civilizations' cultivators (the Jade Void Holy Land's library, the Azure Sword Sect's Scripture Hall, the protagonist's first cosmic-tier encounters). The asymmetry is the cosmic economy's central inter-generational feature: the past's catastrophes are the present's resources, and the present's catastrophes (the Thinning; the pantheon's deadlock; the courts' corruption) will be the future's inheritances. The protagonist's eventual dispersal (in the far future, in the Transcend-path variant) will, if the protagonist's law is maintained after the protagonist's death, leave a law-fragment for some future cultivator to study — the protagonist's contribution to the inheritance-economy's asymmetric flow. The inheritance-economy is, in this respect, the cosmic economy's most poignant sector: every inheritance is a grave, and every grave is a treasure.

### 1.2 Cosmic-tier spirit herbs

Mortal-tier spirit herbs (100-year ginseng, 500-year lingzhi, etc., per doc 18 §3.2) are the substrate of mortal alchemy. Cosmic-tier spirit herbs are the herbs that grow only in the peak grotto-heavens or in the Spirit Wilds, and that have properties no mortal-tier herb can replicate.

**10,000-year herbs from peak grotto-heavens.** A peak grotto-heaven (the Jade Void Holy Land's grotto, the Garden Grotto, the Crimson Vow City's former grotto) sustains herbs at a time-rate that allows 10,000-year maturation in 1,000 to 3,000 Acquired-stratum years (the Jade Void's grotto has a 1:10 time-rate; the Garden Grotto has 1:5; the Crimson Vow City had 1:3 before its collapse). A 10,000-year herb (the Garden Grotto's 10,000-year wood-phase spirit peach; the Jade Void's 10,000-year metal-phase spirit lotus) is the alchemical equivalent of a Mahayana-tier treasure — it can sustain a Tribulation Crossing cultivator's breakthrough attempt, repair a Spirit Severance cultivator's anchor-damage, or (rarely) extend a Mahayana cultivator's natural span by a century. The herbs are not sold (no mortal currency can price them); they are gifted, traded for law-fragments or veneration-pulses, or hoarded. The Jade Void Holy Land's 10,000-year spirit lotus is the holy land's most closely guarded treasure; the patriarch has authorized its use twice in 2,100 years.

**Precelestial herbs from the Spirit Wilds.** The Spirit Wilds' plants are alien (per doc 15 §4.3) — they have no phase-affinity, and their effects on an Acquired cultivator's body are unpredictable. A wild herb, gathered by a Spirit Severance cultivator with the skill to identify and harvest it, can produce effects no Acquired herb can replicate: a forced transformation (a Qi Condensation cultivator's qi-state forcibly re-tuned to a different phase-affinity), an accelerated cultivation (a Foundation Establishment cultivator's meridian-integration compressed from years to hours), or a forced dispersion (the wild herb's undifferentiated qi overwhelms the cultivator's anchor and disperses it). The wild herbs are traded in the underworld market (doc 26 §9.2) at prices denominated in law-fragments, veneration-pulses, or (rarely) a Spirit Severance cultivator's qi-authority. The protagonist's companion Liang Zhu knows the underworld market's herb-trade; the trade is one of Liang Zhu's established expertise.

### 1.3 Cosmic-tier spirit beasts

Mortal-tier spirit beasts (100-year beasts, peak beasts with cores worth 10-100 spirit stones, per doc 18 §3.2) are the substrate of mortal hunting. Cosmic-tier beasts are the beings that exist only in the Spirit Wilds or in the deep Precelestial, and that do not follow the five-phase model.

**Spirit Wilds native beasts.** The wilds' beasts (per doc 15 §4.3) have metabolisms that operate on undifferentiated qi directly. A wild beast does not have a phase-affinity; its attacks do not have phase-signatures; its core (if it has one — some wild beasts do not have anchors and therefore do not have cores in the Acquired sense) is a knot of undifferentiated qi that can be refined, with great difficulty, into a Precelestial-tier pill. The wild beasts' body parts (hide, bone, organs) have properties no Acquired beast's parts have — a wild beast's hide, properly tanned, can serve as a Spirit Severance cultivator's armor; a wild beast's bone, properly carved, can serve as a Tribulation Crossing cultivator's formation-substrate. Wild beasts are hunted by Spirit Severance cultivators (the minimum realm at which the hunt is not suicide); the hunters are organized in small lineages (the Wild Hunters, three small lineages in the eastern continent's wild-edge) that trade the parts through the underworld market.

**Substrate-predators.** The Sky-Devouring Beast (per doc 37 §7.2) is the named instance of a substrate-predator — a being whose nature is to consume law-inscriptions. The Beast is sealed; if the seal breaks, the Beast would consume six to ten grotto-heavens before a coalition could re-seal it. Substrate-predators are not hunted (the hunt is, by the pantheon's collective assessment, suicide for any coalition below three Mahayana); they are sealed, and the sealing is the cosmic economy's most expensive verb. The sealing's cost is denominated in Mahayana law-authority (one Mahayana dispersal per sealing, by historical average) and in Tribulation Crossing cultivators' persistence-budgets (the two surviving lineages' Tribulation Crossing cultivators are aging; the seal's maintenance is consuming their budgets).

**Beings that don't follow the five-phase model.** Some wild beings — the Feathered Court's feathered serpents, the Stone-Singing Court's stone-singers, the Serpentine Court's serpentine yao (per doc 38 §7.3) — are foreign powers, not beasts. They are not hunted; they are negotiated with. The Foreign Affairs department's standing commission to the three yao courts (the longest-running foreign-relations commission in the courts' history) trades Acquired-stratum goods (spirit stones, herbs, pills — the yao courts' cultivators can use Acquired resources) for Wild-stratum resources (wild herbs, wild beast parts, law-residue access in the wilds' interior). The trade is the courts' most reliable Precelestial-Acquired-Wild exchange; the trade's annual volume is, by the Foreign Affairs department's annual report, approximately equivalent to a small holy land's annual revenue.

**The yao-courts' trade-goods.** The three yao courts' specific trade-goods are: the Feathered Court's feathers (each feather is a qi-conduit that can be refined into a wind-phase treasure the Acquired-stratum's phase-model does not produce); the Stone-Singing Court's resonant stones (each stone is a law-residue substrate that can host a minor law-inscription the way a grotto-heaven's anchor hosts the grotto's law); the Serpentine Court's shed skins (each skin is a qi-resonant fabric that can serve as a Spirit Severance cultivator's robe, with properties no Acquired-stratum textile can replicate). The trade-goods' exchange rate is denominated in spirit stones (for the feathers and stones, which the yao courts consider tradeable) and in law-fragments (for the shed skins, which the Serpentine Court considers sacred and trades only for high-tier cosmic currency). The Foreign Affairs department's standing commission negotiates the rates annually; the rates have been stable for the last five hundred years (the yao courts' and the courts' mutual suspicion is, by long practice, resolved into a stable trading relationship).

### 1.4 The ancestral court persistence-budget as a resource

Per doc 46 §5, the ancestral court's qi-reservoir is sustained by veneration, and each persistent ancestor has a persistence-budget (the qi-authority the anchor holds, finite, non-regenerating). The persistence-budget is a cosmic resource — it is the currency in which the ancestor's interventions are denominated. A lineage with a well-fed court and a senior ancestor with a full persistence-budget (the Yan Court, the Builder's Court) has a strategic resource no Acquired-stratum wealth can buy; a lineage with a starving court and a fading ancestor (the Mourner's Court, the Pillar Court) has a resource that is, in cosmic terms, a wasting asset.

The persistence-budget is traded, in restricted form, between allied courts. The Garden Court's veneration-pulse donation to the Mourner's Court (per doc 46 §5, six hundred years ago) is the named instance: the Garden Court's senior ancestor (Muyuan) invested a portion of her persistence-budget in a veneration-pulse that traveled along the Garden Lineage's qi-connection to the Garden Court, was bundled by the court's law-inscriptions into a transferable packet, and was conveyed (by a Tribulation Crossing cultivator's personal transit, over three Precelestial days = three Acquired years) to the Mourner's Court. The donation slowed the Mourner's Court's decline but did not reverse it; the donation's cost to Muyuan was approximately one percent of her remaining budget (a noticeable but not existential cost). The transfer is the cosmic economy's analogue of a mortal wealthy household's charitable donation to a poorer village's lineage hall — the same gesture, at a scale where the gesture's qi-cost is felt by the donor.

### 1.5 The time-debt itself as a resource

The Precelestial-Acquired time-debt (1 Precelestial day = 1 Acquired year, per doc 15 §7.1) is the cosmos's central tragic engine. It is also, in a restricted sense, a cosmic resource — a trade-opportunity between strata.

**The trade.** A Precelestial being (a heavenly immortal, a Mahayana cultivator, a persistent ancestor) can, in principle, perform Acquired-stratum work for an Acquired-stratum patron, and the work's Precelestial-time cost is 1/365 of the work's Acquired-time value. A Mahayana cultivator who spends a Precelestial day (one Acquired year) inscribing a law-amendment for an Acquired-stratum holy land's benefit has, in effect, traded one Precelestial day for one Acquired year of work the holy land would have had to perform itself. The trade is, in pure efficiency terms, a 365x multiplier — the Precelestial being's labor is worth 365 Acquired-stratum labor-units.

**Why the trade is restricted.** The trade is restricted by three constraints. First, the time-debt is symmetric: the Precelestial being's Precelestial day is one Acquired year of mortal-world time, and the mortal world the Precelestial being was trying to protect ages past recognition (per doc 15 §7.2). The trade is therefore not free — the Precelestial being pays, in mortal-world time, for the multiplier. Second, the Precelestial being's intervention in the Acquired stratum is bound by Acquired-time during the intervention (per doc 15 §7.4); the Precelestial being, while in the Acquired stratum, is not 365x faster — they are 1x, on Acquired time. The multiplier applies only to the planning and the law-authorship, not to the in-stratum action. Third, the courts' Roads department regulates stratum-crossing travel (per doc 38 §2.4); a Precelestial being's Acquired-stratum visit requires a permit, and the permit's issuance is governed by the courts' political blocs. The Lineage Bloc supports lineage-ancestral interventions; the Conservative Bloc restricts them; the Reform Bloc wants to formalize the permit-system's criteria.

**The trade's actual volume.** The trade's actual volume is small. The pantheon's nine Mahayana collectively visit the Acquired stratum, on average, perhaps once per Precelestial year (= 365 Acquired years); the visits are typically brief (a few Acquired days) and focused (a specific death to attend, a specific law to inspect, a specific lineage to consult). The persistent ancestors' interventions are rarer (per doc 46 §5, the persistence-budget's cost). The trade is therefore not a market in the conventional sense; it is a restricted, high-stakes, low-volume exchange between specific beings for specific purposes. The protagonist's meeting with Bei Huai in a mortal village (per doc 39 §5.1) is an instance of the trade: Bei Huai, visiting the Acquired stratum to attend a mortal's death, spends Acquired-time (a few hours) on a brief consultation with the protagonist. The consultation's value to the protagonist is enormous (the protagonist's path to the Transcend ending is, in part, the consultation's consequence); the consultation's cost to Bei Huai is the time not spent attending other deaths during the visit.

### 1.6 Law-fragments

Law-fragments are the residue of failed law-authorship (per §1.1 above) and the residue of contested law-authorship (when a Mahayana's law is amended, the original law's residue persists in the substrate, fading over millennia). Law-fragments are the cosmic economy's highest-tier currency — they are the only resource that can be used, at Mahayana, to study another Mahayana's law-authorship directly.

**What law-fragments are good for.** A law-fragment can be studied (by a Mahayana cultivator with the law-legacy perception, doc 30 Station 10) for: the original author's law-authorship technique (the specific qi-inscription method, useful as a reference for the student's own authorship), the original law's full text (useful when the law has been amended and the original text is no longer in force), and the original author's lucid final perception (rare; only fragments from authors who dispersed by natural span retain lucid perception). Law-fragments cannot be reused (the law-authorship verb, 創法, requires the author's living anchor; a fragment is a residue, not a living inscription); they cannot be repaired (the substrate's law-paradox perception does not allow partial inscriptions to be completed); they can only be studied.

**Law-fragment degradation.** Law-fragments are not permanent. A fragment fades over millennia, the way a mortal inscription fades on weathered stone. The fade-rate depends on the fragment's substrate-depth (deeper fragments, inscribed in the Precelestial's deeper substrate, fade slower; shallower fragments fade faster) and the fragment's author's realm at dispersal (Mahayana-authored fragments fade slowest; Tribulation Crossing-authored fragments fade faster; Spirit Severance-authored fragments fade fastest). The Records Department's Register of Law-Fragments tracks each known fragment's fade-rate; the register is, in cosmic-economic terms, the cosmic economy's inventory of a depreciating asset. A law-fragment studied in the present decade yields more insight than the same fragment studied in a century; the depreciation is the law-fragment's time-cost, and the time-cost is, in cosmic-economic terms, the cosmic economy's most elegant scarcity mechanism — even the highest-tier currency decays.

**Who controls the law-fragments.** The courts' Records Department tracks the law-fragments (the Register of Law-Fragments, 法殘錄) but does not control access (the fragments are in the substrate, not in the courts' archive). The Mahayana who can perceive a fragment (typically the Mahayana whose law the fragment is from, or the Mahayana's successor) has de facto control. The protagonist's amendment to Gu Wu's bardo-window law (Transcend path) will, when Gu Wu disperses, leave a law-fragment in the substrate — the protagonist's amendment's residue plus Gu Wu's original inscription's residue, fused. The protagonist, as Gu Wu's successor, will perceive this fragment more clearly than any other Mahayana; the fragment will be, for the protagonist, a personal inheritance (the closest thing to Gu Wu's continued presence the cosmos allows).

### 1.7 The Thinning itself as a scarcity driver

The Thinning (per doc 39 §6, doc 37 §1.2) is the Acquired Stratum's slow qi-density decline. The Thinning is the cosmic economy's master scarcity — every other resource is, in the long run, denominated in the qi-authority the Thinning is consuming.

**What the Thinning means for resource availability.** Spirit veins produce less qi per year than they did a millennium ago (the decline is approximately 0.1% per century, per the Records Department's measurements). Spirit herbs take longer to mature (a 100-year ginseng now takes 110 years). Spirit beasts' cores are smaller (a peak beast's core, a millennium ago, was approximately 10% larger than today's). The Thinning is, for mortal and Core Formation cultivators, imperceptible (the decline is too slow to perceive in a cultivator's lifetime); for Nascent Soul cultivators (who live centuries), the decline is faintly perceptible; for Spirit Severance and above, the decline is unmistakable.

**What the Thinning means for cosmic-tier resources.** The cosmic-tier resources are more directly affected. A 10,000-year herb's maturation, in a peak grotto-heaven, now takes 11,000 years (the grotto-heaven's qi-feed is declining with the spirit vein's). The wilds' beasts' cores are smaller (the wilds' undifferentiated qi is, contrary to the Acquired-stratum trend, *not* declining — the wilds' substrate is the Precelestial's own interior, which is sustained by the Origin's generative function, not by the Acquired stratum's qi-density; but the wilds' beasts' metabolisms are slower in the Thinning's edge zones, where the wilds border the Acquired stratum). The ancestral courts' veneration-pulses are weaker (the lineage's living cultivators' qi-authority is declining with the Acquired stratum's qi-density, and the veneration-pulses are proportional to the living cultivators' qi-authority). The First Court's qi-reservoir is declining (Gu Wu's law-maintenance draws qi from the law-substrate, which is thinning); the Mourner's Court's qi-reservoir is declining faster (no Mahayana resident, no law-maintenance, only the thinning veneration).

**The Thinning's timeline.** Per doc 39 §6, the Thinning's trajectory (if unbroken) is: in 3,000-5,000 years, the Acquired stratum's qi-density falls below the Foundation Establishment threshold; in 8,000-10,000 years, the qi-density falls below the Tribulation Crossing sustenance threshold; in 12,000-15,000 years, the substrate itself fails and the pantheon's laws begin to cascade. The cosmic economy's resources are, in this timeline, a wasting asset — every cosmic-tier resource is declining, slowly, and the decline is the background condition of every cosmic-scale transaction.

**The Thinning's asymmetry across strata.** The Thinning's effects are not uniform across the three strata. The Mortal Stratum (the diffuse ambient, per doc 24 §2.6) is most affected (the Mortal Stratum's qi-density was already the lowest; the decline pushes more mortal regions below the cultivation threshold; mortal cultivation becomes harder in the Mortal Stratum's edge regions, and the mortal population's cultivation-rate declines). The Acquired Stratum is moderately affected (the spirit veins' decline is measurable but, for now, sustainable; the grotto-heavens' production declines but does not cease). The Precelestial is, in a strict sense, *not* affected by the Acquired Stratum's Thinning — the Precelestial's substrate is sustained by the Origin's generative function, which is not subject to the Acquired Stratum's decline. However, the Precelestial's institutions (the courts, the ancestral courts, the pantheon's maintained laws) are affected, because those institutions depend on the Acquired Stratum's qi-authority (the courts' Roads department's commission, the ancestral courts' veneration, the pantheon's law-maintenance's qi-feed). The Precelestial's institutions are, in cosmic-economic terms, subsidized by the Acquired Stratum; the subsidy's decline is the Thinning's Precelestial manifestation. The asymmetry is the cosmic economy's central irony: the stratum that is, in itself, least affected by the Thinning (the Precelestial) is the stratum whose institutions are most vulnerable to the Thinning's Acquired-stratum effects, because those institutions are powered by the Acquired stratum's qi-authority.

**The cosmic economy's historicity.** The cosmic economy is not static. Per doc 37 §2, the cosmos has passed through six epochs across 48,000 years, and each epoch's economy was different. In the Generative Epoch (Year 0 to Year ~20,000), there was no economy — no cultivators, no resources, no transactions; the Origin's generative function condensed the Precelestial substrate without exchange. In the Primordial Substrate Epoch (Year ~20,000 to Year ~38,000), the first cultivators developed the realm ladder from Qi Induction to Void Amalgamation, and the cosmic economy's first transactions (in qi-authority and time) emerged. In the First Law Epoch (Year ~38,000 to Year ~40,000), Gu Wu invented law-authorship, and the cosmic economy's highest-tier currency (law-fragments) emerged. In the Sundering Epoch (Year ~40,000 to Year ~40,500), the cosmic economy crashed (the bardo-overwhelm killed billions of anchors; the substrate-law destabilized; the Law Reaches were created) and reorganized (the Courts of Heaven were founded; the licensed-carrier system's precursor was established). In the Long Peace Epoch (Year ~40,500 to Year 47,753), the cosmic economy reached its peak complexity (the grotto-heaven economy's expansion; the ancestral court economy's maturation; the pantheon's fragment-trade's elaboration) and began its decline (the Thinning's first perceptibility; the Silent Hexagram Court's corruption and sealing; the Crimson Vow Alliance's collapse). In the Current Epoch (Year 47,753 to present, the Epoch of the Fading Law), the cosmic economy is in decline — the Thinning is unmistakable, the grotto-heavens are failing, the pantheon is deadlocked, and the protagonist's lifetime is the decline's acceleration point. The cosmic economy the protagonist encounters is the cosmic economy at its most complex and most fragile — the economy's peak and the economy's decline are simultaneous, and the simultaneity is the protagonist's endgame's central condition.

## 2. The cosmic economy

The mortal economy (doc 18) operates in the Acquired stratum, with copper, silver, spirit stones, and grain. The cosmic economy operates across strata, with the cosmic-scale resources specified in §1. This section specifies the cosmic economy's flows.

### 2.1 Precelestial-Acquired trade

**Does trade happen?** Yes, but restricted. The courts' Roads department (per doc 38 §2.4) manages stratum-crossing travel; every stratum-crossing transit requires a Roads department permit. The permits are issued for: official courts business (Ministers' travel, Foreign Affairs yao-court negotiations, Defense border-garrison rotations), lineage-rite travel (the ancestral court retrieval rite's transit, the lineage's senior cultivators' consultations), and (rarely) commercial transit.

**Commercial transit.** Commercial stratum-crossing transit is restricted to a small number of licensed carriers (twelve, by the Roads department's current roster). The carriers are mostly lineage-affiliated (the Yan clan operates two; the Builder's Lineage operates three; the Garden Lineage operates one; the remaining six are independent, licensed by the Roads department under the Rogue Outreach Bloc's "incorporation rather than marginalization" program, per doc 38 §3.4). The carriers transport: cosmic-tier herbs from the peak grotto-heavens to the Acquired-stratum holy lands (the herbs are grown in the grotto-heavens, which are Precelestial-affixed; the herbs' transit to the Acquired stratum is the carriers' most common run); law-fragments from the substrate to the Mahayana councilors' Reach-libraries (rare; only the licensed carriers' three most senior carriers are authorized); veneration-pulses from the Acquired-stratum lineages to their Precelestial ancestral courts (the pulses are bundled by the lineage's senior cultivator at a Roads-department stratum-crossing gate and conveyed by the carrier to the court's threshold).

**What flows down (Precelestial to Acquired).** Cosmic-tier herbs (the peak grotto-heavens' production); law-inscribed treasures (the Builder's Court's architectural qi-inscriptions, commissioned by Acquired-stratum holy lands); ancestor-interventions (rare; the persistence-budget's cost); Mahayana counsel (rarer still; the time-debt's cost). The downward flow's volume is, by the Roads department's annual report, approximately equivalent to a moderate holy land's annual revenue — a small fraction of the Acquired stratum's total economic activity, but a disproportionate fraction of the Acquired stratum's cosmic-tier resource supply.

**What flows up (Acquired to Precelestial).** Veneration-pulses (the lineages' daily and seasonal rites); Acquired-stratum goods for the courts' maintenance (the courts' Roads department's commission, paid in spirit stones by the Acquired-stratum holy lands whose sects use the maintained roads); raw herbs and beast parts for the Precelestial's alchemists (the Precelestial's alchemists are mostly persistent ancestors whose persistence-budget allows the alchemical work; the raw materials come from the Acquired stratum). The upward flow's volume is larger than the downward flow's (the Acquired stratum is, in qi-density terms, the larger economy), but the upward flow's value-per-unit is lower (the Acquired stratum's resources are mortal-tier; the Precelestial's needs are cosmic-tier).

### 2.2 The cosmic currencies

**Spirit stones (the subsidiary currency).** Spirit stones remain the cosmic economy's subsidiary currency — used for transactions below the Nascent Soul tier, for the courts' Roads-department commissions, and for the licensed carriers' fees. The exchange rate (10-50 taels of silver per spirit stone, per doc 24 §3.1) holds across the Acquired stratum; in the Precelestial, the exchange rate is denominated in veneration-pulses (1 spirit stone ≈ a Qi Condensation cultivator's daily veneration-pulse; 100 spirit stones ≈ a Foundation Establishment cultivator's daily veneration-pulse; 10,000 spirit stones ≈ a Core Formation cultivator's daily veneration-pulse). A Nascent Soul cultivator's daily veneration-pulse is, in spirit-stone equivalent, approximately 1,000,000 stones — a sum no Acquired-stratum institution can pay in mortal currency. The exchange rate is therefore theoretical above Nascent Soul; in practice, Nascent Soul and above transactions are denominated in the cosmic currencies below.

**Qi-authority (the reserve currency).** Qi-authority — the substrate's measurable qi-density, in a cultivator's anchor or in a court's reservoir — is the cosmic economy's reserve currency. Qi-authority is finite (per doc 00 §1, the cosmos is non-regenerating) and non-substitutable (every cosmic-scale transaction consumes qi-authority, directly or indirectly). The Thinning is the decline of the Acquired stratum's total qi-authority; the pantheon's deadlock (per doc 39 §6) is, at root, a disagreement about how to allocate the declining qi-authority. Qi-authority is not traded directly (it is too fundamental to be transferable in discrete units); it is conserved, spent, or wasted, and the conservation is the cosmic economy's central practice.

**Veneration (the ancestral court currency).** Veneration (per doc 46 §5) is the qi-pulse a lineage's living cultivators generate by the veneration rite. Veneration is the cosmic economy's lineage-currency — it flows from the living to the dead, sustains the ancestral courts, and is the medium in which the courts' interventions are denominated. Veneration is, in principle, denominated in spirit-stone equivalent (see §2.2 above), but the equivalence is theoretical (a lineage cannot sell its veneration-pulses to another lineage; the veneration is bound to the lineage's qi-connection to its court). Veneration is therefore a non-transferable currency, except in the restricted form of inter-court donations (the Garden Court to the Mourner's Court, per §1.4).

**Law-fragments (the high-tier currency).** Law-fragments (per §1.6) are the cosmic economy's high-tier currency — the medium in which Mahayana-tier transactions are denominated. A law-fragment's value is, by the pantheon's collective assessment, approximately equivalent to a Tribulation Crossing cultivator's persistence-budget (a Tribulation Crossing cultivator would, in principle, trade their entire remaining budget for a law-fragment of a relevant predecessor's law). The trade is rare (the pantheon's nine Mahayana hold, between them, perhaps fifty law-fragments worth studying; the fragments are mostly hoarded); the trade's protocol is the pantheon's Council of Nine (per doc 39 §7), where a Mahayana may offer a fragment for a favor, a vote, or a coalition.

**Time (the constraint, not the currency).** The time-debt (per §1.5) is the cosmic economy's master constraint, not a currency. Time cannot be traded (the 1:365 ratio is lawful and non-negotiable, per doc 15 §7.1); time can only be spent, and the spending's cost is the mortal world's aging. Every cosmic-scale transaction is, in part, a time-cost calculation; the calculation is the cosmic economy's tragic engine.

### 2.3 The grotto-heaven economy

A grotto-heaven (per doc 24 §2.7, doc 37 §6.2) is a Precelestial-affixed cultivation zone sustained by an Acquired spirit vein's main current. The grotto-heaven's qi is denser than the Acquired stratum's ambient; the grotto-heaven's time-rate is faster than the Acquired stratum's (the Crimson Vow City's 1:3, the Garden Grotto's 1:5, the Jade Void's 1:10). The grotto-heavens are the cosmic economy's primary production zones — the places where cosmic-tier herbs are grown, cosmic-tier beasts are raised, and cosmic-tier alchemy is performed.

The cosmos's grotto-heavens number approximately one hundred and forty, by Tu Nan's map (per doc 39 §2.6). The distribution is uneven: the eastern continent holds the most (fifty-two, including the Jade Void Grotto and the Garden Grotto); the central continent holds forty-one (including the Azure Sword Sect's minor grotto); the southern continent holds thirty-nine (mostly wood-phase grottos, sustained by the central highlands' vein system); the northern continent holds eight (the northern continent's qi-density is lower; the Thinning is most advanced there). The Crimson Vow City's former grotto (collapsed, Year ~43,000) is not in the count; the Silent Hexagram Grotto-Heaven (sealed, Year ~45,500) is in the count but inaccessible. The count has declined by approximately ten grotto-heavens over the last three thousand years (the Thinning's effect; some grottos have failed as their spirit veins declined below the sustenance threshold).

**Who controls the grotto-heavens.** Each grotto-heaven is controlled by the holy land or sect that holds its anchor (the surface trace in the Acquired stratum, per doc 24 §2.7). The Jade Void Holy Land holds the Jade Void Grotto (the eastern continent's largest peak grotto-heaven); the Garden Holy Land holds the Garden Grotto; the Azure Sword Sect holds a minor grotto-heaven in the Cangwu Mountains (a 1:2 time-rate grotto, sustaining 500-year herbs, the sect's primary cosmic-tier asset). The courts do not control any grotto-heaven (the courts' jurisdiction does not extend to the grotto-heavens, which are private holdings; the lineage-jurisdiction clause, jurisdiction 5).

**What the holy lands charge for grotto-heaven access.** A holy land leases access to its grotto-heaven to allied sects and cultivators. The lease's terms vary; the Jade Void Holy Land's standard lease (per the holy land's published schedule, accessible at Nascent Soul) charges: 1,000 spirit stones per Acquired-stratum day of access for a Core Formation cultivator; 100 spirit stones per day for a Nascent Soul cultivator (Nascent Soul cultivators are valuable enough to the holy land that the lease is subsidized); free access for Tribulation Crossing cultivators who agree to perform a maintenance service (grotto-heaven law-inscription repair, a 1-day service in exchange for 30 days of access). The Azure Sword Sect's grotto-heaven lease is restricted to the sect's own disciples and to the sect's two allied sects (the Cangwu Sect, the protagonist's first sect, is not among them); the protagonist's first grotto-heaven access is, in Act 2, the Azure Sword Sect's grotto, accessed by a personal favor from the sect master Leng Wushuang (per doc 26, the protagonist's relationship with the Azure Sword Sect).

**The grotto-heaven economy's tragic engine.** The grotto-heavens are sustained by Acquired spirit veins; the spirit veins are declining (the Thinning, per §1.7); the grotto-heavens are, in the long run, a wasting asset. The Jade Void Grotto's qi-feed has declined approximately 5% in the last five thousand years (the Jade Void patriarch's measurement, recorded in the holy land's archive); the decline is, for now, sustainable (the grotto's production is still sufficient for the holy land's needs), but the trajectory is the cosmic economy's master scarcity. In eight to ten thousand years (per doc 39 §6's timeline), the grotto-heavens will fail; the cosmic-tier herbs' production will cease; the cosmic economy's primary production zone will be gone. The pantheon's Builders faction (per doc 39 §6) wants to author a qi-conservation law to slow the decline; the Tenders faction wants to stop authoring and let the decline proceed gracefully; the deadlock is, in economic terms, a deadlock over the grotto-heaven economy's future.

### 2.4 The ancestral court economy

Per doc 46 §5, the ancestral court's qi-economy is: veneration flows up (from living to dead), and the court's persistence-budget flows down (in interventions, blessings, counsel). The ancestral court economy is the cosmic economy's most vertical sector — the resource flows are unidirectional (up) and the reciprocal flows (down) are constrained by the persistence-budget's finitude.

**What flows up.** Veneration (per §2.2). The veneration-pulse's magnitude depends on the lineage's living cultivators' number, realm, and rite-efficiency. A lineage with a Nascent Soul elder, a dozen Core Formation cultivators, and a hundred Foundation Establishment cultivators (the Yan clan's approximate Acquired-stratum remnant) generates a daily veneration-pulse approximately equivalent to 5,000,000 spirit stones — a sum that, in the cosmic economy, is denominated not in spirit stones but in the court's qi-reservoir's daily increment. The court's qi-reservoir, in turn, sustains the persistent ancestors' coherence.

**What flows down.** Counsel (the consultation rite's primary product, per doc 15 §3.3); interventions (rare, per doc 15 §3.4, denominated in persistence-budget); blessings (the most common down-flow, a small qi-authority investment that elevates a living descendant's capability for a short period); curses (rare, expensive, and the form most likely to attract the courts' attention). The down-flows are constrained by the persistence-budget's finitude — every down-flow diminishes the ancestor, and the ancestor's counsel about whether to down-flow is, in part, the ancestor's calculation of whether the down-flow is worth the cost.

**The court's economic relationship to its living lineage.** The court is, in economic terms, the lineage's pension fund — a reservoir the lineage fills during its productive years (when the lineage's cultivators are generating veneration) and draws from during its crisis years (when the lineage's cultivators need counsel, blessings, or interventions). A lineage with a well-fed court (the Yan clan, the Builder's Lineage) has a strategic reserve no Acquired-stratum wealth can buy; a lineage with a starving court (the Mourner's Lineage, the Pillar Dynasty) has a reserve that is, in cosmic terms, a wasting asset. The Wang lineage (the protagonist's lineage, courtless, per doc 46 §3) has no reserve at all — the protagonist's grief for Wang Xiaodi is, in economic terms, the grief of a being whose lineage cannot afford the persistence the cosmos's other lineages take for granted.

### 2.5 The underworld market

The underworld market (per doc 26 §9.2, doc 38 §9) is the cosmic economy's unregulated sector — the trade in goods the courts' Roads department does not permit through the licensed carriers, conducted in the Acquired stratum's wild edges and the Spirit Wilds' border zones. The market is not a single institution; it is a network of brokers, carriers, and fences (Liang Zhu, the protagonist's companion, is one of the brokers) operating across the eastern and central continents' wild paths.

**What the underworld market trades.** Wild herbs (per §1.2) that the courts' Foreign Affairs department's yao-court trade does not acquire; wild beast parts (per §1.3) that the Wild Hunters' lineages do not sell through the licensed carriers; law-fragments (per §1.6) that the pantheon's Mahayana have not hoarded; veneration-pulses (per §2.2) stolen from courts whose retrieval rites have been corrupted (Scribe Yan's bardo-harvest, per doc 26 §2.3, is the named instance of veneration-pulse theft — the harvested anchors' qi-authority is, in effect, a stolen veneration-pulse, redirected from the lawful bardo-processing to the harvester's personal use). The underworld market's prices are denominated in spirit stones (for the lower-tier goods), law-fragments (for the higher-tier goods), and qi-authority (for the highest-tier goods, denominated in the buyer's personal qi-authority, transferred to the seller in a controlled burn).

**Who trades in the underworld market.** Rogue cultivators (the market's primary constituents), corrupt courts officials (Scribe Yan, before his exposure), demonic cultivators (the Crimson Vow Remnant, per doc 37 §6.2, trades fire-phase fragments through the market), and (rarely) holy land cultivators seeking resources the courts' licensed carriers cannot supply. The courts' Roads department considers the market a nuisance; the Foreign Affairs department considers the market a source of intelligence (Liang Zhu's gossip is, in part, the market's gossip); the Defense department considers the market a security risk (the market's wild-edge locations are Spirit Wilds breach-points). The protagonist's companion Liang Zhu is the protagonist's primary access to the market; the protagonist's pursuit of Pei Liang (per doc 26 §3) requires the market's intelligence, and the protagonist's first cosmic-tier resource purchases (a wild herb for a Core Formation breakthrough preparation; a law-fragment for a Nascent Soul inheritance-study) are made through Liang Zhu's broker-network.

**The market's economic function.** The underworld market is, in cosmic-economic terms, the cosmic economy's black market — the sector that exists because the courts' regulated sector cannot supply all the cosmic-tier resources the cosmos's cultivators demand. The market's existence is a structural feature of the cosmos's economic regulation (the courts' licensing of carriers restricts supply; the unmet demand creates the market); the market's persistence is a structural feature of the cosmos's political fragmentation (the courts cannot enforce their jurisdiction in the wild edges; the market operates where the courts' law does not reach). The market's function is, in the cosmic economy, the function of a pressure valve — the market absorbs the demand the regulated sector cannot supply, and the absorption prevents the regulated sector's breakdown. The Reform Bloc's "incorporate the rogues" program (per doc 38 §3.4) is, in economic terms, a proposal to formalize the market's function (bring the brokers into the licensed carriers' roster); the Conservative Bloc's opposition is, in economic terms, a defense of the regulated sector's purity (the market's existence is a moral failing, not an economic necessity). The protagonist's relationship to the market (through Liang Zhu) is, in cosmic-economic terms, the protagonist's first encounter with the cosmic economy's structural imperfection.

### 2.6 The mortal economy's interface with the cosmic economy

The mortal economy (doc 18) and the cosmic economy are not separate systems; they are two layers of a single economic substrate, with the mortal economy as the lower layer and the cosmic economy as the upper layer. The interface between the layers is the spirit-stone exchange rate (10-50 taels of silver per spirit stone, per doc 24 §3.1) and the licensed carriers' stratum-crossing fees (per §2.1).

**Where the mortal economy ends and the cosmic economy begins.** The boundary is, in practice, at the Core Formation tier. A Qi Condensation cultivator operates in the mortal economy (earning spirit stones, buying mortal-tier resources); a Core Formation cultivator operates in both economies simultaneously (earning spirit stones but also encountering cosmic-tier resources they cannot access); a Nascent Soul cultivator operates primarily in the cosmic economy (the spirit-stone economy is, for a Nascent Soul cultivator, irrelevant; the cosmic-currency economy is the Nascent Soul cultivator's actual constraint). The protagonist's economic arc (§4 below) traces the protagonist's passage through this boundary — the passage is the protagonist's central economic transformation, and the passage's cost (the protagonist's mortal kin aging past recognition, per doc 15 §7.2) is the genre's central tragic engine.

**The interface's leakage.** The interface is not perfectly sealed. Cosmic-tier resources occasionally leak into the mortal economy — a wild herb, harvested by a mortal herb-gatherer in the Cangwu Mountains' deep interior, sold at the Qinghe Market Town for silver (the gatherer does not know the herb's cosmic-tier properties; the buyer, if the buyer is a rogue cultivator with the right perception, profits enormously). The leakage is the mortal economy's occasional encounter with the cosmic economy's multiplier, felt as a windfall rather than perceived as a structural feature. The protagonist's first encounter with cosmic-tier resources, in Act 2 (per §4.4 below), is a leakage-encounter — the Pillar Dynasty bark-strip text in the Azure Sword Sect's Scripture Hall is a cosmic-tier resource that has leaked, via a rogue cultivator's trade, into a Core Formation-tier sect's archive. The leakage is the cosmic economy's mortal-world footprint, and the footprint is the protagonist's first perception of the cosmic economy's existence.

## 3. What Mahayana cultivators fight over

The pantheon's nine (per doc 39 §1) are the cosmos's only Mahayana cultivators. They are, by the pantheon's collective assessment, the cosmic economy's only beings whose transactions are denominated in law-authorship. This section specifies what they fight over.

### 3.1 Law Reaches

Per doc 15 §5, the Law Reaches are the Precelestial's regions where the substrate-law has thinned (the Sundering's scars, per doc 37 §5, doc 38 §5). The Reaches are the only venue for Reach-authorship (per doc 24 §1.1, Reach-authorship creates new local substrate where no law previously held). The Reaches are finite; the pantheon has not counted them precisely (the Reaches are not stable; they shift with the substrate-law's slow erosion), but the Cartographer Tu Nan's map (per doc 39 §2.6) identifies approximately **nine major Reaches** and a tail of thirty-seven minor Reaches.

The nine major Reaches, in Tu Nan's map's order (which follows the Precelestial's geography clockwise from the Reach of the Sundered Stratum): (1) the **Reach of the Sundered Stratum** (斷層法域, the largest, holding the Fourth-Stratum Attempt's sealed residue, per doc 37 §7.1); (2) **Mo Shen's Reach** (the silent Reach, per doc 39 §2.7, the pantheon's neutral ground for the Council of Nine); (3) **Lie Fa's Reach** (the ruins, per doc 39 §2.9, holding the partial law-inscription); (4) the **Reach of the Caged Sky** (籠天法域, holding the sealed Sky-Devouring Beast, per doc 37 §7.2); (5) the **Reach of the Western Mirror** (西鏡法域, unclaimed, the Builders' preferred qi-conservation-law venue); (6) the **Reach of the Northern Wind** (北風法域, unclaimed, the Tenders' preferred restraint-law venue); (7) the **Reach of the Eastern Tide** (東潮法域, unclaimed, contested between the Builders and the Tenders); (8) the **Reach of the Southern Stem** (南莖法域, unclaimed, considered too unstable for authorship by most of the pantheon); (9) the **Reach of the Central Quiet** (中寂法域, unclaimed, Mo Shen's adjacent Reach, considered by the pantheon to be Mo Shen's extended holding despite Mo Shen's refusal to claim it).

**Who controls the Reaches.** No one. The Reaches are explicitly outside the courts' jurisdiction (per doc 38 §7.4); the courts maintain watchposts but do not cross the boundary. The Reaches are, by the pantheon's collective assessment, common ground — any Mahayana cultivator may enter any Reach and attempt Reach-authorship. The Reaches' common-ground status is, however, complicated by the pantheon's personal holdings: Mo Shen's Reach (per doc 39 §2.7) is Mo Shen's silent abode; Lie Fa's Reach (per doc 39 §2.9) is Lie Fa's ruins. These two Reaches are, by pantheon custom, not available for other Mahayana's authorship. The remaining seven major Reaches are, in principle, available; in practice, the pantheon has not authored a new Reach-law in eight hundred years (the deadlock, per doc 39 §6), so the question of who would author where has been moot.

**Is there competition for unclaimed Reaches?** Yes, latently. The deadlock's breaking (the protagonist's amendment, per doc 39 §6) will reopen the question. The Builders faction (Fan Xuan, Tu Nan, Lu Shen) has, in private, identified three of the seven unclaimed major Reaches as candidate venues for a qi-conservation law (the Builders' preferred post-deadlock project). The Tenders faction (Yin Mu, Bei Huai, Mo Shen) has identified the same three Reaches as candidates for a different project — a law of restraint that would, in effect, codify the Tenders' position (no new authorship) into cosmic law. The two factions' candidate-Reach lists overlap; if the deadlock breaks and both factions attempt authorship in the same Reach, the Reach's substrate may not sustain two simultaneous authorships (the substrate's law-paradox perception, doc 30 Station 10, would flag the conflict). The conflict would be the pantheon's first Reach-authorship contest in eight hundred years.

### 3.2 The Thinning

Per doc 39 §6, the Thinning is the pantheon's deadlock: should the pantheon author new laws to slow the Thinning (the Builders' position), stop authoring and let the Thinning proceed gracefully (the Tenders' position), or approach the Origin to perceive whether the Origin's generative function can be renewed (the Wanderers' position)? The deadlock has held for two thousand years; the Thinning has accelerated in that time.

**Do Mahayana cultivators compete for the remaining qi?** No — and this is the deadlock's specific texture. The Mahayana cultivators are not, in their personal practice, competing for qi (a Mahayana cultivator's qi-metabolism is sustained by the Precelestial's undifferentiated qi, which is sustained by the Origin's generative function, which is not subject to the Thinning). The Thinning is, for the Mahayana personally, a slow dimming of the Acquired stratum they observe but do not depend on. The deadlock is not about the Mahayana's personal qi-supply; the deadlock is about the Acquired stratum's qi-supply, which is the supply that sustains mortal cultivation, mortal lineages, and the ancestral courts' veneration. The Mahayana's deadlock is, in economic terms, a deadlock over the cosmic economy's substrate — not over the Mahayana's personal resources.

**Do they cooperate to conserve it?** Latently. The Tenders' position (stop authoring) is a cooperation position — the Tenders are willing to forgo new law-authorship to conserve the qi-authorship that new inscriptions would consume. The Builders' position (author conservation laws) is also a cooperation position — the Builders want to author laws that would, in effect, tax the Acquired stratum's qi-authority to fund the grotto-heavens' maintenance. The two positions are both cooperative in intent; the deadlock is over which cooperation to pursue. The Wanderers' position (approach the Origin) is, in economic terms, a speculation position — the Wanderers want to perceive whether the Origin's generative function can be renewed, which would, if successful, end the Thinning; the speculation's cost is the Mahayana's risk (Lie Fa's fall, per doc 39 §2.9, is the cautionary tale).

**What the protagonist's law does, economically.** The protagonist's bardo-harvest-loophole amendment (Transcend path, per doc 39 §5.2, §6) is, in economic terms, a qi-conservation law — it closes a qi-theft (harvested anchors are qi-authority diverted from the lawful bardo-processing to the harvester's personal use). The amendment conserves, by the pantheon's preliminary modeling, approximately 0.01% of the Acquired stratum's annual qi-authority — a small fraction, but the first conservation the pantheon has been able to enact in eight hundred years. The amendment gives the Builders a precedent (qi-conservation is possible); the Tenders a concession (the conservation was achieved by amendment, not by new authorship); the Wanderers nothing (the amendment does not approach the Origin). The deadlock does not break cleanly along the amendment's lines; the deadlock's breaking is, in economic terms, the beginning of a longer negotiation the protagonist's post-game work is to tend.

### 3.3 Predecessors' laws

Per doc 24 §1.1, Acquired-authorship can amend existing Acquired law within a scope; the amendment requires, by the pantheon's procedural law, either the original law's author's ratification or the author's dispersal. The procedural law is the source of the courts' current deadlock (the bardo-window law's author, Gu Wu / Qiu Wuhen, has neither ratified nor dispersed, per doc 38 §8).

**Can a Mahayana cultivator amend another's law?** Yes, with the author's ratification or dispersal. The ratification is the author's consent; the dispersal is the author's death. The procedural law is, in this respect, the cosmic economy's authorship-property law — a Mahayana's law is the Mahayana's property, and the property's transfer requires the owner's consent or death.

**What if the predecessor is dead?** If the predecessor has dispersed (final death, 寂滅), the law is, in procedural terms, ownerless — the law persists (laws outlast their authors, per doc 39 §8), but no Mahayana's ratification is required for amendment. The amendment, in this case, requires only the Council's vote (a simple majority of those present, per doc 38 §4). The ownerless-law amendment is the easiest amendment procedurally; it is also the rarest (most of the cosmos's active laws are still held by their authors, because most authors have not yet dispersed).

**What if the predecessor is Gu Wu, who is dying?** This is the protagonist's specific case (Transcend path, per doc 39 §5.2). Gu Wu is alive (not dispersed) but dying (within the century, per doc 39 §2.1). The protagonist's amendment to Gu Wu's bardo-window law requires either Gu Wu's ratification (Gu Wu has not decided, per doc 38 §8) or Gu Wu's dispersal (Gu Wu has not chosen). The protagonist's path, in the Transcend ending, is to force the issue: the protagonist authors the amendment as an Acquired-authorship act (which the procedural law does not explicitly forbid — the law requires ratification for the amendment to take effect, but the authorship itself is the Mahayana's verb). The amendment, taking effect, is the fait accompli the Council must ratify or contest (per doc 38 §8); the Conservative Bloc's contest (if it comes) is the civil-war risk; the protagonist's survival of the contest (or failure to survive) determines whether the amendment holds. Gu Wu, perceiving the amendment's effect, disperses — the dispersal is the procedural law's resolution (the predecessor's death frees the amendment to proceed without ratification). The economic consequence: the bardo-window law's authorship-property transfers, in cosmic-economy terms, from Gu Wu to the protagonist (the protagonist becomes the law's maintainer, the way Gu Wu was); the transfer is the protagonist's inheritance and the protagonist's burden.

**Can a Mahayana amend a dispersed predecessor's law without the Council's vote?** No. The procedural law is clear (the Council's vote is required for ownerless-law amendments). A Mahayana who attempts an amendment without the Council's vote is, in cosmic-economy terms, attempting a property-transfer without the property-court's approval — the attempt is unlawful, and the courts' Adjudication department would hear the case (the Mahayana's law-tribulation, in this case, would include an Adjudication-department component). The procedural law is, in this respect, the cosmic economy's contract law — the law that makes Mahayana authorship a lawful practice rather than a personal power.

### 3.4 The pantheon's interpersonal economics

The pantheon's nine Mahayana are, per doc 39 §2.10, a senate with alliances, fractures, and silences. The senate's economics are the cosmic economy's highest-tier social layer — the layer where law-fragments trade, where coalition-votes are exchanged, where the pantheon's interpersonal dynamics translate into cosmic-scale resource allocation.

**The fragment-trade.** The pantheon's law-fragment trade is the cosmic economy's most rarefied transaction. A Mahayana offering a fragment (their own predecessor's, or a fragment they acquired in turn from an earlier dispersal) for a Council vote, a coalition, or a favor, is performing the pantheon's equivalent of a mortal auction's high-stakes bid (per doc 18 §2.3). The trade is rare (perhaps two or three fragment-transactions per Precelestial century); the trade's protocol is informal (the pantheon has no formal market; the trade is conducted in private, in the Council of Nine's margins, or in personal visits between Mahayana); the trade's enforcement is the pantheon's collective honor (a Mahayana who reneges on a fragment-trade is, by pantheon custom, no longer traded with; the social cost is the trade's enforcement). Fan Xuan is the pantheon's most prolific fragment-holder (eight fragments, mostly from Fan Xuan's predecessors and allies); Yan Qingshi is the pantheon's most prolific fragment-seeker (Yan Qingshi's records-integrity law requires constant reference to predecessor fragments, and Yan Qingshi has acquired four fragments across two thousand years); Mo Shen is the pantheon's only fragment-abstainer (Mo Shen does not study fragments; Mo Shen considers the study a distraction from the practice of silence).

**The coalition-vote economy.** The pantheon's Council of Nine votes (per doc 39 §7) are the cosmic economy's highest-tier legislative acts. A coalition-vote is traded the way a mortal politician trades a vote — by alliance, by faction, by personal favor. The Builders faction (Fan Xuan, Tu Nan, Lu Shen) votes as a bloc on most qi-conservation questions; the Tenders faction (Yin Mu, Bei Huai, Mo Shen) votes as a bloc on most authorship-restraint questions; the Wanderers faction (You Wuya, Lie Fa) does not vote as a bloc (Lie Fa cannot vote, in any coherent sense; You Wuya votes inconsistently, by personal whim). Gu Wu, as the swing vote (per doc 39 §6), is the pantheon's most courted Mahayana — the pantheon's factions have, for two thousand years, courted Gu Wu's vote on every major question, and Gu Wu has, for two thousand years, declined to commit. The protagonist, joining the pantheon as the ninth (per doc 39 §3), becomes the new swing vote; the protagonist's first decade in the pantheon will be a period of being courted by all three factions. The courting is, in cosmic-economic terms, the pantheon's most active interpersonal economy.

**The personal-favor economy.** Below the fragment-trade and the coalition-vote, the pantheon's interpersonal economy is sustained by personal favors — a Mahayana visiting another Mahayana's holding (the way You Wuya visits Fan Xuan's Council seat, or the way Yin Mu visits Bei Huai's mortal-wandering path); a Mahayana attending another Mahayana's dispersal (the way Bei Huai will attend Gu Wu's dispersal, per doc 39 §5.2); a Mahayana consulting another Mahayana's law-authorship technique (the way Lu Shen consults Fan Xuan's courts-infrastructure law, when a tribulation-trigger law amendment is contemplated). The favors are not denominated in currency; they are denominated in the pantheon's collective memory (a Mahayana who attends another's dispersal is remembered; a Mahayana who refuses a consultation is remembered; the memory persists across millennia). The personal-favor economy is the pantheon's social fabric, and the social fabric is, in the pantheon's eight-thousand-year history, the only thing that has held the senate together through the deadlock. The protagonist, joining the pantheon, joins the personal-favor economy; the protagonist's first favors (visiting Lie Fa's ruins, attending Gu Wu's dispersal, ratifying the protagonist's own law to Fan Xuan's review) are the protagonist's first contributions to the fabric.

**The pantheon's economic solitude.** The pantheon's economy is, in its highest tier, the economy of nine beings who have known each other for millennia and who have, in those millennia, traded everything they have to trade. The fragment-trade is exhausted (most fragments have been studied by everyone who can study them); the coalition-vote economy is deadlocked (the deadlock has held for two thousand years); the personal-favor economy is calcified (the favors are the same favors, exchanged across millennia, between the same nine beings). The pantheon's economy is, in this respect, the cosmic economy's most stagnant sector — the sector where the cosmic economy's finitude is most acutely felt, because the sector's participants have nothing left to acquire and nothing left to trade except their own dispersal. The protagonist, joining the pantheon as the ninth, is the pantheon's first new economic actor in eight hundred years; the protagonist's arrival is, in cosmic-economic terms, the pantheon's first injection of new economic energy since the deadlock began. The protagonist's first decade in the pantheon will be the pantheon's most economically active decade in eight hundred years, not because the protagonist is wealthy (the protagonist is, by pantheon standards, impoverished) but because the protagonist is *new* — and newness, in a finite cosmos's most stagnant sector, is the rarest resource of all.

## 4. The protagonist's economic arc

The protagonist's relationship to resources changes at each tier of the realm ladder. This section traces the arc from mortal poverty to cosmic-scale responsibility.

### 4.1 Mortal poverty (sessions 1-50)

The protagonist begins in a Wang tenant household (per doc 04 §6.3, doc 18 §5.1) running a subsistence deficit. The household's annual cash income is approximately 4,000-6,800 cash (per doc 24 §3.1); the household's annual expenses (food, salt, tools, tax) exceed income by 500-2,000 cash in a normal year; the deficit is covered by the pig sale, weaving, and day labor. The protagonist's relationship to resources is the relationship of a being for whom a single bad harvest means borrowing grain at 50-100% interest, and a second bad harvest means selling a child. The protagonist's desire to cultivate is, in economic terms, the desire to escape the debt cycle (per doc 18 §5.1).

The protagonist's first economic decision (per doc 26 §2, the protagonist's early-game choice to seek Old Chen's teaching rather than remain in the household's labor pool) is, in economic terms, the decision to invest the household's remaining surplus (the protagonist's labor, redirected from farming to cultivation) in a high-risk, high-return asset (cultivation training). The investment's risk is the household's lost labor (the protagonist is not in the fields); the investment's return, if the protagonist reaches Qi Condensation, is the household's transformation. The investment is, in mortal-economic terms, irrational (the expected return, discounted by the probability of failure, is negative); the investment is, in cosmic-economic terms, the only rational choice (the cosmic economy's multiplier, once accessed, overwhelms any mortal-economic calculation). The protagonist's first economic decision is the protagonist's first encounter with the genre's central economic fact, felt as a gamble rather than perceived as a math.

### 4.2 Qi Condensation (sessions 50-200)

The protagonist reaches Qi Condensation. The protagonist can now earn 10-50 spirit stones per month (per doc 18 §5.2, doc 24 §3.1), which is equivalent to 18-55 years of mortal labor per month of cultivation work. The protagonist's relationship to resources inverts: the protagonist can now send silver home (per doc 18 §7) and change the Wang household's trajectory. The protagonist's first major economic act, in Act 1's final sessions, is the gift of silver to the Wang household — a gift that pays the household's debts and elevates the household from tenant to smallholder. The gift is, in cosmic-economic terms, a trivial act (the protagonist earns the silver in a single spirit-stone-paying task); in mortal-economic terms, the gift is the household's transformation.

### 4.3 Foundation Establishment (sessions 200-500)

The protagonist reaches Foundation Establishment. The protagonist can now earn approximately 100-500 spirit stones per month (a Foundation Establishment cultivator's mercenary contracts and alchemical work command ten times a Qi Condensation cultivator's rates). The protagonist's relationship to resources shifts from "earn and send home" to "invest in cultivation" — the protagonist buys better techniques, better pills, better tools. The protagonist joins the Cangwu Sect and experiences the sect economy (per doc 18 §6); the protagonist's first auction (per doc 18 §2.3) is in this tier. The protagonist's mortal kin, by this point, are no longer in subsistence deficit (the protagonist's silver gifts have elevated the household); the protagonist's relationship to the Wang household shifts from "support" to "visit."

### 4.4 Core Formation (sessions 500-1000)

The protagonist reaches Core Formation. The protagonist can now earn approximately 1,000-5,000 spirit stones per month (a Core Formation cultivator's beast-hunting and formation-inscription rates). The protagonist's relationship to resources shifts again: the spirit-stone economy is, for a Core Formation cultivator, no longer constraining (the protagonist can buy any mortal-tier resource in any quantity). The protagonist's constraints shift to: time (the protagonist's breakthrough preparations take decades), qi-authority (the protagonist's Core requires sustained qi-feed), and reputation (the protagonist's standing among Core Formation cultivators determines the protagonist's access to Nascent Soul-tier resources). The protagonist, in this tier, encounters the cosmic-tier resources for the first time — a Pillar Dynasty bark-strip text in the Azure Sword Sect's Scripture Hall (per doc 37 §9.2), a fragment of the Crimson Vow Code in Old Chen's collection (per doc 37 §6.2). The cosmic-tier resources are, for a Core Formation cultivator, mostly inaccessible (the protagonist cannot read them, cannot use them, cannot afford them); they are the protagonist's first perception of the cosmic economy's existence.

### 4.5 Nascent Soul (sessions 1000-1300)

The protagonist reaches Nascent Soul. The protagonist can now earn approximately 10,000-50,000 spirit stones per month (a Nascent Soul cultivator's mercenary contracts and alchemical work command ten times a Core Formation cultivator's rates), but the spirit-stone economy is, for a Nascent Soul cultivator, irrelevant — the protagonist's actual constraints are qi-authority, time, and law-residue. The protagonist, in this tier, encounters the cosmic economy directly: the protagonist's pursuit of Pei Liang crosses the underworld market (per doc 26 §9.2, doc 38 §9), where Liang Zhu knows the law-fragment trade; the protagonist's first spirit-stone-denominated purchase of a cosmic-tier resource (a wild herb, a law-fragment, a veneration-pulse donation) is in this tier.

The protagonist's first law-fragment purchase, in this tier, is a significant economic act. A law-fragment of a minor Spirit Severance cultivator's qi-circulation technique (sold by a Wild Hunter lineage's broker through Liang Zhu's network, priced at approximately 50,000 spirit stones — the protagonist's entire Qi Condensation-era savings plus a Core Formation mercenary contract's proceeds) is the protagonist's first cosmic-tier acquisition. The fragment, studied with the protagonist's Residual Error Sense (per doc 26 §9.2, the protagonist's unique perception), gives the protagonist a Spirit Severance cultivator's qi-circulation insight three realms early — an insight the protagonist will use, in §4.6 below, to refine the protagonist's Spirit Severance breakthrough preparation. The purchase is, in cosmic-economic terms, the protagonist's first investment in cosmic-tier resources; the investment's return is measured in breakthrough-preparation time saved (decades compressed to years), and the return's value is, in the cosmic economy, the protagonist's first encounter with the cosmic economy's multiplier effect at the protagonist's own scale.

The protagonist's relationship to the Wang lineage shifts: the protagonist can now, in principle, perform the lineage-founding rite's precursor (the protagonist is the lineage's first Nascent Soul cultivator; the rite's full form requires Mahayana, but the precursor can be begun). The protagonist's relationship to the courts shifts: the protagonist can now petition the courts (per doc 38 §9, the protagonist's first direct, failed petition is in this tier). The protagonist's perception of the Thinning (per doc 37 §1.2) begins in this tier — a faint, slow dimming the protagonist perceives over centuries.

### 4.6 Spirit Severance (sessions 1300-1400, if reached)

The protagonist reaches Spirit Severance. The protagonist's relationship to resources shifts to: the protagonist's domain (the Spirit Severance cultivator's externalized anchor, per doc 24 §2.3), the protagonist's place-bond (the precursor to Void Amalgamation), and the protagonist's persistence-budget (the protagonist is now, in principle, eligible for ancestral court persistence, per doc 15 §3.2). The protagonist, in this tier, visits an ancestral court for the first time (per doc 46 §6, the protagonist's first visit is to the First Court, in session ~1300-1400, but the protagonist may visit a holy land's allied court earlier). The protagonist's relationship to the cosmic economy is now a relationship of a participant — the protagonist can perceive the law-fragment trade, the veneration-pulse economy, and the Thinning's acceleration, and the protagonist's choices in this tier begin to affect the cosmic economy's flows.

The protagonist's Spirit Severance breakthrough is, in economic terms, the protagonist's first cosmic-tier production act. The breakthrough consumes the protagonist's accumulated Core Formation and Nascent Soul resources (the law-fragment purchased in §4.5; the wild herbs; the Azure Sword Sect's grotto-heaven access; the alchemical pills refined across the Core Formation tier); the breakthrough produces, in cosmic-economic terms, the protagonist's domain — a Spirit Severance cultivator's externalized anchor, which is a small but real cosmic-tier resource (a domain is, in the cosmic economy, a minor ancestral-court-equivalent: a sustained qi-reservoir the cultivator maintains by their personal authority, the way Gu Wu maintains the First Court). The protagonist's domain, sustained across the protagonist's Spirit Severance lifespan (centuries to a millennium), is the protagonist's first cosmic-economy contribution; the contribution is small (a Spirit Severance domain is to a Mahayana's law-authority what a mortal's pig sale is to a Core Formation mercenary contract), but the contribution is real.

### 4.7 Void Amalgamation and Tribulation Crossing (sessions 1400-1500, if reached)

The protagonist reaches Void Amalgamation (station 8) and Tribulation Crossing (station 9). The protagonist's relationship to resources is, at this tier, the relationship of a being whose personal practice is the cosmic economy's constraint — the protagonist's breakthrough preparations consume qi-authority, the protagonist's tribulation will consume more, and the protagonist's persistence (if the protagonist crosses and disperses in the far future) will be a cosmic-tier resource itself. The protagonist, in this tier, prepares for the Tribulation Crossing (per doc 39 §5.2, the meeting with Gu Wu is in this tier); the protagonist's relationship to the Wang lineage, in this tier, is the relationship of a being about to make the lineage-founding choice (the Transcend-path variant, per doc 46 §3).

The protagonist's Tribulation Crossing preparation is, in economic terms, the protagonist's largest pre-Mahayana expenditure. The preparation consumes: the protagonist's entire accumulated law-fragment collection (the fragments are studied for the tribulation's law-paradox component, and the study degrades the fragments beyond further use); the protagonist's domain's qi-reservoir (the domain is sacrificed in the tribulation's lightning component, the way a dam is sacrificed to absorb a flood); the protagonist's persistence-budget (the budget is spent on the heart-demon component's preparation, the way a Nascent Soul cultivator spends their Core on the breakthrough). The tribulation's survival is, in cosmic-economic terms, the protagonist's graduation from the cosmic economy's participant-tier to its contributor-tier; the graduation's cost is everything the protagonist has accumulated below Mahayana.

### 4.8 Mahayana (sessions 1500+, in the Transcend ending)

The protagonist reaches Mahayana. The protagonist's relationship to resources is now the relationship of a pantheon member — the protagonist's law-authorship is the cosmic economy's highest-tier verb; the protagonist's persistence-budget is, in cosmic-economic terms, the protagonist's most precious possession; the protagonist's relationship to the Thinning is the relationship of a being who perceives the cosmic economy's master scarcity as a personal fact. The protagonist's "month's work" at Mahayana, in cosmic-economic terms, is the law-maintenance the protagonist performs on the bardo-window law's amended inscription — a month of Mahayana law-maintenance (a Precelestial month = 30 Acquired years) conserves approximately 0.001% of the Acquired stratum's annual qi-authority. The conservation is, in mortal-economic terms, the equivalent of the protagonist's Qi Condensation month's 18-55 years of mortal labor — but scaled to cosmic tier. The protagonist's month of Mahayana work is, in cosmic-economic terms, worth approximately the same as a small holy land's century of Acquired-stratum production. The scale is the genre's central economic fact (one month of cultivation work ≈ decades of mortal labor), scaled to the cosmic tier; the scale's tragedy is that the conservation, even at cosmic scale, is a small fraction of the Thinning's annual drain. The protagonist's work, like Gu Wu's work before them, is a tending — a small, slow, consequential conservation against a decline the protagonist cannot reverse.

### 4.9 The economic arc's shape

The arc is: scarcity (mortal) → escape (Qi Condensation) → investment (Foundation Establishment) → irrelevance of mortal currency (Core Formation) → encounter with cosmic economy (Nascent Soul) → participation (Spirit Severance) → contribution (Void Amalgamation and Tribulation Crossing) → responsibility (Mahayana). The arc's shape is the genre's central economic fact (cultivation is an escape from poverty so dramatic it makes the mortal economy look like a cage), scaled to the cosmic tier. The cage, at the cosmic tier, is the Thinning — the slow decline the protagonist, at Mahayana, can tend but not escape. The tending is the meaning. The meaning is the tending.

### 4.10 The arc's tragic engine

The arc's tragic engine is the gap between the protagonist's growing power and the protagonist's growing responsibility. At mortal tier, the protagonist's power is small and the protagonist's responsibility is local (the Wang household); the gap is the gap the protagonist's Qi Condensation escape closes. At Core Formation tier, the protagonist's power is moderate and the protagonist's responsibility is regional (the Cangli Riverlands, the Azure Sword Sect); the gap is the gap the protagonist's cosmic-tier resource encounter widens (the protagonist perceives the cosmic economy but cannot affect it). At Nascent Soul tier, the protagonist's power is significant and the protagonist's responsibility is multi-regional (the eastern continent, the courts' petition-system); the gap is the gap the protagonist's first cosmic-tier purchases narrow (the protagonist participates in the cosmic economy). At Mahayana tier, the protagonist's power is cosmic and the protagonist's responsibility is cosmic (the bardo-window law's maintenance, the pantheon's deadlock, the Thinning's tending); the gap is the gap the protagonist's law-maintenance narrows but cannot close (the Thinning's annual drain exceeds the protagonist's annual conservation by a factor of approximately ten thousand).

The gap, at Mahayana tier, is the genre's central tragic engine, scaled to the cosmic tier. The protagonist, at Mahayana, is the being the cosmos's mortals look up to; the protagonist, at Mahayana, is also the being who perceives, more clearly than any mortal can, that the cosmos's decline cannot be reversed by any being, including the protagonist. The tending is the meaning. The meaning is the tending. The tending is the only thing the protagonist can do, and the protagonist does it.
