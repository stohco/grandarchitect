Compliance with 36_COSMIC_GEOGRAPHY §4.2 (the wilds fill the gaps between jurisdictions, the Precelestial's vast interior), 15_PRECELESTIAL_AND_TRIBULATION §4 (the wilds' unregulated nature, the alien ecology, the three reasons cultivators go), 38_THE_COURTS_OF_HEAVEN §7.3 (the courts' advisory jurisdiction, the three yao courts, the Wild Suppression Bloc's campaign), 37_COSMIC_HISTORY §6 (the Pillar Dynasty, the Crimson Vow Alliance, the Silent Hexagram School), §3.2 (Daojun Wuwang), §7.2 (the Sky-Devouring Beast as a Spirit Wild native), 25_FOLK_RELIGION_METAPHYSICS §4 (yao ontology), 39_THE_MAHAYANA_PANTHEON §2.3 (You Wuya walks the wilds), §2.5 (Yin Mu's grotto-heaven in the deep Precelestial), 40_THE_LAW_REACHES (the wilds' boundary with the reaches).

## 0. Why this document exists

The bible has, until now, described the Spirit Wilds (靈荒) as the Precelestial's unregulated interior (doc 15 §4.1), the place where ancient beasts and lost inheritances persist (doc 15 §4.2, §4.3), and the home of three yao courts treated by the courts' Foreign Affairs department as sovereigns (doc 38 §7.3). The wilds have been a category — "unregulated" — without a geography. They have been an ecology — "alien" — without named beings. They have been a venue for inheritances — the Pillar Cities (doc 37 §6.1), the Crimson Vow Grotto-Heaven (doc 37 §6.2), the Silent Hexagram Grotto-Heaven (doc 37 §6.3) — without a context for how those inheritances came to be there or what else is there with them.

This document commits the geography, the ecology, the civilizations, the expeditions, and the relationships. The wilds are not empty. They are not a backdrop. They are the Precelestial's largest inhabited region — a vast, indifferent, ancient interior where the substrate-law still operates (unlike the reaches, where it has thinned) but where no court's, ancestral court's, or Mahayana's inscription holds. The wilds are where the Precelestial's qi is densest *and* most undisturbed. The wilds are where the cosmos keeps its past.

**Conventions.** Hanzi appear in parentheses after the English term on first use. No pinyin romanization with tone marks. Distances in li where sub-heaven-stride scale; in heaven-strides (天步) where cosmic. The 1:365 Precelestial-to-Acquired time-debt (doc 15 §7) holds throughout the wilds (the time-debt inverts only in one Law Reach, per doc 40 §2.8). Horizontal coordinates are preserved across strata (doc 36 §5.4): a wild region in the Precelestial above the eastern continent corresponds, horizontally, to the eastern continent below.

## 1. The wilds' geography

The wilds cover most of the Precelestial's horizontal extent (per doc 36 §4.2: roughly three heaven-strides across, of which the wilds are the bulk). The wilds are not uniform. They have regions, each with its own qi-climate (the Precelestial's undifferentiated qi varies by region in ways the pantheon has spent eight thousand years mapping), its own ecology, its own history. This section names six regions. The six are not exhaustive — the wilds have stretches no Mahayana cultivator has surveyed, and Tu Nan's map (doc 39 §2.6) records the six as the *named* regions, with the remainder marked "undifferentiated wild, unexplored."

### 1.1 The Eastern Wilds (東荒)

**Name.** 東荒, "the Eastern Wild." Named by the pantheon's first survey (Gu Wu's, four thousand years ago) for the region's horizontal correspondence to the eastern continent of the Mortal Stratum. The name is functional, not poetic; the wilds' inhabitants do not use it (the Feathered Court calls the region *the Above*, 無上, in their own qi-modulated speech, which is not translatable into mortal or courts idiom).

**Size.** Approximately 0.8 heaven-strides east-to-west, 1.2 heaven-strides north-to-south — roughly 8,000 by 12,000 li. The largest of the named regions.

**Qi-climate.** The qi here is *lifted*. The Eastern Wilds' qi has a continuous upward flow, a slow vertical current that carries ambient undifferentiated qi from the region's lower altitudes (the wild's "floor," corresponding roughly to the Precelestial's standard qi-density) to its upper altitudes (the wild's "ceiling," where the qi is denser than any other wild region's). The flow is not wind — it is qi moving as a medium, the way water moves in a current. A Tribulation Crossing cultivator standing in the Eastern Wilds perceives the qi as a steady lift, a feeling of being carried upward by the substance of the air itself. The lift is gentle at the floor; the lift is, at the ceiling, strong enough that a Tribulation Crossing cultivator must anchor themselves consciously to avoid being lifted further (the ceiling is, per the Feathered Court's account, the boundary beyond which even yao cannot fly without dispersal). The lift is the reason the Feathered Court is here — the avian yao who founded the Court perceived the lift as the perfect substrate for flight-cultivation, and the Court has been here since.

**What lives there.** The Feathered Court of the Eastern Wilds (東荒羽庭), the largest yao civilization in the wilds (§4.1). The Crimson Vow Grotto-Heaven's collapsed anchor (doc 37 §6.2), on the wild's southern edge, at the horizontal coordinate corresponding to the eastern continent's shore. A scattered population of wild avian beasts (not yao — beasts without anchors, per doc 25 §4.4) that have evolved to ride the lift; the beasts are the Feathered Court's hunting-stock and the Court's cultivators' occasional companions. A sparse population of wild plants — primarily **Cloud-Root Pines** (雲根松), trees whose roots descend from branches rather than trunks, anchoring in the qi-current itself rather than in any soil; the pines' wood is, per the Feathered Court's trade, the lightest wood in the cosmos, and is sought by Acquired-stratum cultivators for flying-sword construction.

### 1.2 The Northern Wilds (北荒)

**Name.** 北荒, "the Northern Wild." Named by the same survey, for the region's correspondence to the northern reaches of the central continent.

**Size.** Approximately 0.6 heaven-strides east-to-west, 0.9 heaven-strides north-to-south — roughly 6,000 by 9,000 li. The second-largest named region.

**Qi-climate.** The qi here is *resonant*. The Northern Wilds' substrate has, in addition to the standard undifferentiated qi, a peculiar acoustic-quality — the qi, when sampled by a cultivator's perception, produces a low humming tone, like a struck bell sustained indefinitely. The tone is not sound; it is the perception of the substrate's law-residue vibrating at a frequency the cultivator's perception reads as tone. The humming is, per the Stone-Singing Court's account, the substrate's own song — the residue of the substrate-law's inscription (the Primordial Authors' work, doc 37 §3) still vibrating in the substrate eight thousand years after the inscription. The humming is, in the wild's interior, loud enough that a Tribulation Crossing cultivator must attune their perception to filter it; cultivators who do not attune find their perception overwhelmed, the humming drowning out other qi-signatures. The attunement is the Stone-Singing Court's first teaching to any visitor; the Court has been attuning visitors for four thousand years.

**What lives there.** The Stone-Singing Court of the Northern Wilds (北荒石詠庭), the lithic yao civilization (§4.2). The Silent Hexagram Grotto-Heaven (doc 37 §6.3), sealed, in the wild's southern hills, at the horizontal coordinate corresponding to the central continent's northern hills. A population of wild lithic beasts — the **Resonance-Beasts** (鳴獸), beasts whose bodies are partly substrate and partly flesh, who communicate by striking their own bodies and producing the substrate's humming-tone. The Resonance-Beasts are the Stone-Singing Court's kin (the Court's lithic yao are Resonance-Beasts that developed anchors); the Court treats the Resonance-Beasts as cousins, not as hunting-stock. A sparse population of wild plants — primarily the **Echo-Trees** (回音樹, §2.6), which the Stone-Singing Court tends for the trees' echo-qi-properties.

### 1.3 The Southern Wilds (南荒)

**Name.** 南荒, "the Southern Wild." Named for the region's correspondence to the southern continent.

**Size.** Approximately 0.7 heaven-strides east-to-west, 0.8 heaven-strides north-to-south — roughly 7,000 by 8,000 li.

**Qi-climate.** The qi here is *rooted*. The Southern Wilds' qi has, in addition to the standard undifferentiated qi, a downward flow — the inverse of the Eastern Wilds' lift. The qi flows downward, into the wild's substrate, the way rain flows into soil. A Tribulation Crossing cultivator standing in the Southern Wilds perceives the qi as a steady downward pressure, a feeling of being drawn toward the substrate's depth. The flow is the reason the Pillar Dynasty (doc 37 §6.1) flourished here — the wood-phase yao of the dynasty cultivated by sinking roots into the downward flow, drawing sustenance from the qi as it descended. The flow is also the reason the Pillar Cities' ruins persist — the ruins' stone (grown by the Pillar Dynasty's wood-phase qi, per doc 37 §6.1) has been sustained, for eight thousand years, by the downward flow's continuous nourishment. The ruins are not decaying; the ruins are growing, very slowly, the way a forest grows.

**What lives there.** The Serpentine Court of the Southern Wilds (南荒蛇庭), the serpentine yao civilization (§4.3). The Pillar Cities (柱城群, doc 37 §6.1), the Pillar Dynasty's ruins, in the wild's central highlands. The Forest of the Last Sap (末汁之林, §3.5), the Pillar Lord of the Last Sap's voluntary dispersal-site, in the wild's far south. A dense population of wild serpentine beasts — the **Coiling-Root Serpents** (盤根蛇), serpents that coil around the Pillar Cities' ruins and the Forest of the Last Sap's trees, drawing sustenance from the downward flow's residue. The Coiling-Root Serpents are the Serpentine Court's kin (the Court's serpentine yao are Coiling-Root Serpents that developed anchors); the Court treats the Coiling-Root Serpents as cousins, not as hunting-stock. A dense population of wild plants — primarily the **Last-Sap Trees** (末汁樹), descendants of the Pillar Dynasty's cultivated trees, still growing in the downward flow, still producing the wood-phase qi that sustained the dynasty.

### 1.4 The Western Wilds (西荒)

**Name.** 西荒, "the Western Wild." Named for the region's correspondence to the western continent. The wild is the only cardinal-direction wild without a yao court (§4); the wild is, in the pantheon's assessment, the wildest of the wilds.

**Size.** Approximately 0.5 heaven-strides east-to-west, 0.7 heaven-strides north-to-south — roughly 5,000 by 7,000 li. The smallest of the cardinal-direction wilds.

**Qi-climate.** The qi here is *still*. The Western Wilds' qi does not flow (no lift, no rooting, no resonance); the qi simply sits, undifferentiated, in a medium so still that a Tribulation Crossing cultivator standing in the wild perceives the qi as a kind of gel — a substance with no current, no movement, no direction. The stillness is, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the closest a cultivator can come to perceiving the substrate's *resting* state, the state the substrate is in when no law is acting on it. The perception is, for most cultivators, profoundly uncomfortable — the qi's stillness mirrors the cultivator's own internal architecture, and the cultivator, perceiving the stillness, perceives their own internal motion (the qi-circulation, the meridian-resonance, the anchor's slow inscription-maintenance) as a kind of noise against the stillness. The discomfort is the reason the Western Wilds is the wildest wild — cultivators do not stay here. The wilds' beings, however, find the stillness hospitable; the wild's ecology is sparse but specialized.

**What lives there.** No yao court; no great ruin. The Last Camp of Daojun Wuwang (無妄道君之末營, §3.4), a small inheritance-site in the wild's interior. A sparse population of wild beasts — primarily the **Stillness-Beasts** (寂獸), beasts that have evolved to match the qi's stillness, that move so slowly they are nearly imperceptible, that cultivate by sustaining their internal stillness against the qi's stillness. The Stillness-Beasts are, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the oldest living beings in the wilds — Tu Nan's dating places some individuals at over six thousand years old, sustained by the qi's stillness into lifespans no Acquired-stratum beast could match. A sparse population of wild plants — primarily the **Windless Pines** (無風松, §2.6), trees that have evolved to grow without any qi-motion in their branches, whose needles cultivate by their absolute non-motion.

### 1.5 The Sea of Reeds (葦海)

**Name.** 葦海, "the Sea of Reeds." Named by Tu Nan, four thousand years ago, for the region's dominant feature: a vast plain of wild reeds, growing in the undifferentiated qi, covering the region's entire extent. The reeds are the only named wild-plant-species that dominates a whole region; the reeds are, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the cosmos's most successful wild-plant.

**Size.** Approximately 0.4 heaven-strides across in any direction — roughly 4,000 li. The smallest of the named regions, but the most ecologically dense.

**Qi-climate.** The qi here is *woven*. The Sea of Reeds' reeds, growing in the undifferentiated qi for eight thousand years, have woven the qi into a kind of fabric — the qi, in the reeds' presence, organizes itself along the reeds' stalks, producing a woven structure that is not law (the reeds do not inscribe) but that resembles law in its regularity. A Tribulation Crossing cultivator standing in the Sea of Reeds perceives the qi as a kind of tapestry, with the reeds' stalks as the warp and the qi's own currents as the weft. The perception is, for most cultivators, the most beautiful perception the wilds offer — the Sea of Reeds is, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the most aesthetically pleasing region in the cosmos. The region is also the safest wild region for cultivators below Mahayana; the woven qi stabilizes the substrate enough that a Spirit Severance cultivator can survive here indefinitely, and a Nascent Soul cultivator can survive for months. The Sea of Reeds is the wilds' most-visited region for this reason.

**What lives there.** The **Mirror-Herd** (鏡群, §2.4), a herd-animal that has evolved to match the reeds' woven qi, that reflects attackers' qi back at them, that is the Sea of Reeds' dominant beast. The **Substrate-Coral** (基珊瑚, §2.9), colonies of small beings that build structure from the woven qi's law-residue, that are the reeds' symbionts. A scattered population of inheritance-sites — small, mostly forgotten, mostly from minor lineages that retreated to the Sea of Reeds in the Sundering's aftermath and dispersed there. The Sea of Reeds is, in this sense, the wilds' graveyard for minor inheritances; the inheritances are too small to be worth a Mahayana cultivator's attention, but they are, for Nascent Soul and Spirit Severance cultivators, the most accessible wild-inheritances.

### 1.6 The Border Twilight (邊薄)

**Name.** 邊薄, "the Border Thinness." Named by Tu Nan, three thousand years ago, for the region's character: the wild band where the wilds' undifferentiated qi thins into the Law Reaches' pending substrate. The Border Twilight is the wilds' outermost ring, the transitional zone between the wilds and the reaches.

**Size.** Variable — the Border Twilight is, at its widest, approximately 200 li across; at its narrowest, approximately 30 li across. The Twilight rings the Precelestial's outer margin, between the wilds and the reaches; the Twilight's total length is approximately the Precelestial's circumference (roughly 9 heaven-strides, per doc 36 §4.1).

**Qi-climate.** The qi here is *thinning*. The Border Twilight's qi is the wilds' undifferentiated qi in the process of becoming the reaches' pending substrate — the qi, in the Twilight, is losing its coherence as the substrate-law thins, and the qi's texture is, for a Tribulation Crossing cultivator, the perception of *fading*. The perception is, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the most unsettling perception the wilds offer — the perception of the substrate itself ceasing to hold, of the cosmos's law thinning into nothing. The perception is, for cultivators below Mahayana, dangerous — a Spirit Severance cultivator who lingers in the Border Twilight finds their anchor-coherence diminishing, the way a candle's flame diminishes in a draft. Mahayana cultivators do not experience the danger (their anchor-coherence is too strong for the Twilight's thinning to affect), but they perceive the thinning clearly, and the perception is, per You Wuya's account (doc 39 §2.3), the perception of the cosmos's finitude most directly. You Wuya walks the Border Twilight often; the walks are, per You Wuya's account, the closest a being can come to perceiving the cosmos's edge without crossing it.

**What lives there.** Sparse, specialized. The **Unborn Beasts** (未生獸, §2.8), beings that exist as potentiality rather than actuality, that are the Border Twilight's only native fauna. A sparse population of wild plants — primarily the **Thinning-Roots** (薄根), plants whose roots have evolved to seek the thinning substrate, that grow at the Border Twilight's outer edge and that are, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the cosmos's most tenacious plants. The Border Twilight is, otherwise, a transitional zone — the wilds' ecology gives way to the reaches' emptiness, and the Twilight is the gradient between them. The Border Twilight is also where wild beings sometimes wander into the reaches, by accident or by intention; the wanderers either disperse (most do) or transform (a few do, becoming the reach-native beings mentioned in doc 40 §5.3, like the Tide-Singer of the Mirror Tides).

## 2. The wilds' ecologies — the alien beasts and plants

The wilds' ecology does not follow the five-phase model (per doc 15 §4.3). The five-phase model is an Acquired-stratum artifact (Yin Mu's five-phase differentiation law, doc 39 §2.5), describing how qi differentiates in the lower world. In the wilds, qi is undifferentiated, and beings native to the wilds have metabolisms that operate on undifferentiated qi directly. A wild beast does not have a phase-affinity. It is a being whose internal architecture is organized around a pattern that does not map onto the five phases at all. This section names ten.

### 2.1 The Substrate-Drinker (飲基獸)

A beast native to the Northern Wilds' interior. The Substrate-Drinker is, in form, a low-slung quadruped roughly the size of a mortal bull, with a body composed of equal parts flesh and substrate — the beast's skin is, in patches, actual substrate, the law-residue of the Primordial Authors' inscription made physical. The Substrate-Drinker's metabolism is the wildest of the wilds' metabolisms: the beast drinks the substrate itself. Not the qi (the substrate's medium) but the substrate (the medium's structure). The drinking is, per the Stone-Singing Court's four-thousand-year observation, a slow consumption — a single Substrate-Drinker consumes approximately one cubic li of substrate over its natural lifespan (~3,000 years), leaving a slow-deepening hollow in the wilds' substrate where it has fed. The Substrate-Drinker is, in this sense, the wilds' slow geographic feature — the hollows the Stone-Singing Court's cartographers have mapped are the Substrate-Drinkers' feeding-traces, accumulated across millennia. The beast is not hunted (the Stone-Singing Court treats it as kin); the beast is, however, occasionally observed by Tribulation Crossing cultivators seeking to understand the substrate's nature, since the Substrate-Drinker is the only known being that consumes substrate directly and that can be observed doing so.

### 2.2 The Phase-Eater (食相獸)

A beast native to the Border Twilight, where the wilds meet the reaches. The Phase-Eater is, in form, a long serpentine being, roughly the length of a mortal river, with a body that is perpetually *almost* taking shape and failing — the beast's body is, at any moment, in the process of differentiating into five-phase qi and then un-differentiating back into undifferentiated qi, in a continuous oscillation. The Phase-Eater's metabolism is the consumption of differentiated qi — the beast feeds on the phase-signatures of Acquired-stratum cultivators who enter the wilds. A Tribulation Crossing cultivator attacked by a Phase-Eater finds their cultivated five-phase qi being drawn out, phase by phase, the differentiation reversed, the qi returned to the undifferentiated state and then absorbed by the beast. The attack is, for the cultivator, lethal if not resisted — a Tribulation Crossing cultivator can resist (with effort), but a Spirit Severance cultivator cannot. The Phase-Eater is, in this sense, the wilds' defense against Acquired-stratum intrusion — the beast does not distinguish between cultivators; the beast consumes any differentiated qi it encounters. The Phase-Eater is hunted, occasionally, by Tribulation Crossing cultivators seeking the beast's core (which, per the Azure Sword Sect's Scripture Hall, is the only known material that can be used to inscribe a five-phase-resonance talisman at the Tribulation Crossing tier).

### 2.3 The Three-Mouthed River (三口川)

Not a beast, not a plant — a being that is also a place. The Three-Mouthed River is a river in the Southern Wilds' interior, roughly 800 li long, that is itself a being. The river's water is the being's body; the river's flow is the being's circulation; the river's three mouths (where the river branches into three distributaries at its delta) are the being's three organs of perception and speech. The Three-Mouthed River is, per the Serpentine Court's account, a wild-being of the second order — a being that has developed an anchor (the river's source, where the substrate's law-residue has cohered into a self-inscribed law-pattern) without ever having been a beast. The Three-Mouthed River is, in this sense, the wilds' proof that anchors can develop in non-animal substrates — that the wilds' ecology can produce persons without producing animals. The Three-Mouthed River speaks, in qi-modulations, through its three mouths; the Serpentine Court has been negotiating with the river for two thousand years, seeking the river's permission for the Court's cultivators to travel the river's length. The river has granted permission three times in two thousand years; the river has refused permission seventeen times. The river's refusals are not explained; the river's grants are not conditional. The river is, per the Serpentine Court's account, the most enigmatic being in the wilds.

### 2.4 The Mirror-Herd (鏡群)

The Sea of Reeds' dominant beast (§1.5). The Mirror-Herd is a herd-animal, roughly the size of a mortal deer, with a body that has evolved to match the reeds' woven qi — the beast's skin is, in texture, the same woven-qi-fabric as the reeds' surroundings, and the beast is, in the reeds, nearly invisible. The Mirror-Herd's name comes from its defense-mechanism: when attacked, the herd's members reflect the attacker's qi back at them, amplified. A Tribulation Crossing cultivator who attacks a Mirror-Herd member finds their own attack returned, at twice the power; the cultivator who does not cease the attack is, eventually, killed by their own returned power. The Mirror-Herd is, in this sense, the wilds' most effective defense against predation — the herd has no natural predators, and the herd's population is limited only by the Sea of Reeds' carrying capacity. The Mirror-Herd is, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the wilds' most studied beast — the herd's reflection-mechanism is, per Tu Nan's modeling, the only known natural mechanism that can amplify qi without inscription, and the mechanism is the subject of ongoing research by several Tribulation Crossing cultivators (the Azure Sword Sect's current Scripture Hall master has, per the sect's records, been studying the Mirror-Herd for two hundred years).

### 2.5 The Walking-Root (行根)

A plant native to the Eastern Wilds' lower altitudes. The Walking-Root is, in form, a small tree (roughly mortal-chest-height) whose roots have evolved to walk — the roots detach from the substrate, reach forward, re-attach, and the tree moves. The walking is slow (a single Walking-Root covers approximately one li per Precelestial year), but the walking is continuous and directional — the Walking-Roots migrate, in patterns the Feathered Court has been mapping for three thousand years, toward the Eastern Wilds' lift-current's source (the place where the lift originates, which no cultivator has reached). The Feathered Court's cartographers believe the Walking-Roots' migration is the plants' cultivation — the plants are walking toward the qi-source the way a cultivator walks toward breakthrough. The Walking-Root is, in this sense, the wilds' most obvious example of plant-cultivation — the plant is, demonstrably, practicing. The Walking-Root's wood, harvested (with the Feathered Court's permission), is the second-lightest wood in the cosmos (after the Cloud-Root Pine's), and is sought by Acquired-stratum cultivators for talisman-construction.

### 2.6 The Windless Pine (無風松) and the Echo-Tree (回音樹)

Two trees, paired here for their parallel cultivation-strategies. The **Windless Pine** (無風松) is native to the Western Wilds (§1.4); the tree has evolved to grow without any qi-motion in its branches, and the tree's needles cultivate by their absolute non-motion. A Windless Pine, in stillness, accumulates qi-authority without circulation — the stillness itself is the cultivation, the way a mortal monk's meditation is the cultivation. The Windless Pine is, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the wilds' slowest cultivator — a single tree, in three thousand years of stillness, accumulates qi-authority equivalent to a mortal Qi-Condensation cultivator's lifetime accumulation. The tree's wood, harvested (rarely; the Western Wilds are sparse), is the cosmos's most still substance, and is sought by Acquired-stratum cultivators for meditation-aids.

The **Echo-Tree** (回音樹) is native to the Northern Wilds (§1.2); the tree has evolved to echo the Northern Wilds' resonant qi, amplifying the substrate's humming-tone in the tree's immediate vicinity. The Echo-Tree's echo is not passive — the tree modulates the echo, in patterns the Stone-Singing Court has been mapping for four thousand years, and the patterns are, per the Court's account, the tree's speech. The Echo-Trees are, in this sense, the Northern Wilds' second civilization — not a civilization of persons (the trees do not have anchors) but a civilization of cultivators, practicing their echo-modulation across millennia. The Stone-Singing Court treats the Echo-Trees as colleagues; the Court's cultivators and the Echo-Trees exchange qi-modulations, in a tradition that predates the Court's founding.

### 2.7 The Grief-Eater (食哀獸)

A beast native to all the wilds' regions, but most common in the Border Twilight. The Grief-Eater is, in form, a small quadruped (roughly mortal-cat-sized), with a body that is, at any moment, partially transparent — the beast's coherence is unstable, the way a Mirage is unstable, and the beast is, in poor qi-conditions, nearly invisible. The Grief-Eater's metabolism is the consumption of grief — the beast feeds on the grief of cultivators who enter the wilds to die (the third reason cultivators go to the wilds, per doc 15 §4.2). The beast is, in this sense, the wilds' undertaker — the beast finds the dying cultivator, sits with them (the beast is, per the accounts of cultivators who have survived a Grief-Eater encounter, gentle, in the way a hospice-worker is gentle), and, when the cultivator disperses, consumes the grief that the dispersing anchor leaves in the substrate. The Grief-Eater is, per Bei Huai's account (doc 39 §2.8, who has observed the beast in the wilds' edges while attending mortal dispersals in the Acquired stratum's correspondence), the wilds' kindest being. The beast is not hunted. The beast is, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the wilds' most precious being — the beast's grief-consumption is the only known mechanism that cleanses the wilds' substrate of accumulated cultivator-grief, and without the beast, the wilds' substrate would be, by now, saturated with eight thousand years of dying cultivators' grief, and the wilds would be uninhabitable.

### 2.8 The Unborn Beast (未生獸)

A being native to the Border Twilight. The Unborn Beast is, in form, *not* — the beast exists as potentiality rather than actuality. The beast is perceived, by Tribulation Crossing cultivators, as a kind of *almost* — a shape that almost coheres, a presence that almost materializes, a being that almost is. The Unborn Beast's ontology is, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the wilds' deepest mystery — the beast is, by every measurement the pantheon has applied, a being (it has internal architecture, it has preferences, it moves, it responds to perception), but the beast has no anchor, no body, no qi-circulation. The beast is, in the pantheon's best theory, a being that is in the process of being born — a being that the wilds' undifferentiated qi is producing, but that has not yet completed the production. The production has been ongoing, per Tu Nan's mapping, for at least four thousand years (the Unborn Beasts' traces are detectable in the Border Twilight's substrate-residue, and the traces' age is approximately four thousand years). The Unborn Beasts (there are several, in the Border Twilight's extent) are, per the modeling, approximately halfway through their gestation. The gestation's completion, if it occurs, will produce a being of unknown nature — the pantheon does not know what the Unborn Beasts will be when they are born. The modeling does not predict. The pantheon waits.

### 2.9 The Substrate-Coral (基珊瑚)

A colony-being native to the Sea of Reeds (§1.5). The Substrate-Coral is, in form, a small sessile being (roughly mortal-fist-sized per individual), but the Substrate-Coral lives only in colonies — a single Substrate-Coral is, per the pantheon's collective assessment, not a being but a fragment, and the colony is the being. The colony's collective metabolism builds structure from the Sea of Reeds' woven qi's law-residue — the colony secretes, over centuries, a kind of coral-substance that is, in texture, halfway between substrate and stone. The coral-substance is, per the Azure Sword Sect's Scripture Hall's analysis, the only known naturally-occurring substance that is partly substrate and partly matter, and the substance is sought by Acquired-stratum cultivators for formation-construction (the substance can be used, in principle, to inscribe formations that operate on the substrate's law-residue directly, bypassing the five-phase model). The Substrate-Coral is, in this sense, the wilds' most commercially valuable being — the Sea of Reeds' Substrate-Coral colonies are, per the Feathered Court's trade records, the wilds' most traded commodity, and the trade has produced, over the millennia, a small but persistent population of wild-path merchants who specialize in Substrate-Coral (§5). The Substrate-Coral is harvested (carefully, with the Sea of Reeds' Mirror-Herd's permission, which is granted through a qi-modulated negotiation that the wild-path merchants have been learning for centuries); the harvest is sustainable (the colonies regrow, slowly, in the harvested sites).

## 3. Lost inheritances — the wilds' ruins

The wilds contain the ruins of cultivators, lineages, and civilizations that did not survive (per doc 15 §4.2). An inheritance — a practice, a treasure, a fragment of a law — may persist in the wilds for millennia after its author's death, sustained by the wilds' undifferentiated qi. This section names five.

### 3.1 The Pillar Cities (柱城群)

**Who left it.** The Pillar Dynasty (柱朝, doc 37 §6.1), a yao civilization of wood-phase yao that flourished in the southern continent's central highlands from Year ~22,000 to Year ~30,000 — eight thousand years of civilization, ending eight thousand years before the Sundering. The dynasty's last Mahayana, the Pillar Lord of the Last Sap (末汁柱, §3.5), dispersed himself voluntarily when the dynasty's spirit vein (the Pillar Root Vein, 柱根脈) was exhausted.

**What's inside.** Stone cities (the yao's wood-phase qi grew stone the way a tree grows wood, in forms they designed), partially overgrown, partially intact. Bark-strip libraries (the dynasty's written script), preserved by the southern continent's central highlands' dry air. A small population of descendants of the dynasty's yao — wood-phase yao beasts, mostly Coiling-Root Serpents' prey, that have persisted in the ruins' vicinity for the eight thousand years since the dynasty's fall. The Azure Sword Sect's Scripture Hall holds a single bark-strip text traded from a rogue cultivator, untranslated, attributed to "the southern mountains" (per doc 37 §6.1); the text is a fragment of the dynasty's cultivation manual, and is the only known Pillar Dynasty text in the Acquired stratum.

**How to find it.** The ruins are in the Southern Wilds' central highlands (§1.3), at the horizontal coordinate corresponding to the southern continent's central highlands. The journey from the Courts of Heaven to the Pillar Cities takes, for a Tribulation Crossing cultivator, approximately three Precelestial months (= thirty Acquired years). The journey is, by pantheon standards, straightforward — the maintained roads extend partway, the wild-paths are well-mapped, and the Southern Wilds' qi-climate (the rooted downward flow) is survivable for Tribulation Crossing cultivators without special preparation. The ruins' interior is, however, defended — the Serpentine Court considers the Pillar Cities part of the Court's territory (the Court's yao are the dynasty's descendants, by the Court's account), and the Court requires negotiation (or, rarely, combat) before allowing cultivators to enter the ruins' libraries.

**Risks.** The Coiling-Root Serpents (§1.3), which defend the ruins as their territory and which are, at peak tier, dangerous even to Tribulation Crossing cultivators. The Serpentine Court's negotiators, who drive hard bargains (the Court's price for library-access is typically a service — a wild-beast hunt, a Survey-report, a qi-residue cataloging — that the cultivator must perform for the Court before access is granted). The ruins' interior itself, which has, in eight thousand years, accumulated its own ecology (wild plants growing through the stone, wild beasts nesting in the libraries) and which is, in places, structurally unsound. The Azure Sword Sect's failed Pillar Cities Survey (§5) is the most recent documented expedition; the Survey's survivors brought back the bark-strip text that is now in the Scripture Hall, and the Survey's dead (three Spirit Severance cultivators, killed by a Coiling-Root Serpent) are memorialized in the Azure Sword Sect's records.

### 3.2 The Crimson Vow Grotto-Heaven's collapsed anchor (赤誓洞天之崩錨)

**Who left it.** The Crimson Vow Alliance (赤誓盟, doc 37 §6.2), an alliance of Core Formation+ sects and lineages that flourished in the eastern continent's central lowlands from Year ~41,000 to Year ~43,000. The alliance's founding Mahayana, Daojun Chiri (赤日道君, the Lord of the Red Sun), dispersed in a law-tribulation ~5,000 years ago; the alliance's member-sects went to war; the grotto-heaven's anchor was destroyed; the grotto was sealed (its 8,000 cultivators trapped inside, presumed dispersed).

**What's inside.** The collapsed anchor — a cave on the eastern continent's shore (in the Mortal Stratum) that once led to the Crimson Vow City (in the Acquired Stratum), now a qi-residue site. The collapse did not, however, fully unwrite the grotto-heaven's interior; the grotto's interior persists, in the Precelestial's Eastern Wilds (at the horizontal coordinate corresponding to the eastern continent's shore), as a kind of half-collapsed pocket — the grotto's space is partially intact, partially unwritten, in a state not unlike the Reach of the Cleaving's ruins (doc 40 §2.4) but smaller and less dangerous. The interior contains the Crimson Vow City's ruins (a fire-phase grotto-heaven city, built for 8,000 cultivators, now empty), the Crimson Vow Code (the alliance's legal code, in fragmentary form, inscribed in the grotto's substrate), and the trapped cultivators' residue (the 8,000 cultivators' anchors, dispersed into the grotto's substrate, persisting as a kind of grief-residue that the wilds' Grief-Eaters (§2.7) have been consuming for five thousand years). The Crimson Vow Remnant (the alliance's surviving demonic cultivators, per doc 37 §6.2) seeks the grotto's interior, periodically, for inheritance; the Remnant's expeditions have, in the last millennium, all failed (the grotto's half-collapsed substrate is lethal to cultivators below Spirit Severance, and the Remnant's cultivators are mostly Core Formation).

**How to find it.** The grotto's interior is in the Eastern Wilds' southern edge (§1.1), at the horizontal coordinate corresponding to the eastern continent's shore. The Mortal-Stratum cave (the collapsed anchor) is the entry-point, but the cave is, in the Mortal Stratum, a qi-residue site that does not lead anywhere — the grotto's interior is accessible only through the Precelestial, by a Tribulation Crossing cultivator who travels to the Eastern Wilds' southern edge and perceives the grotto's half-collapsed pocket. The perception is, per the Azure Sword Sect's Scripture Hall's records, difficult — the pocket's half-collapsed state makes it hard to distinguish from the surrounding wild-substrate, and a Tribulation Crossing cultivator without specific preparation (the Azure Sword Sect's Scripture Hall holds a fragmentary description of the pocket's qi-signature, from the sect's last Survey) will pass by without perceiving it.

**Risks.** The grotto's half-collapsed substrate, which is lethal to cultivators below Spirit Severance and dangerous to Spirit Severance cultivators who linger. The grief-residue (the 8,000 cultivators' dispersed anchors), which produces, in cultivators who enter the grotto's interior, a perception of overwhelming grief — the perception is not harmful in itself, but the perception can, in cultivators with their own unresolved grief (most cultivators), trigger a heart-demon tribulation (doc 15 §6.3 form 2) without the cultivator's preparation or consent. The Crimson Vow Remnant, which guards the grotto's Mortal-Stratum entry-point and which attacks cultivators who approach (the Remnant's cultivators are below the threshold to enter the grotto themselves, but they prevent others from entering, in the hope that the Remnant will eventually produce a Spirit Severance cultivator capable of the entry). The protagonist, in the Transcend path, may choose to enter the grotto's interior as part of their pre-Mahayana cultivation; the choice is optional, and the inheritance (the Crimson Vow Code's fragments, the grief-residue's perception) is, per the protagonist's account (if undertaken), a difficult but instructive experience.

### 3.3 The Silent Hexagram Grotto-Heaven (默卦洞天)

**Who left it.** The Silent Hexagram School (默卦宗, doc 37 §6.3), a divination lineage that flourished in the central continent's northern hills from Year ~43,000 to Year ~45,500. The school's founder, Daoist Silent Hexagram (默卦真人), persisted as an ancestor for 2,500 years and corrupted (per doc 15 §3.4); the school's last Core Formation cultivator, Jing Wuyin (靜無音), severed the lineage rite's connection to the ancestral court and dispersed herself fifty years later, sealing the corrupted ancestor within the court.

**What's inside.** The grotto-heaven's interior, sealed by Jing Wuyin's severing, presumed intact. The school's library (the Silent Hexagram Method's full text, in 36 volumes, of which the Cangli Riverlands' Wang Lun tradition holds a single corrupted fragment, per doc 37 §6.3). The school's divination treasures (hexagram-stones, divination-rod sets, fortune-telling matrices, all presumed to be in the library). The corrupted ancestor (Daoist Silent Hexagram, sealed within the ancestral court's central hall, per doc 37 §7.3 — the Register of Sealed Evils estimates the seal's integrity at approximately two thousand years remaining).

**How to find it.** The grotto's interior is in the Northern Wilds' southern hills (§1.2), at the horizontal coordinate corresponding to the central continent's northern hills. The grotto's anchor (in the Mortal Stratum) was destroyed by Jing Wuyin; the grotto's interior is accessible only through the Precelestial, by a Tribulation Crossing cultivator who travels to the Northern Wilds' southern hills and perceives the grotto's sealed pocket. The Stone-Singing Court (§4.2) claims the grotto's territory as part of the Court's hunting-grounds; the Court requires negotiation before allowing cultivators to approach the grotto. The Court's negotiators are, per the pantheon's collective assessment, more accommodating than the Serpentine Court's — the Stone-Singing Court does not value the grotto's inheritance (the Court's lithic yao do not practice divination) and is willing to grant access in exchange for a nominal service.

**Risks.** The corrupted ancestor (Daoist Silent Hexagram), whose seal is, per the Register's estimate, eroding. The seal's erosion is, per the Stone-Singing Court's monitoring (the Court has been observing the grotto's seal for two thousand years, as the grotto is in the Court's territory), approximately on-schedule — the seal has approximately two thousand years of integrity remaining, at the current Thinning-rate. The seal's breach, if it occurs, would release the corrupted ancestor, whose divination-law has inverted (the ancestor perceives all possible futures and seeks to collapse them into the one in which the ancestor persists, per doc 37 §7.3). The protagonist, in the divination-path endgame (if the protagonist pursues divination, which is a content-design option, per doc 37 §7.3), may interact with the corrupted ancestor through the Wang Lun tradition's fragment; the interaction is, per the protagonist's account (if undertaken), dangerous and instructive. The grotto's interior, beyond the corrupted ancestor's seal, is otherwise safe — the library is intact, the divination treasures are intact, and the grotto's substrate is stable (the Stone-Singing Court's monitoring confirms the grotto's substrate has not thinned measurably in two thousand years).

### 3.4 The Last Camp of Daojun Wuwang (無妄道君之末營)

**Who left it.** Daojun Wuwang (無妄道君, the Lord of the Unconditioned, per doc 37 §3.2), one of the primordial Mahayana cultivators — a Mahayana of the pre-courts era, who flourished in the Primordial Substrate Epoch (Year ~10,000 to ~30,000) and dispersed (or ascended; the records are unclear) before the Sundering. Daojun Wuwang's Dao is, per the fragmentary records Gu Wu preserved (doc 37 §3.2), the Dao of the Unconditioned — the philosophical position that the substrate's law-residue is the cosmos's truest expression, and that cultivation is the practice of perceiving the substrate's un-conditioned nature beneath the conditioned law. The Dao is, in the pantheon's collective assessment, the Dao most opposed to Mo Shen's (doc 39 §2.7) — where Mo Shen's restraint is the choice not to inscribe, Wuwang's practice was the choice to perceive what is already inscribed, beneath the inscription.

**What's inside.** A small camp — a single pavilion, a meditation-seat, a small library of bark-strip texts (the camp's preservation, in the Western Wilds' still qi, is exceptional — the texts are, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the best-preserved pre-Sundering texts in the cosmos). The texts are Wuwang's contemplative notes — not a cultivation manual (Wuwang did not write manuals) but a record of Wuwang's perceptions of the substrate's un-conditioned nature, across three thousand years of meditation. The notes are, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the most demanding text in the cosmos to read — the notes assume the reader can perceive the substrate's un-conditioned nature, and most readers (even Mahayana cultivators) cannot. The notes are, per Mo Shen's account (Mo Shen is the only pantheon member who has read the full library, in a visit eight thousand years ago), the text Mo Shen returns to, in contemplation, when the pantheon's deadlock (doc 39 §6) weighs on Mo Shen most heavily.

**How to find it.** The camp is in the Western Wilds' interior (§1.4), at a horizontal coordinate that does not correspond to any Mortal-Stratum feature (the camp is, per Tu Nan's mapping, in a region of the Western Wilds that has no Mortal-Stratum correspondence — the region's substrate is, per the modeling, slightly older than the Mortal Stratum, in some sense that the modeling does not specify). The journey from the Courts of Heaven to the camp takes, for a Tribulation Crossing cultivator, approximately six Precelestial months (= sixty Acquired years). The journey is difficult — the Western Wilds' still qi is uncomfortable for cultivators, and the camp's specific location is not marked on Tu Nan's map (Mo Shen, who provided the location to Tu Nan, asked that the location not be recorded; Tu Nan has honored the request, recording only that the camp exists). A cultivator seeking the camp must, per Mo Shen's account, find it by perception — by attuning to the Western Wilds' still qi and perceiving, in the stillness, the camp's faint qi-signature (the signature is Wuwang's residue, sustained by the still qi for ten thousand years).

**Risks.** The Western Wilds' still qi (§1.4), which is uncomfortable and which can, in cultivators who linger, induce a stillness-trance that is difficult to break. The Stillness-Beasts (§1.4), which are mostly harmless but which can, if startled, defend themselves with a stillness-attack that paralyzes the cultivator's qi-circulation. The notes themselves, which are not dangerous in the way Lie Fa's partial law-inscription is dangerous (the notes do not induce recursion) but which are dangerous in a different way — the notes' assumptions about the reader's perception can, in a reader who attempts to perceive the substrate's un-conditioned nature without adequate preparation, induce a perception-overload that disperses the reader's anchor. The notes have, in the cosmos's history, dispersed three Mahayana cultivators (per the pantheon's records); the most recent was four thousand years ago. The notes are, for this reason, locked behind a seal Mo Shen maintains; the seal is the only law Mo Shen has authored in nine thousand years of restraint, and the seal is, per Mo Shen's account, the seal Mo Shen most regrets needing to author.

### 3.5 The Forest of the Last Sap (末汁之林)

**Who left it.** The Pillar Lord of the Last Sap (末汁柱), the Pillar Dynasty's last Mahayana (per doc 37 §6.1), who dispersed himself voluntarily in Year ~30,000 when the dynasty's spirit vein was exhausted. The Pillar Lord's dispersal was, per the Serpentine Court's account, the cosmos's first recorded voluntary Mahayana-dispersal — a Mahayana cultivator choosing to disperse, not because the Origin's pull had become irresistible (the Pillar Lord was, per the Serpentine Court's account, only four thousand years into his Mahayana span — young, by Mahayana standards), but because the Pillar Lord concluded that the dynasty could not be saved and that his persistence would only prolong the suffering.

**What's inside.** A grove of trees (descendants of the Pillar Dynasty's cultivated trees, growing in the Southern Wilds' downward qi-flow, still producing the wood-phase qi that sustained the dynasty) at the dispersal-site. The Pillar Lord's residue (the dispersing Mahayana's anchor-residue, sustained by the grove's wood-phase qi, persisting as a kind of grief-marker in the grove's substrate). A single inscription, in the grove's center, in the Pillar Lord's qi-residue: a record of the Pillar Lord's reasoning, written in the moment of dispersal, in the bark-strip script. The inscription is, per the Serpentine Court's account, the cosmos's most moving text — the voluntary Mahayana's last words, written in the act of ceasing. The inscription's content is, per the Court's translation, the Pillar Lord's farewell to the dynasty and the Pillar Lord's instruction to any who find the grove: *the finite is the only thing that can be loved; I loved the dynasty; the dynasty is finite; I let it go.*

**How to find it.** The grove is in the Southern Wilds' far south (§1.3), at the horizontal coordinate corresponding to the southern continent's southern coast. The journey from the Courts of Heaven to the grove takes, for a Tribulation Crossing cultivator, approximately four Precelestial months (= forty Acquired years). The journey is straightforward (the Southern Wilds' qi-climate is survivable); the grove's location is marked on Tu Nan's map (the Serpentine Court, unlike Mo Shen, has no objection to the grove's location being known); the grove is, however, defended by the Serpentine Court's kin (the Coiling-Root Serpents, who consider the grove sacred) and by the grove's own wood-phase qi (which produces, in cultivators who enter the grove without the Court's permission, a wood-phase-resonance that can, in cultivators without wood-phase affinity, induce meridian-dissonance).

**Risks.** The Coiling-Root Serpents (defending the grove as sacred). The wood-phase-resonance (inducing meridian-dissonance in non-wood-phase cultivators). The inscription itself, which is not dangerous in the recursion-sense (the inscription does not induce recursion) but which is, per the Serpentine Court's account, dangerous in the grief-sense — the inscription's content, read by a cultivator with their own unresolved grief, can induce a grief-cascade that, in severe cases, has dispersed cultivators (the Serpentine Court records two such dispersals in the last two thousand years). The inscription is, for this reason, approached by cultivators only with the Court's accompaniment (the Court's cultivators serve as grief-attenuators, modulating the inscription's grief-residue for the visitor). The protagonist, in the Transcend path, may visit the grove as part of their pre-Mahayana cultivation; the visit is, per the protagonist's account (if undertaken), the second-most-affecting experience of the protagonist's cultivation (after Wang Xiaodi's dispersal). The inscription's instruction — *the finite is the only thing that can be loved* — is the instruction the protagonist carries into the Transcend ending's authorship (per doc 26 §7.2, doc 40 §3.2). The instruction is, in the protagonist's account, the instruction Lie Fa also gave (per doc 40 §4.2), in different words: *author small*. The two instructions, the protagonist perceives, are the same instruction.

## 4. The wilds' civilizations

The wilds have civilizations — not human civilizations (no human civilization has ever persisted in the wilds; the wilds' qi-climate is, for humans below Tribulation Crossing, ultimately lethal, and the wilds do not recognize the courts' jurisdiction that human civilizations depend on), but yao civilizations and other forms of organized being. This section names three.

### 4.1 The Feathered Court of the Eastern Wilds (東荒羽庭)

The largest yao civilization in the wilds. The Court is, per the Foreign Affairs department's records (doc 38 §7.3), a confederation of avian yao — primarily crane-yao, eagle-yao, hawk-yao, and heron-yao — that has persisted in the Eastern Wilds for approximately six thousand years. The Court's population is, per the Foreign Affairs department's estimate, approximately 4,000 yao (the yao are long-lived but sparse; the Eastern Wilds' lift-current supports a limited carrying capacity). The Court's government is a council of the oldest yao (the Council of the Eleven Feathers, 十一羽之議), which convenes once per Precelestial year at the Court's central roost (a vast Cloud-Root Pine grove at the Eastern Wilds' lift-current's mid-altitude). The Court's cultivation tradition is flight-cultivation (the yao cultivate by riding the lift-current, per §1.1), and the Court's cultivators are, by Foreign Affairs department assessment, roughly equivalent to Acquired-stratum Nascent Soul cultivators in raw power (with significant advantages in flight and significant disadvantages in grounded combat). The Court's relationship with the courts is, per doc 38 §7.3, sovereign-to-sovereign — the Foreign Affairs department maintains a permanent embassy at the Court's central roost, staffed by two Tribulation Crossing cultivators on rotating assignment. The embassy is, per the Foreign Affairs department's records, the cosmos's longest-running continuous diplomatic mission (4,000 years, since the Courts' Third Expansion).

### 4.2 The Stone-Singing Court of the Northern Wilds (北荒石詠庭)

The second-largest yao civilization. The Court is, per the Foreign Affairs department's records, a confederation of lithic yao — primarily the Resonance-Beasts (§1.2) that have developed anchors — that has persisted in the Northern Wilds for approximately five thousand years. The Court's population is, per the Foreign Affairs department's estimate, approximately 1,500 yao (the lithic yao are sparser than the avian yao; the Northern Wilds' resonant qi supports a smaller carrying capacity). The Court's government is a council of the oldest yao (the Council of the Seven Tones, 七音之議), which convenes by qi-modulated resonance (the Council's members do not meet physically; they communicate by striking their own bodies and producing the substrate's humming-tone, in patterns that carry meaning). The Court's cultivation tradition is resonance-cultivation (the yao cultivate by attuning to the substrate's humming-tone, per §1.2), and the Court's cultivators are, by Foreign Affairs department assessment, roughly equivalent to Acquired-stratum Spirit Severance cultivators (the lithic yao's slower metabolisms produce fewer but more powerful cultivators). The Court's relationship with the courts is sovereign-to-sovereign; the Foreign Affairs department maintains an embassy at the Court's central resonance-site, staffed by one Tribulation Crossing cultivator on rotating assignment. The embassy is, per the Foreign Affairs department's records, the cosmos's most isolating diplomatic mission (the resonance-communication requires years of attunement to learn; the embassy's staff typically serve ten-year assignments and emerge with permanent qi-perception alterations).

### 4.3 The Serpentine Court of the Southern Wilds (南荒蛇庭)

The third yao civilization. The Court is, per the Foreign Affairs department's records, a confederation of serpentine yao — primarily the Coiling-Root Serpents (§1.3) that have developed anchors — that has persisted in the Southern Wilds for approximately four thousand years. The Court's population is, per the Foreign Affairs department's estimate, approximately 2,500 yao (the serpentine yao are between the avian and lithic yao in population density). The Court's government is a matriarchy — the Court's oldest female yao (the Matriarch of the Coiling, 盤母) rules absolutely, with a council of younger female yao as advisors. The Court's cultivation tradition is root-cultivation (the yao cultivate by sinking roots into the Southern Wilds' downward qi-flow, per §1.3, drawing sustenance from the qi as it descends), and the Court's cultivators are, by Foreign Affairs department assessment, roughly equivalent to Acquired-stratum Core Formation cultivators (the serpentine yao's shorter lifespans produce fewer advanced cultivators). The Court's relationship with the courts is sovereign-to-sovereign; the Foreign Affairs department maintains an embassy at the Court's central coil (a vast Coiling-Root Serpent nest at the Pillar Cities' edge), staffed by two Tribulation Crossing cultivators on rotating assignment. The embassy is, per the Foreign Affairs department's records, the cosmos's most contentious diplomatic mission (the Serpentine Court's matriarchy drives hard bargains; the embassy's staff typically return from assignment exhausted and with significant karmic-trace accrual from the Court's demands).

### 4.4 Why there are no human civilizations in the wilds

The wilds' qi-climate is, for humans below Tribulation Crossing, ultimately lethal — not immediately (a Spirit Severance cultivator can survive in the Sea of Reeds indefinitely, per §1.5), but on the timescales a civilization requires. A human civilization requires agriculture (the wilds' plants do not follow the five-phase model and are not cultivatable by human techniques), infrastructure (the wilds' substrate does not hold inscriptions the way the Acquired stratum does, and human infrastructure depends on inscription), and reproduction (humans in the wilds' qi-climate experience fertility rates that, per the Foreign Affairs department's records, drop below replacement within three generations). The wilds' few human settlements — a small population of wild-path merchants (§5) in the Sea of Reeds' edge, a small population of exiles (§6) in the wilds' border zones — are not civilizations; they are outposts, sustained by trade with the Acquired stratum or by the cultivators' personal power, and they do not persist beyond the cultivators' lifespans. The wilds are, in this sense, the yao's territory — not by claim but by adaptation.

## 5. Who goes to the wilds, and why

The wilds are visited, in the cosmos's history, by cultivators for the three reasons specified in doc 15 §4.2 (lost inheritances, ancient beasts, death) and a fourth reason the pantheon does not officially acknowledge (smuggling, §6). This section names the visitors.

### 5.1 The Tribulation Crossing cultivators

Tribulation Crossing cultivators are the wilds' primary human visitors, and the wilds are, per doc 15 §4.2, one of the few sources of tribulation-preparation materials (wild beast cores, wild plant parts) that cannot be acquired through the courts. A Tribulation Crossing cultivator preparing for the Heavenly Stem crossing (doc 15 §8.1) requires, in addition to the standard cultivation-preparation, a wild beast core of appropriate power — typically a Mirror-Herd core (for the Sea of Reeds' cultivators), a Resonance-Beast core (for the Northern Wilds' cultivators), or a Coiling-Root Serpent core (for the Southern Wilds' cultivators). The wild beast core's purpose is, per the Azure Sword Sect's Scripture Hall's analysis, to acclimate the cultivator's anchor to undifferentiated qi before the Stem's test — the wild core's residue, integrated into the cultivator's system, provides a small but significant resistance to the Stem's undifferentiated qi.

**Named expeditions.** The Azure Sword Sect's **Pillar Cities Survey** (§3.1), led by Sect-Master Fang Tianyuan (方天遠) two hundred years ago, was a Spirit Severance expedition to the Pillar Cities; the Survey's survivors brought back the bark-strip text that is now in the Scripture Hall, and the Survey's dead (three Spirit Severance cultivators, killed by a Coiling-Root Serpent) are memorialized in the Azure Sword Sect's records. The Azure Sword Sect's **Silent Hexagram Survey**, led by Scripture Hall Master Lin Wuyu (林無語) one hundred and fifty years ago, was a Nascent Soul expedition to the Silent Hexagram Grotto-Heaven; the Survey obtained the Stone-Singing Court's permission to approach the grotto and confirmed the grotto's seal's integrity. The Azure Sword Sect's **Crimson Vow Survey**, led by Azure Sword Master Pei Liang's predecessor (Pei Liang's master, **Guan Tianci** (關天賜), who held the Azure Sword Master's seat before Pei Liang's betrayal and whose cultivation was Spirit Severance), was a Spirit Severance expedition to the Crimson Vow Grotto-Heaven's collapsed anchor; the Survey confirmed the Remnant's presence at the Mortal-Stratum entry-point and did not attempt the grotto's interior.

**Named explorers.** **Shi Kong** (時空, "Time-Space"), the Tribulation Crossing surveyor dispatched by Tu Nan to the Law Reaches (per doc 40 §2.8, §2.9), also surveyed the wilds' border zones; Shi Kong's wilds-survival (the surveyor survived both the Empty Hourglass and the Sundered Breath, per doc 40) is, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the cosmos's most impressive feat of Tribulation Crossing survival. **Leng Wushuang** (冷無雙, "Cold Without Equal", per doc 40 §6.2), the reach-exile, was a wilds-explorer before his flight to the Mirror Tides; Leng Wushuang's wilds-explorations (twelve hundred years ago, before his courts-adjudication) are the Foreign Affairs department's primary source on the Northern Wilds' interior. **Daojun Chiri** (赤日道君), the Crimson Vow Alliance's founding Mahayana (per doc 37 §6.2), was a wilds-explorer before her Mahayana breakthrough; Chiri's wilds-explorations (in the eastern continent's interior, five thousand years ago) are recorded in the Crimson Vow Code's fragments and are, per the Azure Sword Sect's Scripture Hall's analysis, the most detailed pre-Sundering account of the Eastern Wilds' interior.

### 5.2 The Mahayana cultivators

Mahayana cultivators visit the wilds for three reasons: research (Tu Nan's mapping, You Wuya's walking), inheritance-quests (Yin Mu's occasional visits to the Forest of the Last Sap, for grief-attenuation), and politics (the Council of Nine's gatherings, which are held in Mo Shen's Reach but require the pantheon's members to travel through the wilds to reach the Reach).

**You Wuya** (游無涯, the Wanderer, doc 39 §2.3) is the wilds' most constant Mahayana visitor; You Wuya walks the wilds' every region, repeatedly, and is, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the only being (human or yao) who has walked the wilds' entire extent. You Wuya's walking is, per doc 39 §2.3, You Wuya's primary practice; the wilds are the walking's primary venue. You Wuya's accounts of the wilds (recorded by Tu Nan, in the map-room, across six thousand years of visits) are the pantheon's primary source on the wilds' long-term changes — the Eastern Wilds' lift-current's slow weakening (the Thinning's manifestation), the Northern Wilds' humming-tone's slow shift (the substrate's resonance-frequency drifting), the Southern Wilds' downward flow's slow intensification (the Pillar Cities' ruins' slow growth), the Western Wilds' stillness's slow deepening (the Substrate-Corals' slow expansion). The accounts are, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the cosmos's most detailed long-term ecological record.

**Yin Mu** (茵母, the Gardener, doc 39 §2.5) visits the Forest of the Last Sap (§3.5) approximately once per Precelestial century; the visits are, per Yin Mu's account, the gardener's grief-practice — the Forest's inscription (the Pillar Lord's farewell) is, per Yin Mu, the cosmos's truest text on the relationship between finitude and love, and Yin Mu's reading of it (sustained across the visit's Precelestial week) is, per Yin Mu, the practice that sustains Yin Mu's own Dao through the Thinning's grief. Yin Mu's grotto-heaven (doc 39 §2.5) is in the deep Precelestial, near the Southern Wilds' edge; the grotto's forest is, per Yin Mu's design, a smaller echo of the Pillar Dynasty's forests, and Yin Mu's tending of the grotto's forest is, per Yin Mu, the tending of the grief for the larger forest that did not survive.

**Tu Nan** (圖南, the Cartographer, doc 39 §2.6) visits the wilds' every region, repeatedly, for the map's maintenance; Tu Nan's wilds-visits are, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the most systematic survey of the wilds any being has conducted. Tu Nan's map (doc 39 §2.6) records the wilds' regions, the wilds' inheritances, the wilds' civilizations, and the wilds' ecology in detail that exceeds, per the Foreign Affairs department's grudging acknowledgment, the department's own records. The Foreign Affairs department purchases (with the pantheon's permission) updated copies of Tu Nan's wilds-map for the department's embassy staffs; the purchases are, per the department's records, the department's most valuable intelligence acquisition.

**The pantheon's collective Mahayana-wilds-relationship** is, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the wilds' most important political fact. The pantheon does not claim the wilds (the pantheon's Mahayana cultivators author laws, but they do not govern territory in the courts' sense); the pantheon does, however, perceive the wilds as the cosmos's deepest ecological substrate, and the pantheon's collective perception of the wilds' changes (the Thinning's manifestations) is the pantheon's primary input for the deadlock's (doc 39 §6) modeling. The pantheon's deadlocked response to the Thinning is, in this sense, the pantheon's response to the wilds' slow dying — a response the pantheon cannot, by the deadlock's nature, act on.

### 5.3 The wild-path merchants and the wilds' smugglers

The wilds have, in addition to the cultivator-visitors, a small but persistent population of **wild-path merchants** (野路商) — cultivators (mostly Foundation Establishment and Core Formation, occasionally Nascent Soul) who specialize in the wilds' trade. The merchants are organized in small caravans (typically 5-20 cultivators, with a Tribulation Crossing cultivator as escort for the more dangerous routes) and trade primarily in Substrate-Coral (§2.9), Cloud-Root Pine wood (§1.1), Walking-Root wood (§2.5), and wild beast cores (§5.1). The merchants operate from the Sea of Reeds' edge (the wilds' safest region for cultivators below Spirit Severance, per §1.5), where a small permanent settlement (the **Reed-Market**, 葦市, population approximately 200 cultivators) serves as the wilds' only human settlement. The Reed-Market is, per the Foreign Affairs department's records, the wilds' only recognized trade-port; the Market is, per the department's grudging acknowledgment, the department's only successful wilds-intervention (the department negotiated the Market's founding with the Sea of Reeds' Mirror-Herd, two thousand years ago, and the Market has operated continuously since).

The wilds' smugglers (the wilds' fourth, unofficial, reason for visitation, per §5) are cultivators who trade in goods the courts have prohibited — primarily grief-residue (harvested from the Crimson Vow Grotto-Heaven's grief-residue, per §3.2, against the Foreign Affairs department's prohibition), Phase-Eater cores (the Azure Sword Sect's talisman-material, per §2.2, traded to demonic cultivators), and Coiling-Root Serpent scales (the Serpentine Court prohibits the scales' trade; the smugglers trade them anyway, for the scales' use in demonic cultivation). The smugglers are, per the Foreign Affairs department's records, the wilds' most persistent law-enforcement problem — the smugglers operate from hidden camps in the wilds' border zones, the courts' jurisdiction does not extend into the wilds (per §6), and the smugglers' Tribulation Crossing cultivator-escorts make the smugglers difficult for the courts' Defense department patrols to intercept. The protagonist, in Act 2, may encounter the wilds' smugglers through Liang Zhu's underworld-market connections (per doc 26 §9.2, §4.2); the encounters are, per the narrative spine, part of the protagonist's education in the cosmos's gray economy.

## 6. The wilds' relationship to the courts

The courts claim advisory jurisdiction over the wilds (per the Foreign Affairs department's Third Expansion mandate, two thousand years ago, per doc 38 §7.3). The wilds do not recognize the claim. This section specifies what this means in practice.

### 6.1 The advisory jurisdiction's content

The Foreign Affairs department's advisory jurisdiction is, per the mandate, limited to three functions: (1) the maintenance of the three yao-court embassies (§4.1, §4.2, §4.3); (2) the regulation of the wilds' trade through the Reed-Market (§5.3); (3) the monitoring of the wilds' changes (the Thinning's manifestations, the wilds' ecology's shifts) for the pantheon's modeling. The advisory jurisdiction does not include law-enforcement (the courts cannot enforce their rulings in the wilds), does not include governance (the courts cannot govern the wilds' inhabitants), and does not include sovereignty (the courts do not claim the wilds as courts-territory). The advisory jurisdiction is, in this sense, an administrative fiction — the courts maintain the embassies and the Reed-Market because the wilds' inhabitants tolerate them, and the courts monitor the wilds because the wilds' inhabitants do not care.

### 6.2 The Wild Suppression Bloc's campaign

The Wild Suppression Bloc (doc 38 §3.5) has, for four millennia, advocated for a courts-expansion that would convert the advisory jurisdiction into enforceable jurisdiction. The campaign has been defeated each time (per doc 38 §7.3) by cost (the courts do not have the qi-authority to maintain law in the wilds, even if the cosmology permitted it, which the cosmology does not), by the pantheon's tacit veto (the pantheon does not want the courts in the wilds; the wilds are the pantheon's research-venue and the pantheon's members' walking-venue, and the pantheon does not share), and by the yao courts' collective refusal (the three yao courts have, in their rare coordinated action, threatened to sever diplomatic relations with the courts if the Wild Suppression Bloc's campaign succeeds; the Foreign Affairs department considers the severance the surest way to undo two thousand years of negotiation). The Wild Suppression Bloc's campaign is, in the courts' internal politics, a persistent but marginalized position; the Bloc's most recent defeat (two hundred years ago, in the Council session that followed the Azure Sword Sect's failed Pillar Cities Survey) was, per the Council's records, the most decisive in the campaign's history — the Bloc's motion received three votes (the Bloc's own three councilors), the lowest total in the campaign's four millennia.

### 6.3 Border conflicts, smugglers, exiles

The courts' advisory jurisdiction does not extend to law-enforcement, but the courts' Defense department (doc 38 §2.5) does maintain patrols along the wilds' inner boundary (the side facing the maintained Precelestial). The patrols' purpose is to intercept smugglers (§5.3) crossing from the wilds into the maintained Precelestial with prohibited goods; the patrols' effectiveness is, per the Defense department's records, limited (the smugglers' Tribulation Crossing cultivator-escorts make interception difficult, and the wilds' border zones are too vast for the patrols to cover completely). The patrols' other purpose is to intercept exiles — cultivators fleeing courts-adjudication into the wilds (per doc 38 §7.3, the wilds' qi-climate is, for cultivators below Mahayana, ultimately lethal, but a Tribulation Crossing cultivator can survive in the wilds' safer regions indefinitely, and the wilds have, per the Defense department's records, a small population of courts-exiles in the Sea of Reeds' edge and the Border Twilight's safer stretches). The exiles are, per the Defense department's records, mostly harmless (the exiles who survive the wilds' qi-climate are, typically, cultivators who have lost the appetite for further conflict), but a few (the most recent: a Tribulation Crossing cultivator named **Bai Yun** (白雲, "White Cloud"), who fled the courts' adjudication for lineage-treason eighty years ago) have persisted in the wilds' border zones and have, occasionally, raided the maintained Precelestial's border outposts for supplies. Bai Yun's raids are, per the Defense department's records, the most serious wilds-border-conflict in the last century; Bai Yun has not been apprehended.

### 6.4 What the advisory jurisdiction means in practice

In practice, the courts' advisory jurisdiction means: the embassies operate (with the yao courts' tolerance); the Reed-Market operates (with the Mirror-Herd's tolerance); the Defense department's patrols operate (with limited effectiveness); the Foreign Affairs department's monitoring operates (with the pantheon's tolerance). Beyond these, the wilds are, in practice, beyond the courts' reach. A cultivator in the wilds is subject to no court's law except the law the cultivator can personally enforce. A dispute between two cultivators in the wilds is, in practice, resolved by the cultivators' relative power, not by the courts' adjudication. A crime committed in the wilds is, in practice, not a crime (the courts' law does not hold), unless the crime's perpetrator is later apprehended in the maintained Precelestial (in which case the courts can adjudicate the crime, but the adjudication is, per the courts' procedural law, difficult — the wilds' evidence is not, by the courts' evidentiary standards, admissible without corroboration that the wilds' qi-climate typically degrades). The wilds are, in this sense, the cosmos's largest lawless zone — not lawless in the sense of chaos (the wilds have their own orders, the yao courts' and the wilds' ecology's), but lawless in the sense of the courts' law not holding. The wilds are the courts' constitutional other — the place where the courts' authority is, by the cosmology's structure, absent.

## 7. The wilds' relationship to the Law Reaches

The wilds and the reaches are the Precelestial's two unregulated zones (per doc 15 §4, §5, doc 36 §4.2, §4.4), but they are unregulated in different ways. The wilds are unregulated in the sense that no court's or ancestral court's or Mahayana's inscription holds; the reaches are unregulated in the sense that the substrate-law itself has thinned or not formed. The wilds have law (the substrate-law holds); the reaches do not (the substrate-law is absent). The distinction is the Precelestial's deepest geographic fact.

### 7.1 The boundary

The wilds and the reaches are not separated by a hard line. They are separated by the Border Twilight (§1.6), the wild band where the wilds' undifferentiated qi thins into the reaches' pending substrate. The Border Twilight is, in this sense, the wilds' outermost ring and the reaches' innermost ring — the gradient between the two. A cultivator traveling from the wilds to the reaches crosses the Border Twilight and perceives, in the crossing, the substrate's transition — the wilds' undifferentiated qi (which has structure, even if no inscription) giving way to the reaches' pending substrate (which has no structure, only potentiality). The perception is, per You Wuya's account (§1.6), the perception of the cosmos's law thinning into nothing, and the perception is, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the perception that distinguishes the wilds from the reaches most clearly.

### 7.2 The wilds' beings and the reaches' beings

The wilds' beings (the yao, the wild beasts, the wild plants, §2) are beings that have evolved in the wilds' undifferentiated qi — beings whose internal architecture is organized around patterns that do not map onto the five-phase model but that do, nonetheless, depend on the substrate-law's continued operation. The reaches' beings (the Tide-Singer of the Mirror Tides, per doc 40 §5.3; the other reach-native beings the pantheon has catalogued) are beings that have evolved in the reaches' pending substrate — beings whose internal architecture does not depend on the substrate-law at all, because the substrate-law is absent. The two ecologies are, in this sense, fundamentally different — the wilds' beings are the cosmos's ecology pushed to its extreme (the five-phase model's absence), and the reaches' beings are the cosmos's ecology past its extreme (the substrate-law's absence).

### 7.3 The wilds' beings and the reaches' boundary

The wilds' beings sometimes wander into the reaches, by accident or by intention. The wanderers cross the Border Twilight (§1.6) and enter the reaches' pending substrate; the wanderers' fates are, per the pantheon's collective assessment, mostly dispersal (the reaches' pending substrate does not support the wilds' beings' substrate-dependent architecture) and occasionally transformation (a few wanderers, in the reaches' specific conditions, transform into reach-native beings — the Tide-Singer of the Mirror Tides is, per Tu Nan's four-thousand-year observation, a transformed wild-being, originally a Resonance-Beast that wandered into the Mirror Tides three thousand years ago and transformed into the reach's native consciousness). The transformation is rare and unpredictable; the pantheon does not fully understand the mechanism. The transformation is, per the pantheon's collective assessment, the wilds' and the reaches' deepest ecological connection — the wilds and the reaches are not, in this sense, fully separate ecosystems; they are two ends of a gradient, and beings can, rarely, cross from one to the other.

### 7.4 The wilds' and reaches' political relationship

The wilds and the reaches are, politically, both beyond the courts' jurisdiction (per doc 38 §7.3, §7.4), but they are beyond the courts' jurisdiction in different ways — the wilds are beyond the courts' jurisdiction because the courts' law does not hold there (the substrate-law holds, but no inscription); the reaches are beyond the courts' jurisdiction because the courts' law cannot hold there (the substrate-law itself is absent). The distinction is, in the courts' internal politics, significant — the Wild Suppression Bloc's campaign (§6.2) targets the wilds (which the Bloc argues could, in principle, be brought under the courts' jurisdiction if the courts' qi-authority were sufficient), but does not target the reaches (which the Bloc acknowledges are unrecoverable). The pantheon's relationship to the two zones is also different — the pantheon's members walk the wilds (You Wuya, Tu Nan) and inscribe in the reaches (Mo Shen's holding, the protagonist's authorship-venue); the two zones are the pantheon's two non-courts venues, and the pantheon's collective practice spans both.

## 8. What this document enables

This document specifies the Spirit Wilds in enough detail that the cosmic endgame's secondary venue (the wilds, as distinct from the reaches, the primary venue) can be written against it. Specifically:

- **Six named wild regions,** each with a name, a size, a qi-climate, and an ecology. The wilds are no longer an undifferentiated category; they are specific places the protagonist can travel to, perceive, and (in some cases) inherit from.
- **Ten named wild beings,** each with an ontology, a metabolism, and a relationship to the wilds' ecology. The wilds' alien ecology is no longer abstract; it is populated with specific beings that do not follow the five-phase model.
- **Five named lost inheritances,** each with an author, contents, access-method, and risks. The wilds' inheritance-content has a foundation.
- **Three named yao civilizations,** each with a population, a government, a cultivation tradition, and a relationship to the courts. The wilds are no longer uninhabited; they are the yao's territory.
- **Named expeditions and explorers,** including the Azure Sword Sect's three Surveys and the named Mahayana visitors. The wilds' visitation has a history.
- **The wilds' relationship to the courts specified.** The advisory jurisdiction's content, the Wild Suppression Bloc's campaign, the border conflicts, smugglers, and exiles. The wilds are the courts' constitutional other.
- **The wilds' relationship to the Law Reaches specified.** The boundary (the Border Twilight), the beings' differences, the wilds'-beings' wanderings into the reaches, the political distinction. The wilds and the reaches are the Precelestial's two unregulated zones, distinguished clearly.

The five inheritances unlock the wilds' inheritance-content. The three civilizations unlock the wilds' political landscape. The expeditions and explorers unlock the wilds' visitation history. The courts-relationship unlocks the courts' constitutional other. The reaches-relationship unlocks the wilds' and reaches' distinction.

## 9. What this document does not decide

- **The wilds' deeper pre-Sundering history.** The wilds predate the Sundering (the wilds' substrate-law, in most regions, was inscribed by the Primordial Authors, per doc 37 §3, before the Sundering); the wilds' specific pre-Sundering ecology and civilizations are, per the pantheon's collective assessment, mostly unrecoverable (the Sundering's paradox-propagation damaged the wilds' substrate-residue, and the pre-Sundering records are fragmentary). The pre-Sundering wilds are lore to be developed in a later document, generated against the seed.
- **The Unborn Beasts' nature and the gestation's outcome.** The Unborn Beasts (§2.8) are specified as in-process beings; their nature at gestation's completion is, per the pantheon's modeling, unpredictable. The completion, if it occurs, is a content-design option for the deep post-game.
- **The Three-Mouthed River's specific negotiation-history with the Serpentine Court.** The river's existence and the Court's two-thousand-year negotiation are specified; the negotiation's specific content (the river's permissions and refusals' patterns) is lore to be developed in a later document.
- **The wilds' specific trajectory under the Thinning.** The wilds' regions' changes (the Eastern Wilds' lift-current's weakening, the Northern Wilds' humming-tone's shift, the Southern Wilds' downward flow's intensification, the Western Wilds' stillness's deepening) are specified; the trajectories' specific timelines are, like the Thinning itself (per doc 39 §10), the cosmology's deepest open question. The wilds will, in the Thinning's terminal phase, become uninhabitable for the yao civilizations; the timing is not specified.

These are deferred details, not deferred forks. The decisions this document makes — the six regions, the ten beings, the five inheritances, the three civilizations, the named expeditions and explorers, the courts-relationship, the reaches-relationship — are committed. If they prove wrong in prototype, the prototype's evidence overrides this document, and this document is revised — not appended with a candidate alternative.
