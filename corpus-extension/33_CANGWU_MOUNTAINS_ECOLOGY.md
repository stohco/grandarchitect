Documents 04, 14, 24, 25, 28, and 19 commit to the Cangwu Mountains (蒼梧山脈) as the low range backing the Cangli Riverlands — where Old Chen built his hermitage, where Xu Erniu found a cultivator's cache, where charcoal burners work the lower slopes, where the Cangwu Sect draws its meager qi, and where the Azure Sword Sect surveys for a vein it has wanted for ninety-five years. ## Part 1 — Geography

### 1.1 The range, its extent and character

The Cangwu Mountains (蒼梧山脈) are a low subtropical range running roughly east-northeast to west-southwest for ~180 li, with a north-south breadth of ~90 li at the widest and ~40 li at the narrowest. They are the southern limb of a larger cordillera the provincial maps call the Southern Range (南嶺); the Cangwu are the portion that backs the Cangli Riverlands' coastal plain between Qingzhou Prefectural Seat (300 li north) and the Eastern Sea (200 li east). The range is old, rounded, deeply weathered. The highest peak rises ~1,520 m; most ridges sit between 600 and 1,100 m. The rock is predominantly weathered granite, with bands of slate (giving the western drainages their black silt), limestone (giving the central peaks their caves), and cinnabar-bearing shale (giving the Cinnabar Stream its color). The climate is subtropical monsoon: hot wet summers, cool dry winters, a plum-rain (梅雨) season in early summer that floods the western valleys, and autumn typhoons that occasionally reach the foothills.

The northern edge of the range is the **Northern Foothills (北麓)** — a 5-to-12-li-wide belt of forested ridges, coppiced slopes, and charcoal-burners' clearings that begins roughly 15 li south of Wang Family Bend and rises from the paddies at ~30 m elevation to the first real ridge crests at ~400 m. This is the belt the villagers actually enter: for firewood, for charcoal, for chestnuts and mushrooms, for the qi-faint herbs that grow where the foothills' wood-phase qi diffuses down from the inner range. The Northern Foothills are the player's first mountain zone.

### 1.2 The Northern Foothills — what is where

- **Old Chen's hermitage (陳老草廬)** sits on a 280-m spur of the northernmost Cangwu ridge, above Li Family Creek (李家溪), 4 li east of Wang Family Bend. The spur is the range's first above-flood-line prominence east of the river bend; its south face catches the morning sun, its north face is sheltered from the winter wind, and a small spring (one of the headwaters of White Birch Rill) rises 60 paces above the hut. Old Chen chose the site fifty years ago for its wood-phase qi: the spur sits over a faint offshoot of the Western Cangwu Vein (see §1.6), and the qi is dense enough to sustain a Qi Condensation-peak cultivator's practice but not so dense as to attract mid-tier beasts. A low stone wall, a vegetable garden, a medicinal herb bed, and a small library of hand-copied texts complete the compound.
- **The charcoal burners' camps (炭窯)** are strung along the lower north slopes between the 4-li and 11-li marks south of the river. There are four active camps in the 14th year of Jianhe: the Sun family camp at Black Pine Ridge's lower toe (sun-aspect, 480 m), the Li family camp at White Birch Hollow (north-aspect, 360 m), the widower Zhao's solo camp at the head of the Cinnabar Stream (560 m), and a rotating-share camp at Iron Crag's western col (640 m). The camps are occupied from Grain Rain (穀雨) to Frost's Descent (霜降); in winter the burners retreat to the lowland villages. The charcoal is sold at Qinghe Market Town; the wood is cut under a loose rotation that the lineage heads enforce by social pressure, not law.
- **Xu Erniu's axe** was found by the search party (per doc 28 §2.2) at the Black Creek crossing, ~9 li south-southwest of Wang Family Bend, at ~520 m elevation, just inside the boundary where the Northern Foothills give way to the inner range. The cache cave (see Part 4) is a further 200 paces up-slope, behind a collapsed stone, on the south bank of the creek.

### 1.3 The peaks

The five named peaks of the inner Cangwu, from west to east:

- **Iron Crag Peak (鐵岩峰)** — 1,180 m. Metal-phase dominant, with fire secondary (the cinnabar shale burns faintly in the summer heat). Bare granite upper slopes, talus fields of rust-streaked boulders, and a wind-cut saddle to the main ridge. The peak's metal-phase qi is dense enough to sustain low- and mid-tier metal-affinity beasts and a sparse community of metal-phase herbs (血草 blood-grass as indicator, 鐵味蒿 iron-tasting wormwood along the brooks). The Cangwu Sect's outer disciples occasionally climb to the saddle to gather ore for talisman-forging; they go armed and in pairs. The White-Bone Vulture (§2.7) nests on the upper crags.
- **Cangwu Main Peak (蒼梧主峰)** — 1,520 m. Wood-phase dominant, water secondary. The tallest peak in the range, clad in old-growth broadleaf forest to 1,300 m, then a belt of dwarf cypress and rhododendron, then bare granite with a small alpine meadow at the summit. The peak is the range's ecological heart: the wood-phase qi here is the densest in the Cangwu, sustained by a minor crown of the Western Cangwu Vein that surfaces under the summit meadow. The Cloud-Leopard (§2.4), the range's apex predator, holds the summit forest as its territory. Mortals do not climb above 1,200 m; the Cangwu Sect's Foundation Establishment elder (the sect's only one) visits the summit meadow once a year at Summer Solstice to gather a single White-Veined Leaf (§3.7) and pays for the herb with a half-day's qi-tribute to the leopard.
- **Cloudy Widow Peak (雲寡峰)** — 1,340 m. Water-phase dominant, with wood secondary. Named for the perpetual cloud-cap that clings to its summit from Grain in Ear (芒種) to Frost's Descent. The peak's water-phase qi is fed by snowmelt (a small snowpack lingers in the north cirque until Minor Heat) and by the cloud condensation itself — the cloud is partly qi-driven, a self-sustaining water-phase microclimate. The upper slopes host water-phase herbs (雲霧茶 cloud-mist tea, 琉璃蘆 glass-clear reed along the rills) and the Cloud-Mist Centipede (§2.13). The Glass-clear Creek (§1.4) rises from the cirque.
- **Yellow Loam Peak (黃壤峰)** — 820 m. Earth-phase dominant. The lowest of the named peaks, with the heaviest soil — a deep yellow-brown loess derived from wind-blown silt trapped against the peak's south face by the range's prevailing south-westerlies. The earth-phase qi is slow, dense, stable; the peak's herbs are slow-growing and deep-rooted (黃壤根 yellow-loam root, 石血地衣 stone-blood lichen on the lower talus). The Yellow-Loam Badger (§2.10) dens here. Mortal villagers sometimes climb the lower slopes for yellow clay (used for kiln-firing and for the lineage hall's annual plastering); they do not stay past dusk.
- **Black Pine Ridge (黑松嶺)** — 960 m. Wood-phase with fire secondary (the ridge was a charcoal-burn zone two generations ago; the regrowth is fire-adapted black pine and chestnut). The ridge is the most accessible of the inner peaks — a mortal can walk its crest in a day — and is the zone where mid-tier beast incidents are most common. The Iron-Pelt Boar (§2.5) and the Crimson-Mane Wolf (§2.10) both den here. The Cangwu Sect's hunting disciples patrol the ridge's north crest weekly from Awakening of Insects (驚蟄) to Frost's Descent.

### 1.4 The streams

The Cangwu's northern drainage feeds the Cangli River; the southern drainage feeds smaller coastal rivers. The named streams of the northern slope:

- **White Birch Rill (白樺澗)** — wood-phase with water secondary. Rises at Old Chen's spring, flows north-northwest to the Cangli at Li Family Creek. Clear water, white-birch banks, low mineral content. Wood-phase qi diffuses gently along its entire length; low-tier wood-affinity herbs (白樺汁 white-birch sap, gathered by tapping in early spring) grow along its upper reaches. The rill is safe year-round; the village children play in its lower pools.
- **Cinnabar Stream (硃砂溪)** — metal-phase with fire secondary. Rises on Iron Crag's east shoulder, flows north to the Cangli at a ford 8 li east of Wang Family Bend. The water runs rust-red where it crosses the cinnabar shale; the qi is sharp, hot, slightly astringent to a Qi-Induction cultivator's perception. Fire-phase herbs (硃砂葉鼠尾草 cinnabar-leaf sage) grow along the middle reaches; the widower Zhao's charcoal camp is at the headwaters. The stream is fordable except in plum-rain floods.
- **Glass-clear Creek (琉璃溪)** — pure water-phase, the cleanest qi in the range. Rises in Cloudy Widow Peak's north cirque, flows north through a narrow forested valley to the Cangli 14 li east of Wang Family Bend. The water is cold, clear, faintly sweet to the taste; a Qi-Induction cultivator perceives its qi as a smooth, coherent, pale-blue current. Water-phase herbs (琉璃蘆 glass-clear reed, 琉璃鯉 glass-carp in the lower pools) line its banks. The creek is the Cangwu Sect's preferred water source for alchemy — they send an outer disciple to draw water at Spring Equinox (春分) and Autumn Equinox (秋分).
- **Iron-tasting Brook (鐵味溪)** — heavy metal-phase. A small tributary of the Cinnabar Stream, rising on Iron Crag's west slope. The water tastes of iron and leaves a red stain on the rocks. Metal-phase indicator herbs (血草 blood-grass, 鐵味蒿 iron-tasting wormwood) grow densely along its banks; the Iron-Tasting Crab (§2.12) lives in its pools. The brook is not potable; the charcoal burners at Iron Crag's western col carry their drinking water from a spring above the metal-phase band.
- **Black Creek (黑溪)** — water-phase, yin-dominant, with a faint metal-phase contamination from the slate bed and a deeper yin signature from the Black Creek Serpent's (§2.1) long residence. See §1.5.

### 1.5 The Black Creek (黑溪)

The Black Creek rises in a small limestone cave on the south shoulder of Yellow Loam Peak, flows west-southwest through a steep-sided wooded valley for ~6 li, then turns north and joins the Cinnabar Stream 3 li above the Cangli ford. The creek is named for its bed: a black slate that stains the water dark even at low flow, so that the creek looks deeper than it is and reflects the canopy like polished obsidian. At normal flow it is 4-6 m wide, knee-deep, crossable by stepping stones; at plum-rain flood (see Part 5) it can rise 2 m in a night and become a brown-black torrent that carries the slate's qi-contaminated silt downstream.

The Black Creek valley is the territory of the Black Creek Serpent (§2.1), a yao of Foundation Establishment-equivalent cultivation that has held the valley for three centuries. The serpent does not negotiate, does not speak, and does not leave; mortal charcoal burners and herb-gatherers who enter the valley and do not retreat at the first warning sign (a low hiss from the undergrowth, a particular stillness in the birds) do not come back. Eleven humans have died in the valley in the last century, per document 25 §4.6. The Cangwu Sect treats the valley as forbidden ground; the Azure Sword Sect treats it as a Cangwu Sect problem. The Black-Silt Lotus (§3.3), a yin-water spirit herb of considerable potency, grows only in the valley's back-pools — which is why gatherers still risk the serpent.

Xu Erniu's axe was found at the creek's north crossing, where the Foothills Trail fords the creek at a set of mossy stepping stones. The cache cave (Part 4) is 200 paces up-slope on the south bank, behind a collapsed stone. The creek at this point is just outside the serpent's core territory — the serpent's regular patrol reaches to a large boulder 100 paces downstream, marked with a faded red cloth tied by the Cangwu Sect two generations ago as a boundary warning. Erniu, cutting firewood on the safe side of the boulder, leaned on a stone that should not have crumbled; the cache's degraded formation had finally thinned the rock enough that a mortal's weight collapsed it.

### 1.6 The Cangwu Sect's spirit vein — the Western Cangwu Vein (蒼梧西脈)

The Cangwu Sect (蒼梧派, 100 li west, per doc 24 §4.3) draws its qi from the **Western Cangwu Vein (蒼梧西脈)** — a mid-tier mixed-phase spirit vein running roughly north-northwest from a crown under Cangwu Main Peak's summit meadow, beneath the range's granite spine, to a shallow subsurface terminus ~3 li east of the sect's compound. The vein's dominant phase is wood (the old-growth forest above it); its secondary phase is water (the snowmelt and cloud-condensation that feed the summit meadow). Its yin-yang polarity is mixed: the deep current is yin-dominant (cold, still, condensing); the shallow crown under the sect's compound is yang-dominant (warm, moving, dispersing).

The vein's output is modest — enough to sustain ~30 Qi Condensation disciples and one Foundation Establishment elder at a comfortable practice rate, with a small surplus for alchemy and talisman-crafting. It is not enough for a Core Formation attempt; the sect master Wu Changqing (52, Qi Condensation peak, per doc 24 §4.3) has known this since he took the seat and has not attempted the crossing. The vein's surface trace produces the indicator ecology described in document 14 §1.3: a grove of anomalously old cypress on the sect's east slope, a spring that never freezes behind the sect's alchemy hall, and a faint reddish blood-grass along the sect's north path.

The vein has a minor offshoot that surfaces at Old Chen's hermitage spur (§1.2) — enough to sustain a single Qi Condensation-peak cultivator, no more. This offshoot is why Old Chen chose the site and why the Cangwu Sect has never asked him to leave: he is, in effect, a free watchman on the vein's eastern trace, and his presence keeps the foothills' low-tier beasts from clustering on a qi-source the sect would otherwise have to patrol.

### 1.7 The Latent River Vein (潛江脈) — the vein the Azure Sword Sect wants

Beneath the Cangli Riverlands, at a depth of ~600-900 m, runs a deep, dormant water-phase spirit vein the Azure Sword Sect's surveyors call the **Latent River Vein (潛江脈)**. It is roughly coextensive with the Cangli's modern floodplain for ~80 li of the river's middle reach, from 10 li west of Wang Family Bend to 30 li east of Cangli County Seat, with a crown that should — in a healthy vein — surface somewhere in the riverlands' south-central lowlands, between 8 and 15 li south of Wang Family Bend.

The vein is **latent** because its crown subsided ~600 years ago. A tectonic adjustment along the southern range's fault zone dropped the crown's surface trace ~300 m deeper into the bedrock, burying it beneath a layer of impermeable clay that the diffusion plume cannot penetrate at useful density. The vein's deep current still flows — water-phase qi still moves along its 80-li length — but its surface trace is too thin and too deep to sustain indicator ecology above the clay cap. The riverlands' mortal ecology is, in qi terms, a desert sitting on top of an ocean.

The Azure Sword Sect (碧劍宗, 500 li north, per doc 24 §4.3) identified the vein 95 years ago, during the pursuit of Zhou Wugui. The pursuit brought the Azure Sword Sect's previous sect master — a Core Formation cultivator named Leng Wushuang's master — through the Cangwu foothills; he perceived the latent vein's deep plume while tracking Zhou Wugui's qi-residue and noted it in his campaign journal. The Azure Sword Sect has wanted the vein ever since. A latent vein's crown, if reopened by a Core Formation cultivator with the right earth-phase technique (or by a sufficiently lucky tectonic event), would produce water-phase qi output equivalent to two or three minor veins — enough to support a great sect's secondary compound, enough to justify the political cost of claiming riverlands territory from the Cangwu Sect's loose sphere of influence.

The vein's surface trace is not entirely absent. In three lowland copses 8-15 li south of Wang Family Bend — at the headwater springs of three small Cangli tributaries — a faint seep of water-phase qi finds its way up through fractures in the clay cap. The seep is too thin to sustain a true spirit herb ecology, but a single species of moss (§3.11) grows only at these seeps, and the moss's distribution is the indicator the Azure Sword Sect's quiet surveyors have been mapping for the past decade. They have not yet told the Cangwu Sect, the Cangli County yamen, or the Wang lineage what they are looking for.

## Part 2 — The Spirit Beast Roster

The beasts below are the named nodes of the Cangwu food web. They are not a complete catalog; they are the species a player walking the range would encounter, hear of, or be warned about. Tiers follow document 14 §2.2: low (Qi Induction equivalent), mid (Qi Condensation equivalent), high (Foundation Establishment equivalent), peak (Core Formation equivalent). Yao (妖, per doc 25 §4) are beasts with true anchors and are marked as such.

### 2.1 The Black Creek Serpent (黑溪蛇妖) — yao, Foundation Establishment-equivalent

The Black Creek Serpent is a king cobra (Ophiophagus hannah) that achieved peak-tier spirit beast status ~600 years ago and completed the anchor-transition ~300 years ago, per document 25 §4.6. It is the range's **recurring threat**: eleven humans killed in the last century, all charcoal burners or herb-gatherers who entered its valley. Its territory is the 5-li Black Creek valley (§1.5); it hunts other snakes, small mammals, and the occasional mid-tier beast that wanders in. It does not speak, does not take human form, does not negotiate. Its cultivation is Foundation Establishment-equivalent in raw power but cognitively it remains a peak-tier beast — the anchor-transition completed without the cognitive development that usually accompanies it.

- **Phase:** water-phase, yin-dominant, with a faint metal taint from the slate bed.
- **Habitat:** the Black Creek valley, 5 li, dense broadleaf canopy, dark understory, permanent mist in the lower reaches.
- **Diet:** other snakes (its primary food), mid-tier spirit beasts it can overpower, occasional mortal.
- **Behavior:** territorial, ambush-predator, active at dusk and dawn. Warns once (a low hiss from the undergrowth, a stilling of the birds); attacks without further warning if the intruder does not retreat.
- **Cultivation:** Foundation Establishment-equivalent (mid).
- **Valuable parts:** the inner core (內丹, a proto-anchor that never completed but is dense and water-phase-pure) — worth a small sect's annual income; the venom (a yin-water paralytic used in Foundation Establishment-preparation alchemy); the shed skin (a talisman substrate for water-phase concealment).
- **Danger to mortals:** lethal. A mortal in the valley without retreat is dead.
- **Danger to cultivators:** a Qi Condensation cultivator is prey; a Foundation Establishment cultivator is a peer and can fight it to a draw; a Core Formation cultivator could disperse it (but the Azure Sword Sect declines, on lawful grounds — per doc 25 §4.6, the yao has an anchor and is a person under alliance law).

### 2.2 Old Cypress (古柏樹精) — yao, Foundation Establishment-equivalent

A cypress tree (Platycladus orientalis) that achieved peak-tier status ~800 years ago and completed the anchor-transition ~500 years ago, per document 25 §4.7. Old Cypress is the sessile guardian of the eastern Cangwu's old-growth cypress grove, on the south shoulder of Cangwu Main Peak at ~1,100 m. It is both a yao and the place-spirit of its grove (per doc 25 §4.7, the categories overlap when the yao's body is the place).

- **Phase:** wood-phase, deeply yang-dominant.
- **Habitat:** its grove, ~3 li circumference, half-li perception radius.
- **Diet:** none in the animal sense; it photosynthesizes and draws wood-phase qi from the vein crown beneath its roots.
- **Behavior:** sessile, slow cognition (tree-time), protective. Kills cypress-cutters by routing a wood-phase qi-surge through the soil that stops the mortal's heart. Blesses venerators with a season's constitution-strengthening.
- **Cultivation:** late Foundation Establishment.
- **Valuable parts:** none harvestable without killing the yao (which would be unlawful and would require a Core Formation cultivator's sustained effort); its shed needles and fallen seeds are gathered respectfully by the three Cangwu-village lineages that venerate it and have minor wood-phase medicinal value.
- **Danger to mortals:** lethal if the mortal cuts a cypress in the grove; benign otherwise.
- **Danger to cultivators:** a hostile Qi Condensation cultivator in the grove is suppressed; a hostile Foundation Establishment cultivable is fought as a peer (Old Cypress's quasi-domain makes it the equal of a mid-Foundation Establishment human within the grove).

### 2.3 Lady Bai (白氏狐仙) — yao, late Foundation Establishment

A red fox (Vulpes vulpes) that achieved peak-tier status ~400 years ago and completed the anchor-transition ~250 years ago, per document 25 §4.5. She lives in a copse of old-growth oak on Cangwu Main Peak's eastern shoulder, above the wood-phase vein offshoot that sustained her peak-tier cultivation. She takes stable human form — a young woman of about twenty, dark hair, dark eyes, a faint reddish cast to her shadow in moments of inattention.

- **Phase:** wood-phase, neutral yin-yang.
- **Habitat:** the eastern shoulder of Cangwu Main Peak, 1,000-1,200 m.
- **Diet:** small game (rabbits, pheasants, the occasional low-tier spirit rodent), wood-phase herbs she seeks out medicinally.
- **Behavior:** curious about humans, walks in Qinghe Market Town occasionally, has twice in a century taken a mortal lover. Has never harmed a mortal unprovoked.
- **Cultivation:** late Foundation Establishment; preparing for the Core Formation form-tribulation for thirty years.
- **Valuable parts:** none that are harvestable without killing her (unlawful); her shed fur (gathered where she has been in fox-form) has minor wood-phase talismanic use and is occasionally found by the Cangwu Sect's outer disciples.
- **Danger to mortals:** none directly; she is benign. Indirect danger: a mortal who befriends her in human form and follows her into the inner range may encounter beasts she does not protect against.
- **Danger to cultivators:** a hostile Foundation Establishment cultivator would find her a peer; she would avoid rather than fight.

### 2.4 The Cloud-Leopard (雲豹) — peak-tier spirit beast, the range's apex predator

The Cloud-Leopard is a large felid (Neofelis nebulosa) that has held the summit forest of Cangwu Main Peak as its territory for ~180 years — three fox-lifetimes, two human generations. It is the **apex predator of the Cangwu range**: nothing in the range preys on it, and it preys on everything below it in the food web. It achieved peak-tier status ~120 years ago and has formed a proto-anchor (內丹) but has not attempted the yao-transition; the leopard is, by disposition, uninterested in personhood.

- **Phase:** water-phase primary (the cloud-cap), wood-phase secondary (the forest).
- **Habitat:** the summit forest of Cangwu Main Peak, 1,300-1,520 m, with hunting range down to 1,000 m on the north slope and 1,100 m on the south.
- **Diet:** mid-tier spirit deer, mid-tier spirit macaques, the occasional mid-tier wolf that strays up from Black Pine Ridge. It does not prey on humans (the summit is too high for routine mortal traffic) but has killed two Cangwu Sect outer disciples who climbed past 1,300 m armed and in pairs against the sect's explicit warning.
- **Behavior:** solitary except in spring mating season; territorial but not aggressive against retreat; hunts by ambush from the canopy.
- **Cultivation:** peak-tier (mid-Core Formation equivalent in raw qi-density; the proto-anchor is dense).
- **Valuable parts:** the proto-anchor / inner core (extremely valuable, water-wood dual-phase); the pelt (a Qi-Condensation-tier qi-shielding garment if tanned correctly); the claws and teeth (metal-phase secondary, talisman substrates).
- **Danger to mortals:** lethal above 1,200 m on Cangwu Main Peak.
- **Danger to cultivators:** a Foundation Establishment cultivator is prey in the leopard's territory; a Core Formation cultivator is a peer. The Cangwu Sect's Foundation Establishment elder treats the leopard as a sovereign neighbor and pays an annual qi-tribute for the White-Veined Leaf (§3.7).

### 2.5 The Iron-Pelt Boar (鐵毛野豬) — mid-tier, metal-phase

A Sus scrofa lineage that has dens in Black Pine Ridge's chestnut groves for at least five generations. The boars' bristles have absorbed enough metal-phase qi from the ridge's ore-bearing soil that a mid-tier boar's pelt turns arrowheads at oblique angles — a source of constant complaint from the Cangwu Sect's hunting disciples.

- **Phase:** metal-phase primary, earth-phase secondary.
- **Habitat:** Black Pine Ridge, 600-960 m, with seasonal descent to the Northern Foothills' chestnut groves in autumn.
- **Diet:** chestnuts, roots, grubs, the occasional low-tier spirit rodent, carrion.
- **Behavior:** herds of 8-20 sows and young led by a mid-tier matriarch; solitary mid-tier boars are territorial and aggressive. Digs wallows in metal-phase seeps, which concentrates the qi in its pelt.
- **Cultivation:** mid-tier (low-Qi-Condensation equivalent).
- **Valuable parts:** the pelt (metal-phase qi-shielding, used in mortal-tier armor); the tusks (talisman substrate); the gallbladder (metal-phase alchemical base).
- **Danger to mortals:** a single boar can kill a hunting party of three. The Cangwu Sect posts warnings at the ridge's north trailhead at Awakening of Insects.
- **Danger to cultivators:** a Qi Condensation cultivator's peer; a routine hunt for a coordinated pair.

### 2.6 The Crimson-Mane Wolf (赤鬃狼) — mid-tier, fire-phase; the Zhou Wugui era's recurring foothills threat

A Cuon-like pack canid whose great-grandsire was driven out of Black Pine Ridge's upper slopes 95 years ago, during the Azure Sword Sect's pursuit of Zhou Wugui (per doc 24 §4.4). The pursuit's qi-disturbance — sustained Core-Formation-grade combat qi-residue across the central range — collapsed the pack's upper-ridge territory and pushed the lineage into the lower Northern Foothills, where it has denned for four generations. The Crimson-Mane Wolf is the **foothills' recurring threat**: it has taken livestock in every generation since, and three village children in the last sixty years. The lineage's name for the current pack alpha is Old Red (老赤).

- **Phase:** fire-phase primary (the rust-red mane), metal-phase secondary (the teeth and claws).
- **Habitat:** the Northern Foothills' lower ridges, 350-700 m, with a primary den on Black Pine Ridge's north toe.
- **Diet:** village livestock (chickens, pigs, the occasional calf), low-tier spirit deer, the occasional mortal child.
- **Behavior:** pack hunter, 6-10 adults, disciplined. Hunts at dusk and dawn; avoids Qi Condensation cultivator qi-signatures; will range into village paddies in hard winters.
- **Cultivation:** mid-tier (low-to-mid Qi Condensation equivalent).
- **Valuable parts:** the mane-pelt (fire-phase, used in fire-affinity talismans); the alpha's fire-phase core-region heart (Foundation Establishment-preparation alchemical ingredient for fire-rooted cultivators).
- **Danger to mortals:** high. A lone woodcutter is prey; a village militia can drive a pack off but cannot extirpate it.
- **Danger to cultivators:** a Qi Condensation cultivator is a peer of the alpha; a coordinated Qi Condensation pair can take the pack.

### 2.7 The White-Bone Vulture (白骨鷲) — high-tier, metal-phase

A large vulture (Aegypius lineage) that nests on Iron Crag Peak's upper crags at ~1,050 m. ## Part 4 — The Cultivator's Cache

### 4.1 Who left it: Daoist Cangwu Yun (蒼梧雲真人)

The cache was left by a loose cultivator named **Daoist Cangwu Yun (蒼梧雲真人, "Cloud of Cangwu")**, born ~290 years ago in a Cangwu foothills village that no longer exists (the village was abandoned during a famine ~240 years ago and its ruins are now chestnut groves). Cangwu Yun was a wood-water dual-phase cultivator who reached mid-Foundation Establishment by his sixtieth year — a respectable achievement for a loose cultivator with no sect backing. He lived as a hermit-alchemist in the Northern Foothills for ~50 years, on the south bank of the Black Creek (just outside the Black Creek Serpent's core territory — the Serpent was already resident in his youth), practicing, refining pills, and quietly studying the Western Cangwu Vein's surface trace.

Cangwu Yun died ~230 years ago, attempting Core Formation. The attempt was deliberate, prepared, and unsurvivable: he knew his wood-water dual-phase roots were not quite balanced enough for the crossing, and he judged (correctly, as it proved) that he would not have another decade to prepare. He pre-sealed his cache before the attempt, hoping a successor would find it — a successor with better roots, or more time, or the luck he had lacked. The breakthrough tribulation killed him; his body was never found (the tribulation's qi-burst scattered his remains; the Black Creek Serpent, drawn by the disturbance, consumed what was left). The cache remained sealed.

### 4.2 What's inside: a partial inheritance

The cache is a sandalwood box, ~60 cm cubed, sitting on a low stone plinth in a 3-m-square natural limestone chamber 4 m inside the foothills' slope. The chamber was a hermitage practice-cell in Cangwu Yun's time; the cache sits where his meditation mat would have been. The box contains:

1. **The Cangwu Cloud-Practice Manual (蒼梧雲修法, Cangwu Yun Xiufa)** — a hand-bound volume of ~120 bamboo slips, ink on bamboo, in a sandalwood case. The manual is a Foundation Establishment-stage wood-water dual-phase practice, complete except for the **integration chapter** (the chapter describing the body-qi-anchor integration at the Foundation Establishment threshold). The integration chapter is missing because Cangwu Yun took it with him to his breakthrough attempt (he needed it for reference) and it was destroyed with him. The manual's remaining chapters — qi-circulation, phase-balancing, deviation-prevention, and the wood-water dual-phase signature technique — are intact and represent a genuine mid-Foundation-Establishment inheritance. **Unknown to anyone living:** Old Chen's hermitage library contains a hand-copied fragment (the integration chapter, copied from a different source decades ago, that Old Chen has never recognized as part of a larger work). The player may eventually realize the two are halves of one practice.

2. **Three talismans**, in a silk pouch:
   - **One exhausted qi-shielding talisman (氣盾符, exhausted)** — a single-use Foundation Establishment-tier shield, already discharged. Worthless as a talisman; valuable as a study specimen of Foundation Establishment-tier talisman-craft.
   - **One partially-charged qi-shielding talisman (氣盾符, ~30% charge)** — a single-use Foundation Establishment-tier shield with enough residual charge for ~3 seconds of Foundation Establishment-grade protection, or ~10 seconds of Qi Condensation-grade. One-time use.
   - **One sealing talisman (封符)** — this is the talisman that originally powered the cache's sealing formation. Its charge is now ~5% (the formation has been drawing on it for 230 years); when it fully discharges, the formation will collapse and the cache will be exposed.

3. **A small qi-store**, in a sandalwood box within the box:
   - **8 low-grade spirit stones**, in a silk bag. The stones have been slowly drained by the formation's sustenance loop for 230 years; each retains ~20% of a fresh low-grade stone's charge. Combined, ~1.6 fresh stones' worth — useful to a Qi Condensation cultivator, modest.
   - **A palm-sized jade bottle containing 3 Foundation Establishment-preparation pills (築基丹)**, wood-water phase, Yellow-Loam Root and Three-Vein Ginseng base. One pill is degraded (the binding agent has crystallized; the pill is now a mild toxin). Two pills remain viable. A viable Foundation Establishment-preparation pill of this grade is worth ~80 spirit stones at the Qingzhou market.

### 4.3 The formation that sealed it

The cache is sealed by a **storage-sealing formation (封藏陣, Fengzang Zhen)** — a Foundation Establishment-tier array of the *concealment-with-sustenance* subtype. Its design: three nested layers.

- **Outer layer — concealment (隱).** A qi-baffling field that makes the cache chamber and its entrance feel, to Qi-Induction and Qi-Condensation perception, like ordinary limestone — the field patterns the ambient qi around the chamber so that no qi-anomaly is detectable. This layer was designed to last ~100 years on the formation's original qi-budget. It has lasted 230, because the Northern Foothills' ambient wood-phase qi has been slowly feeding the formation's maintenance loop — the concealment layer draws a trickle of wood-phase qi from the surrounding soil, which extends its life far beyond design. **Status: degraded.** The baffling has thinned; a Qi-Induction cultivator standing within 5 m of the entrance now perceives a faint wood-water anomaly (which the player, if they have begun Qi Induction, will notice during the search for Erniu). A mortal perceives nothing — but the rock over the entrance has become brittle, which is why Xu Erniu, leaning on it, collapsed it.

- **Middle layer — sustenance (養).** A qi-circulation loop that draws a small amount of ambient qi to maintain the contents' qi-integrity (the manual's bamboo, the talismans' substrate, the pills' binding agent). This layer is *over*-functioning: it has been drawing more qi than designed, because the formation's reservoir (the sealing talisman) is nearly exhausted and the loop has resorted to scavenging from any qi-source it can reach — including the qi of any living being inside the chamber. **This is why Xu Erniu is in a coma.** He triggered the inner layer by entering; the sustenance loop, finding his body's qi as the nearest available source, began to drain him. He is alive but unconscious, his qi being slowly siphoned to feed the formation's maintenance.

- **Inner layer — defense (禦).** A paralysis-sleep effect that holds any intruder in torpor while the formation completes a *verification cycle* — a 7-day assessment of whether the intruder's qi-signature is compatible with the manual's wood-water phase-affinity. The cycle is a security measure: a wood-water compatible cultivator would be recognized as a legitimate successor and the formation would yield; an incompatible cultivator would be held until the formation's reservoir collapsed, then released (with the cache destroyed). **Status: hung.** Erniu, a mortal with no cultivation, has no qi-signature for the cycle to verify. The cycle has been running for three days (per doc 28) and will never complete; the formation will continue to drain Erniu until either (a) the formation's reservoir collapses (~2 more weeks, after which Erniu wakes but the cache is destroyed — the manual's bamboo crumbles, the talismans discharge, the pills lose their binding) or (b) a cultivator intervenes.

### 4.4 What the player can do with it

The cache is the player's first encounter with a cultivator's legacy and the first concrete test of their tier.

- **At Qi Induction (the player's tier during the Xu Erniu search, per doc 28 §2.2):** The player perceives the qi-residue around the cache entrance — a faint, old, wood-water signature that the player's newly-awakened perception reads as "this place was attended to, deliberately, a long time ago." The player can also perceive, faintly, Erniu's drained qi-signature inside — a mortal's life-qi at low ebb, recognizable as a living being in distress. The player cannot unravel the formation; they cannot even perceive its layered structure (Qi Induction perceives the *residue*, not the formation's *active pattern*). The player's correct action is to recognize the cache as a cultivator's work, perceive Erniu's distress, and report to Old Chen — who (per doc 28 §3.5) already knows about the cache and sealed it himself 40 years ago. Old Chen will guide the next steps. A player who tries to enter the cache at Qi Induction triggers the inner defense layer and is themselves paralyzed; Old Chen (notified by the player's absence) retrieves them within a day, but the experience costs the player a week of qi-recovery.

- **At Qi Condensation:** The player can perceive the formation's layered structure — concealment, sustenance, defense — and can begin to unravel it. The outer concealment layer is straightforward to dispel (a Qi Condensation cultivator's qi-output exceeds the layer's residual coherence). The inner defense layer can be cracked by a Qi Condensation cultivator who reads the verification cycle and supplies a wood-water compatible qi-signature to satisfy it — at which point the cycle releases Erniu (the verification reads the cultivator, not the mortal, and the formation yields to its recognized successor). The middle sustenance loop, however, cannot be safely unbound by a Qi Condensation cultivator: 230 years of accumulated qi-load, once unbound, would discharge into the unbound in a backlash that would seriously injure (and possibly derail the cultivation of) a Qi Condensation cultivator. The player must re-seal the cache (with Old Chen's guidance — Old Chen knows the re-sealing technique) and leave the contents for later. Erniu is freed; the cache remains for the player's return at Foundation Establishment.

- **At Foundation Establishment:** The player can fully disarm the formation — dispel the concealment, satisfy the defense's verification, and safely absorb the sustenance loop's 230-year backlog (the Foundation Establishment cultivator's reservoir is large enough to take the discharge without injury). The contents are accessible. The player may keep the inheritance, share it with Old Chen (who will weep — the integration chapter he has carried for decades is the missing half of a complete Foundation Establishment practice, and the discovery vindicates his long-held suspicion that his copy was part of a larger work), or — politically fraught — present it to the Cangwu Sect as a courtesy. The Cangwu Sect's Foundation Establishment elder would be grateful and would offer a substantial favor in return; the Azure Sword Sect, hearing of it through its informants, would be irritated that a loose cultivator's inheritance has strengthened a small sect it has been quietly planning to absorb. The viable Foundation Establishment-preparation pills are the player's to use or sell.

### 5.5 The four ecological windows a player should learn

1. **The plum-rain flood window (Grain in Ear to Minor Heat):** the Black Creek is impassable; the latent vein's surface trace is most perceptible; the Azure Sword Sect's surveyors are in the lowlands. A player who learns this window learns to time cross-range travel, herb-gathering, and counter-surveillance.
2. **The spring mating window (Grain Rain to Grain in Ear):** mid-tier beasts are most aggressive. The Foothills Trail is dangerous; the Crimson-Mane Wolf and Iron-Pelt Boar are at peak threat. The Cangwu Sect's hunting disciples patrol most intensively.
3. **The summit harvest week (Summer Solstice):** the White-Veined Leaf is harvestable for one week; the Cloud-Leopard is most active; the Cangwu Sect elder visits the summit. A player who would take the leaf must negotiate with (or evade) both.
4. **The winter-stillness window (Winter Solstice to Minor Cold):** the Black Creek Serpent is in deepest torpor; the Black-Silt Lotus is harvestable by a sufficiently powerful cultivator; the Crimson-Mane Wolf ranges into the paddies. A player who learns this window learns that winter is not safe — it is safe in one place and dangerous in another.
