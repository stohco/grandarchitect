# 20 — The Cultivation World

**Status:** Candidate canon. The political structure above the mortal world.
**Date:** 2026-08-03

---

## 0. What this document is

The "cultivation world" (修真界) is not a place. It is a social structure — the network of relationships, institutions, and norms that govern cultivators' interactions with each other and with mortal society. This document specifies that structure: who the powers are, how they relate, how disputes are resolved, and how the player enters and navigates this world.

---

## 1. The hierarchy of power

### 1.1 The mortal world (凡塵)

The bottom layer. Mortals live, labor, pay tax, and die. They are aware of cultivators (stories, occasional sightings) but do not interact with the cultivation world directly. The magistrate (per document 04 §5.2) is the mortal world's highest authority. Above the magistrate: the county, the prefecture, the province, the throne. All mortal.

### 1.2 The cultivation world (修真界)

The layer above. Cultivators (Qi Condensation and up) are members of this world, not the mortal world. They are subject to cultivation-world norms, not mortal law (in most cases). The cultivation world has its own institutions (sects, the Immortal Alliance, the Courts of Heaven), its own economy (spirit stones, not silver), and its own politics.

The relationship between mortal and cultivation worlds is not clean separation. It is a gradient:
- A Qi Condensation cultivator in a mortal village is still partially mortal (they have kin, obligations, a household)
- A Foundation Establishment cultivator is mostly cultivation-world (their mortal kin are aging, their household is a memory)
- A Core Formation cultivator is entirely cultivation-world (their mortal origins are a footnote; they may not have visited their birth village in centuries)

### 1.3 The great powers

The cultivation world's political structure is not a single government. It is a balance of power between:

- **The great sects** (大宗門): 5-10 per region. Each has a Core Formation+ sect master, hundreds of disciples, multiple spirit veins, and one or more grotto-heavens. They are the cultivation world's "nations."
- **The holy lands** (聖地): 3-5 per continent. Each has a Nascent Soul+ patriarch, thousands of disciples, and a peak grotto-heaven. They are the cultivation world's "empires."
- **The Courts of Heaven** (天庭): per document 15. The Precelestial institution with jurisdiction over records of the dead and heaven-sealed oaths. Not omnipotent, but respected (or feared) by all.
- **The Immortal Alliance** (仙盟): the political body where sects resolve disputes. Per document 12 §6. Weak (it has no enforcement power beyond collective action) but necessary (without it, the cultivation world is constant war).
- **The rogue cultivators** (散修): cultivators with no sect affiliation. Some are powerful (a rogue Nascent Soul is a force unto themselves). Most are poor, vulnerable, and despised. The player begins as a rogue (Old Chen is a retired rogue).

## 2. The geography of power

### 2.1 The region

The player's starting region (the lower Cangli watershed) is a backwater. The nearest sect is a small sect (Qi Condensation sect master, 30 disciples) 100 li west. The nearest great sect is 500 li north. The nearest holy land is on another continent. The nearest grotto-heaven is 200 li away, held by a mid-size sect.

This is deliberate. The player begins at the bottom of the cultivation world's geography, in a region where mortal life is still the dominant reality and cultivators are rare, distant figures.

### 2.2 The cultivation map

The cultivation world has its own map, overlaying the mortal map:
- **Sect territories**: the region around a sect where it has authority (collects tithes, enforces norms, protects mortals from beasts)
- **Neutral zones**: unclaimed regions where rogue cultivators and small sects operate
- **Dangerous zones**: regions with high beast density, contamination, or unstable law — where only Core Formation+ cultivators travel safely
- **Grotto-heaven anchors**: the points where pocket worlds can be accessed
- **Cultivation markets**: the trading hubs (per document 18 §2.2)

### 2.3 Travel

A Qi Condensation cultivator travels at ~15-20 m/s (sustained sprint). A Foundation Establishment cultivator can fly (specific techniques required). A Core Formation cultivator can fly faster and farther. Travel between regions takes days (Qi Condensation) to hours (Core Formation). The player's first journey from Wang Family Bend to the nearest cultivation market is a multi-day trip — a real journey, not a fast-travel.

## 3. The norms of the cultivation world

### 3.1 The strong eat the weak (弱肉強食)

The cultivation world's fundamental norm. A Core Formation cultivator can take what they want from a Qi Condensation cultivator, and there is no authority that will stop them (unless the Qi Condensation cultivator has a powerful backer). This is not cruelty; it is the logic of a world where power differences are vast and enforcement is weak.

The player experiences this directly: at Qi Condensation, they are vulnerable to any Foundation Establishment cultivator who wants what they have. At Foundation Establishment, they are vulnerable to Core Formation. The only protections are: a sect's backing, personal power, or the threat of retaliation from allies.

### 3.2 Face (面子)

Reputation is power. A sect's face (its reputation for strength, fairness, and willingness to defend its members) is its most valuable asset. Losing face (a disciple is killed and the sect does nothing, a sect master is humiliated in public) weakens the sect more than losing a spirit vein.

The player accumulates face: by winning duels, by completing notable deeds, by having powerful allies, by being seen as honorable (or fearsome). Face opens doors (sect elders will meet with you, markets will offer better prices) and closes them (a faceless rogue is turned away).

### 3.3 The debt of gratitude (恩)

A favor received is a debt owed. The cultivation world runs on恩 — the web of mutual obligation between cultivators. A teacher who teaches you owes you nothing; you owe them everything. A sect that shelters you can call on you decades later. A cultivator who saves your life has a claim on your life.

The player accumulates恩: Old Chen teaches you (you owe him), the Wang lineage shelters you (you owe them), a fellow cultivator saves you in a fight (you owe them). These debts are tracked by the simulation and can be called in.

### 3.4 The prohibition on mortals

A Foundation Establishment+ cultivator who uses their power against mortals (in a dispute, for gain, for amusement) violates a norm that the Courts of Heaven and the Immortal Alliance both enforce. The prohibition is not moral; it is practical: if cultivators preyed on mortals openly, the mortal world would collapse, and the cultivation world depends on the mortal world for food, labor, and new disciples.

The prohibition is enforced by:
- The Courts of Heaven (for extreme violations — wiping out a village)
- The local sect (for moderate violations — extorting a village)
- Social pressure (for minor violations — a cultivator who bullies mortals loses face)

The player who violates this norm (kills a mortal in a dispute, extorts a village) faces consequences: loss of face, sect censure, and eventually Courts of Heaven attention.

## 4. How the player enters

### 4.1 The threshold

The player crosses from the mortal world to the cultivation world when they reach Qi Condensation and register with a sect or declare as a rogue. Before this, they are a mortal who happens to perceive qi. After this, they are a cultivator, subject to cultivation-world norms.

### 4.2 The first sect

The player's first sect interaction (per Golden Scene 2) is with Old Chen, who is not a sect — he is a retired rogue. The player's first actual sect interaction will be when they travel to the nearest small sect (the one 100 li west) and attempt to join as an outer disciple. This is a future scene (not in document 06, which covers the first five scenes).

### 4.3 The choice: sect or rogue

The player must choose:
- **Join a sect**: gain resources, protection, a teacher, a community. Lose independence, owe dues, follow orders, become entangled in sect politics.
- **Remain a rogue**: keep independence, follow your own path. Lose access to resources, be vulnerable to any sect cultivator who wants what you have, travel constantly to find cultivation opportunities.

This is the genre's central social choice. The doctrine (Part 3) says "confront the central tension directly." The tension: the genre wants the protagonist to be independent (the rogue cultivator who rises through talent and luck), but the doctrine's scarcity model makes the rogue path extremely hard. The player who chooses rogue must be willing to accept the difficulty. The player who chooses sect must be willing to accept the obligation.

---

## 5. What this document enables

- The player's social trajectory is specified: mortal → rogue cultivator → sect member (or independent rogue) → sect elder (or independent power) → lineage founder → political force
- The cultivation world's geography is specified: the player begins in a backwater and must travel to find resources
- The norms (弱肉強食, face, 恩, the prohibition on mortals) are tracked by the simulation and produce consequences
- The great powers (sects, holy lands, Courts, Alliance) provide the political structure for mid- and late-game plot
- The choice between sect and rogue is the player's first major social decision, with consequences that persist for the entire playthrough

The cultivation world is not a menu of factions. It is a social structure with its own logic, its own cruelty, and its own beauty. The player who enters it is changed by it. The player who rises in it has paid for every step.
