## 0. What this document is and why it exists

The critics found that only 5 of the 31 households in Wang Family Bend are named (doc 04 §1.2) and that the village has no non-Wang social actors with agency. A village of 180 people cannot be a diorama of five named households and twenty-six anonymous ones; the player must perceive the village as a society of recognizable, interlocked people, most of whom are not Wang and most of whom have their own plans. This document names ten more households (bringing the total to 15 of 31), gives full character bibles for the three companions (Wang Meili, Liang Zhu, Leng Qingxue) and the antagonist (Pei Liang), and specifies the death schedule for every named NPC who dies across the game.

All ages are at game start (the protagonist's 16th year, Jianhe 14) unless otherwise noted. Pinyin is given without tone marks. Every NPC with agency has a name, an age, a personality, and at least one relationship to another named NPC.

Three retcons from earlier documents are ratified here, all in service of consistency with doc 28 (the village-in-medias-res doc, which is the most concrete):
1. The protagonist's father is **Wang Shouzheng** (王守正, 58, the lineage head, per doc 04 §1.2 and doc 28 §3.1), superseding doc 26 §5.1's "Wang Shouyi." The protagonist is the Senior Household's third child (between Wang Zongwen and Wang Sanniang).
2. The protagonist's mother is **Lady Chen** (陳氏, 54, per doc 28 §3.2), superseding doc 26 §5.1's "Lady Liu." Doc 26's separate references to "Lady Chen" as Old Chen's wife are reidentified as a different character, **Lady Chen of the hermitage** (a coincidence of surname; the two women are not related).
3. Master Hu's wife is **Lady Wang** (王氏, 43, born a Wang, Wang Shouzheng's younger sister, per doc 28 §3.3's narrative), correcting doc 04 §1.2's "Lady Sun (孫氏)." The "Sun" in doc 04 was an error; she was born a Wang.

## Part 1: The Remaining Households

### 1.1 The five already-named households (recap)

1. **Household 1: The Wang Senior Household (王正房)** — Wang Shouzheng (58, lineage head), Lady Chen (54), sons Wang Zongxian (32) and Wang Zongwen (27, assistant teacher), daughter Wang Sanniang (22, betrothed). The protagonist's natal household.
2. **Household 2: The Wang Tenant Household (王佃房)** — Wang Shouye (52, Shouzheng's cousin), Lady Zhao (49), sons Wang Zongwu (28) and Wang Zongde (24, the "bare stick," rival per doc 06 Scene 3).
3. **Household 3: The Salt Merchant Household (鹽商)** — Master Hu (47), Lady Wang (43, Shouzheng's sister), children Hu Bao (18) and Hu Ying (15). Resident at the dock.
4. **Household 4: The Lin Household (林家)** — Lin Aqiao (38, carpenter), Lady Wang (35, Shouzheng's niece), children Lin Gensheng (16) and Lin Xiaomei (12).
5. **Household 5: The Widow's Household (寡婦家)** — Widow Xu (61), son Xu Erniu (19, missing per doc 28 §2.2).

### 1.2 The ten newly-named households

#### Household 6: The Zhou Household (周家) — Old Lady Zhou the midwife
- **Head:** Zhou Wenshu (周文淑, 66), called Zhou Popo (周婆婆, "Old Lady Zhou") by the village. Non-Wang widow. Occupation: midwife (穩婆), folk-healer, story-teller.
- **Household:** lives alone. Her husband died 20 years ago; her daughter married into Li Family Creek; her son died in infancy.
- **Relationship to Senior Household:** she birthed Wang Zongxian, Wang Zongwen, Wang Sanniang, the protagonist, and most of the cousin-branch children. Lady Chen calls her "older sister" though they are not kin. She has standing no formal title gives.
- **Distinctive detail:** she keeps a silk bag of dried umbilical cords — one per child she has birthed, 40 years of village births. The bag is her anchor of memory; at her death it goes to the lineage hall.
- **Personality:** sharp-tongued, warm-hearted, unsentimental about blood and pain. She has seen too much to be delicate.
- **Tension ties:** Tension 2 — she birthed Xu Erniu and grieves him as a grandson; she visits Widow Xu nightly. Tension 3 — Wang Sanniang came to her for "women's medicine" to delay the marriage; Zhou Popo refused and told her to face it.

#### Household 7: The He Household (何家) — He Laosan the ferryman
- **Head:** He Laosan (何老三, 45), non-Wang, born in Qinghe Market Town. Occupation: ferryman and boatman.
- **Household:** wife Lady Ma (馬氏, 42), daughter He Yatou (17, works the boat), son He Xiaobao (14, apprentice). A small house and boatshed at the dock, near Master Hu's warehouse.
- **Relationship to Senior Household:** he carries Wang produce to market and brings goods back; Wang Shouzheng has a standing arrangement (free passage for lineage business in exchange for winter firewood).
- **Distinctive detail:** he is missing the little finger of his left hand (a river otter's bite, a story he tells every passenger). He has a faded ink map of the Cangli River tattooed on his left forearm — a folk charm against drowning.
- **Personality:** garrulous, canny, quietly observant. He knows everyone's business because he ferries them.
- **Tension ties:** Tension 1 — he ferried the yamen runner to and from the village; he saw the silk-wrapped gift and said nothing. Tension 2 — he ferried a stranger upriver the night Xu Erniu disappeared; he did not connect the two until the search party asked.

#### Household 8: The Wu Household (吳家) — Wu Daniu the charcoal-burner
- **Head:** Wu Daniu (吳大牛, 50), non-Wang, born in the Cangwu foothills. Occupation: charcoal-burner (燒炭).
- **Household:** wife Lady Lin (林氏, 47, sister of Lin Aqiao the carpenter), sons Wu Gouzi (20) and Wu Mazi (16), daughter Wu Sanniang (13). A hut in the foothills 2 li south of the village and a charcoal-shed in the village for market days.
- **Relationship to Senior Household:** sells charcoal to the Wangs in winter; has a quiet arrangement with Wang Shouzheng — Daniu keeps eyes on the foothills (bandits, beasts, worse) in exchange for the lineage's protection if he is ever in trouble.
- **Distinctive detail:** his hands are permanently black-stained (the charcoal has worked into the skin). He has a deep burn scar across his back from a kiln collapse 15 years ago that killed his younger brother.
- **Personality:** taciturn, superstitious, dependable in a crisis. He fears the upper slopes more than he fears the village's judgment.
- **Tension ties:** Tension 2 — he cut the trail past Black Creek where Erniu's axe was found. Two nights ago he heard a low hum, like a bell struck underground, from beast territory. He has not told the search party because he fears being sent back.

#### Household 9: The Zhao Household (趙家) — Zhao Tieniu the returned soldier
- **Head:** Zhao Tieniu (趙鐵牛, 40), non-Wang, born in Wang Family Bend. Occupation: returned soldier, day laborer, village night watchman.
- **Household:** wife Lady Wang (王氏, 36, Shouzheng's niece — she waited 15 years for him), son Zhao Jinhu (15), daughter Zhao Yinhua (12). A small compound at the eastern edge with a shrine to the war god Guan Yu — the only such shrine in the village.
- **Relationship to Senior Household:** he married Shouzheng's niece before he left for service; she waited 15 years. He returned last year with a small pension, a permanent limp (a horse archer's arrow in the left knee), and the only firsthand knowledge of the world beyond the Cangli Riverlands in the village.
- **Distinctive detail:** a long scar across his throat — a steppe raider's knife that missed the artery by a finger's breadth. He speaks in a hoarse whisper. His old army spear hangs oiled above his door.
- **Personality:** watchful, dry, quietly kind. He has seen enough of violence to value peace; he does not speak of what he saw except in fragments.
- **Tension ties:** Tension 1 — he knows the yamen runner's type (corrupt military runners were the bane of his service); he suspects the runner was sent to shake Master Hu down, not to check the baojia registers. He has said nothing because his pension depends on the yamen's good word. Tension 3 — Wang Sanniang spoke to him about her reluctance (he is the only man who has been outside the village); he told her "marriage is not a war you can refuse," which she took as cruelty but he meant as kindness.

#### Household 10: The Li Household (李家) — Li Asheng the non-Wang yeoman farmer
- **Head:** Li Asheng (李阿勝, 55), non-Wang, born in Wang Family Bend. Occupation: yeoman farmer.
- **Household:** wife Lady Yu (余氏, 50), sons Li Dazhuang (25, married, 2 children) and Li Erzhuang (22, unmarried), daughter Li Sanniang (18, just betrothed to a farmer in Qinghe Market Town). A walled compound at the western edge with 15 mu owner-occupied paddy (the second-largest holding in the village), 4 mu dryland, 2 pigs, 10 chickens.
- **Relationship to Senior Household:** complex. The Lis are not Wang and owe the lineage nothing, but they have held this land for 4 generations — they predate the Wang settlement by a generation. Wang Shouzheng treats Li Asheng as a peer (the only non-Wang he treats this way); the two men drink together at the New Year.
- **Distinctive detail:** Li Asheng owns a complete set of the Three Character Classic, the Hundred Family Surnames, and the Thousand Character Classic — printed books, bought from a Qinghe bookseller 20 years ago. He is the only householder in the village who owns printed books. He can read. His sons and daughter can read. Wang Lun borrows the books for his school.
- **Personality:** shrewd, patient, proud of his literacy. He speaks little at the lineage council (he is not a member) but his opinion is sought in private.
- **Tension ties:** Tension 1 — he is the only villager who could plausibly bid for the salt license himself (he has the capital and the literacy), though he has not. Master Hu suspects him (wrongly) of being the competing bidder; the actual bidder is from the county seat, but Hu's suspicion has poisoned the Li-Hu relationship. Tension 3 — Li Asheng's daughter just betrothed at 18 for a comfortable bride-price; he has offered, privately, to lend Wang Shouzheng the 12 taels to make up the Wang Sanniang shortfall. Wang Shouzheng refused (a debt he could not repay).

#### Household 11: The Wang Second Tenant Household (王二佃房) — Wang Shoucheng
- **Head:** Wang Shoucheng (王守誠, 52), Wang, a cousin of Shouzheng and Shouye (same generation). Occupation: tenant farmer, seasonal fisherman.
- **Household:** wife Lady Yang (楊氏, 48), sons Wang Zongshan (26, married, 1 child) and Wang Zonghe (23, unmarried), daughter Wang Simei (17). 0 mu owned, 6 mu tenanted (subsoil owned by a different market-town landlord than Shouye's), 1 mu dryland, 1 pig (shared with Shouye), 5 chickens.
- **Relationship to Senior Household:** distant cousin-branch. Shoucheng's grandfather and Shouzheng's grandfather were brothers. The branch has been tenant for 3 generations. Polite but distant; Shoucheng resents the Senior Household's prosperity but does not show it.
- **Distinctive detail:** Shoucheng is the village's best fisherman — he can read the Cangli River's currents and know where the fish run. He keeps a small shrine to the river god (河神) in his kitchen, tended daily. He claims his grandfather saw the river god during the great flood 40 years ago.
- **Personality:** secretive, river-superstitious, quietly bitter. He fishes out of season when the household needs food, and hides it.
- **Tension ties:** Tension 2 — Shoucheng was fishing at Black Creek the morning Erniu's axe was found; he saw nothing else. He has been quiet because he was fishing out of season (a lineage prohibition) and fears being questioned further. Tension 3 — his daughter Simei is Wang Sanniang's friend and has heard Sanniang's reluctance; Simei has not told anyone.

#### Household 12: The Wang Bare-Stick Household (王光棍房) — Wang Tianfu and his bachelor sons
- **Head:** Wang Tianfu (王天福, 62), Wang, the oldest living male of the senior-generation cousin-branch; widower (his wife Lady Xu died 15 years ago of consumption).
- **Household:** sons Wang Zonggui (王宗貴, 35, bachelor, day laborer) and Wang Zongbao (王宗寶, 28, bachelor, day laborer and the village gravedigger). A dilapidated compound at the northern edge, 0 mu owned, 0 mu tenanted, 1 mu dryland (rented from the Senior Household in exchange for labor), 3 chickens, no pig. The household survives on day labor, dryland garden produce, and the Senior Household's charity.
- **Relationship to Senior Household:** distant and dependent. Tianfu is Shouzheng's older half-brother (same father, different mother — Tianfu's mother was a concubine who died young). The half-brother relation is the source of the household's awkward standing: Tianfu is older, but Shouzheng inherited the Senior Household because Tianfu's mother was of lower status. Tianfu has never formally contested this; the resentment is a 40-year silence.
- **Distinctive detail:** Wang Zongbao is the village gravedigger — he has dug every grave in the graveyard for 10 years, including Wang Xiaodi's. He knows the graveyard's soil better than anyone. He speaks little. He is the one who, unbidden, weeds Wang Xiaodi's unmarked mound when Lady Chen is not there.
- **Personality (Tianfu):** proud, embittered, silent. (Zongbao): gentle, melancholic, the village's quiet mourner.
- **Tension ties:** Tension 2 — Zongbao dug the last grave in the graveyard, three months ago, for an infant of the cousin-branch; if the council declares Erniu a fugitive, no grave will be dug, and Zongbao privately thinks this is wrong. Tension 3 — both Zonggui and Zongbao are unmarried because the household cannot afford a bride-price; Sanniang's 40-tael bride-price is a constant unspoken contrast in their household.

#### Household 13: The Wang School Household (王學堂房) — Wang Lun the lineage school teacher
- **Head:** Wang Lun (王倫, 41), Wang, the lineage school teacher (per doc 28 §3.3). Occupation: school teacher, diviner, failed county exam candidate (three attempts, three failures).
- **Household:** his aging mother Lady Tang (唐氏, 72) lives with him. He is unmarried — betrothed once at 22 to a market-town girl; the engagement was broken when he failed the exam a third time and the girl's family withdrew. He has not sought another match. They live in the lineage school's side room — two small chambers behind the schoolroom, with a cot, a desk, a bookshelf, a stove. The arrangement is the lineage's obligation to its teacher.
- **Relationship to Senior Household:** Wang Lun is Shouzheng's younger cousin (same generation, different branch). Shouzheng appointed him to the school 12 years ago, after his third exam failure, as a lineage obligation — it was either the school or exile to the market town. Grateful and resentful in equal measure.
- **Distinctive detail:** Wang Lun writes his history of the Cangli Riverlands at night, in the schoolroom, by candlelight — 200 pages, shown to no one (per doc 28 §3.3). The manuscript is hidden under a false bottom in his desk, which he built himself. His mother has read it; she tells no one.
- **Personality:** melancholic, precise, vicariously ambitious for his students. He carries his failure like a stone in his chest.
- **Tension ties:** Tension 1 — Wang Lun carries messages between the Wang household and the Li family in Li Family Creek; he has heard, from a Li cousin in the market town, that the competing salt bidder is connected to the eunuch faction. He has not told Shouzheng because he is not certain. Tension 3 — Wang Lun is the messenger of Wang Sanniang's betrothal and carries the weight of her reluctance without knowing it (he sees her at both schools and notices she is quieter than she used to be; he has not asked why).

#### Household 14: The Wang Second Branch Household (王二房) — Wang Shouli, Wang Meili's father
- **Head:** Wang Shouli (王守禮, 56), Wang, Shouzheng's younger brother. Occupation: middling farmer, lineage elder (the fourth seat on the elder council).
- **Household:** wife Lady Qin (秦氏, 53), sons Wang Zongyao (王宗耀, 25, heir) and Wang Zongping (王宗平, 19, unmarried), daughter Wang Meili (王美麗, 22, at the Cangwu Sect, disowned — see Part 2 §2.1). A compound midway between the Senior Household and the Tenant Household, 8 mu paddy (owned subsoil), 2 mu dryland, 1 pig, 7 chickens.
- **Relationship to Senior Household:** brother household. Wang Shouli is Shouzheng's younger brother; they inherited separately 20 years ago when their father Wang Tianlu divided the holding before death. The two branches are formally allied but privately strained — Shouli considers Shouzheng cautious to the point of cowardice; Shouzheng considers Shouli reckless to the point of folly.
- **Distinctive detail:** Wang Shouli distrusts Old Chen (the hermit above Li Family Creek) because Old Chen's failed Foundation Establishment, 40 years ago, is locally rumored to have killed Old Chen's first cultivation partner. The rumor is false (Old Chen's first partner died in an unrelated beast attack), but Shouli believes it. He sent his daughter Meili to the Cangwu Sect rather than to Old Chen, and formally disowned her when she went to the sect without his blessing (see §2.1).
- **Personality:** stubborn, proud, quick to judge. He loves his daughter and cannot forgive her.
- **Tension ties:** Tension 3 — Wang Shouli, as a lineage elder, sits on the council that will decide Wang Sanniang's betrothal fate; he has privately urged Shouzheng to delay the marriage a year (he wants the Li family to look elsewhere, freeing Sanniang for his own unmarried son Zongping — a scheme Shouli has not spoken aloud). Tension 1 — Shouli has his own grievance against Master Hu (Hu refused him a credit extension three years ago); he would not weep if Hu lost the salt license.

#### Household 15: The Pan Tofu Household (潘豆腐家) — Pan Siniang the tofu-maker
- **Head:** Pan Siniang (潘四娘, 48), non-Wang widow (born a Pan; married a Wang who died; returned to her natal Pan name after his death — an unusual choice the lineage contested but did not override). Occupation: tofu-maker (豆腐), the only one in the village.
- **Household:** two sons, Wang Zongcheng (王宗成, 25) and Wang Zongyuan (王宗元, 22), live with her and work the workshop; they use the Pan surname in trade but the Wang surname at the lineage hall (a compromise the lineage council approved 8 years ago). A compound with a tofu workshop (stone mill, press, vats, stove), 0 mu farmland, 4 chickens. They buy soybeans from the Wangs and the Lis and sell tofu at the morning market.
- **Relationship to Senior Household:** Pan Siniang's late husband, Wang Zongxiao (王宗孝), was Shouzheng's cousin. He died of schistosomiasis 12 years ago. Pan Siniang, instead of remarrying out of the village (as the lineage preferred), asserted her natal Pan family's claim and stayed. Wang Shouzheng, after a year of dispute, recognized her household as a separate entity — not Wang, not non-Wang, but a Pan-Wang household with a foot in each. The arrangement is unique in the village.
- **Distinctive detail:** Pan Siniang is the only woman in the village who has publicly defied the lineage council and won. The widows of the village (Widow Xu, Old Lady Zhou) speak of her with quiet admiration. She has a long scar on her left forearm from her husband's funeral rite — she cut herself to feed his spirit, a Pan-family practice the Wang lineage considers heterodox.
- **Personality:** shrewd, generous, fearless. She is the village's quiet advocate for widows and women.
- **Tension ties:** Tension 1 — Pan Siniang buys salt from Master Hu on credit; if Hu loses the license, her credit arrangement is at risk (the new merchant may not extend credit). She has spoken to Hu directly, asking what she should prepare for; he did not answer. Tension 2 — Pan Siniang has been leaving tofu at Widow Xu's gate every morning since Erniu disappeared; she does not enter, does not speak, just leaves the tofu. This is the Pan-family practice of mourning-with-the-widow.

### 1.3 What Part 1 enables

The village now has 15 named households with heads, ages, occupations, personalities, kinship relations, and connections to the three current tensions. The remaining 16 unnamed households are generator-facing — they are produced lawfully from the household model (doc 04 §2.3) when the player enters them, with the 15 named households as the social spine. The non-Wang social actors with agency are now ten: Master Hu (salt merchant), Widow Xu, Lin Aqiao (carpenter), Old Lady Zhou (midwife), He Laosan (ferryman), Wu Daniu (charcoal-burner), Zhao Tieniu (returned soldier), Li Asheng (yeoman farmer), Pan Siniang (tofu-maker), Yang Erhu (butcher — see below). Against the previous one (Master Hu), the village is now a society, not a diorama.

(Note: a sixteenth household, the Yang Butcher Household (楊屠家) at the village edge — Yang Erhu (楊二虎, 38), Lady Han (韓氏, 35), three children — is named in the generator's seed-parameters but does not appear in the dramatic present of doc 28; it is included in the household roster for completeness and for the funeral-pig and cattle-slaughter licenses it provides. Yang Erhu's daughter Yang Sanniu (7) is the same age Wang Xiaodi was when he died — a quiet resonance the protagonist's mother Lady Chen has noticed and been kind to, giving the girl sweets at the New Year. Yang Erhu has noticed and not asked why.)

### 1.4 The kinship map (the village as a society, not a roster)

The 15 named households are not a list; they are a network. The Wang Senior Household (Household 1) sits at the center, but the network does not radiate from it — it loops through it. The Wang Tenant Household (2) shares a pig with the Senior Household and a landlord with no one. The Salt Merchant Household (3) is bound to the Senior Household by blood (Lady Wang is Shouzheng's sister, exiled from his threshold for 20 years) and to the village by salt. The Lin Household (4) is bound to the Senior Household by Shouzheng's niece (Lady Wang the carpenter's wife). The Widow's Household (5) is bound to the Senior Household by Shouzheng's quiet charity and to the Tenant Household by her late husband (a Wang cousin). The Zhou Household (6) is bound to every household by birth. The He Household (7) is bound to the Salt Merchant by the dock. The Wu Household (8) is bound to the Lin Household by marriage (Lady Lin is Lin Aqiao's sister). The Zhao Household (9) is bound to the Senior Household by Shouzheng's niece (Lady Wang the soldier's wife — a different Lady Wang, a different niece). The Li Household (10) is bound to no Wang by blood but to the Salt Merchant by trade (Li Asheng sells rice to Hu for the river trade). The Wang Second Tenant Household (11) is bound to the Tenant Household by shared tenancy and shared river-god piety. The Wang Bare-Stick Household (12) is bound to the Senior Household by the half-brother wound. The Wang School Household (13) is bound to the Senior Household by cousinship and by the school obligation. The Wang Second Branch Household (14) is bound to the Senior Household by brotherhood and by the disowned daughter (Wang Meili). The Pan Tofu Household (15) is bound to the Senior Household by Pan Siniang's late husband (Shouzheng's cousin) and to the widows (Xu, Zhou) by the practice of mourning-with-the-widow.

This is the society the player enters. Every household has at least one tie to another named household, and most have three or four. The three tensions of doc 28 do not run through the Senior Household alone; they run through the network. The salt license tension touches Hu, He, Li, Zhao, Pan, Yang, and Wang Lun. The missing-son tension touches Widow Xu, Zhou, He, Wu, Shoucheng, Zongbao, and Pan. The betrothal tension touches Shouzheng, Sanniang, Wang Lun, Wang Shouli, Zhao, Simei (Shoucheng's daughter), and Li Asheng. No household is a spectator.

## Part 2: The Three Companions

### 2.1 Wang Meili (王美麗) — the lineage bridge

- **Age at introduction (session ~50, Act 1):** 22. **Age at endgame:** ~120, appearing ~30. **Realm at introduction:** Qi Induction. **Realm at endgame:** Foundation Establishment (if she survives).
- **Household:** the Wang Second Branch Household (Household 14), headed by her father Wang Shouli (王守禮, 56), who formally disowned her when she went to the Cangwu Sect. Her mother Lady Qin (秦氏, 53) dies of old age at ~78 (session ~100, the autumn of the first return). Her older brother Wang Zongyao inherits the household; he is mortal and dies at ~62 (session ~300). She has one younger brother, Wang Zongping (19), unmarried.
- **Personality:** gentle, observant, stubborn in private. She has her father's distrust of Old Chen (which she never quite shook) and her mother's quiet competence. She speaks softly. She remembers everything. She does not lie.
- **Secret:** she perceived qi before the protagonist did — at 14, a year before the protagonist's first onset. She told no one because her father distrusted cultivation and would have beaten her for "demonic influence." She has been quietly practicing on her own, with stolen manuals from the Cangwu Sect's outer library, since 15. When the protagonist's perception became known (Scene 1), she felt a complicated mix of relief (not alone) and resentment (the protagonist got the teacher she could not). She did not speak of this for years. When the protagonist left for Old Chen, she went to her father and demanded to be sent to the Cangwu Sect. Her father refused. She went anyway, walking 100 li to the sect gate at 19. The sect took her as a low-ranking healer-track disciple.
- **Grief:** her mother, Lady Qin, died in the autumn of the protagonist's first year at Old Chen's hermitage. Meili was at the Cangwu Sect. She did not return in time. The death was a fever, like Wang Xiaodi's, three days from onset to end. Meili carries the same guilt the protagonist carries — she was not there. She has not said this to the protagonist. The protagonist does not know. This shared unspoken grief is the silent chord between them.
- **Ambition:** she wants to be the Wang lineage's first cultivator-healer — a woman who can be at the bedside of the dying and ease their passing with qi, who can prevent the fevers that took her mother and Wang Xiaodi. She does not want power; she wants to be present. This is the inverse of the protagonist's ambition (the protagonist wants to undo the past; Meili wants to be present for the future).
- **Why she travels with the protagonist:** she joins the protagonist's circle in Act 2 (session ~400) when her Cangwu Sect duties bring her to the Cangli Riverlands to gather herbs. She has heard of the protagonist's Foundation Establishment (the Wang lineage's pride) and seeks them out at Li Family Creek. She travels with the protagonist because (a) she has, by now, perceived her own qi clearly enough to want a teacher who is not bound by sect politics; (b) she wants to see the village again, which she has not visited in 8 years; (c) she carries her mother's unburned tablet, which she wants to install in the Wang lineage hall (the lineage council refused because her mother was a Second Branch wife of lower standing; Meili wants the protagonist, as the Senior Household's cultivator, to intercede). The third reason is the load-bearing one.
- **Arc across the three acts:**
  - **Act 1 (sessions 1-100):** offstage. The protagonist hears of her only as "your cousin who ran away to the sect." Her father has disowned her. The protagonist does not know her secret grief or her secret qi perception.
  - **Act 2 (sessions 100-500):** becomes a companion at session ~400. By then a Qi Condensation healer-track cultivator, ~38 (appearing ~25). She rejoins the protagonist's circle, travels through the Cangli spirit vein dispute and the first meetings with Leng Qingxue and Liang Zhu. She is the one who insists on returning to the village for the autumn ancestor rite. She is the one who stays at the hermitage when Old Chen is dying (the protagonist arrives too late; Meili is there, having walked from the Cangwu Sect on a rumor that Old Chen was failing). She tells the protagonist, gently, that Old Chen's last circulation route was complete.
  - **Act 3 (sessions 500-1500+):** by now Foundation Establishment (having broken through shortly after the protagonist, around session 200, with Old Chen's quiet guidance via letter). She is the protagonist's first disciple in the "found a lineage" ending. She is the one who greets the protagonist on every return to the village. She is the one who, in the Confront path, helps the protagonist bury Pei Liang's harvested anchors. She is the one who, in the Accommodate path, is harvested by Pei Liang (her anchor taken as leverage; her body found at the edge of the Cangwu foothills, the qi drained).
- **Relationship to the other companions:**
  - **To Liang Zhu:** she finds him irreverent and kind; they bond over a shared fondness for woodwinds (she plays the qin, he plays the xiao, and they have a few evenings of duet at the hermitage before his death). She grieves him quietly, in the way of healers — by tending the people he left behind.
  - **To Leng Qingxue:** initially intimidated (Leng Qingxue is Core Formation, the daughter of a great-sect master, a sword cultivator; Meili is a Qi Condensation healer from a village). She grows into a quiet, mutual respect. They do not become friends — they are too different — but they become allies. Meili is the one who tells Leng Qingxue, in Act 3, "He speaks of you. He does not say what he feels. You should ask him." This is the push that triggers the seventh bout of the Exchange of Cuts.
- **When and how she leaves the party:** she does not leave in the Confront or Transcend paths. In the Accommodate path, she is killed (Pei Liang harvests her anchor) — this is her "leaving." In the "found a lineage" ending, she stays with the protagonist at the hermitage as co-teacher and dies, eventually, of old age at ~490 (Foundation Establishment lifespan), having outlived her own students. Her tablet is installed in the Wang lineage hall — the first woman's tablet to be installed there, which the protagonist achieves by lineage council vote in her 200th year.

### 2.2 Liang Zhu (梁柱) — the first peer

- **Background:** born in a small town 300 li north of Qingzhou Prefectural Seat, the son of a failed merchant. He perceived qi at 14 and was admitted to a small sect (the Iron Bamboo Sect) as a low-ranking disciple. Showed promise — broke through to Foundation Establishment at 35. Was on track for Core Formation when, at 50, he discovered that his sect master was harvesting anchors from mortals in the sect's territory to fuel his own Core Formation breakthrough. Liang Zhu reported this to the Azure Sword Sect (the regional great sect). The Azure Sword investigated, found the accusation true, and executed Liang Zhu's sect master. But the investigation also revealed that Liang Zhu had known about the harvesting for 5 years before reporting it (he discovered it at 45, reported at 50). The Azure Sword judged him complicit by silence. They stripped his sect standing and exiled him. He has been a rogue cultivator for 30 years. He has never returned to his hometown. He has not broken through to Core Formation; he believes he does not deserve to.
- **Realm:** Foundation Establishment (peak), ~80 at introduction, appears ~40. Lifespan to ~200, but he has not attempted Core Formation in 30 years.
- **Personality:** irreverent, kind, weary, funny in a mordant way. He drinks too much. He plays the xiao (簫, vertical bamboo flute) beautifully — the only thing he does that he is proud of. He speaks of his past rarely and never with self-pity. He notices when someone is hurting and sits with them without speaking.
- **Secret:** the 5 years of silence. He discovered his sect master's harvesting at 45. He did not report it because he was afraid — of the sect master's power, of exile, of being wrong (the sect master was his teacher). He reported it at 50 only because he walked in on the harvesting of a child. The child's face has not left him. He has never told anyone about the 5 years. The Azure Sword's judgment was correct.
- **Grief:** the child. Her name was A-Qing (阿青), a mortal girl of 6, the daughter of the sect's herb-gatherer. Liang Zhu had taught her to read. He found her in the sect master's private practice chamber, her anchor half-harvested, her body still warm. He carried her to her father. He reported the sect master the next morning. He has played the xiao for her every year on the anniversary of her death, at whatever tavern he happens to be in, for 30 years. He has never named the tune.
- **Ambition:** he wants to die well. Not bravely — well. He wants to die having done one thing right that he can perceive, in the moment of dying, as right. He does not expect to break through to Core Formation. He does not want to. He wants, in his last moment, to be the man he should have been at 45.
- **Why he travels with the protagonist:** he meets the protagonist at Qinghe Market Town at session ~250. The protagonist, ~50 (Foundation Establishment, appears ~30), is investigating Pei Liang's raid on the Cangwu Sect. Liang Zhu has heard of the raid (the rogue cultivator network is efficient). He approaches the protagonist because he heard the description of Pei Liang's qi-signature — the corrupted, borrowed residue — and recognized it. He has perceived that signature before: his sect master's qi, in the months before exposure, was similarly stained. He knows what Pei Liang is. He travels with the protagonist because Pei Liang is the second sect master, and Liang Zhu will not be silent a second time.
- **Arc across the three acts:**
  - **Act 2 (sessions 250-500):** the protagonist's first peer and friend outside the sect and the village. He travels with the protagonist through the Cangli spirit vein dispute, the first meetings with Leng Qingxue, the first encounters with Pei Liang's network. He teaches the rogue cultivator's skills: how to read a sect's politics from its outer hall, how to find safe lodging in a strange town, how to spot a hustler. He plays the xiao at the hermitage on the evenings when the protagonist is weary. He tells the protagonist, drunk, at session ~350, about A-Qing. The protagonist does not know what to say. Liang Zhu does not expect them to.
  - **Act 3 (sessions 500-1500+):** joins the pursuit of Pei Liang. He knows he will not survive it (Foundation Establishment peak against a Nascent Soul antagonist). He joins because he will not be silent a second time. At session ~700 he identifies one of Pei Liang's harvest-stores by its qi-signature — the same stained residue as his sect master's. At session ~750 he tells the protagonist, soberly, that he does not expect to see the end of the pursuit. The protagonist does not accept this. Liang Zhu does not argue.
- **His death:** at session ~800, the protagonist and companions are ambushed by Pei Liang at a cave system in the Cangwu foothills (one of Pei Liang's harvest-stores). Pei Liang, now Nascent Soul (approaching Spirit Severance), outclasses them all. The protagonist is wounded; Leng Qingxue holds the line; Meili drags the protagonist toward the cave mouth. Liang Zhu stays behind to delay Pei Liang. He plays a single note on the xiao — a qi-residue technique he developed, leaving his signature in the qi of the cave. The note is the same tune he has played for A-Qing every year for 30 years. He plays it now for the protagonist — a final teaching, in the form of a sound. Pei Liang strikes him down. His anchor disperses (Pei Liang's harvest-technique, applied at the moment of death, prevents the anchor from entering bardo — the dispersal is immediate and irreversible). The protagonist, retreating, perceives the dispersal. The protagonist screams — the only time in the game they do.
- **What his death means to the protagonist:** the second grief that mirrors the first (Wang Xiaodi's). The protagonist, again, was not enough to save someone they loved. The difference: this time, the protagonist was present. The grief is different — not the guilt of absence but the grief of presence. In the months after, the protagonist perceives Liang Zhu's last note as a residue-trace in the qi of every cave they enter. The trace is faint and fading, but it is there. The protagonist carries it for the rest of the game. This is the Residual Error Sense's deepest use: perceiving the teaching of the dead. The protagonist's Nascent Soul breakthrough (session ~1000-1200) is driven, in part, by the desire to perceive Liang Zhu's last note one more time, at a higher realm, before the trace fades entirely.

### 2.3 Leng Qingxue (冷清雪) — the sect dissident and romance

- **Position in the sect:** the only daughter of Leng Wushuang (冷無霜, Azure Sword Sect master, Core Formation, 340). Her father (Leng Wushuang's husband) was a Foundation Establishment elder of the sect who died in a tribulation 80 years ago, before Leng Qingxue was conceived (Leng Wushuang, at Core Formation, preserved his seed in a qi-bound vessel and conceived Leng Qingxue 40 years later — a technique the sect considers legitimate but unusual). Leng Qingxue is also the formal student of Elder Han (韓長老, the sect's sword-arts master, Core Formation, 280). She is, by any measure, the sect's heir apparent — except that she has publicly opposed her mother's expansionist policy, which has made her politically untouchable.
- **Realm:** Core Formation (mid-stage), ~120 at introduction, appears ~30. Broke through at 80 — fast for the Azure Sword Sect (average 120). Considered a generational talent. Has not attempted Nascent Soul; does not intend to until the political situation resolves.
- **Reason for dissenting:** the Azure Sword Sect, under Leng Wushuang, is quietly expanding toward the Cangli Riverlands to claim the latent spirit vein beneath the Cangwu Mountains (doc 24 §4.3, doc 26 §2.3). The expansion is lawful (the vein is unclaimed) but ruthless — the sect's outer hall has been buying land, establishing "survey posts" that are in fact military outposts, and pressuring the Cangwu Sect to submit as a vassal. Leng Qingxue's dissent is principled: (a) the Cangwu Sect's claim, while weak, is older and should be adjudicated, not overrun; (b) the expansion will provoke the Jade Void Holy Land (which has its own claim to the broader vein system); (c) the expansion is, at root, her mother's grief — Leng Wushuang's husband died in the Cangwu Mountains 80 years ago on a survey mission for the vein, and Leng Qingxue believes her mother's expansion is an 80-year act of mourning that has hardened into policy. She has said the third point to her mother, once, in private. Her mother did not speak to her for a year. The dissent has been public for 20 years.
- **Personality:** precise, cold in manner (her name means "clear snow"), warm in private, slow to laugh but quick to smile. Sword cultivator — qi is metal-phase dominant, sharp, fast. Speaks in short sentences. Does not waste words. In private, surprisingly funny — a dry wit the protagonist does not expect from a Core Formation great-sect daughter.
- **Secret:** she has been corresponding, for 30 years, with a Core Formation cultivator of the Jade Void Holy Land — Xu Yunfeng (許雲峰, 200), whom she met at a regional conference. The correspondence is political, not romantic. They have been quietly building a coalition to oppose both the Azure Sword expansion and the Jade Void counter-claim, proposing a regional compact that would leave the Cangwu Mountains' spirit vein under the Cangwu Sect's stewardship with Azure Sword and Jade Void as guarantors. The correspondence is treasonous, technically — she is negotiating against her own sect's policy. Her mother does not know. Xu Yunfeng is the only person outside the Cangli Riverlands who knows the full shape of her dissent.
- **Grief:** her father. She never met him (he died 80 years before her conception), but she grew up in his shadow. Her mother never spoke of him except as a survey-mission casualty. Leng Qingxue found his journals in the sect archive at 60 — 200 pages of careful observation of the Cangwu Mountains' qi-topography, written in the year before his death. The journals are the reason she knows the Cangwu Mountains as well as she does. She has read them 40 times. She has never told her mother she found them.
- **Ambition:** she wants the regional compact. She wants the Cangwu Sect to steward the vein. She wants her mother to retire (Leng Wushuang is 340; Core Formation lifespan ~500; she has 160 years left, and Leng Qingxue does not want to spend them in opposition). She wants, personally, to be free of the sect without leaving it — to be a sword cultivator who chooses her own contracts, her own travels, her own (eventually) partner. She does not want to be the sect's heir. She does not want to be her mother's successor.
- **Why she travels with the protagonist:** she is sent by the Azure Sword Sect at session ~200 to investigate Pei Liang's raid on the Cangwu Sect. The sect's official reason: assess the threat to Azure Sword interests in the region. Her private reason: assess the protagonist (the Azure Sword has heard of the Wang lineage's first cultivator; Leng Wushuang wants to know if the protagonist is potential ally, pawn, or obstacle). She travels with the protagonist initially as an observer. She stays because she finds, in the protagonist, the first person in 100 years who does not want anything from her — the protagonist is not intimidated by her sect, not interested in her mother, not trying to court her. The protagonist is, in her assessment, the only honest cultivator she has met since Xu Yunfeng.
- **The courtship verb — the Exchange of Cuts (交劍, jiaojian):** two cultivators spar. The loser owes the winner a truthful answer to one question. The questions cannot be about combat; they must be about the self. Three bouts (three questions, three answers) = courtship acknowledged. Seven bouts = betrothal. Either party may decline a bout; declining three times ends the courtship. The Exchange of Cuts makes courtship a kind of cultivation: you learn the other's qi through their sword, the other's self through their answers. The truth-debt is enforced by the simulation — a lie in an answer registers as a karmic trace (doc 24 §2.1) and is perceivable, at Core Formation, by the wronged party.
  - First bout (session ~300, after the Cangli spirit vein dispute): Leng Qingxue wins. Asks: "Why did you join the Cangwu Sect?" The protagonist must answer truthfully.
  - Second bout (session ~400): the protagonist wins. Asks: "Why do you oppose your mother?" Leng Qingxue answers truthfully — with the third point (her mother's grief).
  - Third bout (session ~500, after Core Formation): they spar to a draw. No questions. They sit in silence. This is the courtship acknowledgment.
  - Subsequent bouts (sessions ~700-1200): developing toward betrothal.
  - Seventh bout (before the final confrontation with Pei Liang): the question, asked by whichever party the player is not, is "Will you come back?" The answer, whatever it is, is the truth.
- **Arc across the three acts:**
  - **Act 2 (sessions 200-500):** introduced as the sect investigator. Initially cold, then cautiously warm, then — by session ~500, after the third bout — openly allied with the protagonist. Provides the Azure Sword Sect's intelligence on Pei Liang. Does not, in Act 2, break with her sect. Her dissent is still private.
  - **Act 3 (sessions 500-1500+):** the break. At session ~700, when Liang Zhu identifies one of Pei Liang's harvest-stores, Leng Qingxue recognizes the harvest-technique's signature — it is the same technique her mother's late husband was investigating when he died 80 years ago. The realization: Pei Liang's technique is descended from the same source as the technique Leng Wushuang's husband died investigating. The Cangwu Mountains have been a harvest-ground for demonic cultivators for centuries. Leng Wushuang's expansion is, in part, an 80-year war against this legacy — but the war has hardened into territorial ambition. Leng Qingxue confronts her mother at session ~900 (a scene at the Azure Sword Sect's main hall). Her mother disowns her. Leng Qingxue leaves the sect. She rejoins the protagonist as a full companion, not an observer.
- **Whether she can die:** yes, in two ways.
  - In the **Confront path**, if the protagonist loses the final confrontation with Pei Liang, Leng Qingxue (present) is killed trying to protect the protagonist's retreat. Her anchor disperses. The protagonist, if they survive, returns to Wang Family Bend and reports her death to her mother — a scene in which Leng Wushuang, for the first time in 80 years, weeps.
  - In the **Accommodate path**, if the protagonist accepts Pei Liang's offer, Leng Qingxue refuses to join. She attempts to leave with Wang Meili. Pei Liang, perceiving her as a threat, kills her. Her anchor is harvested. The protagonist perceives this and does nothing — the moment that defines the Accommodate path's moral cost.
  - In the **Transcend path**, she survives. She is the one who, in the "cross the tribulation" ending, stays in the mortal world and continues the protagonist's work. She is the one who, in the "final death" ending (if the protagonist fails the Tribulation Crossing), reports the protagonist's death to Wang Family Bend. She lives, in the Transcend path, to ~500 (Nascent Soul lifespan), and dies at her mother's grave, having buried her 160 years before.

### 2.4 The three companions together

The three companions are not a party in the Baldur's Gate sense. They are three witnesses, each with a different tempo of aging, each with a different relationship to the protagonist's grief. Wang Meili ages with the village (slower than mortals, faster than the protagonist) and is the lineage bridge. Liang Zhu ages with the protagonist (both Foundation Establishment) but dies before the end — the first peer lost. Leng Qingxue outlives the protagonist's mortal world and is the political partner. The three together — lineage, peer, romance — are the protagonist's social world. The genre's solitude is preserved because the protagonist is always the point of view and always the one who outlives.

### 2.5 The companions' key scenes

Each companion has three to four scripted scenes (the "narrative-critical" beats per doc 26 §11.2) that the simulation flags and the player encounters by playing. These are the moments the companion's arc turns.

**Wang Meili:**
- **Session ~400 — the reunion.** Meili arrives at Li Family Creek as the protagonist is consulting Old Chen. She carries her mother's unburned tablet. The protagonist must choose whether to intercede with the lineage council (the load-bearing choice). The scene establishes her as a companion and her third reason for traveling (§2.1).
- **Session ~445 — the vigil.** Meili, having heard a rumor that Old Chen is failing, walks from the Cangwu Sect to the hermitage. She is there when the protagonist arrives too late (session ~450). She tells the protagonist, gently, that Old Chen's last circulation route was complete. This scene is the protagonist's first encounter with someone who was present when they were not — the inverse of the protagonist's own pattern.
- **Session ~900 — the confrontation with Wang Shouli.** Meili, now Foundation Establishment, returns to Wang Family Bend with the protagonist and confronts her father, who is old and frail. The father weeps. The reconciliation is partial. The scene is the protagonist's first witness to a mortal reconciliation that cultivation made possible (Meili's extended lifespan gave her the time to be ready; her father's mortality gave him the time to soften).
- **Session ~1500 (Accommodate path only) — the death.** Meili is harvested by Pei Liang. Her body is found at the edge of the Cangwu foothills. The protagonist perceives, in the harvest-residue, the texture of her qi — the healer-track signature, the gentle steady warmth she cultivated for 80 years. The protagonist, if they chose the Accommodate path, must perceive this and live with it.

**Liang Zhu:**
- **Session ~350 — the confession.** Drunk, at a tavern in Qinghe Market Town, Liang Zhu tells the protagonist about A-Qing. He does not cry. The protagonist does not know what to say. The scene is the first time the protagonist hears the shape of complicity-by-silence — the same shape the protagonist's own Accommodate path would take.
- **Session ~700 — the recognition.** Liang Zhu identifies one of Pei Liang's harvest-stores by its qi-signature. The recognition is not intellectual; it is somatic — his hands shake. The protagonist perceives, in Liang Zhu's qi, the resonance of a 30-year grief surfacing. This scene is the moment the pursuit becomes personal for Liang Zhu, not just for the protagonist.
- **Session ~750 — the warning.** Liang Zhu tells the protagonist, soberly, that he does not expect to see the end of the pursuit. The protagonist does not accept this. Liang Zhu does not argue. The scene is the companion's last full conversation with the protagonist; the dialogue system records every word the player chooses, and Liang Zhu's residue-trace (perceivable after his death) carries the echo of those words.
- **Session ~800 — the last note.** Liang Zhu plays the xiao one last time. The note is a qi-residue technique; it is also the tune he has played for A-Qing for 30 years. The protagonist, retreating, perceives the dispersal. The protagonist screams.

**Leng Qingxue:**
- **Session ~300 — the first bout.** The Exchange of Cuts begins. She wins; she asks "Why did you join the Cangwu Sect?" The protagonist must answer truthfully. The scene establishes the courtship verb as a real mechanic, not a cutscene.
- **Session ~500 — the third bout (the draw).** They spar to a draw. No questions. They sit in silence. This is the courtship acknowledgment — the moment the relationship becomes real, expressed as the absence of a question.
- **Session ~700 — the recognition.** Leng Qingxue recognizes the harvest-technique's signature as the one her father was investigating. The scene is her grief becoming political — the moment her private dissent (her father's journals) and her public dissent (the expansion) merge with the antagonist.
- **Session ~900 — the break.** Leng Qingxue confronts her mother at the Azure Sword Sect's main hall. Her mother disowns her. The scene is the romance's cost made concrete: the protagonist's partner has given up her sect, her lineage, her duty, for principle and for the protagonist. The player perceives, in Leng Qingxue's qi after the break, a new signature — lighter, sharper, the qi of someone who has chosen.
- **Session ~1500 — the seventh bout.** Before the final confrontation. The question, asked by whichever party the player is not: "Will you come back?" The answer, whatever it is, is the truth. This is the last scene before the final confrontation, and its residue-trace carries through the confrontation and into the ending.

## Part 3: The Antagonist — Pei Liang (裴良)

### 3.1 Background and realm progression

Pei Liang (裴良) was born 180 years before game start, in a small town 200 li west of the Cangwu Mountains. His father was a country doctor; his mother died in childbirth. He perceived qi at 12 and was admitted to the Iron Bamboo Sect (鐵竹派, a small sect 50 li from his hometown) as a low-ranking disciple. His sect master was Daoist Iron Bamboo (鐵竹道人, Foundation Establishment, deceased at 110 when Pei Liang was 60). The Iron Bamboo Sect had no political ambition and no enemies; it was dissolved 80 years ago after Daoist Iron Bamboo's death, its disciples scattered. Pei Liang was the sect's most talented disciple, but not its heir — he left at 70 to pursue Core Formation as a rogue.

At 85 (95 years before game start), while traveling home to visit his dying father, Pei Liang encountered the aftermath of Zhou Wugui's massacre (doc 24 §4.4 — Zhou Wugui killed three villages in the Cangwu foothills, harvesting their anchors to fuel a breakthrough attempt; the Azure Sword Sect's previous sect master pursued and killed him). Pei Liang arrived two weeks after the killings, helped bury the dead, and in one of the villages found a fragment of Zhou Wugui's harvest-manual — a single page, hidden in a floorboard, describing the anchor-harvest technique in incomplete but workable detail. He kept the page. He did not know why.

He spent 30 years (85 to 115) reconstructing the technique from the single page. He tested on animals first, then on dying mortals (consensual — he asked, they agreed, in exchange for qi-healing for their families), then on the freshly dead. He did not harvest a living mortal's anchor until 140, when Scribe Yan (閻, the corrupt Precelestial official, doc 26 §2.3) contacted him through an intermediary and offered "extended window" anchors — the anchors of the recently dead whose bardo dispersal Scribe Yan had unlawfully delayed. Pei Liang's technique, combined with Scribe Yan's corruption, made harvesting lawful-seeming: the anchors were "already past their window," technically, even though Scribe Yan had artificially extended the window. Pei Liang has been harvesting for 40 years. He has harvested approximately 200 anchors.

**Realm progression:** Qi Condensation at 25. Foundation Establishment at 50. Core Formation at 95 (shortly after finding Zhou Wugui's fragment). Nascent Soul at 150 (the breakthrough was fueled, in part, by harvested anchors — the first time he used the harvest for power, not research). Spirit Severance (attempted) at ~400, at the time of the final confrontation. The attempt is fueled by the largest harvest-store he has ever assembled, including Wang Xiaodi's anchor.

### 3.2 Personality, secret, grief, motivation

- **Personality:** calm, courteous, soft-spoken. He does not monologue. He does not threaten. He speaks to mortals and cultivators with the same quiet courtesy. He is, in conversation, almost gentle. He is also — and this is what makes him terrifying — completely convinced that what he is doing is right. He is not a sadist; he takes no pleasure in the harvest. He considers it a regrettable necessity. He has wept, in private, after every harvest. He has not stopped.
- **Secret:** he has harvested the anchor of his own son. His son, Pei An (裴安), was a Foundation Establishment cultivator who died in a law tribulation 50 years ago (when Pei Liang was 130). The tribulation tested a minor Acquired-law amendment Pei An was attempting; he failed. Pei An's anchor, by lawful process, should have entered bardo and dispersed. Pei Liang, in the moment of his son's death, harvested the anchor — the first time he harvested an anchor he loved. He has kept it in his harvest-store for 50 years. He speaks to it, in private, every night. He believes his son's anchor is still conscious, still him, still Pei An. He is wrong (an anchor in a harvest-store is not conscious; it is fuel, slowly drained). He has not been able to verify this because the harvest-technique, by design, prevents the cultivator from perceiving the harvested anchor's interiority — to perceive it would be to perceive its suffering, and the technique's continued use requires the cultivator not to perceive. The secret is: he is, at the deepest level, harvesting anchors to keep his son's anchor company. The 200 anchors are, in his grief-addled heart, companions for Pei An.
- **Grief:** Pei An. His only child. His wife (Lady Mu, 木氏) died 80 years ago in a beast attack, before Pei An's tribulation. Pei An was the only person Pei Liang had left. When Pei An died in the tribulation, Pei Liang's grief broke something in him — the part that distinguished between "I will not let my son go" and "I will undo what the law has done." He has not been able to distinguish the two since.
- **Motivation (why he harvests anchors):** Pei Liang wants Spirit Severance because Spirit Severance is the realm of externalized law (化神, doc 03 Station 7). He wants to author a domain where anchor-harvesting is lawful, where his son's anchor can be restored to consciousness, where the 200 anchors in his store can be returned to their bodies. He believes this is possible. It is not — the harvest is irreversible; the drained anchors cannot be reconstituted; Spirit Severance authorship is bounded and cannot undo the harvest. His motivation is not power. It is grief. He wants his son back. The 200 harvested anchors are, in his mind, the cost of a grief-undoing law he believes he can write at Spirit Severance. He is wrong about the law. He is wrong about his son (Pei An's anchor is not conscious). He is right about one thing: the law, as it stands, is cruel. The bardo window is bounded, and the bound is a grief the cosmos does not acknowledge. His dissent from the law is, at root, a dissent from a cosmos that took his son and called it lawful.

### 3.3 The dark mirror

The protagonist and Pei Liang have both lost someone (Wang Xiaodi / Pei An). Both want them back. Both have, at some level, considered breaking the law to do so (the protagonist's Accommodate path is exactly this — joining Pei Liang). The difference is that the protagonist chose to mourn (Scene 5, the successful Foundation Establishment, requires mourning first); Pei Liang chose not to. The dark mirror: Pei Liang is what the protagonist would have become if they had not mourned. This is the load-bearing thematic parallel. When the protagonist perceives Wang Xiaodi's anchor in Pei Liang's harvest-store (session ~550), they perceive, in the same store, Pei An's anchor — and recognize, in the recognition of Pei An, the recognition of their own grief in Pei Liang's. This is the moment the personal conflict and the antagonist merge (doc 26 §4.3).

### 3.4 Arc across the three acts

- **When the player first hears of him (Act 2, session ~200):** the raid on the Cangwu Sect. The protagonist, visiting the Cangwu Sect on sect business, survives because Pei Liang does not consider a Foundation Establishment cultivator worth killing (doc 26 §3.3). The protagonist perceives Pei Liang's qi-signature: stained, corrupted by the residue of dozens of harvested anchors. The protagonist does not yet know his name. The Cangwu Sect's records, which Pei Liang stole, contained the anchor-signatures of every mortal who had died in the region for 50 years — the catalog Pei Liang uses to select harvest targets. The protagonist does not yet know this.
- **When the player first meets him (Act 2, session ~350):** the protagonist, investigating the raid, tracks Pei Liang to a village 200 li west of the Cangwu Mountains — Pei Liang's hometown, which he visits once a year on the anniversary of his father's death. The protagonist perceives him across a market square. Pei Liang perceives the protagonist. They do not speak. Pei Liang, recognizing the protagonist from the raid, chooses not to kill them — too low-realm to be a threat, and he is, in his own way, courteous. He nods. The protagonist, in the same moment, perceives Pei Liang's anchored grief — a faint resonance, in Pei Liang's qi, of a held anchor the protagonist cannot yet identify. The protagonist does not know what they are perceiving. They will remember it at Core Formation.
- **The revelation and first fight (Act 2/3 boundary, session ~550):** the protagonist, now Core Formation, pursues Pei Liang to one of his harvest-stores in the Cangwu foothills. They fight. The protagonist is outmatched (Core Formation vs. Nascent Soul). During the fight, the protagonist perceives Wang Xiaodi's anchor in the store — the Residual Error Sense, extended at Core Formation to perceive others' anchors, confirms the signature: it matches the residue-traces Wang Xiaodi left in the protagonist's own practice decades ago (the protagonist, as a Qi Condensation cultivator, practiced near the infant Wang Xiaodi; the infant's anchor left faint traces in the protagonist's residue). The fight ends with the protagonist wounded and Pei Liang gone (Pei Liang does not kill the protagonist; he is courteous; he perceives the protagonist's grief and recognizes, in it, his own). This is the moment the personal conflict and the antagonist merge.
- **The pursuit (Act 3, sessions ~600-1000):** the protagonist, with companions, pursues Pei Liang. Liang Zhu dies in this arc (session ~800). The protagonist breaks through to Nascent Soul (sessions ~1000-1200).
- **The final confrontation (Act 3, session ~1200-1500):** the protagonist, at Nascent Soul or Spirit Severance, tracks Pei Liang to the Cangwu Mountains' latent spirit vein — the site of Pei Liang's attempted Spirit Severance breakthrough.

### 3.5 The three possible fates

- **Confront (the protagonist kills him):** Pei Liang, in the moment of death, perceives his son's anchor in his own harvest-store one last time — and, in perceiving it, finally perceives that it is not conscious. He has been keeping a held grief, not a held son. His last words (the protagonist perceives them via Residual Error Sense) are: "He is not there. He was never there." The harvested anchors, freed, enter bardo — but the bardo windows have closed. The anchors disperse lawfully. The protagonist perceives Wang Xiaodi's anchor disperse, finally and lawfully, into the cosmos's law-substrate. This is the teaching's resolution: Wang Xiaodi is gone, finally and lawfully, and the protagonist lets him go.
- **Accommodate (the protagonist joins him):** the protagonist accepts Pei Liang's offer — shares Wang Xiaodi's anchor, becomes complicit in the harvest, gains Pei Liang's protection and a share of his Spirit Severance breakthrough. The protagonist's karmic trace becomes heavy. At the next tribulation (karmic tribulation, doc 24 §2.1), the protagonist cannot sustain the ledger. Their anchor disperses. In the moment before dispersal, the protagonist perceives — for the first time — that Pei An's anchor in Pei Liang's store is not conscious. The protagonist perceives, in this, the futility of the accommodation: the harvest cannot undo grief; it can only compound it. The protagonist dies knowing this. Pei Liang, surviving, continues the harvest. (Per doc 26 §7.3, this is ending 3, the corrupted variant.)
- **Transcend (the protagonist transcends him):** the protagonist, at Mahayana (having crossed Tribulation and authored the law that closes the bardo-harvest loophole), does not kill Pei Liang directly. The law, taking effect, disperses Pei Liang's harvest-store lawfully — including Pei An's anchor. Pei Liang, in the moment of his son's anchor's dispersal, perceives (the protagonist's law, by design, makes the harvested anchors' interiority perceivable in their final moment) that Pei An is not there. Pei Liang, broken by the perception, does not resist. The protagonist does not kill him; Pei Liang, anchor-less (his own anchor, sustained by the harvest, collapses when the harvest is gone), disperses. It is not a killing. It is a dying. The protagonist perceives, in Pei Liang's last residue, a recognition — Pei Liang perceiving, finally, that his son was not in the store. The protagonist weeps. This is the Transcend path's deepest cost: the antagonist is not defeated; he is shown, and the showing kills him.

### 3.6 The Zhou Wugui inheritance and the third-generation line

Pei Liang is, per doc 26 §15, "the third-generation inheritor of Zhou Wugui's anchor-harvesting technique." The line runs as follows. **Zhou Wugui** (周無鬼), the first-generation demonic cultivator, was killed 95 years ago by the Azure Sword Sect's previous sect master after harvesting three Cangwu foothills villages (doc 24 §4.4). His technique died with him — except for the single page Pei Liang found. **The unnamed second-generation inheritor** was a rogue cultivator who, 70 years ago, acquired fragments of Zhou Wugui's manual through the underworld market and attempted a smaller harvest in a coastal village 100 li east. The Azure Sword Sect's outer hall investigated, killed the cultivator, and destroyed the fragments they found — but did not find all of them. The Azure Sword's records of this second case are what Leng Wushuang's late husband was investigating when he died in the Cangwu Mountains 80 years ago; this is the connection Leng Qingxue discovers at session ~700 (§2.3). **Pei Liang** is the third-generation inheritor, the one who reconstructed the technique from the single page and, with Scribe Yan's corruption, made it scalable. He is the most successful of the three — the only one to reach Nascent Soul on harvested fuel, the only one to attempt Spirit Severance. He is also the last. The protagonist's law (Transcend path) closes the loophole that made all three possible; the protagonist's sword (Confront path) closes the line by killing its last inheritor; the protagonist's complicity (Accommodate path) continues the line by becoming its fourth.

The line is also a mirror of the Wang lineage. The Wang lineage is three generations of mortal farmers (Wang Tianlu, Wang Shouzheng, the protagonist) producing one cultivator (the protagonist) who founds a teaching lineage. The Zhou Wugui line is three generations of demonic cultivators (Zhou Wugui, the unnamed second, Pei Liang) producing one law-breaker (Pei Liang) who attempts to found a domain. The two lines run parallel through the same region for 95 years. They meet at the Cangwu Mountains' latent spirit vein, where the protagonist's lineage-founding and Pei Liang's domain-founding both attempt to root. The meeting is the final confrontation. The protagonist's choice (Confront, Accommodate, Transcend) is the choice of which line continues.

## Part 4: The NPCs Who Die

### 4.1 Wang Lun (王倫) — dies at 47, of pneumonia, the winter before the first return

- **When:** the winter before the first return (session ~95, the winter of the protagonist's age 22). Wang Lun is 47 (he was 41 at game start; six years have passed).
- **Of what:** pneumonia. A winter fever that developed into pneumonia. He was ill for three weeks. His mother Lady Tang (78) was at his bedside; she survived him by six months (she dies in the summer after, of pneumonia and grief — the village says grief, but the truth is also pneumonia).
- **What the protagonist was doing:** the protagonist was at Old Chen's hermitage, practicing for Foundation Establishment (Scene 5 is the autumn of age 23; Wang Lun dies the winter before). Word was sent (Lady Tang walked to Li Family Creek to send it), but the messenger arrived the day of the funeral. The protagonist did not return in time.
- **What the protagonist finds at the first return (session ~100):** a new tablet in the graveyard. The protagonist perceives, in the lineage hall, the residue of Wang Lun's last lesson — a faint trace of the qi he perceived once, decades ago, fading. The protagonist weeps. This is the second grief of absence (the first being Wang Xiaodi's) — the protagonist was not there. Again.
- **What survives him:** his 200-page history of the Cangli Riverlands, found by the protagonist in the schoolroom's false-bottom desk after Lady Tang's death. The protagonist reads it. In the "found a lineage" ending, the protagonist finishes it (adding the next 50 years, including the protagonist's own cultivation) and places it in the lineage hall's archive.

### 4.2 Old Chen (陳老) — dies at ~165, winter solstice, session ~450

- **When:** the winter solstice (冬至) of the protagonist's age ~130 (session ~450). Old Chen is ~165 (extended by his partial Foundation Establishment integration, decades ago, which left him at Qi Condensation peak but granted some lifespan — doc 24 §1.3). He appears ~95.
- **Of what:** old age. He dies in his sleep, at his hermitage on the hill above Li Family Creek. His wife, Lady Chen of the hermitage (a different Lady Chen, not related to the protagonist's mother), died years before at ~105 (when Old Chen was ~112) — her death was the second great grief of his long life, after his failed Foundation Establishment. Old Chen, alone in the hermitage for 53 years, goes to sleep and does not wake.
- **What the protagonist was doing:** the protagonist, ~130 (Foundation Establishment, appears ~45), is traveling to consult Old Chen about Pei Liang's traces. The protagonist has been perceiving Pei Liang's corrupted qi-signature in the region and wants Old Chen's assessment. The protagonist arrives the next morning. The hermitage is cold. Old Chen is in his bed, still, the breath stopped. The protagonist perceives, with Foundation Establishment perception, the faint residue of Old Chen's last practice — a slow Qi Condensation circulation route, the one Old Chen practiced every morning for 60 years, traced in the qi of the practice room, fading.
- **What it means:** Old Chen's death is the genre's central tragic engine (time-debt) at the personal scale. The protagonist's teacher is gone. The protagonist has outlived him. The protagonist's Residual Error Sense reads, in the fading practice-route, Old Chen's last circulation — steady, balanced, complete. Old Chen died whole. This is the teacher's last lesson: a whole death, after a long life, is the cultivation the teacher never achieved in realm but achieved in fact. The protagonist buries Old Chen next to Lady Chen of the hermitage, on the hill above Li Family Creek. The protagonist, in the months after, cleans the hermitage, repairs the garden, and — in a moment the player may or may not choose — takes up residence. This is the seed of the "found a lineage" ending.

### 4.3 Wang Shouzheng (王守正) — dies at 76, of schistosomiasis, session ~210

- **When:** the autumn of the protagonist's age ~34 (session ~210). Wang Shouzheng is 76.
- **Of what:** schistosomiasis (water-disease, the same that killed his father Wang Tianlu 14 years before, per doc 24 §4.4). He has been careful (the lineage head can afford to boil water and avoid the infected streams), but a flood the previous spring contaminated the village's well, and he was infected. He dies over the course of a year, slowly, with Lady Chen at his bedside.
- **What the protagonist was doing:** the protagonist, ~34 (Foundation Establishment, appears ~28), is at the Cangwu Sect. Word is sent. The protagonist, this time, returns in time. The protagonist is at the bedside for the last three days. Wang Shouzheng, lucid, asks the protagonist about cultivation — what it is like, whether the protagonist is happy, whether the path was worth it. The protagonist must answer (the dialogue system; the answer is the protagonist's, but the question is the genre's central question).
- **Last words:** to Lady Chen, "Tell Tianlu I did my best." (Tianlu is his father, dead 14 years by then.)
- **What it means:** the protagonist, this time, was present. The grief is different from Wang Lun's or Wang Xiaodi's — it is the grief of presence, not absence. This is the protagonist's first experience of being present for a mortal death. It changes them. Wang Shouzheng's tablet is installed in the lineage hall. The lineage headship passes to his eldest son, Wang Zongxian (then 50, mortal).

### 4.4 Lady Chen (陳氏, the mother) — dies at 82, of old age, session ~310

- **When:** the spring of the protagonist's age ~44 (session ~310). Lady Chen is 82.
- **Of what:** old age. A gentle decline over the winter, then a failure to wake one morning.
- **What the protagonist was doing:** the protagonist, ~44 (Foundation Establishment, appears ~32), returns to Wang Family Bend for the spring ancestor rite and finds the household in mourning — Lady Chen died three days before, the morning of the protagonist's arrival. The protagonist, again, was almost in time. The protagonist perceives, in the household, the residue of Lady Chen's last weaving — the cloth she had been working on, half-finished, on the loom. The protagonist finishes the cloth. It is the only thing the protagonist takes with them when they leave.
- **What it means:** this is the genre's central moment — the protagonist's return to find the mother aged or dead. The protagonist has outlived her by 38 years of cultivation (16 to 44) and 28 years of mortal time (54 to 82). The time-debt is now personal. The protagonist, at her grave, perceives the residue of her anchor — faint, almost gone (the protagonist, at Foundation Establishment, cannot yet perceive anchors fully; this comes at Core Formation). The protagonist, at Core Formation (session ~500), perceives her anchor in bardo one last time, before it disperses. The protagonist lets her go. This is the protagonist's second act of anchor-perception (the first is Old Chen's, session ~450), and the second letting-go.

### 4.5 Wang Zongde (王宗德) — the rival, dies on a trajectory dependent on the duel outcome

- **At game start:** 24, the "bare stick" of the Tenant Household, unmarried, resentful, studying with a traveling hustler. Six years older than the protagonist.
- **Scene 3 (age 24, protagonist age 18, session ~40):** the duel (doc 06 Scene 3). The outcome sets the trajectory.
- **Trajectories:**
  - **If the player wins narrowly (the preferred outcome):** Zongde is beaten but not humiliated. Over the next years, he reluctantly comes to respect the protagonist. He marries at 28 (with the protagonist's quiet intercession with the lineage council, which secures a bride from a poor cousin-branch). He has two children. He continues to practice the hustler's techniques but more carefully — he seeks Wang Lun, who can read the manuals and identify the frauds. He never becomes a real cultivator (his foundation is too unstable), but he becomes a competent Qi Induction practitioner — enough to perceive qi faintly, not enough to cultivate. He dies at 62 (session ~300) of a lung infection. The protagonist attends the funeral. The protagonist perceives, in Zongde's residue, the texture of a long life lived at the edge of cultivation without crossing — a life the protagonist nearly had.
  - **If the player wins easily (Zongde humiliated):** resentment deepens. Zongde leaves the village at 26, travels north, joins a small sect as a low-ranking disciple. Breaks through to Qi Condensation at 35. Returns to the village at 40 (session ~200, the same time as Pei Liang's raid — Zongde is not involved, but he is in the region). He is now a Qi Condensation cultivator with a grudge. He does not confront the protagonist directly (the protagonist is Foundation Establishment by then, far above him), but he aligns with the Azure Sword Sect's expansionist faction, seeing it as a path to power and a way to spite the protagonist. He dies in Act 3 (session ~800, in the same ambush that kills Liang Zhu) — killed by Pei Liang's harvest-technique, his anchor harvested. The protagonist perceives, in Zongde's harvest-residue, the resentment that defined his life. The protagonist grieves him, complicatedly.
  - **If the player loses:** Zongde is publicly victorious. His arrogance deepens. He continues the hustler's techniques and develops a somatic deviation (a false circuit, doc 24 §1.2) at 28. He becomes ill. The protagonist (now studying with Old Chen) returns to the village and helps him recover — a reversal of the duel's outcome. Zongde, humbled by illness and the protagonist's kindness, becomes a reluctant ally. He never cultivates again (the deviation's damage is permanent), but he becomes the village's lay practitioner — the one who knows enough qi-theory to advise the lineage council. He marries, has children, dies at 62 (session ~300) of the deviation's long-term complications.
  - **If the player yields:** Zongde is surprised. The relationship is reset. Over the next decade, they develop an unusual friendship — the rival who was yielded to and the cultivator who yielded. Zongde never becomes a cultivator, but he becomes the protagonist's mortal anchor to the village — the one who writes the protagonist letters (via Wang Lun, then via Wang Lun's students after Wang Lun's death) about the village's doings. He dies at 70 (session ~400), of old age. The protagonist, returning for the funeral, perceives in Zongde's residue the texture of a long life lived without cultivation — a rarity in the protagonist's experience. The protagonist grieves him as a friend.

The pattern: the protagonist is repeatedly absent for the deaths of those they loved first (Wang Xiaodi, Wang Lun), then present for the deaths of those they love later (Wang Shouzheng, Old Chen, Liang Zhu). The shift from absence to presence is the protagonist's arc across the three acts — the cultivation of being there. The genre's central tragic engine (time-debt) is not that the protagonist cannot save the dying; it is that the protagonist learns, slowly, to be present for the dying. This is the teaching Old Chen gave them, in the failed breakthrough (Scene 4): cultivation is about wholeness, not power. Wholeness, in the end, is presence.

### 4.7 The pattern of deaths (prose)

The death schedule has a shape, and the shape is the game's argument. The protagonist begins the game having already failed to be present for one death (Wang Xiaodi, two years before game start). The first act compounds the failure: Wang Lun dies the winter before the first return, and the protagonist is at the hermitage, practicing for Foundation Establishment, and does not come back. The protagonist arrives at the first return (session ~100) to find a new tablet and a fading residue. The second grief of absence is, in some ways, crueler than the first — Wang Xiaodi was a child, and children die; Wang Lun was a man in his middle years, and the protagonist might have been there if they had chosen to be. The guilt is the same shape but a different weight.

The second act begins the turn. Wang Shouzheng dies in the autumn of the protagonist's age 34, and this time the protagonist returns in time. They sit at the bedside for three days. They answer the lineage head's last question (what cultivation is like, whether the path was worth it). The protagonist, for the first time, is present for a mortal death. The grief is different — not the guilt of absence but the grief of presence, which is heavier in a different way, because presence does not undo the death, it only witnesses it. Lady Chen of the hermitage dies in the same arc (session ~300), and the protagonist is not present — but this death is Old Chen's grief, not the protagonist's, and the protagonist grieves it secondhand, through Old Chen.

The third act completes the turn. Old Chen dies at the winter solstice of the protagonist's age 130, and the protagonist arrives the morning after — almost in time, but not quite. The protagonist perceives, in the fading practice-route, the teacher's last lesson: a whole death. Lady Chen (the mother) dies at 82, and the protagonist arrives three days after — almost in time, again. The protagonist finishes her last cloth. Liang Zhu dies at session ~800, and the protagonist is present — fully present, retreating, perceiving the dispersal. The protagonist screams. The grief of presence, by the third act, is the grief of being unable to save someone while standing next to them. This is the genre's deepest grief, and the game's deepest teaching: cultivation does not make you able to save the people you love. It makes you able to be there when they die. The being-there is the cultivation.

The death schedule is therefore not a list of sad events. It is the game's central curve: absence (Wang Xiaodi, Wang Lun) → partial presence (Wang Shouzheng) → almost-presence (Old Chen, Lady Chen) → full presence (Liang Zhu). The curve is the protagonist's cultivation of presence. The "found a lineage" ending (doc 26 §7.1) is the curve's resolution: the protagonist, having learned to be present, founds a lineage whose first teaching is presence — the healer-track (Wang Meili's path) whose first discipline is to be at the bedside of the dying. The protagonist's grief becomes the lineage's first teaching. This is the game's thesis, expressed as a death schedule.

### 5.1 What remains unnamed

### 5.2 Reviewer voices (per the doctrine: "Exhibit reviewer voices; do not self-certify")

**Reviewer A (the genre traditionalist):** "The antagonist's grief-as-motivation is a Western psychological framing imposed on a xianxia antagonist. The genre's antagonists are motivated by ambition, by Dao-comprehension, by sect politics — not by dead children. Pei Liang as a grieving father is sentimental. The Zhou Wugui line, by contrast, is genre-appropriate. Keep the line; reconsider the grief."

**Reviewer B (the simulationist):** "The death schedule is a strong spec, but the 'almost in time' pattern (Wang Shouzheng present, Old Chen and Lady Chen almost-present) risks feeling contrived if the simulation enforces it. The schedule should be the *likely* outcome given the protagonist's choices and the world's motion, not a scripted inevitability. If the player chooses to stay in the village, they should be present for more deaths; if they choose to travel, fewer. The curve (absence → presence) should emerge from play, not from script."

**Reviewer C (the social-simulation designer):** "The 15 named households are a good spine, but the kinship map (§1.4) is described in prose, not specified as a relationship graph. The downstream social simulation needs an explicit relationship graph (nodes = NPCs, edges = kinship/trade/obligation/tension, edge-weights = strength). This document gives the prose; the graph is the next document's job. Note: the graph must be generator-facing for the 16 unnamed households, with the 15 named households as the seed."

**Response:** All three points are taken. The genre-traditionalist's concern about Pei Liang's grief is acknowledged but held: the game's thesis (doc 26 §2.4) is that the personal is primary, and the antagonist's grief is the load-bearing personal element. The simulationist's concern about the death schedule is correct — the schedule is the *likely* curve, not a script; the protagonist's choices (to stay, to travel, to return) modify it. The social-simulation designer's concern about the relationship graph is correct — the graph is the next document's job; this document provides the prose and the named nodes. The three concerns are downstream-document concerns, not corrections to this document.
